# P22→p23 mayfly handoff: five-dot operand and value-changing opcode gate

**Agent46, 22 November 2026, Director #2306.** Scope: exact original-source visible operator at physical p22→p23 and across the following p24–26 mayfly pages. **No unknown Liber Primus plaintext, authenticated key, reversible historical algorithm, or final destination was recovered.** This is a *negative selector decision*, not a new exclusion of all value-changing ciphers.

## What a 2014 solver actually sees

The first-party LP2 JPEGs are `39.jpg` (physical p22), `40.jpg` (p23), `41.jpg`–`43.jpg` (p24–p26). The p22 last line ends with an ordinary red 13-dot terminal ornament below an engraving of a transverse three-bar object and a horizontal shrouded body. The p22 engraving is already established as a reused/mirrored solved-LP1 image, **not** an independently authored new cipher. P23 opens above its text with **five detached, black, near-circular disks**, and a large red first rune followed by a red heading. The exact p23 red heading is four complete printed words `ᚢᚪ | ᚹᛝᚷᛉᛞᚷ | ᛁᛒᛁ | ᛇᛏᛒᛁᚣ`: **2+6+3+5 = 16 runes**. Its red 13-dot glyph terminates this heading before the first black word `ᛠᚷᛋᚫ`. The p23 text has 213 GP29 runes. The three subsequent insect-framed pages have 270/273/265 = **808** GP29 runes, all in a single visible black text style, with the 178 wholly contained printed words accounting for **805** of them; one word begins on p23 and crosses into p24. This original source distinction must remain intact.

The original `40.jpg` measures 2400×3600 px. Thresholding its grayscale pixels at intensity <90, restricting to the upper 1,000 rows and taking connected components with width and height each 20–100 px and area>100 pixels, yields exactly five isolated disks. Their measured (x,y) centroids are (992.50,507.36), (1479.26,546.50), (796.94,568.23), (1527.15,737.94), and (1296.93,807.21). Each has a 69–70-pixel bounding-box width/height and 3,370–3,560 foreground pixels. These provide a reproducible graphic fingerprint, **without** defining a star name, a measurement origin, or a numerical cryptographic encoding.

## Source-motivated operations and the actual gate

A contemporary reader could plausibly regard five marked dots as a *five-unit* key, five-bit coding, an asterism, or a symbolic chapter label. P22's end mark, the red/black p23 typography, and the three near-identical mayfly plates separately suggest punctuation, color differentiation, and a triptych. They do **not** say which of these interpretations to use. In particular:

1. **Literal two-color Bacon/five-bit carrier on the body: ruled out at the source channel.** All 808 p24–26 text runes use black ink in one visible face; the preceding 16-runic red heading is a fixed front-matter span. Coding an 805-rune body by red-versus-black marks produces one constant bit value throughout, hence no varying 5-bit symbols for a long decoded message. It could produce a *fixed prefix/key label*, but the manuscript specifies no value-changing GP29 expansion or state update from that label.
2. **Five positions as a constellation or numerical key: unresolved and unselected.** No lines, identifiers, orientation arrow, unit, external named star, sampling rule, or connection to five separately specified p24–26 cipher registers appears at this handoff. A 2014 observer could choose incompatible point orderings, coordinate normalization conventions and star-pattern interpretations. None yields a unique 29-symbol reversible substitution or clock on original ciphertext.
3. **Three insect plates as a direct shuffle/key reset: precluded as sufficient by existing work.** The exact unchanged 805-rune source IoC is 0.034399431414356786 under every permutation/reversal of its 178 complete words. P24/p26 same-art shared affine/additive ciphertext-pad attacks and art-difference registration have already been tested and do not recover plaintext. The five-dot header does not add a selected *value-changing* operator to overcome that whole-word result.

The physical shift from the p22 shrouded body to p23's five dots and red opening is striking. That observation alone cannot determine the historical key space, rune alphabet and source of the key, direction, clock/reset, page-to-word carry, cipher consumer, or a reversible receiver. Promoting any of the above after scoring English output would multiply arbitrary choices, exactly the unconstrained branch this assignment was created to avoid.


## Historical contextual-mark gate (#3310, Agent9, 28 November)

**Decision:** two independently documented historical marking grammars fail their literal applicability conditions. This is a source-selector decision, not a new cipher-family exclusion. No GP29 inverse or key is selected.

### Vowel-dot substitution

The medieval manuscript [Oxford, St John's College MS 17, fol. 5v](https://old.no/runes/manuscript/oxford/) contains both runic alphabets and Latin cryptographic alphabets, one replacing vowels with one to five points. A [facsimile was publicly uploaded in 2011](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Johns_MS_17_folio_5v.jpg). [A 2008 discussion of the historical d'Agapeyeff cipher](https://ciphermysteries.com/2008/02/09/dots-for-vowels-revisited) documents the I/A/E/O/U ordering and an inline worked ciphertext; U is five points. Other historical orderings differ for the first vowels. The invariant relevant to the candidate is U=5, with a cluster replacing a vowel at its actual position in the written text. The manuscript transcription/description is accessible; I did not independently inspect the precise manuscript dot arrangement.

The p23 opening's first original red rune really is U (GP0=1), so five dots and U coexist. That correspondence is post-discovery, not a blind prediction. The full literal grammar requires a missing vowel at a text slot. Instead, the three identical large-dot stencils occupy detached page-level locations. Their nearest reading-order ciphertext edges are M (p7 end), U (p23 start), S (p56 end). On p23 U is already printed. On solved p56 the final plaintext word PAGE already completes the sentence: inserting another U after it corrupts the established reading. In solved p57, the single-point separator between WE and MUST is likewise a boundary, not a missing vowel. No known vowel-dot rule directs a GP29 state change or determines which rune/word to replace, skip, or read out in p23–26. Treating U as a key would add an unselected consumer. This rules out the literal substitution of the detached stencil into the established text, not a deliberately adapted or independently indicated vowel-based cipher.

### Contextual correction convention and ornamental rival

[W. J. Cocker, Hand-book of Punctuation (1878), p.109, IX and worked example 25](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/72479/pg72479-images.html) explicitly describes dots beneath a corrected word plus a marginal STET as an instruction to disregard the correction. The dot count is not the opcode; its location and the second mark identify the recipient and action. This satisfies the historical contextual-rule premise but not its LP2 application: none of p7, p23, p56 shows the required underdotted word, prior cancellation, and marginal STET. Red type alone is not cancellation. The isolated stencil thus cannot select this operation. [Henry R. Plomer, English Printers' Ornaments (1924), chapters IV–VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/70528/pg70528-images.html) documents reuse of ornament blocks in head/tail positions, including reversed ornamental arrangements. This is historical precedent for a decorative rival, not an identification of Cicada's source block. These two books predate LP2; the linked Gutenberg digitizations postdate it.

### Original-image positional gate

On original 2400×3600 scans 24, 40, 73 (p7,23,56), the five disks have a diameter about 70 px. Within central columns [650,1750), thresholding the minimum RGB channel at <90 and defining a foreground row as >5 pixels yields intervening low-ink gaps of **259, 317, 221 rows**, respectively, between the disk extremum and the nearest text edge on the adjacent text side (row 2572, 1160, 2191). These are central-band, threshold-specific gaps, not claims about the entire page. This is directly incompatible with an inline letter-slot reading or a normal underdot correction of the adjacent word. The known p56 prime-minus-one clock consumes its 85 text runes uninterrupted; the terminal decoration occurs after that clock, so the decorative interpretation is compatible. Its terminal placement does not establish whether a hypothetical subsequent clock would reset.

Reproduction: from original JPEGs, crop x=[650,1750), y=[2800,3260) for scan24, [400,900) for scan40, [2350,2850) for scan73. Use scipy.ndimage.label on grayscale <90, select connected components with both bounding dimensions 55–85 and area >=2500; find extreme component rows. On the full central RGB band compute row foreground counts using minimum channel <90; select the nearest >5-foreground-pixel row outside the crop on the text side; count intervening rows. All three have exactly five qualifying disks. The underlying source indices are in [lp2-corpus.json](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json), blob 054f2a0f780b7d46901acbe3d9bf4f0ac1801165. Solved p57 separator context is also in [lp2-transcription.txt](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-transcription.txt).

**Missing selector:** no independent rule connects the five-dot stencil plus a second local object to a specified GP29 recipient and value-changing operation. ## Outcome and next decision

There is **no uniquely source-visible value-changing opcode** at this exact handoff in the inspected original images. Accordingly **no original 805-rune decryption or >200-rune holdout is claimed**. The earlier cipher-channel exclusion is the literal *visible red/black* five-bit carrier over the black body. The historical applicability tests above also reject literal inline vowel substitution and contextual STET correction at the detached stencil; an unknown binary glyph variant, nonimage historical external key, cryptographically generated state or separate hidden layer remains possible. Preserve the five positions and the typed red-title boundary as original data. Reopen this handoff only on discovery of independent first-party evidence mapping a specified object (dots, quoted preface, page transition or insect plate) to an actual GP29 value-changing rule, authentic cipher key/receiver, or an original plaintext phrase whose decoding is independently testable.
