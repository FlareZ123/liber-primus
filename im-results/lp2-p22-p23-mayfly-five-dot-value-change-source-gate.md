## Source-motivated operations and the actual gate

P22's end mark, the red/black p23 typography, and the three near-identical mayfly plates separately suggest punctuation, color differentiation, and a triptych. They do **not** say which of these interpretations to use. In particular:

1. **Literal two-color Bacon/five-bit carrier on the body: ruled out at the source channel.** All 808 p24–26 text runes use black ink in one visible face; the preceding 16-runic red heading is a fixed front-matter span. Coding an 805-rune body by red-versus-black marks produces one constant bit value throughout, hence no varying 5-bit symbols for a long decoded message. It could produce a *fixed prefix/key label*, but the manuscript specifies no value-changing GP29 expansion or state update from that label.
2. **Five positions as a constellation or numerical key: unresolved and unselected.** No lines, identifiers, orientation arrow, unit, external named star, sampling rule, or connection to five separately specified p24–26 cipher registers appears at this handoff. A 2014 observer could choose incompatible point orderings, coordinate normalization conventions and star-pattern interpretations. None yields a unique 29-symbol reversible substitution or clock on original ciphertext.
3. **Three insect plates as a direct shuffle/key reset: precluded as sufficient by existing work.** The exact unchanged 805-rune source IoC is 0.034399431414356786 under every permutation/reversal of its 178 complete words. P24/p26 same-art shared affine/additive ciphertext-pad attacks and art-difference registration have already been tested and do not recover plaintext. The five-dot header does not add a selected *value-changing* operator to overcome that whole-word result.

The physical shift from the p22 shrouded body to p23's five dots and red opening is striking. That observation alone cannot determine the historical key space, rune alphabet and source of the key, direction, clock/reset, page-to-word carry, cipher consumer, or a reversible receiver.
