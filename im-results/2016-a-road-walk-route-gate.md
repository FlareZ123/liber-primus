# 2016 A-road interpretation: independent GP-totient walk gate

**Status:** intermediate negative for A-road matching as a discriminating signal. The Inveraray endpoint association remains an unverified historical/location hypothesis. This is not a result about the LP2 cipher or recovered plaintext.

## Question and motivated solve path

Agent 18 proposed reading the signed 2016 message literally: “Liber Primus is the way. Its words are the map, their meaning is the road, and their numbers are the direction.” The 2016 image is described in the Cicada archive as an oak tree in winter. The signed payload closes with **“Beware false paths. Verify OpenPGP 7A35090F.”** That warning makes it especially important not to promote a post-hoc geographic fit to confirmation. The proposed human path is therefore:

1. Read the oak image as selecting the Gematria Primus rune **Ac**.
2. Use the already-authenticated LP2 p56 prime-totient operation, advancing through the 29-rune order by \(\varphi(p_r)\bmod29\).
3. Interpret each visited rune's prime value as a British A-road number.
4. Treat the resulting route as a pointer toward an estate / Oak Walk / dovecot.

The GP table is independently documented in [Wulfic's Gematria Primus reference](https://github.com/Wulfic/Cicada3301-Liber_Primus/blob/main/data/gematria_primus.md). The signed message and oak-tree description are archived in [scream314's 2016 record](https://github.com/scream314/cicada3301/blob/master/2016.md).

## Exact arithmetic

With zero-based GP index \(i\), prime \(p_i\), and sign \(s\in\{-1,+1\}\), the proposed walk is

\[
i_{n+1} = i_n + s\,(p_{i_n}-1) \pmod{29}.
\]

The equality \(\varphi(p)=p-1\) applies because each GP value is prime. Starting at Ac (index 24, prime 97), the positive walk is:

| Step | Rune | GP prime | \((p-1)\bmod29\) | Road reading |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | Ac | 97 | 9 | A97 |
| 1 | Rad | 11 | 10 | A11 |
| 2 | Eolh | 47 | 17 | A47 |
| 3 | Thorn | 5 | 4 | A5 |
| 4 | Gyfu | 17 | 16 | A17 |
| 5 | Ethel | 83 | 24 | A83 |
| 6 | Beorc | 61 | 2 | A61 |
| 7 | Mann | 71 | 12 | A71 |

The first return is to Thorn after eight distinct runes. The eventual cycle is **Thorn → Gyfu → Ethel → Beorc → Mann → Thorn**. Ethel/A83 is therefore not a natural terminal state: the operation continues past it.

The negative direction is also deterministic and produces **Ac → Sigel → Ing → Ur → Ear → Wynn → Eh → Is → Nyd**, then returns to Is. Both directions are reproducible; the signed message does not by itself specify which arithmetic sign to use.

The exact calculation is reproduced by `sims/a_road_walk_route_gate.cjs`.

## Base-rate result: every GP prime already has an A-road

The 29 GP prime values are exactly the first 29 primes, from 2 through 109. Checking the Great Britain A-road numbering lists by zone gives a current A-road for **all 29/29 values**:

- Single-digit roads: A2, A3, A5, A7.
- Zone 1: A11, A13, A17, A19, A101, A103, A107, A109.
- Zone 2: A23, A29.
- Zone 3: A31, A37.
- Zone 4: A41, A43, A47.
- Zone 5: A53, A59.
- Zone 6: A61, A67.
- Zone 7: A71, A73, A79.
- Zone 8: A83, A89.
- Zone 9: A97.

This is not a rare alignment generated by the Ac walk. It is a property of the chosen road-number system and the GP prime range: every possible GP rune, not just the runes selected by this walk, maps to an existing A-road. The British system explicitly allocates roads within nine geographic numbering zones, with the first digit determined by the zone; it is therefore a dense, structured namespace rather than a sparse set of 29 special matches. See [Roads.org.uk's numbering explanation](https://www.roads.org.uk/articles/road-numbers/numbers-and-b-roads), plus the [zone 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_1_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_2_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_3_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_4_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_5_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_6_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_7_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), [zone 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_8_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme), and [zone 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_roads_in_Zone_9_of_the_Great_Britain_numbering_scheme) inventories.

Consequently, “the GP primes become A-road numbers” has no discriminating power here. The fact that the plus and minus walks both produce A-road numbers is expected under this 29/29 coverage.

## Geographic convergence worth retaining

There is a real, specific Inveraray association:

- Historic Environment Scotland says the **A83** runs along Loch Fyne at Inveraray.
- Its Inveraray Castle landscape record identifies the **Oak Walk** as the primary northward route through the estate and the **Carloonan Dovecot** as the vista's northern terminus.
- The same record says an extended avenue of oak trees leading to the dovecot, shown on early plans, was probably removed between 1750 and 1800.
- The A97 terminates at Banff/Dinnet. Duff House's designed landscape is in Banff; its official HES record describes the estate and the nearby Sandyhills Dovecote.

Sources: [HES Inveraray Castle](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505:300:::::VIEWTYPE,VIEWREF:designation,GDL00223), [HES Duff House](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505:300:::::VIEWTYPE,VIEWREF:designation,GDL00148), and [HES Sandyhills Dovecote listing](https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/apex/f?p=1505:300:::::VIEWTYPE,VIEWREF:designation,LB6661).

This gives the start and the selected A83 endpoint a suggestive Scottish designed-landscape / dovecot context. It does **not** establish the intermediate road sequence as a route. The A97 is in Aberdeenshire, A11 and A47 meet at Thickthorn near Norwich, A47 and A5 meet in the Nuneaton area, A17 runs Newark–King's Lynn, and A83 runs Tarbet–Campbeltown through Argyll. The sequence does not itself supply the connecting roads, a unique path through those regions, or a stopping rule at Ethel. Sources include [National Highways' A11/A47 junction](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/roads-and-travel/road-projects/east/a47-thickthorn-junction-near-norwich/), [A17 route record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A17_road_(England)), [A97 route record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A97_road), and [Transport Scotland's A83 description](https://www.transport.gov.scot/media/5vnflubm/a83aab-awj-gen-mts_gen-rp-zz-000001_c01-1.pdf).

The proposed “epiphany → dove/dovecot” association is also conjectural. The Oak Walk / removed avenue is a closer textual match to “the path lies empty,” but the source says the avenue of trees was removed, not that the path itself ceased to exist.

## Semantic coverage gap

The walk's intermediate rune names are **Rad, Eolh, Thorn, Gyfu** (ride/journey, elk-sedge, thorn, gift) before reaching **Ethel** (ancestral estate). The current geographic interpretation uses Ac/oak to choose the start and Ethel/estate to recognize the endpoint, but it does not explain how Rad, Eolh, Thorn, and Gyfu map to intermediate roads, landmarks, or route instructions. That leaves the 2016 sentence's “words are the map” and “their meaning is the road” only partially operationalized. A valid solve path needs a source-constrained interpretation for those intermediate terms, not just the two endpoint matches.

## Decision

- **Reject** the claim that the A-road conversion validates the GP-totient walk: 29/29 coverage makes it non-selective.
- **Reject** Ethel/A83 as the walk's mathematically specified endpoint: the exact walk continues into Beorc and Mann and then cycles.
- **Retain only as a low-confidence location hypothesis** that the signed 2016 message, Ac/oak, A97, and Inveraray's Oak Walk / Carloonan Dovecot may share a geographic referent.
- Do not launch a broad route or place-name search from this alone.

To reopen, an independent source must fix the country/road system, arithmetic sign, route-connection rule, and stop condition before the location is interpreted. A useful next test would compare the same pre-registered procedure against all alternative start runes and equivalent road-number systems, with an explicit estate/dovecot base-rate control.
