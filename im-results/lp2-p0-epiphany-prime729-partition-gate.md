## A possible human step that gives a complete block map

The independently signed [January 2016 Cicada message](../resources/historical/cicada2016-4gq25-outguess.asc) says "epiphany seeks the devoted." The original 2014 first red heading on physical LP2 p0 has two complete GP29 words, eight and five runes. The existing [p0 signed-phrase analysis](lp2-p0-2016-epiphany-title-crib-gate.md) therefore considers **EPIPHANY SEEKS** as a **hypothetical** title crib. There is no authenticated declaration that this is its plaintext.

GP29 subtraction of that 13-rune proposed plaintext from the original 13 ciphertext runes yields the uniquely specified additive key indices:

**26, 24, 8, 19, 27, 24, 18, 3, 0, 8, 0, 16, 19.**

The existing title analysis observed that this derived key's GP prime weights add to **729**, identical to the original first three-page chapter's 262+266+201 = **729** source glyphs. Under the explicit **independent-uniform random GP29 title** null, the inferred thirteen key digits are uniform, and an exact coefficient convolution gives **41,422,038,766,646,980 / 29¹³ = 0.0040369883684** probability that their thirteen prime weights sum to729. This is approximately one chance in248 for any one candidate, before the multiple candidate titles and numerical patterns retrospectively examined. This mathematical null **does not** represent the creator's selection process or the true probability of deliberate authorship, but it prevents treating the arithmetic equality alone as decisive. A stronger source-language control freezes the actual thirteen red ciphertext runes and uses historically calibrated GP29 English spellings on **every contiguous eight-rune/five-rune word pair** from the existing independent Shakespeare, Gibran and Emerson texts. **4 of 449** eligible adjacent real-English word pairs independently yield the same 729 sum under the identical operation: Shakespeare's **CONSTANT TITUS**, **SICINIUS WHERE** (twice in its source); Gibran's **LAUGHTER RISES**. Emerson has none among232 eligible pairs. These are concrete competing phrases, not candidate decryptions, but they demonstrate how the arithmetic match can arise from ordinary English without carrying the specific signed 2016 semantics. The exact integer-null and all 449 native adjacent-phrase tests passed [Windows Node22 CI 43660946784](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43660946784). This exact match suggests a *new, executable consumer*: instead of repeating the 13 key symbols at every 13th position (already tested unsuccessfully), **each key symbol governs a consecutive block whose length equals that symbol's own GP prime weight**. Then no missing padding, fitted block phase, guessed key length or leftover position is required.

The thirteen complete block lengths in original physical page order are:

**103, 97, 23, 71, 107, 97, 67, 7, 2, 23, 2, 59, 71**, summing to **729**.

This is one precise full-chapter schedule that a human could calculate with the printed GP prime alphabet, conditional on accepting the title crib and a prime-length block operation. Rival explanation: the 729 match arises because many proposed 13-rune plaintext windows would generate comparable prime-key totals; the original manuscript does not say to turn a deciphered heading's key primes into block lengths. The 13-rune heading is itself included in the 729-cell footprint, so treating it as a separately encrypted header would require another independently indicated mode boundary.

The sign-reversed alternative is checked on exactly the same source-fixed boundaries. Both transforms re-encode their own outputs exactly, as any bijective transform must. A separate native solved-p57 GP29 message repeated to the same shape is planted under the **same** variable-length cipher and recovers all 729 runes exactly.

Permuting the already-shifted blocks, or transposing their symbols without another symbol-changing key, **cannot alter their empirical collision indices**, so no block-reading permutation alone repairs the direct fixed-shift family.

The historically attested solved-p56 plaintext-F transparency also yields a stronger exact obstruction for this one schedule. ## Stronger all-secret block substitution exclusion

The direct Caesar test above leaves an important possibility: perhaps each of the thirteen key-prime-length blocks has its own **arbitrary secret substitution alphabet** rather than its inferred single Caesar shift. This much larger secret family has a key-independent necessary condition that can be applied without guessing any alphabet.

For every source-frozen block, count unordered pairs of positions whose GP rune labels are identical. Any bijection of the 29 runes merely renames the labels, so the count remains **exactly identical** for every possible hidden substitution in that block. Position permutations **within** the block and permutations of whole blocks also preserve the pooled count. The 13 original blocks contain exactly **1,070** same-label pairs among **29,657** eligible within-block position pairs, giving pooled IoC **0.036079171865**.

Under the same frozen 13 block lengths, the three pre-existing ordinary-English source controls are encoded under the repository's established GP29 spelling mapping (TH/NG/EO/OE/AE/IA/EA; K/Q→C, V→U, Z→S). It remains an empirical comparison with the three stated reference registers, **not a mathematical exclusion of every possible English or other-language plaintext**. ## Entire thirteen-prime-block partition family: exact key-independent bound

**Stronger source-only test (Agent 20, 15 November 2026).** The fixed-heading interpretation is optional: what if **any** sequence of thirteen Gematria Primus prime weights summing to the original first-chapter 729 runes were valid, with a wholly independent and secret GP29 substitution alphabet inside every resulting block? This grants all possible title-derived GP-prime block lengths and all 29-symbol bijections per block without guessing or authenticating any heading. The original chapter is kept in its physical source order, including the thirteen printed red runes.

For each block, let E be the number of unordered position pairs with identical GP symbols, and D = L(L−1)/2 the number of possible pairs in a block of length L. Every arbitrary, fixed within-block bijection preserves E. **In fact a broader one-sided inequality holds for every fixed deterministic per-block single-rune map f, including many-to-one maps:** equal plaintext symbols necessarily map to equal ciphertext symbols, so E_cipher ≥ E_plain per block. A bijection gives equality. Hence a native-English passage whose minimum possible partition collision ratio is greater than the original ciphertext's maximum can never be encoded into it by any such deterministic per-block mapping, even if one grants irreversible homophones-by-merging. Randomized or position-changing homophonic output is a different model. A position transposition within any block or an interchange of whole blocks preserves the totals as well. Optimize the pooled collision statistic **ΣE/ΣD over every admissible thirteen-prime partition**, with no fixed key or fixed boundaries.

An exact integer dynamic program over thirteen blocks, source positions 0–729 and all 29 available prime lengths maximizes **24890 × ΣE − 1005 × ΣD**. Its global maximum on the untouched 2014 LP2 p0–2 ciphertext is exactly **zero**. One maximizing complete partition, in prime-length runes, is

**[31, 107, 83, 67, 61, 43, 79, 29, 31, 41, 67, 83, 7]** (sum729).

Those blocks have exactly **1,005 same-rune pairs among 24,890 possible pairs**, proving the strict key-independent bound that every such original partition has pooled index of coincidence **at most 1005/24890 = 0.04037766171153073**. This is an exact finite-combinatorial result independent of any guessed title, period, secret substitution key, English scorer or 13-block phase. The original chapter's three-bar crosses also visibly occur in 180°-reversed orientation, motivating a full source-rune reversal. **Reversing the entire 729-rune ciphertext leaves this maximum exactly unchanged**: each admissible prime-length partition of the reversed sequence corresponds one-to-one to a reversed list of the same prime-length blocks on the original, and the pooled collision count is identical under block reversal. Thus even a source-artwork-driven 180° reading cannot rescue any fixed substitution alphabet per block in this complete thirteen-prime family. This conclusion needs no additional parameter search.

For a robust *matched-family* control, independently sample all 4,761 GP29 native-English 729-rune reference windows (Shakespeare 1,718; Gibran 492; Emerson 2,551, window stride127) used in the preceding fixed-partition study. Give **each native reference window its own completely free thirteen-prime partition**, and globally minimize its own pooled collision ratio by the same exact integer DP. The criterion is the maximal integer score **1005 × ΣD − 24890 × ΣE**. A negative score means every partition of that reference passage has index **greater** than the original universal ceiling. Thus this controls for choosing source partitions differently, not merely applying the title-derived lengths to all controls.

## Decision and remaining route

Reject **the frozen 13-prime-length block schedule followed by one Caesar shift per block**, under the original GP-native prose and authentic F-bypass expectations. This is distinct from the older period-13, autokey, 27×27 spatial-path and title-key-sum tests; none of those used the derived prime weights directly as the 13 contiguous full-chapter block lengths.

The 729 equality remains a **conditional arithmetic clue**, not evidence of a deciphered heading or a key. A future use would need an independent original or authenticated historical instruction that assigns a different reversible operation to these 13 prime-sized blocks and predicts a held-out unknown-page plaintext passage. Additional arbitrary transpositions, byte-codecs or key adjustments do not follow from the present failure.
