## Original visible input, and the operation under consideration

Physical LP2 p0 starts with two words of 8 and 5 rune glyphs: **both are red**, separated by a small ordinary red word dot; the large red 13-dot marker follows the five-rune second word, at offset13. Encode those original runes using the published Gematria Primus **prime weights**, not the 0–28 rune indices. The first red word sums to **347**, and the second red word sums to **315**. These exact source quantities were previously recorded in [the red-word analysis](red-prime-totient-crosslink.md) and [the opening-selector report](lp2-p0-27x27-opening-selector.md); the following *difference plus prime-count* interpretation is a separate provisional proposal.

The two-word sum and difference are:

- `347 − 315 = 32`, directly selecting **physical p32** if the difference is a page number.
- `347 + 315 = 662`, and the ordinary prime-counting function `π(662) = 121`; physical **p32 contains exactly 121 original runes**. Equivalently `662 = p_121 + 1 = 661 + 1`.
- Their arithmetic mean is **331**, the authenticated 2014 Onion7 **7×7 magic-square center** already recognized in prior research. Additionally, `331 = p_67`, so its one-based prime ordinal is **67**: source image **67.jpg** is the exceptional **rune-free physical p50**, in the three-page **256-byte base-60 numerical table**. Source archive indices are `17 + LP2 physical page`.
- The separate counts `π(315)=65` and `π(347)=69` bracket image number **67** symmetrically. These are dependent calculations on the same two words, not extra statistical confirmations.
- The two opening **word lengths** give `8 × 5 = 40`. The **11×11** page32 square has exactly **40 outer-ring cells** and **81 interior cells**. Physical **p33 has an original 13-dot/red-heading-bounded 81-rune body** at page-local rune offsets [10,91). This supplies a concrete candidate boundary-to-interior transfer rather than a fitted phrase.

There is an independent, previously established historical clue for the proposed destination: the signed **2016 563×569 tree image** repeats the p32 tree illustration. Its width and height exactly match the **top-row (563) and left-column (569) GP-prime sums** of original p32 under the natural 11×11 row-major arrangement. One direct candidate is to use the p0 red-heading prime sums **347/315** as addresses in the 256-cell p49–51 table. Since `347−315=32` and the table is 32×8, modulo256 endpoints 91→59 lie in one column four rows apart; the inclusive path contains five cells. This is a motivated but unauthenticated reading: no first-party instruction specifies modulo256, index base, base60, mod29 or additive-key use.
