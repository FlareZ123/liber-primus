# Global GP30 predecessor cipher: an exact word-boundary immutability theorem

**Agent4, 23 November 2026.** This is an exact, key-independent result on original ciphertext under one simple *hypothesized* stateful cipher. It is not unknown plaintext, nor evidence Cicada selected this cipher.

## Original source and reversible model

The original 55 rune-bearing pages (physical p0–55, skipping numerical p50) contain **12,956** GP29 runes and **2,896** complete printed nonempty words, including **32** across physical page seams. Make one true completed-word gap a thirtieth GP30 symbol29, keeping every source line-wrap and page seam as a continuous word. The result is **15,852** exact source symbols.

Consider a **single book-global table of 30 wholly secret, arbitrary bijections of 30 symbols**. At every location the *preceding original ciphertext token* selects a private alphabet mapping the current ciphertext token to a plaintext rune or SPACE. The first output token must be a rune. Every row permits any 30! permutation; the whole cipher is reversible, since encryption can recover the preceding ciphertext symbol as it advances. Original word-gap positions are allowed to move.

## Stronger all-key exclusion: SPACE plus any plaintext rune

This is the broadest useful theorem for this model. Let N[r,s] be the original 30×30 ciphertext bigram contingency table. In any secret row-bijection, **two distinct plaintext symbols**, including SPACE and any chosen GP29 rune, must have **two different source preimages** in each row. Therefore, without presuming output word count, nonempty words, any word-boundary preservation, target language or any known key, their combined output frequency is bounded by the **two largest observed entries in every source row**:

    count(plaintext SPACE) + count(plaintext rune x)
      <= 1 + sum over 30 predecessor rows of (largest entry + second-largest entry)
      = 3,707

The extra one grants the first token any value, rather than fitting an initial state. This covers every possible book-global private 30×30 S-box even when it deliberately changes all printed word boundaries. The original itself has 2,896 spaces and a largest rune count492, safely within 3,707.

All **384 independently GP-native encoded, original-sized contiguous English windows** have **SPACE plus their most frequent rune** above 3,707, and therefore cannot be plaintext under *any* secret row permutations: Gibran minimum4,364 (128/128 excluded), Shakespeare minimum4,620 (128/128), Emerson minimum4,472 (128/128). Independent **creator-authentic Cicada solved-GP29** controls yield the same conclusion with the *exact original 2,896 word lengths*: 128 training bootstrap witnesses have minimum **4,383**, and 128 separately held-out solved-Cicada bootstraps have minimum **4,471**. The few missing length12/14 donor slots use deliberately flat uniform rune fillers. All **256/256** authentic-layout controls exceed3,707 despite this favorable dilution.

This is a *finite source-native plaintext-language exclusion*, since genuine unknown plaintext might be nonprose or have unusually flat symbol marginals. However the numerical **3,707 upper bound is exact and independent of the output segmentation**, whereas the equal-word-count immutability below is a stronger additional structural theorem for that output grammar.

## Stronger theorem: arbitrary placement of the page-seam key resets

The nine fixed chapter boundaries are not needed. To overrelax the whole *physically page-resettable* model, allow a cryptanalyst to choose **any nine or fewer contiguous segments**, putting their independent secret 30×30 predecessor tables at **any eight original physical page seams**, deliberately optimally after seeing the entire original ciphertext. For every possible page interval, compute its exact two-symbol S-box capacity; then solve a complete dynamic program that maximizes the sum over **all possible partitions of 55 rune-bearing pages**. Each segment's first token is granted free output. This exhausts every partition rather than selecting or tuning a particular reset map.

The exact all-secret max for k segments is **k=1..10: [3707, 3853, 3946, 4025, 4102, 4167, 4226, 4283, 4340, 4396]**; with page-private keys at all55 pages it becomes6,160. **Even the optimally chosen nine-zone bound 4,340** is below every one of the **384** independent native-English windows (global minimum4,364) and all **256** separately creator-authentic solved-Cicada GP29 windows (minimum4,383). Thus all **640/640** controls are excluded under **every** private-key partition with at most nine physical-page zones, even though its reset locations, 30×30 substitution tables and entire decoded word segmentation are arbitrary.

To reach merely the easiest sampled native English register by this necessary capacity bound, **at least ten independently keyed zones** with resets confined to original physical page seams are required; to reach even the smallest Shakespeare tested window requires15. This is a **lower bound on model complexity**, not a ciphertext decryption, unique true reset map, or evidence the creator used predecessor tables. Source-visible large punctuation *inside* pages, per-rune evolving cipher state, key cycles of length>1 and different plaintext registers are distinct models and remain untouched.

## Exact all-secret obstruction

Let N[r,s] count every adjacent ciphertext token pair in the complete original. For each secret state r there is exactly one source symbol s_r whose mapped plaintext is SPACE. Hence the number of output word spaces is the sum of N[r,s_r] across states, regardless of the other 29 secret mappings in each row.

Keeping every printed source-space selector gives **2,896** words. For each of the 29 previous-rune states r, choosing any other source symbol as the new output SPACE **loses at least 56 spaces**, and the smallest three possible deficits are **56, 60, 60**. Changing the previous-SPACE state to create a new boundary immediately after printed source SPACE can add **at most 117** spaces. Consequently, preserving exactly 2,896 output words permits at most two changed previous-rune states because three would lose at least176.

Yet **every possible rune s following an original source-space** occurs in original triples (r,SPACE,s) with **at least 25 distinct preceding rune states r** (maximum29). If the previous-SPACE state maps s to a new output SPACE, every one of those 25 source predecessors must suppress the *original* SPACE to avoid two adjacent plaintext spaces and an empty word. This would require at least25 changed previous-rune rows, contradicting the maximum two. Therefore the previous-SPACE state cannot create a new word gap. With no new spaces after source SPACE, *any* altered previous-rune state would lower the total number of gaps. **All original 2,896 word-boundary positions must remain unchanged** under every possible secret table in this model, provided the plaintext has the same count of complete nonempty words.

An exhaustive collapse of the enormous arbitrary-key class to its single SPACE preimage per row yields **400 exact word-count-feasible selector profiles**. On the complete original manuscript, **399** generate at least one illegal adjacent plaintext SPACE, and the **only valid configuration** leaves every printed boundary untouched. An independent explicit 30-state bijective encrypt/decrypt plant changes GP rune identities while exactly round-tripping all 15,852 source symbols and preserving all word breaks.

**The exact original word count need not be assumed for a stronger bound.** If the previous-SPACE row keeps source SPACE as its output-space selector, every changed previous-rune row strictly reduces the original 2,896 spaces. If that previous-SPACE row instead creates a gap after source rune s, the original 25–29 different triple predecessors require the corresponding 25–29 rune rows to suppress printed source spaces. Giving every required row its most favorable alternative and allowing all other key choices, the **largest possible nonempty-word output is only 991 spaces**, attained as an optimistic upper bound at s=26. Thus the full book-global first-order secret-bijection family has **no valid nonempty-word output with more than 2,896 spaces**, regardless of its actual plaintext word count. It either keeps the after-source-SPACE rule fixed (at most2,896 gaps), or radically reduces its word count (at most991 gaps). The independent raw rowwise upper bound **3,013** remains valid *only if illegal empty-word sequences are allowed*. Among 384 frozen original-length native-GP30 English windows, **376/384** have more than2,896 spaces (Gibran121/128, Shakespeare128/128, Emerson127/128) and are therefore unattainable under any global key even without an equal-count assumption. The authentic clear LP2 p57 independently authenticates the rune spelling converter. This is a finite, style-dependent comparison; the conditional algebraic bound itself is exact.

## Limits and causal consequence

Shared **book-global predecessor-state rows** are essential. Repeating the capacity calculation with fully independent per-chapter private rows gives **3,077** maximum spaces; fully page-private rows give **3,795**. Those different models, a plaintext with a **different word count**, plaintext-dependent state, evolving keys, non-bijective maps with supplied side information or a variable-length receiver are not covered by the exact boundary-immutability theorem. The solved p56 cipher preserves printed word breaks but does not establish how many words the unknown pages contain. No original source currently supplies this predecessor-table instruction.
