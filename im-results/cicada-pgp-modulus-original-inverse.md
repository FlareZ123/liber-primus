# Historical Cicada signing-key modulus as an LP2 cipher source

**Evidence:** source-verified historical cryptographic key, reproducible original-input negative for an explicit reversible cipher family, and independent native-GP recovery controls. **No unknown LP2 plaintext, authentic cipher key, or hidden destination recovered.**

## Source and hypothesis

Cicada's authenticated 2012 OpenPGP key (fingerprint **`6D854CD7933322A601C3286D181F01E57A35090F`**) precedes the Liber Primus release. The key's public RSA modulus is a concrete 4,096-bit source artifact. A 2012 puzzle message also said *"The key has always been right in front of your eyes."* In its original setting this was a PGP-authentication clue, so its reuse as an LP2 keystream is **only a hypothesis**. The 2012 UID integer `845145127=3301×509×503` was the well-known original image-dimension solution; its factorization is historical prior art rather than a new finding here.

The exact public-key armor is retained in [resources/historical/cicada-3301-2012-public-key.asc](../resources/historical/cicada-3301-2012-public-key.asc), mirrored from [krisyotam/cicada3301 PGP archive](https://github.com/krisyotam/cicada3301/blob/main/pgp/key/cicada-3301-public-key.asc), source Git blob `d44aec389e2f346c6b9d149d4c61e890f62979c9`. The [CommonJS reproducer](../sims/cicada_pgp_modulus_square_inverse.cjs) parses the first OpenPGP v4 packet, asserts RSA exponent 65,537, exactly 4,096 modulus bits and the complete SHA-1 public-key fingerprint *before* constructing a candidate key. The modulus satisfies **N mod 4 = 1**; no knowledge of its prime factors or the Blum-prime condition is claimed. The following is a **modular-squaring generator**, and should not be represented as a proven secure Blum–Blum–Shub instance.

## Fully specified original-source inverse

For each fixed historical seed `3301`, `3299` (highlighted twin prime in the LP2 p15 square), `0x7A35090F` (the original key ID), and `1595277641` (the prior 2013 audio clue), generate `x_(t+1)=x_t² mod N`. Sample either the low **eight bits of each successive state** or eight successive **low bits assembled most-significant-first**. Use the starting integer or one initial squaring before the first sample; reset at the original entire book, published section positions, or physical rune-page boundaries. The decoded register is either `P=(C−s*(byte mod29)) mod29` or the exact invertible GP28 ciphertext-difference logarithm with the EA zero-step rule, with both `s=−1,+1`. All seeds, sample conventions, initial states, reset boundaries, GP register directions and signs are fixed independently of the original plaintext.

This yields **192 explicit finite original-cipher inversions** over all **12,956** unsolved LP2 ciphertext runes. The best candidate was audio-source seed 1,595,277,641, book clock, one source-square per output byte, no initial warm-up, GP28 difference carrier and sign−. Its measured native Shakespeare-GP unigram average is **−4.20268512 nats/rune**, IoC **0.0352669231**, and GP-E count **475**. A separate 12,956-rune native-GP source control has **−2.95984494 nats/rune**, IoC **0.0601328440**, and **1,481** E runes. Original ciphertext itself has IoC **0.0344783979**, only **439** E runes. The original result remains close to uniform and yields no independently readable unknown plaintext.

Each of the 192 candidate original inversions re-encrypts its entire original ciphertext under the exact same convention. Separately, 192 native-source GP-English plants have their genuine key applied and recover the entire planted text, **2,487,552/2,487,552** rune positions in aggregate. Since all reversible keys automatically re-encrypt source ciphertext, only the independent plants validate method correctness; neither establishes an LP2 key.

For the separate **256-byte numerical table**, eight direct source masks (four seeds × two modular-squaring sampling conventions, book clock, no initial warm-up) were XORed with original printed row-major bytes. They produced between **84 and 112/256 ASCII-printable** bytes. The highest-printable output begins `2a2626bb60ec7f1858f5c833a3b3b929`. All eight 256-byte planted ASCII originals recover exactly with the true mask. This finite mask family offers no recognizable original table plaintext.

## Literal RSA-modulus byte pad: a separate fixed source reading

The original 4,096-bit public modulus also provides **512 authentic source bytes** directly, without invoking RSA squaring or a new hash. A separate source-locked inverse takes those bytes as a 512-periodic key, in original or reverse order; resets its clock per book, published rune section, or physical rune page; and uses either sign in the same exact GP29 additive or reversible GP28 differential/EA register. These are **24** explicit original-input models, rather than a hidden-parameter search. Each original model re-encrypts its 12,956 source runes, and an independently planted native Shakespeare-GP text is recovered in all **310,944/310,944** aggregated test positions.

The highest original source-language score is only **−4.21797991 nats/rune** (forward modulus bytes, per-page clock, positive additive sign), with IoC **0.03449091** and 470 E runes. The independent native-GP control scores **−2.95984494 nats/rune**, with IoC **0.06013284** and 1,481 E runes. No original text is revealed. ## A ciphertext-constrained soft-rejection inverse

The observed original ciphertext has an unusually low adjacent-doublet frequency. A proposed *soft rejection* layer might discard tentative repeated ciphertext symbols while consuming an additional keystream draw. This raises a real cryptanalytic obstacle: even possession of the correct source keystream will seem to fail if secret skipped draws are not restored.

A useful exact condition substantially reduces the uncertainty. Write tentative additive encryption as `T_i(j)=P_i+s K_j (mod 29)`, with original accepted glyph `C_i`, previous accepted glyph `C_(i−1)`, and a fixed *same* plaintext rune `P_i` for all retries. If the encoder rejects `r≥1` tentative glyphs at clock positions `t,...,t+r−1` because each equals `C_(i−1)`, then finally emits `C_i` with key draw `K_(t+r)`, **necessarily**

`(C_i−s K_(t+r)) mod29 = (C_(i−1)−s K_(t+j)) mod29` for **every** `0≤j<r`.

This condition is independent of unknown plaintext and its natural-language scorer. For `r≥2` it additionally forces all rejected keystream residues equal modulo29. It permits a **key-checked skip graph** in which each continuation uses 0–3 hidden draws and only mathematically admissible extra draws are proposed. The [reproducible CommonJS HMM](../sims/pgp_modulus_skip_hmm.cjs) uses a 384-hypothesis Viterbi beam with smoothed GP trigrams trained on a disjoint Shakespeare portion. It assigns a fixed 0.82 probability of rejecting a tentative repeat and 0.18 of retaining it; the 0.82 reflects the independently observed original doublet deficit, not a fitted historical key. This is a conditional inverse of a precisely stated cipher architecture, and its guarantee does not extend to other rejection/update rules.

**Blind original-source training test, no skip locations supplied to the decoder:** an independent **2,048-rune Shakespeare GP** passage encrypted under authentic signing-modulus modular squaring (source seed 3301, low-byte sampling, additive plus sign) has **56 actual rejected draws**. The HMM independently identifies **56** and reconstructs **2,048/2,048** original plaintext runes exactly. Crucially, this plant is *outside* the GP-trigram training interval. A second independent GP plant comes from **2,048 authentic solved LP1 source runes**: the same known key and source-locked soft filter reject **55** draws, and the identical HMM predicts **55** and recovers **2,030/2,048** original runes (99.12%), including substantially correct known historical prose. The latter control is out-of-domain for the Shakespeare language model.

Against **the actual first 2,048 unsolved LP2 ciphertext runes**, fixed initial key clocks derived from source 3301 or the authentic signer key ID, both byte-sampling rules and both additive signs give **eight** full HMM original-input trials. The best original mean GP-trigram objective is only **−4.23416047 per rune** (3301 seed, eight successive low bits, positive sign), versus **−2.31003397** for the exact Shakespeare plant and **−2.45829093** for the solved-Cicada LP1 plant. Their original outputs are visibly non-prose. No plaintext or key has been recovered from the source-original LP2 material under these eight fixed book-start conventions. The strong independent positive controls make the negative informative for **this particular key family and rejection rule**.

### Eight-section challenge, complete original manuscript coverage

The same fixed-key HMM was then evaluated independently over the source-transcribed rune sections, of lengths **729, 1,145, 1,738, 1,894, 1,021, 1,433, 1,680 and 3,316** (12,956 positions in total). Each section resets its key clock exactly at the canonical transcription's section marker. Both historically fixed signing-key seeds, both byte samplers, and both additive signs are frozen in advance: **8 original-data hypotheses per section, 64 tests in all**. The known 91-rune ambiguity between the sixth transcription section marker and the later red visual heading remains a boundary convention, rather than a key-selected shift.

The original section-by-section **best-of-eight** native GP-trigram scores range from **−4.22154886 to −4.19058245 nats/rune**. No section decodes into independently readable plaintext; allowing the soft repeat-rejection layer has not unlocked even an isolated original passage. A distinct original-length native-GP source control is encrypted under one authentic seed per section with the same unknown rejection clock, then decrypted without revealing any of its hidden skipped positions. Across all eight authentic lengths, the HMM exactly predicts **each section's total number of rejected draws** and recovers **12,943/12,956 planted runes (99.89966%)**. Its own GP-trigram scores range from **−2.43547 to −2.24466**, strongly separated from every original candidate. Training begins at rune 22,000 in the source Shakespeare sample, while planted controls use source runes 3,000–15,955, so the source-control samples are disjoint from language-model training.

This is a **full original-section-coverage negative with demonstrated native-plaintext sensitivity** for the defined source-locked RSA modular-square stream, deterministic skip admissibility, sign and counter-reset conventions. It is not a general disproof of the soft-rejection hypothesis or of all original LP2 keys. ## Cryptanalytic decision

These source-locked modular-squaring output conventions **do not produce native-GP prose or an obvious plaintext table payload**. This limits specific public-modulus seed/sampling/carrier interpretations. It does not exclude historically unknown IVs, nonlinear key schedules, different output samplers, a binary intermediary, altered segmentation, or different plaintext languages. The signing key was historically used for authentication; no evidence currently connects its RSA modulus to LP2 encryption. Future work should require an independent original clue specifying the modulation before expanding the family.
