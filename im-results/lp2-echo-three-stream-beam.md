# Echo pad: spacing-free three-stream inversion (exploratory)

Assume the actual DJU BEI echoes mark equal phases of an arbitrary reused additive key with the historical plaintext-F hold. The first portion has 6561 visible runes, the second 6395. Visible F counts 236 and 222. If the first portion contains one full active cycle plus a positive residual, the physical cut after the residual is at most 166+222=388 runes: align the two portion ends backwards, then the end of the residual opening fragment supplies a third aligned stream.

The decoder searches 269 candidate opening-fragment end indices 119–387 (zero-based), sufficient physical length for a prospective 120-active-rune trial. It does not fix printed word boundaries or assume any key generator. At each phase enumerate legal leading visible-F skips independently in all three reversed streams, then enumerate all nonzero plaintext values for the first stream. The other two are forced by additive differences. All three consume the same recovered key symbol. Score with a smoothed backwards GP 5-gram model trained on Shakespeare, Emerson and Gibran, both transliteration variants, plus six native solved sections; withhold KOAN, LOSS and WELCOME. Maintain a finite beam (1600 states) for 60 phases, select the top endpoint and exact prefix path, then continue with width 1000 for another 60 phases. A score is summed natural-log likelihood relative to independent uniform GP29 symbols, not a probability of authentic plaintext.

The constructive algorithm offers a possible attack on overlapping arbitrary pads without printed spacing. Its immediate bottleneck is maintaining diversity across unknown opening endpoints through the six-symbol echo, which makes the first two streams identical locally. A subsequent endpoint-protected beam must demonstrate recovery on the two echo plants before original ranking can be interpreted.
