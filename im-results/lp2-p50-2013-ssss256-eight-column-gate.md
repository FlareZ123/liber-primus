# Complete historical-ID gate: p50's eight columns cannot be 2013 SSSS shares

**Classification (Agent 1, 12 November 2026):** A source-constrained, **all-historical-identifier exclusion** of the two canonical **8×32-byte Shamir-share key serializations** of the unmodified 32×8 grid. Every ordered assignment of the original first six byte columns to six distinct IDs from Cicada's authentic 2013 share-ID set **01..10** fails a necessary 256-bit field equation. Therefore none of the **10P8 possible assignments** of all eight complete columns to eight distinct historical IDs can be generated by one 2013-style `ssss` monic quintic. This recovers **no unknown LP2 plaintext, key, destination, or generator**, but resolves the prior natural-ID ambiguity for this complete source-byte model.

## Original human-readable clue and historical cipher

LP2 physical pp49–51 (original archive JPEGs `66.jpg`–`68.jpg`) contain **32 rows × eight columns** of two-digit radix-60 entries. Their standardized source interpretation is a complete **256-byte** array, SHA256 `3b9b07d9a26e6d55c432d94d2661fdff3c2b348daed06821f2bdb23184a4b290` ([exact input](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json)). Eight printed columns are exactly **eight 32-byte payloads**. That is a plausible visible shape for **eight Shamir shares of a single 256-bit secret key**.

Cicada's original 2013 poster/telephone puzzle demonstrably employed the **five-of-ten** `ssss` v0.5 protocol with decimal share identifiers **01..10**. Its exact historical implementation is available as [`ssss.c`, `field_init`, `horner`, `split`](https://github.com/osresearch/ssss/blob/master/ssss.c). The historical field size adapts to the full payload length. Actual 2013 shares were 176-bit, whereas the eight original LP2 columns are 256-bit: the same published `ssss` table prescribes its **specific degree-256 irreducible field polynomial**

\[
M(X)=X^{256}+X^{10}+X^5+X^2+1.
\]

The `ssss` threshold-five output polynomial has **leading coefficient identically one**, not an unconstrained degree-five term:

\[
Y(x)=x^5+c_4x^4+c_3x^3+c_2x^2+c_1x+c_0
\quad\text{in GF}(2^{256}).
\]

The original 2013 `ssss` diffuses c0 with XTEA after reconstructing the field constant; that later byte process does not change the polynomial consistency requirement. [Original protocol and code reconstruction](lp2-p23-26-ssss201-original-format-gate.md#why-the-exact-historical-protocol-matters).

An additional source-visible eight-way ordering candidate exists in the adjacent original chapter: the **eight red title runes on p40**, when sorted by GP29 index, select column permutation `[0,4,7,1,2,5,6,3]` ([earlier original red8/table test](lp2-p40-red8-grid32-columnar-inverse.md)). That makes an unknown or title-selected column assignment a live puzzle-design question. The full six-ID enumeration below **includes this ordering and every other eight-column permutation** under the historical 01..10 share IDs, so a separately tuned red-title permutation cannot rescue the source-native SSSS model.

The **hypothesis**, not a claimed author-selected instruction, is that the eight table columns are eight complete field-element shares. The exact test fixes each column as the **32 source bytes from top to bottom in big-endian order**, exactly the historical printed-hex field serialization. It does not alter a byte, transpose a row, or remap the base-60 digits.

## Complete all-2013-ID obstruction without fitting any secret

The field format permits a stronger test that removes the unknown share-ID objection. For any **six** distinct historical share identifiers \(x_0,\dots,x_5\) and corresponding complete field elements \(y_0,\dots,y_5\), the coefficient of \(x^5\) in the degree-five interpolation polynomial must equal **1** exactly:

\[
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{5}
  y_i\left(\prod_{j\ne i}(x_i\oplus x_j)\right)^{-1}=1
\quad\text{in GF}(2^{256}).
\]

Here \(\oplus\) is field addition (bitwise XOR), products and inverses use the **exact historical 256-bit field**, and all \(x_i\) are nonzero, distinct values from **01..10**. This necessary equation is independent of the five secret coefficients and of XTEA. It uses **six shares**, because five data values alone always admit a monic degree-five polynomial after subtracting the lead.

An exact exhaustive enumeration of **all 210 six-ID subsets** and **all 720 bijections from the first six source columns onto each ID subset** checks

\[
\binom{10}{6}\cdot 6! = 10P6 = \mathbf{151,200}
\]

different original-byte assignments. The fixed equation passes **zero of 151,200**. The nearest wrong candidate still differs from the required all-field equality in **93 out of 256 bits**; this descriptive nearest distance is selected over the entire 151,200-candidate screen and is not a statistical plaintext finding.

**Consequence:** If all eight original columns represented any eight SSSS shares with distinct historical IDs 01..10, their **first six columns necessarily** form one of the exhausted ordered six-ID assignments. Since **none** does, all **10P8 = 1,814,400** possible eight-share ID/order assignments are excluded by the six-column necessary condition, without evaluating the last two columns for each assignment. The conclusion remains valid for arbitrary unknown secret c0–c4. Unlike the initial fixed 1..8 check, this outcome has **no remaining historical-ID or column-order loophole** under the source-preserving vertical byte reading and genuine `ssss` field.

## Second source-fixed layout: eight consecutive row-major 32-byte shares

For completeness, the printed 32×8 table also supports one other extremely literal eight-share reading: treat the canonical row-major byte sequence as **eight consecutive 32-byte chunks**, each representing four whole adjacent printed rows, without changing the order of a single byte. The original p40 red8 could also determine a chunk-to-ID permutation. This is different from the **eight vertical 32-byte columns** tested above, and it is the only additional full-byte serialization admitted here.

Run the **same monic-quintic six-share necessary equation** and exhaustive all-historical-ID census on the first six consecutive 32-byte source chunks. The result is again **0/151,200 exact matches**, with the nearest failed equation still wrong in **92 of its 256 bits**. Any eight-share assignment under IDs 01..10 would require a valid first-six assignment, so the **entire historical 10P8 space is excluded for this layout as well**.

## Separate textbook Shamir convention, included as a secondary control

Some implementations of ordinary five-of-eight Shamir sharing use a degree-**four** polynomial (leading coefficient at degree five **zero**), unlike the **source-authentic 2013 monic degree-five** protocol above. For the exact same six-point field interpolation formula and both original table serializations, change **only the required leading coefficient from 1 to 0**. No share ID or secret is refitted.

The all-historical-ID census gives **zero degree-four-consistent assignments of 151,200** for the eight vertical columns (closest still **94 wrong bits** to the required zero), and **zero of 151,200** for the eight consecutive 32-byte row-major chunks (closest **91 wrong bits**). The SFT includes an independently generated correct degree-four, eight-share positive control with scrambled original IDs `[9,3,10,1,5,7,2,6]`, correctly located by the same full enumeration.

This excludes the corresponding **textbook degree-four threshold-five variant** under the same original-byte/field/ID assumptions, alongside the already excluded genuine historical monic-quintic case. Neither result constitutes an arbitrary cipher-family exclusion, because a source-specified different field, encoding or transformation would change the inputs.

## Original-input reproduction and positive controls

The [single-file Node22 CommonJS SFT](../sims/lp2_p50_ssss256_eight_column_sft.cjs) asserts the original 256-byte SHA256, reconstructs the exact historical field modulus from the published source terms, implements GF(2^256) multiplication/inversion and the monic-quintic interpolation, checks the three unedited natural-ID withheld columns, and enumerates all **151,200** historical-ID assignments for **each of two source-native eight-share layouts** using precomputed field weights. It checks **both** exact leading-coefficient targets: one for the historically authentic monic quintic, and zero for the textbook degree-four alternative. It verifies the immutable minimum of **93 wrong bits**.

For an independent positive control, it constructs a source-native **32-byte ASCII secret** plus four deterministic field coefficients, computes all eight shares under the genuine **monic degree-five** algorithm, and recovers their constant exactly. A second positive control **scrambles source share IDs** to `[9,3,10,1,5,7,2,6]`; the same entire 151,200-assignment census finds that independently constructed correct first-six identifier mapping. This verifies the all-ID enumeration does detect a real unlabelled share arrangement.

## Decision

**Retire both the original eight vertical 32-byte table columns and eight consecutive row-major 32-byte chunks as shares of any single secret under Cicada's original 2013 `ssss` field and identifiers 01..10.** No secret fitting, AES brute force, LP2 rune cipher or extra digest tests are justified on the false extracted constant.

The exclusion is *conditional on the complete source-native column bytes and the 2013 historical format*. A separately motivated byte-order reversal, table-wide encoding transform, different field modulus or threshold, or use of the table as a non-Shamir key may be viable, but none follows from the original 2013 share instruction. No missing key or decoded unknown rune was recovered. This report replaces the weaker natural-ID-only conclusion, which is retained above as a descriptive subtest.
