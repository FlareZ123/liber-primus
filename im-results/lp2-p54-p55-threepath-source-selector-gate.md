## Two apparent selectors, and why neither is authenticated

### 1. Five-dot ornament → fifth prime before 1033

The floral artwork on original p55 visibly contains five-dot clusters. Prime **1009** is
exactly the fifth ordinary prime immediately preceding the historically emphasized 1033:

```text
1031, 1021, 1019, 1013, 1009
```

Thus a rule "start five primes before 1033" would uniquely select initial index168 and the
third surviving trajectory before looking at its decoded output.

This is attractive but fails source selection. The same five-point ornamental masks repeat
without pixel change throughout pp40–55; they are not a terminal-page-specific numeral.
The earlier source audit in #421 already establishes that fact. More importantly, the
same exact all-start prime/totient automaton has **no complete inverse on p40–53 under any
start**. The visible five-dot motif therefore cannot by itself be a demonstrated
prime-cursor initializer for the ornamented region. No original instruction says "five
primes before 1033" or supplies the backward direction.

### 2. p54 red heading → 168 by one-based rune ordinals

The original p54 chapter starts with a nine-rune red heading. Its canonical GP29 indices are

```text
24,19,21,23,27,2,14,10,19
```

whose zero-based sum is **159**. Summing the corresponding one-based ordinals
`index+1` gives **168**, exactly the surviving zero-based prime start that points to 1009.

This is also a post-hoc convention rather than an authenticated GP operation. Historical
Cicada book codes do provide precedent for **one-based coordinates**, but known Gematria
numeric operations use published prime weights or base-29 values; no solved stage was found
that sums `index+1` rune ordinals to choose a prime-stream start.

There is also a direct solved-page control against treating red-heading ordinal sum as a
general cursor rule: the five-rune red p56 heading has GP indices
`25,11,22,15,4`, one-based ordinal sum **82**, whereas the authentic p56
prime/totient stream starts at zero-based prime index **0** (prime2), not 82.

The p54 coincidence can therefore be preserved as a candidate relation, but it is not an
established source instruction.
