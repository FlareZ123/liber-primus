# Issue #2654: catchword field gate

## Result

Negative. A historical catchword is a detached word or word-fragment at the foot of a page that predicts the opening text of the next page. Ordinary continuation of a divided word does not meet that duplication condition. No typed catchword field was identified in the supplied 75-page original JPEG set (LP1 files 00–16; LP2 files 17–74). This is a visual field-identification result, not a theorem excluding arbitrary hidden encodings.

## Historical positive

Timothy Bright's *Characterie* (1588), 1888 exact-spacing/pagination reprint, preserves the detached footer **tarch** following a divided Plu- and the next page begins tarch; the following dedication leaf ends communi- and repeats **cate** at the footer, with the next leaf beginning cate. This is especially useful because even a split-word catchword still repeats the receiver text, rather than merely supplying a continuation. The 1888 reprint is available at https://www.forschungsstaette.de/PDF/Originale/Bright%20-%20Characterie%201588.pdf (advertisement and Epistle Dedicatorie). The PDF text/visual-element description was accessible; screenshot retrieval returned cache misses, so I do not claim direct visual inspection of the historical scan. Contemporary pre-LP2 discussion with examples: https://english.stackexchange.com/questions/65963/in-old-books-why-is-the-first-word-of-the-next-page-printed-at-the-bottom-of-th . TEI 2011 examples: https://tei-c.org/Vault/P5/2.0.0/doc/tei-p5-doc/en/html/examples-catchwords.html . Etherington and Roberts (1982), definition: https://cool.culturalheritage.org/don/dt/dt0611.html .

## Original-image comparison

I opened contact sheets of every supplied JPEG and separately magnified the upper/lower margins of LP2 p33–39 (JPEG50–56; top/bottom 30%). The strongest apparent match is the detached, two-row, six-sign angular motif beneath the p33 body, repeated above the p34–39 bodies. It is not the opening rune word of p34 or any of the six different bodies. Furthermore, p34–38 do not reproduce the motif at their feet. It therefore behaves as a running section motif rather than the historical footer-to-next-body-word receiver. Its semantics remain unknown; no claim is made that it is merely decorative or that it cannot be another kind of operand.

Other inspected cases: p0–2 small repeated loop-like ornament; p7 red final body line/dots; p49–51 multi-page numeric table; p53 final red body lines and moth; solved p56–57 body layout and ornaments; LP1 borders and numeric panels. None displays a detached lexical receiver predicting the next body opening. The previously established 32 carried words remain ordinary contiguous split groups, not duplicated catchwords.

## Decision

No selected source-side catchword receiver. Do not fund a catchword-driven permutation or broad seam-pair search. This does not constrain other functions of the p33–39 motif. Prior #2646 and #1345 provide separate historical-layout negatives. No plaintext or key recovered.
