## Exact necessary-condition theorem

For five base28 digits as a conventional 24-bit integer, \(28^5=17,210,368\) while \(2^{24}=16,777,216\). Therefore if the **first digit is 27**, the second digit can only be one of **0 through 8**; a second digit ≥9 necessarily represents an out-of-range integer even when all other digits are zero. This gives a decisive *secret-alphabet-independent* condition: among every group-leading whitened **source label**, at least one possible label must have **at most nine distinct following labels**. Whichever hidden source label is assigned to digit27 must satisfy that condition.

Every one of the **128** original five-digit models violates the necessary condition. The **smallest** observed number of distinct followers, minimized over all candidate leading source labels, is **21–26** depending on model. Every possible secret fixed 28-symbol alphabet therefore makes some observed packet exceed the 24-bit range. With the visible identity alphabet, 47–93 of the 2,563 full original five-digit groups lie outside 24 bits, but the stronger all-secret bound eliminates alphabet substitution as an escape.

For ten base28 digits as a conventional 48-bit integer, \(28^{10}>2^{48}\), and any group **starting in digit27** is out of range irrespective of the other nine digits. Thus at least one of the 28 whitened source labels must be absent from all packet-leading positions before selecting a secret 28-way permutation. In **all 128** source-key readings, **all 28 source labels** occur as first digits among the **1,281 complete ten-digit groups**. Again no secret fixed alphabet can make every standard 48-bit packet valid.

## Cryptanalytic consequence

The table bytes do **not** rescue the simplest variable-key version of the source-shaped binary packet idea, even if the apparent GP28 digits conceal an entirely secret fixed alphabet. This is a stronger, independent obstruction than a bad ASCII score or a failed PNG header. It excludes exactly the two unescaped, complete, fixed-alphabet packet codings with the 128 stated table applications and original framing. A source-instructed **position-changing alphabet**, packet escape sidecar, nonlinear block operation, or unknown variable-length transport would be a different model, and this experiment cannot reject it.

The currently actionable missing input is an authenticated instruction that both chooses a **binary-packet transport** and supplies its **key or full reversible state schedule**. A 256-cell table and a number of divisible GP28 steps alone do not justify constructing arbitrary new whiteners. No successor claim of plaintext or historical encryption architecture is warranted from this result.
