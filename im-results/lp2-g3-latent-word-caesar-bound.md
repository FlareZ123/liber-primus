## Stronger count-only bound: arbitrary replacement lengths

Drop the entire printed length multiset. Keep only the count K, max true word length L, OOV budget B=floor(.05K), contiguous ordered rune tape. Every known word has length at most 3 plus its positive excess, and every OOV word length at most L. Compute the exact maximum G of summed positive excess l−3 across disjoint matching intervals of lengths 4..L using a backward weighted interval DP that even permits uncovered gaps and ignores number of chosen intervals. Thus every valid segmentation has

    N <= 3K + (L−3)B + G.

Across the full tape there are 1,181 matching length-four intervals, 66 length-five, seven length-six, none length7–24. Maximum G=1,024 (the relaxed interval optimum). For L=14 the upper bound is 11,296 runes, needing >=295 OOV groups. Even granting L=24, upper bound 12,736 <12,956, needing >=155 OOV vs budget144. All nine independent chapter scopes also fail at both caps; the 24-rune lower OOV bounds in chapter order are 10,14,21,22,13,17,20,38,4 versus budgets 7,12,19,21,11,16,18,33,3. These are exact necessary conditions for the stated finite lexicon, not universal claims about English or every imaginable word length. The larger 24-rune bound is deliberately generous, and its margin is modest. If true word count is different, this bound changes: solving the exact necessary inequality `3K + (L−3)floor(0.05K) + 1024 ≥ 12956` gives K≥3,362 at L=14 and K≥2,949 at L=24. The latter is only 53 above the printed count (about 1.83%). These are relaxed necessary minima, not feasible segmentation witnesses. Thus the conserved-count negative cannot be promoted to a general H-decoy exclusion; an independently justified bound on the true count or a stronger count-free receiver would be needed. The subsequent specific A1 test does not assume K.
