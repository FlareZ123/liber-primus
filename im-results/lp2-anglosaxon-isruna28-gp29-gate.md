# English coordinate runes and the LP2 opening staves

The p0 `3|3` ornaments are compatible with English group-and-position encoding. Two English manuscript witnesses independently give **E** from group 3, position 3. Both red p0 heading words have E in third position. A complete medieval English branch-rune chart with a source-fixed coordinate for Ior (GP IA) remains unestablished. Haigh's independent 1872 *editorial* coordinate chart supplies Ior at 4:5 and admits a complete GP29 projection, but does not supply an ancient worked cipher or a typed LP2 receiver. The historical evidence supplies a local reading and leaves the task of finding a decrypting operation open.

## Two manuscript anchors and the missing coordinate

| Witness | Worked or explicit registration | Ior evidence | Conditional p0 `3|3` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salzburg, St Peter a IX 32 | The tract explicitly specifies 8+8+8+4, group first. Three devices give `RACCO` from `1/5 4/1 1/6 1/6 1/4`. The branch-rune version instead prints `1/5 4/1 1/6 1/5 1/4` and reads `RACRO`, which Derolez treats as a copying error. Both sequences exercise fourth-group coordinate `4/1=A`. | Its 28-symbol row omits Ior. | **E** |
| Cotton Domitian A IX f.11v | Two dots sit immediately before H and three before T. Derolez reads them as ordinal markers for the old eight-rune divisions. The next two runes from T are B and E. | The first hand includes the rune with value `io` after Ear, on the next physical line. No comparable group marker occurs in the last 17 runes. | **E** |
| Hackness as reconstructed by Sermon | Figure 5 provides a four-by-eight chart covering every GP symbol. The ancient stone leaves 24 possible assignments between its branch counts and these rows. The preferred Bosa reading chooses `[4,1,2,3]`. | Fig. 5 supplies Ior in the late row. The preferred branch registration puts it at `1/5`, outside the eight reliably read coordinates. | **I** under Bosa registration, **E** under natural rows |

Salzburg is the sole Isruna-tract witness that explicitly separates the last four runes into a fourth group. The other four witnesses support eight-rune divisions, with the status of their added runes unresolved. Their worked words draw on the first 24 positions. See René Derolez, *Runica Manuscripta* (1954), pp. 116–117, 120–121, 132–133. [Digitized critical edition](https://old.katab.asia/special/Rene_Derolez_-_Runica_Manuscripta.pdf).

Domitian is independent of the five copies of the Isruna tract. Plate I (PDF p. 259), Fig. 5 (PDF p. 34) and printed pp. 7–15 show the two dotted anchors, the first-hand `io` value, the physical line break and its unresolved meaning. The visible anchors preserve E under at least three complete readings of the initial 33-rune material. With 8+8+17, Ior is `3/13`. With 8+8+12+5 and the last physical line starting group 4, it is `4/1`. With a regular eight-rune extension, 8+8+8+9, it is `4/5`. These are explicit witnesses to the ambiguity, and the source provides no worked fourth-group coordinate. ## Why the Hackness fragment cannot settle Ior

Sermon's Tables 4–5 use `4/8, 4/2, 2/4, 3/8, 1/1, 3/5, 2/4, 3/2`. Permutation 19 reproduces the eight symbols `D B O S A E O N` with Sermon's labels. Its sixth symbol is the GP `EO` at natural row 2 position 5, which has a different GP label from the E in row 3. Once the late futhorc row is assigned to observed group 1, **none of its six compatible row permutations touches Ior**. Swapping Ear and Ior in the late row preserves all eight worked outputs with the preferred permutation and changes Ior from `1/5` to `1/4`. The swap is an exact ambiguity witness for the stone coordinates. Figure 5 still fixes the order Sermon himself printed. [Sermon 2002, figs. 4–5, tables 4–5](https://proceedings.caaconference.org/files/2001/66_Sermon_CAA_2001.pdf).

R. I. Page's independent assessment calls Hackness the only known English rune-stone of this type. He treats the fragments as too worn to fix the chart and suggests the third observed group may require at least nine positions. If that reading is correct, a four-by-eight model cannot accommodate the full inscription. The Oxford St John's MS 17 f.5v and related Cotton Galba A II material contain mixed rune lists and adjacent Latin symbol codes rather than a group-position example for English Ior. The Franks Casket uses altered vowel signs. Those candidate witnesses provide no missing chart. [Page 1999, pp. 83–87](https://rodnovery.ru/images/knigi/an-introduction-to-english-runes.pdf) · [Hackness catalogue](https://chacklepie.com/ascorpus/catvol3.php?pageNum_urls=21&totalRows_urls=288) · [Oxford f.5v facsimile, publicly dated 2011](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Johns_MS_17_folio_5v.jpg) · [Folio column inventory](https://old.no/runes/manuscript/oxford/).

## Independent 1872 editorial coordinate chart (Director #3307, Agent8)

Daniel H. Haigh, *Notes in Illustration of the Runic Monuments of Kent*, Archaeologia Cantiana VIII (1872), pp. 167–170 and Plate I, [original article](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/65df7835178a9d2b20f8d501/t/675c1dbaeb41f6545f8fde00/1734090189511/kas-arch-cant-vol-008-1872-section-13-notes-illustration-runic-monuments-kent.pdf), explicitly numbers runes by eight-position classes. His p170 composite table places Dæg/Oethil at III.7/III.8 and Ear/Ior at IV.4/IV.5. The first three positions of class III are Tir, Beorc, Eh, giving 3:3=E independently of a guessed Hackness row permutation. His full late row is Ac, Æsc, Yr, Ear, Ior, Queorth, Calc, Stan; the final three are outside GP29 as separate glyphs. By historical rune identity (not by Haigh's sometimes different sound values), the GP29 projection is complete and bijective, with IV.6–8 undefined on GP29. The first 24 include the standard Eoh and Ger identities in II.5 and II.4. Relative to signed GP29 order, only D/OE and IA/EA exchange positions. A period reader could have obtained this editorial convention well before LP2. The PDF text table was accessible; the page-image renderer returned a cache miss, so the exact glyph shapes on Plate I were not independently adjudicated.

This is evidence for an available coordinate **alphabet**, not an ancient 29-symbol cipher. Haigh describes the class/position notation as his editorial reference device (p169); on p173 he uses IV.5 for the form of Ior in a discussion of the Kentish monuments, noting that the same form can denote G in other alphabets. He supplies no example of a message actually enciphered with his complete late row. His chart reproduces the older Salzburg RACCO coordinates because those use only shared cells, but reading the independent Scandinavian Rotbrunna coordinates with Haigh's English chart gives J I L I NG L rather than AIRIKR. The mismatch is expected across the traditions and is not evidence against the historical Scandinavian cipher. It demonstrates that the held inscription cannot validate the GP29 projection. The original LP2 stave supplies one coordinate only; the chart does not tell us how to obtain coordinates from the ordinary GP ciphertext or route them into plaintext. Compound GP tokens remain atomic by glyph identity; no merging of IO with J, OE with E, or EA with A is allowed. Printed word cuts are unchanged in the bounded receiver.

The distinct cross-chart test deliberately grants arbitrary independent permutations of the two coordinate tapes, using Haigh's 29-symbol projection and signed GP order on opposite sides. See [complete original test and controls](lp2-isruna-coordinate-multiset-inverse.md#haigh-1872-to-gp29-cross-chart-receiver-director-3307-agent8). It is negative in both directions, including the separate physical p3 continuation. Thus the newly sourced chart removes the purely editorial missing-cell problem but fails to supply an authentic cipher mechanism. An actual worked late-cell cipher and an independent LP2 routing operation remain missing.

## LP2 decision

The independent Domitian dot anchors strengthen E at the opening under English coordinate numbering. The two original red heading offsets 2 and 10 are E, and the black ciphertext at offset 13 is TH. The red correspondence does not give a new held-out discovery because it was already known before the Domitian check. Repeated copies of the stave do not supply a second coordinate. The constant E shifts already failed in the [original ciphertext gate](lp2-p0-isruna-E-constant-shift-gate.md). The distinct Scandinavian 6:5:5 convention still gives TH under the historically controlled reversed order, without providing a full GP29 chart. No English full-chart receiver, new plaintext or key follows. Reopen the medieval English fourth-row route on an actual worked Ior cipher coordinate or an explicit fourth-group boundary tied to the branch device. Haigh's 1872 editorial chart now supplies a distinct complete projection, but its tested cross-chart routing receiver is negative.
