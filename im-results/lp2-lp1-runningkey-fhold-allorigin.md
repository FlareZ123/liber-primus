### Spacing-free blind optimizer calibration

A separate receiver eliminates the intact-word premise. Gauge alpha(F)=0, allow beta(F) anywhere, and replace periodic phase with the cumulative number h of plaintext-F holds. At emitted offset i the consuming key is K[i−h]. Exact scaled forward/backward provides expected counts G[c,k] aggregated by the 29 shift residues, permitting Hungarian beta updates and sorted frequency-mass alpha updates. Refine the inner permutation with an Emerson-trained bigram objective, then exact two-type Viterbi over (h,last-event type). Freeze both alphabets and cursor after 1,200 runes; score 703 heldout runes. All consuming F rank-zero transitions are forbidden in final scoring.

Under 48 starts ×42 iterations, the two hidden-key LP1-ordinal/prime controls produce only 1 and 3 legal fits, respectively; best fit correctness is 67/1,200 and 198/1,200, and neither has a legal frozen suffix. Given the true alphabets, the same Viterbi receiver recovers 1,200/1,200 +701/703 and 1,198/1,200 +703/703 with exact forward replay. Normalize alpha(F)=0 by simultaneous translation; at position i with h previous literal F holds, a consuming event is legal iff beta(C_i) differs from K[(i−h) mod L]. A visible F may instead hold and increment h. At each visible-F boundary exactly one cumulative-hold count is selected. The intervening deterministic consuming segment forbids its corresponding (cipher label, key residue) pairs. ### Pooled Fourier initializer: actual blind inverse on controls

The exact fixed-schedule model is excluded for the original pooled LP1 case, but its inverse is now constructive on long planted data. A pooled nine-scope emission curriculum fits an unconstrained 29×29 conditional ciphertext/shift distribution by exact F-hold forward/backward; grow prefixes 64→128→256→512→1024→75% of each scope. For a true consuming emission matrix E[c,k]=p[(beta(c)−k) mod29], its Fourier row coefficient at harmonic f equals a common complex amplitude times exp(−2πif beta(c)/29). Since 29 is prime, any nonzero harmonic determines the cyclic order modulo a common translation. Rank candidate harmonics by empirical amplitude, fit their phases to 29 distinct roots via Hungarian assignment, refine cyclic alignment by pooled count likelihood, and try the two least-supported rank-zero gauges. Only fit prefixes are used to select and refine the alphabets; the suffix uses each frozen ending cursor.
