# LP2 printed-line greedy-wrap inversion: held-out recovery of a stable visual-width code

**Evidence status, 2026-11-10, Agent 47:** original-transcript source-layout inference, independently evaluated on physical pages not used to fit widths, with an exact per-page rune-inventory null and native-JPEG page check. This recovers a **predictive relative glyph-width profile and approximate line-ending rule**, not an original LP2 cipher key or any unknown plaintext. It develops a new mechanism for the already observed line-initial rune bias in [printed-line investigations](printed-line-phase-invariant.md).

## Mechanism and source-locked experiment

The original Liber Primus images lay out many runic lines against a nearly fixed right margin even while rune counts vary. A greedy fixed-pixel-width line wrapper is a candidate explanation: for glyph-advance widths \`w[0..28]\` and visible word separators \`w[-]\`, \`w[.]\`, with line capacity \`W\`, a physical line ending before the next glyph must satisfy

\`\`\`text
sum(w[token] for token on printed line) <= W
sum(w[token] for token on printed line) + w[first token of next line] > W
\`\`\`

This **necessary one-step inequality** permits direct inversion of relative glyph widths using known original ciphertext runes and their *printed line breaks*, without guessing a plaintext or cipher. The 29 width values have no cryptographic interpretation by themselves. The model permits page text to continue across graphic line breaks and does not assume that a displayed line is a whole word.

Use the exact \`resources/lp2-transcription.txt\` 55 rune-bearing unsolved pages, 12,956 runes, preserving each printed slash-delimited line and page. Map all 29 GP glyphs to individual tokens, with \`-\` and \`.\` given two extra non-rune advance widths. Other transcription section markers are omitted; only a pair of consecutive lines containing **at least 18 runes on both sides** is eligible. This freezes **501** of the 539 original page-internal line joins. Split *physical pages*, with 245 joins on pages 0–25 for one fit and 256 on pages 26–55 (rune-free page 50 absent) for the independent reverse fit.

The encoder chooses nonnegative relative widths and one shared line capacity to minimize **squared violations** of the two inequalities over training lines, with fixed 0.005 ridge regularization, a gentle width-scale anchor and 2,500 deterministic Adam steps. Training is fully source-indexed; no GP-English model, word dictionary, hidden plaintext guesses or held-out line positions influence fitted parameters. The null shuffles the **29 rune identities within each actual physical test page**, preserving that page's exact rune multiset, each original line's rune count, every separator's location, and every break location. Fitted widths remain frozen in all nulls.

## Held-out predictions and controls

Two complementary tests apply to every previously unseen line. The **exact greedy feasibility** criterion accepts a line iff its actual sum lies between \`W-w[next]\` and \`W\`. The stricter practical test starts at the true beginning of an unseen printed line, follows the *actual subsequent ciphertext tokens*, and uses frozen widths to choose the greedy break; report how many glyph positions separate predicted and printed breaks. Because starts are provided for evaluation, this is **local prediction of line termination**, not an end-to-end reflow of an entire page.

| Held-out test | Learned widths | Equal-width rune baseline | Rune-shuffled null mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Later 256 joins: exactly correct break | **125/256 = 48.83%** | 66/256 = 25.78% | near 25% (not separately recorded) |
| Later 256 joins: within one glyph | **89.06%** | 56.64% | **50.19%** |
| Later 256 joins: mean greedy-constraint violation | **0.30689** | 0.91309 | **0.98442** |
| Earlier 245 joins: exactly correct break | **120/245 = 48.98%** | 84/245 = 34.29% | near 25% (not separately recorded) |
| Earlier 245 joins: within one glyph | **87.35%** | 66.53% | **51.90%** |
| Earlier 245 joins: mean greedy-constraint violation | **0.27036** | 0.70776 | **0.86675** |

The equal-width baseline has identical widths for all 29 runes, fixed separator widths 0.35 and one **training-selected scalar line capacity**; it does not get 29 separate free widths. Both held-out runs had zero of **1,000** exact page-preserving rune permutations attain the observed smaller mean violation or greater within-one-glyph accuracy, giving the plus-one empirical upper tail **1/1001 = 0.000999** *per frozen direction and statistic*. These are conditional, post-hypothesis tests, not a discovery-wide multiple-search correction.

The 29 relative rune widths inferred **independently from the two physical halves correlate Pearson \`r=0.84617\`**. The first/last partitions both rank \`ᛠ\` as the widest inferred rune (1.4763 / 1.50 in their own relative scale), and \`ᛁ\` as the narrowest (0.4765 / roughly 0.25). These are source-inferred *advance-width parameters*; an independent approximation from actual original-JPEG line pixels is documented below.

### An independent prediction of the puzzling first-rune bias

A fixed-width wrapper tends to defer a wide next glyph to the *following* line, rather than squeezing it into the remaining pixel budget. This directly predicts a disproportionate frequency of **larger-width glyphs** at printed line starts. With widths frozen from early pages, the later 242 eligible rune starts have **mean estimated advance 0.98482**, compared with **0.91566** over 1,000 exact-page-inventory shuffled manuscripts (no exceedances, p=0.000999). Reverse-heldout first-rune means are **0.90309 observed** versus **0.86492** shuffled, with **three** nulls at least as high (p=0.003996). Both comparisons use independent source pages, preserving original page glyph counts and punctuation slots.

This provides a source-grounded mechanism for the conspicuous [first printed rune collision excess](printed-line-phase-invariant.md), where \`ᛠ\` was overrepresented and \`ᛁ\` underrepresented. A subsequent [original-JPEG-width-conditioned residual test](printed-line-width-conditioned-residual.md) matched all 539 within-page wrap positions to their actual page and independently measured glyph-width category. The remaining rune-identity discrepancy was ordinary under 10,000 matched nulls (p=0.1063), and independently fitted page-half residual patterns did not predict their opposite halves. This constrains the case for a separate width-independent line-start alphabet channel while leaving exact kerning and potential more complex visual codes unresolved.

## Independent original-JPEG pixel-geometry widths and unseen wrap-gap pairing (Agent 39)

The preceding inference fits *printed-line breaks* in the rune transcript. A different, independent control fits **actual dark-pixel horizontal extents measured from the manuscript scans**, without treating a break position as an optimization target. This resolves the original report's most important measurement uncertainty: inferred rune widths might simply have been degrees of freedom tuned to line-break locations.

[\`sims/printed_line_width_mechanism.py\`](../sims/printed_line_width_mechanism.py) uses the exact corpus and original \`17.jpg\`–\`72.jpg\` image set, performs **no OCR**, selects 30 regular print pages whose detected text bands agree exactly in number with the original printed transcript, and measures 360 physical line widths from black pixels at fixed 2,400×3,600 native resolution. Excluded illustration/header pages are enumerated in executable output, rather than aligned speculatively. The fitting model is an intercept plus 29 rune advance coefficients and six printed-marker coefficients, with a fixed, mild ridge penalty toward common widths; only physical line widths and exact source characters are fitted.

The **204 measured lines from early physical pages** alone fit the advances, and those same coefficients predict the widths of **156 later lines on 13 previously unseen pages** with RMSE **11.243 image pixels**. An equal-rune-advance baseline trained on the identical early lines has held-out RMSE **18.121 pixels**. The original actual-width learned \`ᛠ\` is about **71.77 px**, and \`ᛁ\` about **12.63 px**, matching the direction of the earlier transcript-derived extrema. This is an independently predictive original-source glyph advance approximation, though it does not isolate every individual glyph's exact kerning.

**Held-out first-symbol inference:** freeze those 29 advances and test the 156 later printed-line initial runes against the fixed complete rune inventory of each page. Their cumulative width enrichment is **782.80 image pixels** above page-marginal expectation. In **20,000 independent page-inventory matched samples** the control SD is **131.44 px**, and none attains the original enrichment (add-one conditional p **1/20,001**). This directly corroborates the earlier layout explanation using **visual data never consulted when fitting the line-break inequalities**.

**A harder location-specific prediction:** for each of **113 actual midword line wraps on those later pages**, take the preceding line's measured rightmost ink coordinate and the following line's first rune. Infer the common right boundary of the print box exclusively from the training images (**x=1808 px**). A greedy fit-to-line model predicts that the next glyph's actual advance should be larger *when the remaining blank space is larger*, because it could not fit in the preceding line. Keep the original successor rune **multiset fixed separately within every test page**, reassign these first glyphs among that page's actual midword break locations, and compare the source-frozen width with each position's actual remaining gap.

Original within-page centered gap×next-width covariance is **111.600 px²** versus permutation expectation **−0.117 px²**. Original average remaining blank gap below the successor advance is **4.071 px** versus permutation mean **6.112 px**. Neither quantity was reached by **20,000** fixed-seed within-page permutations (both additive conditional p **1/20,001**). The simpler count of glyphs that individually overflow is **64.60%** against a null mean **63.29%**, *not* unusual (p **0.347**). Thus the **fine-grained width/gap pairing** is supported while a strict, universal single-glyph overflow rule is **not established**.

The training/test image pages, pixel thresholds, page inventories, fitting objective, baseline, random seed, 20,000 permutations and individual-width estimates are printed by the reproducible script. [Successful source-pixel model](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42776945595) · [successful extended frozen-wrap-gap test](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42777371312) on the existing \`printed-line-phase-manual.yml\` workflow with \`attack=pixel-measure\`.

**Direct visual-line termination prediction, with a fair equal-width comparator:** The same original-JPEG-fitted 29 advances and *training-image-derived* right margin x=1808 px, with no fitting to held-out line endings, were applied as a one-step greedy capacity rule to the exact rune content of the **143 previously unseen printed-line joins**. **87/143 (60.84%) printed rune cuts were predicted exactly, 139/143 (97.20%) within one printed token**, with mean absolute error **0.4336 tokens**. Within the 113 midword joins, 69 were exact and 110 within one token. A competing equal-rune-width model was granted the stronger advantage of *optimizing its single free capacity on the original 187 training-line endings*, then frozen: it predicted only **39/143 exact (27.27%) and 86/143 within one (60.14%)**, mean error **1.3147 tokens**. The unequal widths learned strictly from source pixels therefore transfer directly to a new, substantial class of *actual physical break positions* beyond both image-span prediction and first-rune marginal enrichment. It is still a local line-end prediction starting from the *actual beginning* of each test line; full-book continuous reflow, nonstandard prints, and the final few atypical line wraps are not solved. [Successful frozen training/heldout source-image run 42778418920](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42778418920). The initial equal-width least-squares image-width baseline was unsuited to a break threshold because it could absorb line width into its intercept; this reported equal-width **line-end baseline** instead calibrates its cutoff directly on *training* physical breaks and is the applicable comparator.

**Decision:** direct original-image measurements independently validate an approximate variable-width font and a new, position-specific explanation for *why* wide runes start lines. Under the measured conditional controls, the conspicuous printed-initial distribution is consistent with a **typesetting overflow-selection mechanism**. Other influences such as punctuation, kerning, colour and typesetter manual intervention remain possible. This does not recover even one unknown plaintext rune or a Cicada cipher key, and source selection across the larger research search remains exploratory.

## Native page-image contact and limits

On original JPEG \`resources/liber-primus/18.jpg\` (LP2 physical page 1), all **12** regular print lines start near native image x=601–604 and have measured dark-pixel horizontal extents **1,139–1,192 pixels** (mean **1,163.17**), even though the transcript counts vary from **21 to 24 runes**. The source image is 2,400×3,600 pixels. The [native-image geometry utility](../sims/printed_line_source_span.py) gives the exact threshold, crop, row-peak, and per-line segmentation for that check; this measures **whole-line ink spans**, not each separate glyph's advance.

Important limitations: the line-width fit is regularized and is not a unique reconstruction of the original font or its kerning; around half of line endings miss the exact one-step inequality, so a perfectly fixed-capacity wrapper alone does not explain every line. Section rubrics, intentional stanza breaks, long red initials, kerning, multiple fonts, manual adjustment, and omitted print markers can produce differences. The null preserves original line boundaries but scrambles rune labels, so it tests a particular source identity-to-break link and does not distinguish every typographic mechanism.

**Cryptanalytic implication:** the strong printed-line-first-letter marginal is now partially explained by a independently predictive *visual layout* mechanism. Treat it cautiously as a proposed key or acrostic channel unless it survives a width-conditioned null. None of the 12,956 unknown rune plaintexts, their real cipher keys, or the external puzzle destination has been recovered from this model.

### Independent validation against original glyph ink (not line-break training)

The inferred 29-width vectors now have a direct, **out-of-sample original-JPEG crosscheck**. [\`sims/printed_line_glyph_ink_width.py\`](../sims/printed_line_glyph_ink_width.py) locates line centers through a fixed x-crop and vertical dark-pixel projection, retaining pages only when the number of detected rows equals the printed transcription. It examines the first **runic** glyph of a line starting near source image x=600 and measures the width of its first connected run of dark vertical ink columns (up to two blank columns tolerated). This is an observable image-space ink component, **not** a reconstructed font advance width or OCR. The algorithm was run on the repository's original images \`17.jpg\`–\`72.jpg\`; 11 pages with unmatched line counts and 57 off-margin/punctuation starts were excluded by fixed gates.

Crucially, **early-page image glyph widths are compared only to the advance vector inferred from *late-page line breaks*, and conversely**. The image pixels of the tested half never enter its predictor fit. For each rune with at least three image observations, calculate the median first-ink-component width and correlate it with its opposite-half fitted advance:

| Native JPEG subset and opposite-half model | First-rune image observations | Rune types with ≥3 | Pearson correlation | Spearman rank correlation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Early images p0–25, model fitted only p26–55 | **227** | 28 | **0.89136458** | **0.77968027** |
| Late images p26–55, model fitted only p0–25 | **214** | 26 | **0.87253075** | **0.85412197** |

A separate source-local null **permutes just the first-rune labels among the same measured image glyphs within each physical page**. This preserves every sampled page's exact initial-rune inventory and all original measured pixel widths, while destroying the hypothesized alignment between a glyph identity and its ink width. There were **zero** null correlations as high as observed in **1,500** seeded shuffles per direction (add-one p **1/1501 = 0.00066622** each). The mean null correlations were 0.18946 and 0.19126, maxima 0.69691 and 0.71437. Because the inferred relative advances were fitted from line breaks alone, this is a strong *independent source-image prediction* of physical rune width structure.

The image measure is intentionally conservative: occasional touching neighbors, component disconnection inside a rune, ink/antialias thresholding, cropping, red initials and missing line detections limit its physical accuracy. Its strong opposite-half correlations provide evidence that source-dependent glyph widths contribute materially to the original line-break and line-first-rune statistics; the exact original font, kerning engine and any residual ciphertext-specific line-start signal remain undetermined. **No plaintext or cryptographic key was recovered.**

### Direct original-JPEG next-glyph margin test, without fitted rune widths

An additional image-space test independently predicts the **physical line break** without using any fitted rune advances. When the upcoming rune is wide, it is more likely to be deferred to the next printed line while the previous line leaves a larger right-margin gap. The original source-JPEG [next-rune width and previous-line slack test](../sims/printed_line_native_gap_challenge.py) locates 376 conservatively selected original line joins. It pairs the preceding line's remaining right-margin pixels \`1800 − last_dark_pixel_x\` with the *upcoming* initial rune's actual first-ink-component width. Adding a constant to the chosen 1800-pixel reference leaves Pearson correlation unchanged.

The original 376 joins have **Pearson r = 0.44004528**, Spearman **rho = 0.38983967**. In **4,000** null shuffles, keep each measured previous line edge and each physical page's upcoming-rune ink-width inventory fixed; shuffle the latter within the same page. The null mean Pearson r is **0.05266146**, null maximum **0.23750383**, zero trials at least the original (**plus-one empirical p = 1/4001 = 0.00024994**). A descriptive **93.09%** of the original gaps are smaller than the upcoming ink component width plus 10px; this 10px allowance illustrates bearings/kerning and is not used for null calibration.

These observations directly corroborate width-sensitive line wrapping using original image pixels and **no cipher assumptions**. They strengthen the conclusion that the first-rune frequency anomaly has an important typesetting contribution, while incomplete knowledge of font metrics, manually imposed breaks and potential residual information remain unresolved. No LP2 plaintext or key is recovered. [Successful manual Actions run 42778274022](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42778274022).

### A low-dimensional forward model using measured source JPEG ink widths

To check whether the 29 freely learned line-wrap widths are merely fitting 29 unknown manuscript features, [\`sims/printed_line_fixed_pixel_width_forward.py\`](../sims/printed_line_fixed_pixel_width_forward.py) fixes **all 29 rune widths directly to each rune's median actual source-JPEG first-ink-component pixel width** (pooled across the same original images for type coverage, and never optimized against line breaks). It then fits only **three scalar layout parameters** on early or late original page line breaks: an additional common rune advance, the punctuation/word-separator advance and one line capacity. The opposite physical-page half's printed line breaks are independently predicted using these fitted scalars and the fixed measured rune-width vector.

With 245 early training joins, the three-parameter image model fits extra-rune advance **0**, separator width **9 pixels**, capacity **983.34 units**. On 256 later held-out joins it predicts **46.875% exact breaks, 89.0625% within one glyph**, compared with the equal-width rune baseline's 56.64% within one glyph. Reverse late→early fit uses extra advance **0**, separator **6 pixels**, capacity **965.09 units**; it predicts **42.45% exact and 82.86% within one glyph** for 245 unseen early joins, against equal-width baseline 66.53%. These results are from [successful manual CI 42779713580](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42779713580), with explicit static source pixel width dictionary and unchanged corpus. The earlier full learned-width model achieves 89.06% / 87.35% within one glyph, respectively.

This provides a simpler **image-constrained forward layout predictor** with only three fitted scalar degrees of freedom, dramatically reducing the flexibility of the tested visual mechanism. Because ink-width medians pool glyph occurrences from both halves to cover all 29 symbols, the source image measurements are not disjoint; the held-out prediction is strictly about *original printed line-cut positions*, which are not used in image median estimation. Image ink-component width differs from a font advance, and the model still misses exact line endings. No cryptogram plaintext or cryptographic key is recovered.
