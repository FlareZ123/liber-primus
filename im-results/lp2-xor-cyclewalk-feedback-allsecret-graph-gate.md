# Original LP2 all-secret 5-bit XOR cycle-walk feedback graph obstruction

Neither independently selects the model, the five-bit labels, or its feedback. This is a distinct non-additive group law rather than another prime-rank parameter or arbitrary secret lookup. No original plaintext recovered.

## Complete reversible model

Choose an arbitrary 29-element subset A of GF(2)^5 (three missing labels), arbitrary plaintext and ciphertext bijections S,C from GP29 to A, and arbitrary fixed five-bit mask K. For previous observed ciphertext rune u, set q=C(u) XOR K. Define T_q(x)=x XOR q if that result belongs to A, and x otherwise. Because XOR by q is an involution, this is its cycle-walk permutation on A, also an involution. Encode ciphertext rune v=C^-1(T_q(S(p))). Decode p=S^-1(T_q(C(v))). The previous ciphertext is public; first predecessor is fixed at rune0 and may otherwise be arbitrary without affecting the observed singleton edges. This preserves every GP29 symbol including F, every printed word boundary, and all page carries; no key skip or hidden escape flags.

Three full independent solved-source plants use different private 29-label subsets, separate plaintext/cipher alphabets and secret masks. All 2,979 source runes each (8,937 aggregate) invert exactly.

## All-secret graph theorem

Let the singleton plaintext vocabulary have at most k symbols; call their embedded labels B⊆A. Consider the directed observed predecessor/current pairs u→v at every complete one-rune word. If v's ciphertext label C(v) is not in B, then the cycle-walk fallback cannot be the actual decoded plaintext label: fallback would return C(v), not in B. Hence for each such edge the direct XOR candidate must satisfy

C(u) XOR C(v) = K XOR S(p) ∈ K XOR B.

Remove incoming edges to the k or fewer possible current glyphs with C(v)∈B. In the remaining undirected graph on ciphertext glyph identities, every vertex has at most k neighbors, because each offset gives at most one neighbor under an injective embedding. Moreover each connected component lies in one coset of the subgroup spanned by the ≤k offsets and has size at most 2^k. These statements hold for **every possible label subset, separate secret alphabet, mask, and assignment of singleton plaintext glyphs**, even granting arbitrary fallback on all deleted incoming edges. Enumerating possible target glyph sets is enough; no secret permutation search. Grant exactly k deletions since adding deletions cannot invalidate either necessary graph bound.

## Extension: arbitrary stationary XOR feedback, any bit width

To account for five-position returns, consider rotating the predecessor's five-bit label before XOR. More generally let the mask q(u) be **any** function of the preceding ciphertext glyph, with no requirement that it be linear, injective or low-complexity. The exact same T_q encoder and inverse apply, over any common 29-element subset of GF(2)^m (m≥5). This encompasses rotational, affine, nonlinear and arbitrary predecessor-conditioned masks.

Delete all incoming singleton edges to the candidate set C⁻¹(B) as before. For two remaining targets v,w sharing predecessor u, C(v) XOR C(w) ∈ B XOR B. The span of B XOR B has dimension at most k−1, since after choosing b₀∈B it is generated by {b XOR b₀ : b∈B, b≠b₀}. Therefore the target co-neighbor graph (connect two targets if they share a predecessor) has every component contained in one affine coset of that span. Its maximum component size is at most 2^(k−1), irrespective of m, q, the embedding, or the alphabet permutation. This is stronger than degree counting and does not require injective q. For injective q, the corresponding source co-neighbor bound also holds with q(u) as the embedding. Every source has at most k distinct targets regardless of q.

Thus the ordinary singleton register excludes **every fixed predecessor-dependent XOR mask**, even an arbitrary private nonlinear lookup, any bit width, and arbitrary separate GP label embeddings. It also covers masks that depend on printed word length and within-word position in addition to the preceding glyph, because these additional coordinates are constant on singleton words. It does not cover arbitrary changing clocks, state involving other preceding glyphs, alternate segmentation, or six or more native singleton types.

Two new solved-source plants (rotational feedback and a noninjective masked-rotation rival, 2,979 runes each) invert exactly and their true target sets obey the component theorem. Original graph excludes the entire family at k≤5 before key inference; consequently rotating the five bits or fitting another predecessor lookup cannot yield ordinary native prose. The rotation hypothesis is not source-selected and the five-back observation does not rescue it.

## Decision

Reject the entire stationary predecessor-dependent XOR cycle-walk feedback family under the ordinary source-native singleton vocabulary, independently of every private 29-of-32 label embedding and separate GP alphabet. The graph gate does not cover changing masks per word/position, changed segmentation or six-plus singleton types; a different plaintext register or state law needs independent evidence. No source-visible selector or authenticated unknown plaintext is supplied. Do not optimize alphabet embeddings for this model.
