## Exact theorem for every secret 29! row alphabet

Give the decoder a different, unrestricted GP29 symbol permutation for each `(p50 state z, A partner K)` row. Its direct first-output inverse has the form

`P_i = S_(z_i,K_i)(C_i)`, with every `S_(z,K)` an arbitrary 29-symbol **bijection**.

No secret key or alphabet entry is fitted, and there is no assumption that one row's permutation resembles any other. For every row, a bijection preserves the event

`C_i=C_j if and only if P_i=P_j` whenever `(z_i,K_i)=(z_j,K_j)`.

Consequently the source-original ordered pair collision ratio

`R = Σ_(z,K,c) n[z,K,c](n[z,K,c]−1) / Σ_(z,K) N[z,K](N[z,K]−1)`

is **identical** for every possible choice of secret alphabets. This holds regardless of the astronomical count of possible row keys. Here `n[z,K,c]` is the observed frequency of original ciphertext label `c` in a frozen source row, and `N` sums its 29 bins. The theorem is exact; comparing its value with native-English plaintext requires a language-register assumption.

A second exact invariant uses **original printed word starts**. The source transcription has 2,896 complete words over the 12,956 original rune symbols; 6,395 B runes contain 4,963 noninitial sites and 1,432 original word-initial sites. A secret 29! row maps at most **one** observed ciphertext label to any chosen plaintext rune. Thus the *maximum* number of one decoded GP rune at noninitial positions is exactly bounded by the sum, over all original `(z,K)` rows, of the most common original C rune in that row **restricted to noninitial positions**. This is a conservative all-secret cap, ignoring whether different rows' winning labels can be coherently assigned.
