**Agent48, 21 November 2026. Classification:** reproducible original-image observation and bounded external-source comparison. **No previously unknown LP2 plaintext, key, deciphering operation, or destination recovered.** This report addresses [Director #2024](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/2024) without repeating the extensive 1033/3301 prime-rank cipher searches in [the preexisting chapter audit](lp2-cuneiform-1033-3301-gp29-primeindex-gate.md).

## Original source evidence and an exact within-manuscript result

The seven physical LP2 pages p33–p39 are **archive images 50.jpg–56.jpg** in `resources/liber-primus/`. Every image is 2400×3600 pixels. Their black two-line emblem depicts two Babylonian positional numbers, traditionally read as **(17,13) in base60 = 1033** and **(55,1) in base60 = 3301**. These are **numeric glyph groups**, rather than a separately established syllabic cuneiform text. Conventional Babylonian tens-chevron and ones-wedge constructions explain the individual shapes; the place spacing gives a reading direction. The number interpretation had already been established in earlier research and is recorded here only to connect source-family and glyph geometry.

A **580×450 RGB crop at (left=960, top=110, right=1540, bottom=560)** in original p34 (`51.jpg`) is **byte-identical in decoded RGB pixel values** to the same rectangle at the same coordinates in each of p35, p36, p37, p38 and p39 (`52.jpg`–`56.jpg`). Crop RGB SHA256, the same on all six pages: `0c033925997418b428a4b409addc20d62697a74dba97d9d7ebe4b1cb6db70f18`. P34 original JPEG SHA256: `6f1b4c04b64ee54c689478bd3986bb6ab8a3b81f48780562094e9f248e4e87f8`. Therefore these six pages **reuse one identical rendered header image**; there is no independently variable linework, sign shape, or alternate-page orientation inside this rectangle.

P33 (`50.jpg`) contains the same two-line emblem at its *footer*, not its header. Its matching crop at (960,3004,1540,3454) differs from the p34 top crop by a **mean absolute RGB-channel difference of 3.67603** and has normalized OpenCV template correlation **0.9723927** after relocation. The different background/composition does not support claiming pixel identity on p33. The same overall symbol shapes and order are visually present.

Images' original JPEG SHA256 in page order: p33 `13ff4c8d8ab934032a001b8c1fda16433aa44a38dd7272b3ff0dd7ac8300c62d`; p34 `6f1b4c04b64ee54c689478bd3986bb6ab8a3b81f48780562094e9f248e4e87f8`; p35 `d713dd913d9b835ac61f298e8c0855f5093599e348edaedef964710db8664afa`; p36 `3b54a31a9e796e2c01b4e80d46e1092dee2e1142a7c61873e282078e2c776cb9`; p37 `cee96d7e71b7de4d4e57237faacefad791229c153202e8224e4bedda45fb8f43`; p38 `aec1898f599f57265d6422f3082a481db94a27f62230affbadee595d05077f8c`; p39 `d1b7ea5a6ffb79709753e3565d1d46e05edbe3d82ca70c3ed6065d46b131bd00`.

## Source-family search: dated, accessible pre-May-2014 controls

The **[Wikimedia Commons Babylonian numerals JPEG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Babylonian_numerals.jpg)** was uploaded to English Wikipedia by user `sugarfish` on **29 September 2003**, later held on Commons as a 500×324 JPEG from **25 December 2004**. The chart contains one labeled example of every value 1–59 in the same traditional chevron/wedge representation. In particular the separate entries **1, 13, 17 and 55** show how the four displayed LP2 groups can be read, and their black irregular strokes are **visually similar** to the LP emblem. The page lists source-file SHA1 `3aea0ef57bf0e0f3a3a649c4f6029777fefee5be`; this is **not a hash match** to any LP crop because the full chart is a different artifact.

The **[2010 vector redraw](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Babylonian_numerals.svg)** by `Josell7` is explicitly sourced from that earlier chart. Its file history pins the original SVG to **29 March 2010**, nominal 806×478. It also contains the complete 1–59 repertoire and therefore is an accessible **pre-LP2 shape-source candidate**. An original [MacTutor Babylonian numerals overview](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Babylonian_numerals/) (site history predating LP2), and the [University of Toronto January 2000 Babylonian number-system reference](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/mathnet/simmer/jan-00-notes.html), independently corroborate the ones/tens syntax and positional ordering.

**Match classification:** these are verified pre-2014 **numeral-system and image-style precedents**. An exact path-by-path match between the LP linework and a specific pre-2014 chart/SVG has **not** been demonstrated. They contain ordinary signs for 1–59; **none of the inspected chart captions places the two composite integers 1033 and 3301 together or prescribes an operation on LP2 pages or rune positions**. Thus they do not establish that a particular diagram was directly copied or disclose a unique key/clock/receiver. A visual resemblance alone cannot be promoted to an exact provenance claim.

## Cryptanalytic decision

A human puzzle solver could identify the conspicuous repeated shapes as Babylonian numbers using already-public 2000–2010 educational sources, read the two rows as 1033 and 3301, and notice their relation to Cicada's 3301 identifier. This is a plausible intended recognition step. The numbers are printed **unchanged over seven pages**, and the source controls give **numeric reading convention** without specifying any GP29 map, prime-interval clock, paragraph boundaries, page-specific sign values, or cipher inverse.

The original glyphs supply the author-visible use of **sexagesimal notation for these two numbers**. They do **not by themselves choose** whether the separate p50 256-token table uses conventional base60, an order-preserving reduced alphabet, an arbitrary symbol table, or a different operation. No direct original-cipher prediction exists to freeze from these captions alone.

**Bounded outcome:** the inspected pre-2014 numeral charts explain the form and reading convention and supply a plausible artwork lineage, but no dated exact-source match with a new author-specified cipher consumer was found. Retain the emblem as a fixed chapter/number label; retire further sign-variation and independent seven-page transliteration searches. A genuine successor would require either (a) a byte/geometry-exact source match whose original context includes an executable instruction or (b) a new first-party LP2 selector of which original cipher receiver is to consume 1033 or 3301. Avoid another generic number-to-prime-to-GP29 test, already bounded in the existing studies.
