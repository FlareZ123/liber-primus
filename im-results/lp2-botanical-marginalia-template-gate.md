# LP2 p8–14 marginal trees and p24–26 wing ornaments: source-template gate

**Original-image evidence, Agent 17, 11 November 2026, Director Issue #187; historical source gate, Agent 3, 11 November 2026, Director Issue #325.** The p8–14 tree artwork remains a bounded visual-source investigation, not a decryption. No unknown LP2 plaintext, key, or destination was recovered.

## Source and the human-solvable route

LP2 original scans (physical page = JPEG number minus 17) show upright sparse **tree/roots drawings** in the outside margins of **p8–14, JPEG25–31**, alternating left/right with page side. Original **p24–26, JPEG41–43**, show an entirely **different visual family**, two-ended dragonfly-like wing figures on *both* sides of the text. The page-14 central flying insect is separate from its marginal tree. These are two kinds of source artwork rather than a sequence of distinct tree branch states.

The signed 2016 oak image carries the authenticated message that Liber Primus words are the map, their meaning is the road and their numbers the direction. The earlier documented image lineage joins the oak to the blurred p32 tree and outlined p55 tree in [tree-outline-blur-crosspage.md](tree-outline-blur-crosspage.md). No source instruction identifies or enumerates the sparse p8–14 trees as numeric coordinates. A human could notice the seven-page run, roots and page placement, but a unique page/word/rune address needs distinguishable states or a rule selecting an exact branch.

## Historical Fibonacci-tree candidate (Director Issue #325)

A genuinely pre-2014 source supplies a plausible model, but not an exact image-source identification. The [OEIS A000045 Fibonacci-tree page](https://oeis.org/A000045/a000045.html) credits Henry Bottomley's diagram to 27 July 2000. Its second representation says that each mature branch sprouts a new branch annually, new branches take a year to mature, **mature branches are heavy lines**, and the nth year has F_n branches. A later [Uncovering Cicada community page](https://uncovering-cicada.fandom.com/wiki/Liber_Primus_Ideas_and_Suggestions) explicitly proposes that the LP2 p8–14 saplings are a Fibonacci tree, counts 13 branches, and lists highlighted positions **[2,3,5,6,8,10,11,13]**. This is a plausible human-solver lead: 13=F_7, with 8 mature and 5 new branches. The community page is conjecture, not a Cicada-signed source; the LP2 image is not an exact copy of the OEIS diagram, and the branch segmentation/numbering is not printed on the LP2 page.

The candidate 13-position mask has two tempting arithmetic coincidences. Its listed positions sum to **58**, the LP2 page count; applying the same positions to the already documented p0 red-span GP-prime weights `[53,23,67,7,17,71,107,2,53,103,67,79,13]` selects `[23,67,17,71,2,103,67,13]`, whose **decimal** sum is **363**. The p56 SHA-512 target begins with the hexadecimal characters `363`, but this is not a numeric checksum: decimal 363 is `0x16B`, whereas hexadecimal `0x363` is 867. The literal three-character match is therefore only a string coincidence. The sum-of-branch-indices 58 is likewise not a rule stated by OEIS. Moreover, 5 of the 1,287 possible 8-of-13 subsets of the p0 weights sum to 363, so the arithmetic does not uniquely validate this mask.

The source does define a mature/new distinction, but it does not prescribe how to number LP2 branches, which side to start from, which ciphertext span to address, or how to transform runes. The LP2 trees also repeat as static templates (p10/p12/p14 left crops are byte-identical; p9/p11/p13 right crops are byte-identical; p8 is a near-template), so there is no page-by-page branch progression. The old community mask is worth preserving as a hypothesis, but it does not yield a source-fixed traversal or a blind original-cipher prediction.

## Physical p14 central mayfly: original-art source and instruction gate (Agent 28, 11 November)

**Distinct original visual cue.** Physical LP2 p14 (`31.jpg`) has a central lateral flying insect outside the previously measured marginal tree crops. Its original silhouette has a long recurving segmented body, conspicuous veined wings and long tail filaments, compatible with a **mayfly** (Ephemeroptera). No exact species or copied source plate is established. The repeated side trees are a separate static image template. The later double-ended ornaments on pp24–26 and p27 single wing belong to distinguishable artwork contexts; the original images provide no numbered progression of insect stages.

**Bounded historical comparator set.** Pre-2014 illustrated sources examined for attribution include Halford's *Dry-Fly Entomology* (1897; https://www.ephemeroptera-galactica.com/pubs/pub_h/pubhalfordf1897p1.pdf), the 1917 Smithsonian Ephemeroptera plate (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ephemeroptera_illustration.jpg), and Baughan Wisely's 1965 Ephemeroptera study (https://www.ephemeroptera-galactica.com/pubs/pub_w/pubwiselyb1965p398.pdf). The 1917 reference is principally a multi-wing dorsal specimen, unlike the manuscript's lateral profile. None has been identified as an **exact reproduced LP2 p14 illustration**. The two PDF sources are potential era-plausible comparators, not pixel-proven matches. This is a finite source gate, not an exhaustive catalogue.

**Historically authenticated connection, without an operation.** Original 2013 `761.MP3` metadata contains *The Instar Emergence* and the text later recovered on solved LP2 p57. Thus the instar/emergence theme is genuine Cicada-era evidence, yet it supplies no instruction to select the p14 insect as a GP cipher key, word address, source title, stage count or direction. The p27 insect-wing and 2013-title crib were already independently tested and rejected for a specified direct placement in [the existing crib gate](p27-instar-title-original-crib-gate.md); repeating that placement here would not create new evidence.

**Old English Rune Poem visual association (Agent 3, issue #697, 13 November 2026).** A separate pre-2014 textual witness makes the proposed mayfly/plant reading concrete: the Anglo-Saxon *Rune Poem* contains 29 futhorc stanzas; `Eolh-secg` describes a sharp marsh sedge growing in water, and `Dæg` describes day. The proposed mayfly could suggest day-fly (a historically documented common name) and aquatic-nymph ecology; those associations are recognizable before touching the ciphertext. Witness and independent controls: [UMass 2013 Old English course source](https://people.umass.edu/sharris/general/UM791_OE_F2013.pdf), [Eolh-secg/Dæg Old English texts](https://www.tha-engliscan-gesithas.org.uk/written-and-spoken-old-english/old-english-alphabet-2/the-anglo-saxon-rune-poem/), [pre-2014 Wild Trout Trust mayfly biology](https://www.wildtrout.org/assets/reports/teachers_introduction_to_mayfly_in_the_classroom.pdf). GP Eolhx=`X` (index 14, prime 47) and Dæg=`D` (index 23, prime 89), but 47+89=136 compared with the page's 137 runes is a postselected coincidence and **not a key selector**.

The **only page-local illustration potentially identifying Dæg is the central insect**. The putative Eolh-secg plant is the unchanged left-margin tree stencil also printed, pixel-for-pixel, on physical pp10 and 12; the same stencil family appears on pp8,9,11,13. In the source p14 illustration there is no depicted fen/water, labeled sedge, sharp leaf edge or visible stanza quotation to distinguish `Eolh-secg` from generic vegetation or the other tree-associated futhorc names `Beorc` and `Ac`. This is an obstacle to treating the proposed two-stanza association as author-selected. Reading the poem in its historical order gives Eolh-secg before Dæg, but the manuscript supplies no instruction to take stanza initials, verse words, stanza boundaries or an output symbol schedule. The earlier frozen direct additive and RSA interpretations were tested negatively by Agent 23; issue #704 independently addresses whether the full historical 29-stanza poem supplies a unique conventional codebook. **Decision for #697:** preserve the image/poem association as a specifically sourced thematic hypothesis, with no reversible decoder, no unknown plaintext and no independently selected target. More speculative key permutations or numeral retunings would add no evidence.

**Director Issue #419 decision:** no exact pre-May-2014 artistic source, decipherment operator, state schedule or withheld unknown-ciphertext prediction is available from the p8–14 trees or central p14 mayfly. The original p14 mayfly is useful as a distinct clue inventory item, with a verified cross-round *emergence* motif, but does not select a decoder. An exact independently dated image-outline attribution or a creator-authenticated instruction linking this specific image to an untested text location and reversible process would justify reopening. No plaintext or key was recovered.

## Paired p24–26 mayfly: original-source attribution and operator gate (Director #738, 13 November 2026)

**Decision.** The distinctive p24–26 margins are a *made composite of vertically reflected insects*, rather than a succession of biological life stages. Their common illustrated referent is a mayfly, and another manuscript appearance, the dotted solved p57 mayfly, is linked to p24 artwork by publicly documented source-image reconstruction. **No dated exact external artwork/caption, artist-supplied key, source-fixed rune operation, or unknown plaintext was recovered.** This retires the *exact historical graphic/source-selected key* branch at the present evidence level, while leaving different hidden-image mechanisms open.

**Original JPEG geometry.** On physical p24 / archive `41.jpg`, compare the unchanged left-margin grayscale upper half `x=0:600, y=1168:1688` against the bottom half `x=0:600, y=1888:2408` after vertical reflection and an original-image horizontal offset of two pixels. Pearson image correlation on the aligned 520×598 arrays is **0.925131643**. Frozen controls are **0.537269887** with no reflection, **0.092854940** with horizontal-only reflection, and **0.164509732** with 180° rotation. Their wing silhouette, thick thorax, thin abdomen and tail-filament diamond are therefore explained by a mechanically repeated, vertically mirrored one-insect element. The crops are from the complete original page raster, independent of any rune value. The [existing original-image SFT](../sims/lp2_botanical_template_source_sft.py) now certifies the reflection and its negative orientation controls alongside earlier exact p24/p26 border reuse. This confirms *visual copy construction*, without assuming a cipher transposition.

**Authentic p57 context.** The solved p57 central mayfly is visibly a dissolved rendition of the same winged insect form. A separate 2026 original-image investigation reports a page24-based opacity reference with **F1=0.899069** for 2,033 observed black cells and a stronger **F1=0.960096** after 31 fitted geometric/tonal parameters, with **166 mismatched cells** remaining. Its documented historical **GIMP 2.8.10** Dissolve implementation is available before LP2 (tag dated 28 November 2013) and contains the default random seed **314159265**. See [SECRA primary-artifact evidence IR-01–IR-08](https://secra.tech/research/liber-primus-strongest-findings/evidence/index.html) and its [public synthesis](https://secra.tech/research/liber-primus-strongest-findings/). That work establishes a compelling *rendering* lineage rather than a proven exact original design, independent authored key, or instruction to feed pixel noise into a 29-rune inverse. The fixed numerical seed is an implementation default and is unsuitable as an independent key-selection claim.

**Pre-2014 external-source check.** The dated [1861 John George Wood *Boy's Own Book of Natural History* mayfly plate](https://etc.usf.edu/clipart/53400/53494/53494_mayfly.htm), [1900/1917 natural-history illustrations](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ephemeroptera_illustration.jpg), and historical anatomy descriptions fix the *mayfly* subject but fail distinctive wing/leg/body/filament landmark identity and contain no adjacent cryptographic caption. A contemporary community [art inventory](https://uncovering-cicada.fandom.com/wiki/Symbols/Drawing/Art_of_Liber_Primus) calls pp25–27 mayflies and suggests an ephemeral-key reading, but provides neither a dated artist source nor a reversible operator. This is a targeted historical-source search, **not proof that no original graphic survives**. Natural-language associations with ephemeral keys, mirroring, insect emergence, or GIMP Dissolve give multiple competing transform choices; none dictates ciphertext target, GP29 key/state, a rune clock, or a ≥40-rune untouched prediction.

**Research fork.** Only a dated *exact* pre-May-2014 source illustration with distinctive landmarks and caption prescribing a specific reader-available operation, or a creator-authenticated authorial instruction linking this particular doubled ornament to text/key extraction, can justify a new image→cipher inverse. An independently verified historical renderer alone is a production explanation. Do not re-run static border-template comparisons, speculative species-key words, p57 noise-offset sweeps, or generic PRNG key scans without that selection evidence.

### Identical border and a shared page-clock pad: exact all-secret test

The exact p24/p26 marginal copies independently invite a sharper cryptographic question: did an intentional *same picture means same page-start key* convention reuse a GP29 additive one-time pad on these pages? Under a common pad of any length or entropy, aligned plaintexts obey the key-free necessary identity **P24[i] − P26[i] ≡ C24[i] − C26[i] (mod 29)**. This test can eliminate the entire page-aligned additive class under an explicit native-word grammar without finding the pad itself. It does not assume the images **actually** prescribe repeated encryption; repeated decoration remains the competing null.

Original physical p24 holds **270 runes in 64 printed word segments**; p26 holds **265 in 59**. Exact rune streams are independently checked against the numeric corpus and source transcription; all **265 overlapping original rune positions**, original GP29 indices and printed boundaries are frozen. An exact two-trie dynamic program searches every possible pair of words from the source-calibrated **32,081-distinct GP-native English word inventory**, built from existing Shakespeare, Gibran, Emerson and TextBlob/Norvig controls. It considers all possible 265-symbol common pad values algebraically, without key search or language-score threshold. On the original pair, **8 possible word-state pairs remain after rune 31, and no pair survives rune 32**. As a positive control, two independent source-native GP text streams with *the same printed word lengths* encrypted under one freely generated 265-symbol shared secret pad remain exactly feasible at all 265 positions (**13 terminal states**) and preserve every original difference identity. Reproducer: [CommonJS original-input SFT](../sims/lp2_p24_p26_same_mayfly_pad_sft.cjs).

**Decision:** the image-motivated fixed page-initial shared GP29 additive pad is incompatible with ordinary native-English whole-word plaintext from this *finite, declared* vocabulary and exact printed boundaries. Different languages, absent dictionary words, changed boundaries, nonadditive key reuse, or different page clocks remain possible. The failure supplies no source-selected new operator or unknown plaintext; refitting a shift or secret skip pattern solely to repair rune 32 would destroy the independent clue constraint.

## Cipher gate and falsifiable limits

An already executed [p10/p11 sevenfold to p55 gate](tree-facing-pages-sevenfold-p55.md) grants an **arbitrary secret stationary output codebook** and obtains a maximum original p55 fit of only **34/76** runes. Its four-rune trim and target assignment were never authenticated by the illustrations. Independently, the genuine solved-2014 [seven-position operation on p8–14](2014-outguess-period7-lp2-p8to14-inverse-gate.md) did not recover LP2 plaintext. These exact tests should not be repeated merely because the imagery occupies seven pages.

The 2016 oak clue does not prescribe a sparse-tree branch order, page/word/line clock, or reversible original-runic inverse. The OEIS Fibonacci-tree model supplies a candidate heavy/light maturity distinction, but no LP2 branch numbering or target span; the community's 13/8/5 interpretation remains conjectural. Repeating fixed art **excludes differential per-page branch-state extraction** from these crop regions, although a constant mnemonic remains possible. The p0 red-span/363 and branch-index/58 correspondences are retrospective and do not constitute a withheld ciphertext prediction. A new authenticated instruction or exact source image fixing the branch traversal and cipher operation would be required.

## Director decision

**Retire the p8–14 tree and central insect as immediate cipher selectors, while retaining the Fibonacci-tree analogy and instar/emergence imagery as provisional interpretations.** The pre-2014 OEIS model and the later community 13/8/5 interpretation make the human route plausible, but exact branch topology, segmentation, numbering, target text, and operation are not source-selected. The 363 decimal sum is not numerically equal to the p56 hexadecimal prefix; the 58 index sum is not an OEIS instruction. The original images show repeated static templates, not a page-varying progression. No unique original-cipher prediction follows. Close Issue #325 without a successor; reopen only if a primary artifact or exact source image fixes the branch readout and predicts an untouched ciphertext interval. Keep p24–26 wing art as a separate family.
