# LP2 original-visual clue → operation map

**Status:** intermediate audit; no new LP2 plaintext, cipher key, or decryption recovered. This is a decision map, not an established result.

**Scope:** inspected the 58 original LP2 page images (resources/liber-primus/00.jpg–57.jpg) against the canonical rune corpus. I did not inspect or manipulate red ink or the printed 32×8 table; issue #9 explicitly requires coordination with issue #3 first.

## Ranked source-visible routes

### 1. p7/p56 dot patterns → constellation geometry (closed: standard 13-dot punctuation)

A 2019 interview transcript reports that dots on LP2 p7 and p56 relate to constellations ([transcript, lines 426–443](https://therealsamizdat.com/2019/08/30/cicada-files-ghost3301-interview/)). The speaker's claimed insider status is unverified, so this was a testable external hint rather than established creator testimony.

**Original-JPEG decision (Issue #9, closed).** The red marks on physical p7 and p56 are the same normalized 3-column × 5-row **13-dot punctuation stencil** used by all 31 LP2 13-dot marks: `.#./###/###/###/###`. A catalogue fit based only on normalized occupancy therefore scores identically for every mark; the matched page-label null has exact score-rank fraction **1**. The supposed page-distinctive red constellation geometry is not identifiable and does not select a key, cipher or LP2 destination. This bounded result does not disprove a hypothetical global star-name convention. The independent five-black-dot constellation screen is also negative; do not repeat either catalogue search.

The original cross-release census additionally finds that LP1's 11-dot stencil becomes LP2's 13-dot stencil by adding the two lower-corner dots, while the 10-dot stencil persists. This is a genuine source-design change but has no authenticated cipher operation; see [the cross-release report](lp1-lp2-red-stencil-cross-release.md). A successor must find an independent instruction selecting a use of that change before interpreting ciphertext.

### 2. Five-slot tree ornament → 13/31 stencil counts → 32×8 table (issue #3 owns this line)

**Original locations:** all decorated LP2 pages **40–55** (original scans `57.jpg`–`72.jpg`), including the grid on pp49–51. The identical border artwork was initially studied on the three table pages, but its wider distribution was subsequently established.

An Agent 41 source audit shows that the same five-slot tree ornament appears in the **identical border on all sixteen decorated pages**, so its physical placement is not specific to the 32×8 table. Mirroring the two borders isolates two selectively outlined dot positions as the only black-ink difference components larger than 32 pixels. Under one explicit convention—topmost slot first, clockwise, solid=1 and hollow=0—the left and right ornaments read 01101₂=13 and 11111₂=31. The audit also reports 31 instances of the 13-dot stencil, yielding 32 intervening fragments, which matches the table's 32 rows.

**What a solver could infer:** the repeated tree ornament and dot stencil may select a five-bit reading convention, then a 31-separator/32-fragment alignment with the printed table. The distinct fill/outline slots are an original geometric fact. This remains a retrospective visual-to-table candidate, without a table-specific placement argument; orientation/polarity and all arithmetic uses of the bit masks remain hypotheses. A 640-model final-sixteen-page AES-128/GP28 source-grid inverse (four byte table interpretations and exact repeat-marker roundtrip controls) produced no original plaintext: its held-out IoC and language score match the GP28 rank/marker-conditional null ([CI 42890122260](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42890122260)).

The evidence and current tests are already consolidated in [im-results/red-256-event-grid-correspondence.md](red-256-event-grid-correspondence.md); this belongs to issue #3. I did not touch the table, red ink, or repeat its experiments. Do not duplicate that work under issue #9; coordinate with issue #3 before any further use.

### 3. Babylonian numerals → 2014 magic squares → a seven-page source interval

**Source correction (original JPEG pixel match):** the two-line cuneiform numeral motif occurs **seven consecutive times**, first at the bottom of physical LP2 **p33** (archive `50.jpg`), then at the top of **every p34–p39** (archive `51.jpg`–`56.jpg`). The earlier inventory and Cicada Notebook session 16 described only four sampled locations (p33,35,38,39), thereby omitting three genuine clones. An exact full-58-page original-image search finds only pp33–39 above NCC0.95, with best outside-group NCC0.30788; all **six** original RGB/BGR illustration crops on pp34–39 are **byte-identical**, SHA-256 `21d5cb4cb00e0a0d7d5b8adbbe14e497128951623ea4e98e10213f5c7288938f`. The p33 bottom illustration matches the repeated top patch after relocation (NCC≈0.9700), though its underlying JPEG crop differs. [Source-image test](../sims/lp2_cuneiform_header_sft.py).

The inscription reads sexagesimal **17,13** and **55,1**, yielding **1033** and **3301**. Both match earlier 2014 Cicada [OpenPuff magic-square constants](../resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt): a **7×7** square sums to **1033** per row, and a **5×5** square sums to **3301** per row. Known prior work on [the page-15 prime spiral](../results/fibonacci-prime-spiral.md) connects their square-centre values to a prime-counting operation and the GP letters LP. This is a historically motivated source connection, without an authentic LP2 key.

**Complete, newly fixed section geometry.** The second red p33 heading starts at local rune91 (global8,051), before the first numeral at the bottom of that page. The illustrated passage continues across **seven physical pages**, with original unsolved rune lengths **[123,261,271,238,228,228,240]** and source-body global interval **[8,051,9,640)**. The complete motif-aligned boundary offsets relative to the heading are **[0,123,384,655,893,1121,1349,1589]**. Earlier four-motif analysis grouped the middle three complete pages into **737** runes and used five blocks [123,261,737,228,240]. Its tested 124 additive/block-shift configurations remain valid **only for that incomplete, historically sampled four-marker partition**. The original art actually appears at each page boundary; those tests cannot be cited as a complete motif-defined key search. Full source alignment also supports a separate **91-rune/23-word** p33 interlude if the first and second p33 headings are *both* retained as boundaries. [Three-clock layout and exact offsets](../workplace/lp2-section-clock-ambiguity.md).

**One source-locked 7+5 cross-round inverse (negative).** The same seven-page illustrated passage contains the visibly numbered **five** red Arabic-ordinal paragraphs on pp36–38, with exact lengths **[139,91,98,96,129]** and global span **[8,805,9,358)**. The 2014 7×7/1033 and 5×5/3301 squares thereby have a motivated joint structural assignment: take one row of the historic 7×7 square for each of the seven illustrated pages and one row of its historic 5×5 square for each of the five numbered paragraphs. The deliberately frozen **direct GP29 additive** interpretation repeats each page's seven row entries from its visible start and overlays each numbered item's five row entries from its printed label, reducing all entries mod29. Under `P=C-K7-K5 (mod29)`, all **1,589** original runes have candidate plaintext IoC **0.034442555**, close to original **0.034349820** and uniform **1/29≈0.034482759**; the seven-page component alone gives **0.034578882**, and five-item component alone **0.034186544**. None is readable. Authentic solved-p56 plaintext has IoC **0.070028011**; a source-native full-length planted positive inverts and exactly reencrypts. This rejects the specified source-page/item additive-row interpretation, *not* the historical squares' possible use as a transposition, cipher state or non-GP key. [Original-offset positive-controlled SFT](../sims/lp2_cuneiform_seven_five_square_sft.cjs) · [successful manual CI 42899416187](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42899416187).

**Previously tested interpretations.** The original four-marker study's 32 continuous/reset φ(prime) variants starting from 1033/3301 or their prime ordinals yielded no plaintext; [the simpler independent six-run source-clock implementation](../sims/lp2_cuneiform_prime_totient_sft.cjs) reproduces that bounded negative across the now-correctly mapped p33/p34 starts ([successful CI 42898117856](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42898117856)). The existing 2014-square direct row-major additive and per-block Hill tests on the whole manuscript also failed [in their specified scopes](openpuff-magic-square-remaining-gate.md). The first four-marker study's orientation and five-block numerical residues therefore supply no surviving decryption evidence.

**Non-additive square/read-order source gate (Director #244, 11 November 2026).** The archived 2014 Onion6 form preserves the signed instruction to **paste the magic squares into the appropriate textareas** and provide an onion service URL; the surrounding, unsigned HTML labels the three inputs in order **5×5 / 3301, 7×7 / 1033, 5×5 / 1033**. Its recorded purpose is a submission interface, not a page cipher or transposition instruction ([verbatim signed-form/HTML source and source-boundary manifest](../resources/external/cicada2014-onion6-magic-form-and-response.json); [original three arrays](../resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt)). Thus **two** historical five-by-five arrays, not a unique one, could be paired speculatively with the five red-numbered items. The original book fixes a natural page order p33–39 and the printed item numbers **1 on p36, 2–4 on p37, 5 on p38**, but does not assign any square row/column, text unit, coordinate orientation, within-unit order, or invertible index operation. The seven-page and five-paragraph lengths are unequal; a complete simultaneous column readout would additionally need an independently specified fill/padding/continuation rule.

A finite, key-independent obstruction rules out the simplest missing mapping: in the three **as-archived** arrays, the number of distinct residues **per row modulo the corresponding dimension** is, respectively, **[4,4,3,4,4]**, **[6,4,2,2,2,4,6]**, and **[3,4,2,4,3]**. Thus **no row** supplies a 5- or 7-position permutation through bare modular reduction; collisions require a new tie-break or rank convention. Each original square also has **four distinct dihedral orientations** preserving its dimensions, entries, and magic line sums, so the sum alone cannot select an orientation. Sorting entries, constructing rank permutations, cycling rows or choosing a page/paragraph-to-cell mapping would introduce unsourced rules. No alleged plaintext can be accepted from a post-selected arrangement. These checks are direct finite consequences of the pinned three matrices, independently of unknown ciphertext.

**Decision:** preserve the seven-page illustration and five numbered items as genuine source geometry and their numerical correspondence to the 2014 squares as a plausible cross-round clue. The documented historical square operation is form submission. No primary instruction source-selects a **lossless non-additive page/paragraph permutation**, and the direct modulo-index interpretation fails bijectivity before plaintext testing. The finite gate therefore stops without conducting 7!×5! permutations, fabricating a wrong-orientation success criterion, or recycling the negative additive pad. Reopen only on a dated creator-origin instruction that specifies the original text unit, a complete permutation with tie handling, and independent plaintext/key predictions. No unknown LP2 plaintext, source-derived key, cipher mechanism or destination is recovered.

### 4. Möbius / infinity figure → opening-page traversal or Möbius inversion

**Corrected original-image mapping and geometry (Issue #172).** The 75-file archive is sequential: LP2 physical page n is archive scan n+17. Thus pp0–2 are `17.jpg`–`19.jpg`, and p15 is `32.jpg`; `00.jpg`–`16.jpg` are LP1. This corrects the earlier filename parenthetical in this map.

Each of pp0–2 has the same small horizontal figure-eight/Möbius-like mark above and below its text (six copies); p15 has a larger instance immediately below the 4×4 Fibonacci-prime square. In the 2400×3600 originals, the small top marks occupy x=1132–1247/1248, y=618–659; the bottom marks occupy x=1132–1247, y=2943–2984; the p15 mark occupies x=875–1426, y=1508–1699. All seven retain the same left-to-right orientation, the same lower-left and upper-right inward-facing tips, and the same apparent central crossover/gap arrangement. None is mirrored or rotated. The three bottom crops are pixel-identical; the p1/p2 top crops are pixel-identical. The p0-top crop is distinct, but its best normalized match is still identity (IoU 0.741 versus best nonidentity dihedral transform 0.203); the enlarged p15 mark likewise matches that orientation (0.786 versus 0.201). These are shape/orientation diagnostics, not proof that every stroke is the same source drawing; see the [original-image geometry SFT](../sims/lp2_mobius_motif_geometry_sft.py).

**What a solver could infer:** the unequal lobes and inward-facing tips plausibly suggest a general left-right path or cyclic/twisted topology. But the art has no arrow, uniquely marked terminal, page-specific reflection/rotation, or differentiated seam. The same orientation is repeated at both ends of every opening page, so neither tip is singled out as the book's entry or exit. The enlarged p15 copy changes scale and placement, not orientation. A reverse reading of the two tips remains equally consistent with the image. The motif therefore does not select the starting cell, row/column fill, traversal direction, wrap boundary, or twist for a 27×27 arrangement. The equality 729=27² is arithmetic motivation, not a source-fixed grid instruction.

**Previously tested interpretations.** On the opening 729 runes, μ(n) additive masks and one-dimensional Dirichlet Möbius inversion were tested in row-major and centre-out spiral order, alongside two-dimensional divisor-lattice Möbius inversion on a 27×27 grid. The same one-dimensional operations were tested on the separate p15–22 span (1,903 runes), continuously and with page resets. Results remained near the uniform 29-symbol baseline; the best opening-block IoC was 0.03485 and the best p15–22 IoC was 0.03466. No output was readable. The direct 4×4 Hill interpretation of the p15 square is also extensively screened; do not repeat it. See [the source-derived Hill audit](clue-matrix-hill.md) and [the p15 block test](p15-hill-block-test.md).

**Decision:** the repeated figure is real and may be a general cyclicity/twist hint, but the original images do not select a unique seam or executable traversal. Neither the icon nor 729=27² specifies a reversible operation, so no original-cipher prediction is justified. Retire this specific topology/operation route; reopen only if an independent source marks an entry/exit or fixes the coordinate rule. Do not widen to arbitrary grid transforms, seams, or key phases.

### 5. Opening stave figures → Younger Futhark twig/cipher-rune candidate (Issue #194 reopened)

**Original evidence and geometry.** The two large p0 black figures are on opposite margins of the opening page; the same 180°-opposed pair is reused on pp1–2. They are not Ogham: the marks are not parallel, regularly spaced Ogham scores and do not supply a plausible Ogham transcription. The previous Ogham/directional-traversal rejection remains valid.

**New branch-rune reading.** A different, historically documented notation fits the shapes much better: Younger Futhark twig-runes / cipher runes encode a rune by the number of branches on either side of a vertical stave. On fixed p0 crops, the stave axis is the column with the largest vertical dark-pixel projection; lateral extents beyond an 8 px shaft margin are smoothed with σ=15 px and local maxima separated by at least 80 px are counted. Both p0 staves give **three branch groups on the left and three on the right**; the count remains 3|3 at prominence thresholds 80, 85 and 90:
- p0-left: left rows 359, 638, 983; right rows 437, 603, 977.
- p0-right: left rows 471, 844, 1012; right rows 465, 811, 1090.

Menota Handbook 4.0 β, §18.3.13, describes cipher runes as a row/ǽtt plus position encoded by branches on either side of a stave; it gives 3|5 = r and describes rows bottom-to-top and positions left-to-right: [Menota §18.3.13](https://www.menota.org/HB4_ch18.xml#18.3.13). Michael P. Barnes, “Runic Tradition in Orkney” (2003), describes the Younger Futhark 6:5:5 grouping and reverse group numbering; its third group is f-u-þ-a/o-r-k. Under that specific Orkney convention, **3|3 = þ**: [Barnes 2003 PDF](https://www.arild-hauge.com/PDF/Barnes_Orkney_2003_pp_4-17.pdf).

This gives a striking original-artifact check: the first black GP rune immediately after p0's 13-rune red span / red punctuation boundary is **ᚦ**, transliterated **TH**. The p0 annotation places the red marker at offset 13; the first rune after it is GP index 2 / TH. **Among all 18 annotated red spans, p0 is the only one whose immediately following rune is TH.** The p1 and p2 first runes are A and OE, so the repeated staves do not predict every page's first rune; they may mark the opening section or serve as a p0-specific checksum. The two staves are 180° copies and both have symmetric 3|3 counts.

This is a **candidate source-selected cipher-rune reading**, not proof of the author's exact convention or a plaintext recovery. The Orkney 6:5:5 ordering is a particular historical variant, not universal; the mapping must be checked against alternative branch-rune tables and a second source-visible control. The p22 horizontal object is not template-identical and has no established 3|3 reading.

**Relationship to the separate 27×27 p0 seed candidate.** The p0 red span has GP-prime-weight sum 662; φ(662)=330≡11 mod29 (J), uniquely among the 18 annotated red spans. φ(13)=12 selects a candidate column; φ(J)=10 gives a transformed centre I. The red boundary after 13 runes supplies a candidate 14-cell displacement: zero-based node start 334 to ciphertext start 348; the next one-based position after the boundary is 14. The first post-marker black line has length 7. With C−K, the candidate first-block output is `ASIGOTHE`; offset13, offset15, untransformed J and K−C controls do not produce the same output. This remains a candidate seed block, not established plaintext or proof of a 27×27 row-major architecture.

**Do not double-count the branch geometry.** The same 3|3 strokes may encode Þ as a twig-rune; they must not simultaneously be treated as independent evidence for a three-rune-node size in the 27×27 proposal. That node-size hypothesis now needs a separate source selector. The proposed φ(next-node-centre) step / half-length rule fits block02 but fails block03; the 38-block route and its later path starts/lengths remain unauthenticated. See [the external proposal audit](lp2-27x27-published-nextblock-branching.md).

**Decision (Issue #194 reopened).** Keep the Ogham and standalone directional-traversal branches retired, but reopen the stave investigation for the 3|3 Younger Futhark twig/cipher-rune hypothesis. The original-artifact match to p0 TH is too specific to discard as decoration. Next work should validate the branch count and alphabet convention independently, then test whether other source-visible marks yield further branch-rune values. Do not expand into arbitrary stroke-bit encodings or generic steganography.

### 6. Dotted mayfly → GIMP Dissolve image operation, not a cipher key

**Original location:** LP2 page 57 (57.jpg).

An external image reconstruction reports a strong match between the dotted insect and GIMP's historical Dissolve layer mode ([SECRA research and evidence index](https://secra.tech/research/liber-primus-strongest-findings/evidence/index.html)). GIMP's 2.8 manual describes Dissolve as producing a random pixel pattern in partially transparent regions. Crucially, 314159265 is a long-standing GIMP implementation constant: it appears as RANDOM_SEED in GIMP v1.2-derived source and is hard-coded in the modern Dissolve implementation to initialize the 4,096-entry table. It is therefore not evidence that the puzzle author deliberately selected π as a cryptographic key. The source clarification is recorded in [im-results/gimp-dissolve-source-pad-gate.md](gimp-dissolve-source-pad-gate.md).

**What a solver could infer:** reproduce an image-production effect to explain the stippling. This may identify the artwork workflow, but it does not select a plaintext transform or establish a keystream. The repository's existing GIMP-field-as-pad tests remain negative.

**Decision:** treat this as an image-rendering fingerprint, not an encryption key. Do not reuse the digits of the seed as a π-derived pad.
