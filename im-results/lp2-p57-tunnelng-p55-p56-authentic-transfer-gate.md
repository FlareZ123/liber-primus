## Decision

The frozen p57 word graph gives a unusually concrete terminal-page selector:

- `TUNNELNG` has canonical GP0 sum **94**;
- p50 cell94 prints `0u`;
- the same printed token decodes to **55** under the documented base59 alphabet and **56** under the documented base60 alphabet.

Those are exactly the adjacent unknown p55 and independently solved p56 physical pages.

This newly motivates one receiver that previously lacked this upstream selection: apply the **authentic p56 prime/totient/F-transparent cipher mechanism** to p55 in its original printed order.

That receiver is already decided by an exact original-source inverse. P55 as printed becomes impossible at zero-based rune **39**. P57 `TUNNELNG` and the 55/56 token pair do not select that reflection. All three surviving reflected outputs are nonprose and begin with the nonnative first word `TTHO`.

Close the direct TUNNELNG-selected authentic-p56-rule transfer. Preserve the `0u → 55/56` page-pair alignment as a source-internal clue.

## Why the authentic p56 rule is the first receiver to test

P56 has an independently verified historical decryption. Its native rule is fully specified:

- GP29 plaintext/ciphertext alphabet;
- consecutive ordinary primes beginning at2;
- shift by Euler `phi(p)=p-1` modulo29 for each non-F plaintext rune;
- literal plaintext F passes through unchanged and does not consume a prime.

The published p56 plaintext forward-encrypts to **85/85** original p56 ciphertext runes under this rule.

Once the TUNNELNG edge independently selects the p55/p56 pair, carrying that exact known adjacent-page mechanism onto p55 is cheaper and more source-constrained than inventing a new key schedule.
