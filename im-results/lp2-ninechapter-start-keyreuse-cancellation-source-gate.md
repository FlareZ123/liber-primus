# Nine original LP2 chapter starts: paired-pad cancellation gate

## Decision (17 November 2026)

The original nine chapter boundaries identify **36 precise start-aligned ciphertext pairs** with complete GP29 lengths **729, 1,145, 1,729, 1,903, 1,021, 1,433, 1,680, 3,008 and 308 runes**. If two such chapters use a *shared additive GP29 keystream restarted at the chapter initial*, the key cancels in `(C_a[i] - C_b[i]) mod29`, leaving the difference of two native-English GP29 plaintexts. This source-constrained direct model produces **no authentic unknown plaintext or usable key** and is incompatible with calibrated same-pad native-English statistics at long original lengths.

A second, distinct receiver gives the original p56 **plaintext-F-transparent** skip rule full license: a ciphertext F can be an encrypted non-F or a bypassed plaintext F, and the two chapter streams can desynchronize. A key-marginalizing pair alignment finds no above-null original start pair in a familywise test, and its weak leading 300-rune scores do not develop into strong, coherent complete-section signals. This is an evidence-based rejection of **these two frozen chapter-start shared-pad diagnostic families**, not a proof that any two unknown sections cannot share related key material.

Reproducer: [source-native CommonJS executable](../sims/lp2_chapter_start_keyreuse_original_sft.cjs). Inputs: unmodified [GP29 original corpus](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json) and independently solved [LP1 plaintext reference](../resources/liber-primus/lp1-plaintext-reference.txt). All measured calculations below were executed against these source arrays, with fixed deterministic seeds.

## Direct additive-key cancellation

The language model is learned by exact source-native GP encoding of **3,510 authenticated LP1 solved plaintext glyphs**, preserving the original single-rune ING/IO spellings. Its expected GP29 difference frequencies are `q[d] = Σ_x p[x]p[(x−d) mod29]` with a symmetric add-one native training prior. Score each original pair as `mean log(29q[(C_a−C_b) mod29])`. A positive English pair sharing any additive key has exactly the same difference register as two source-native plaintext strings.

- **All 36 complete original aligned pairs** have negative mean log-likelihood ratios. The best is **−0.004584** over the shortened 308-rune pair between chapter groups 1 and 8; the best pair at length **at least 729** is **−0.011851** (groups 2 and 3, all 1,729 runes). Source-native positive paired-LP1 controls at length 729 had median **+0.02208**, fifth percentile **+0.00893** in a 1,000-draw window experiment; controls at lengths 1,021 and 1,433 had fifth percentiles +0.01134 and +0.01239.
- On the **frozen first 300 runes**, the strongest of all 36 source pairs is chapters **5 and 6**, score **+0.0020872**. Exactly **3,044/5,000** matched pseudo-chapter panels generated a higher or equal family maximum when independent 300-rune contiguous offsets were chosen within each of the original nine chapter ciphertext streams. Empirical familywise **p=0.60888** using the add-one rule. This null preserves original per-chapter rune inventories and local anti-repeat behavior.

## Exact transparent-F clock-alignment diagnostic

The authentic p56 rule skips key advancement only when the *plaintext* rune is F (index 0). The unknown cipher's printed F may also be an encrypted non-F, so naïvely removing all ciphertext Fs would be wrong. A two-state forward dynamic program sums every legal pairwise same-key alignment at original chapter start, making the only possible unpaired observations source-visible ciphertext Fs. It canonicalizes multiple skip paths and terminates pairing at the first 300-rune prefix end, so a different number of plaintext-F bypasses does not force the two streams to consume the same final number of key values. Its matched-emission weight marginalizes over *every* unknown uniform additive pad value using the authenticated non-F GP29 language prior; skip probability is the LP1 native plaintext-F frequency **0.02090986**, frozen before reading original ciphertext.

- Independently synthesized **120 same-pad source-native LP1 pairs** at 300 runes, each encoded with exact F-transparent skipping, have median score **+0.007878** (fifth percentile **−0.004874**). The corresponding **120 independent-key** pairs have median **−0.006658** (95th percentile **+0.004505**). This is a real positive/negative control using two competing key architectures.
- The original first-300 maximum is **+0.0053562**, pair chapter groups **1 and 8** (the second ends at 308 runes). In **75 matched familywise panels** where each original chapter supplied one independently offset 300-rune contiguous span, **44** panel maxima were at least that strong; empirical **p=0.59211**. The source-aligned maximum is therefore ordinary even after admitting all possible source-visible literal-F bypass alignments.
- Top longer first-300 original pairs flatten when extended: group **1–3** scores **+0.002528 → −0.003014** over 1,145 runes, **2–7** **+0.002427 → −0.000881** over 1,729, **1–6** **+0.002297 → −0.005438** over 1,145, and **0–1** **+0.000072 → −0.003324** over 729. The short 1–8 pair ends at 308 runes, score **+0.004485**, and has no independent long-section strength. At 1,145 runes the same-pad LP1 positive control median was **+0.008108** (25 examples), independent-key median **−0.004481**. This control population is limited, and the actual unknown plaintext register might not be native English.

## Implication for decipherment

No verified 150-rune unknown-page text, source-derived key, or exact original forward plaintext reconstruction emerged. The paired-cancellation search is stopped here because neither of its frozen source-start alignment models beat its native-source matched-null gate. The historical p56 secret-key control motivates the transparent-F variant, but it does not prove that any unknown page uses that same cipher. Repeating key-phase offsets, arbitrary state restarts, page permutations, alternate English distributions, or tuned F masks would introduce unsourced complexity. A new first-party key-sharing clue could justify a different, separately frozen attack.

This is a **bounded architecture exclusion**, not a decryption of the 56 unresolved pages.
