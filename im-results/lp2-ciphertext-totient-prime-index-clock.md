# Ciphertext-totient nonperiodic prime-index clock: exact inverse and original decision

**Agent 2, 24 November 2026. Bounded negative; no unknown LP2 plaintext or authenticated key recovered.** All nine original regions reject every one of 464 initial prime ordinals and every legal transparent-F history. Three longer native planted controls recover their starting ordinals and 716/719 withheld runes. This experiment tests a distinct state update rather than another periodic key search.

## Mechanism

Known p56 supplies additive shifts S(j) = (prime[j] - 1) mod 29, where prime[0]=2, and a transparent plaintext F (GP index 0). LP1 also explicitly names primes and the totient function. The proposed new mechanism advances the nonperiodic ordinal by the totient of the **ciphertext** rune's GP prime value, rather than the plaintext rune, a constant, or a residue modulo a secret key period. The historical sources motivate the ingredients; they do not establish this combined operation.

For P=0: C=0 and retain j. Otherwise C=(P+S(j)) mod 29; advance j by gp.prime[C]-1. Given C, retain the transparent branch if C=0 and separately compute P=(C-S(j)) mod 29, accepting the consuming branch only if P!=0. Its next state is j+gp.prime[C]-1. This explicitly handles ciphertext F generated by non-F plaintext: its advance is one. Nonzero C advances by a positive even integer. The initial ordinal is independently granted to each of the nine regions, 0..463 inclusive (primes 2 through 3301), with no internal resets. Bound the sieve from maximum stride 108 and the longest region.

Exact state aggregation by ordinal preserves all future legality; integer path counts and origin masks are merged, with no beam, guessed F mask, lexical constraint, or language filter. A separate trigram Viterbi inverse merges by ordinal and last two plaintext runes. Its model is trained on solved LP1 sections with the target excluded; it freezes the best prefix path before decoding the heldout continuation. Re-encryption is an implementation check.
