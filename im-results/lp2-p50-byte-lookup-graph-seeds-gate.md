# Original 256-byte grid as a complete one-byte successor function

**Decision, 15 November 2026:** An overlooked, type-compatible interpretation of the printed **256** two-character values is the exact byte-addressed successor function **f(i) = B[i]**, where i and the canonical base60-decoded cell value both range from **0 through 255**. This defines a complete directed functional graph without guessing keys, a radix-specific modulus, unprinted row labels or a geometric traversal. It is a credible *technical object to inspect*, although **no source explicitly says to follow the byte-value successor rule**. The graph has precisely two cycles, lengths **11 and 2**. The historically printed 2014 values **3301** and **1033**, reduced modulo256 to legitimate byte indices, enter different cycles. A frozen direct GP29 additive consumer fails the **180 independently known terminal runes**, so no p55 plaintext, cryptographic key or continuation is recovered.

## Original source and why the function is one plausible type

The unchanged original pages LP2 **49–51** are archive scans **66–68**: first 10×8 table rows, then 13×8, then 9×8. The source-derived [canonical grid](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json) contains **256** two-character tokens with 60-symbol digit alphabet \`0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwx\`. Under the externally independently matched base60 interpretation, its [256 exact original-byte values](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json) have SHA-256 **\`3b9b07d9a26e6d55c432d94d2661fdff3c2b348daed06821f2bdb23184a4b290\`** and range 0–255 with **161 distinct** values. It is therefore a perfectly typed byte-addressed function on a complete 256-element domain. Because many values repeat, it is **not** a byte permutation and cannot itself be an invertible bytewise substitution cipher.

The contemporaneous **2014 original LP1 Wisdom 1033 / 3301 numerical squares**, the authentic [2014 OpenPuff magic-squares payload](../resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt), and the later signed 2016 clue “the numbers are the direction” offer potential motivation to examine **numeric movement**. The latter statement postdates LP2 and never specifies \`f(i)=B[i]\`, base60, zero-based positions or reduction modulo256. The original grid carries no printed row/column addresses or explicit stop convention. Thus this is an **investigator-selected but low-parameter typed hypothesis**, distinct from the prior q/r→nine-direction keypad graph; see the [different finished keypad gate](lp2-p50-directional-functional-graph-prime-ordinal-starts.md). The observed missing lowercase \`f\` in the second digits remains a known base59 alternative, so the canonical byte register is conditional.

## Exact graph and historical seed readouts

Following a value as the next zero-based position gives the complete original graph:

| Component | Directed cycle in exact source order | Number of starting addresses in basin | Maximum non-cyclic prefix |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Principal | \`0 → 203 → 68 → 199 → 47 → 76 → 114 → 197 → 115 → 247 → 192 → 0\` | **252 / 256** | 24 |
| Small | \`249 ↔ 158\` | **4 / 256** | 1 |

The entire small basin consists of addresses **9, 158, 227 and 249**. Distinct first-party historical numerical constants from the solved puzzle, *if* interpreted as byte-address seeds, have the exact behaviors:

- **\`3301 mod256=229\`**; \`f(229)=0\`, then the 11-cycle above repeats forever
- **\`1033 mod256=9\`**; \`f(9)=249\`, \`f(249)=158\`, \`f(158)=249\`, entering the 2-cycle in one move

These are fully reproducible **original-table structural observations**. The fact that the two source numbers fall into different components, and the small one enters the tiny 4-node basin, might attract a solver's attention. However the starting values, modulo convention, and particular outcomes were connected **retrospectively**. Even a numerically rare result is not a decryption or creator instruction. A deterministic seeded 100,000-permutation control of the exact 256-byte multiset found **772** random shuffles with \`f(229)=0\`, **385** in which \`9\` first enters a nontrivial 2-cycle, and **3** with both conditions. The last count is **a descriptive post-selection frequency, not a valid p-value for author intent**, because the observed two conditions were noticed after inspecting the graph and there are many other possible grid operations and interpretations.

The **11+2=13** cycle-node total resembles some earlier 13-character LP1 keys but the cycle values reduced modulo29 do **not** spell a known key. No independent author clue selects concatenation, their direction or any rune decoding. Do not turn that numeral into evidence of a recovered 13-symbol secret.

## Known 180-rune holdout before unknown p55

A concrete, frozen consumer interprets the pointer-generated byte state as a direct GP29 additive keystream: \`s[0] = historical_value mod256\`, \`s[i+1]=B[s[i]]\`, \`P[i]=C[i] ± (s[i] mod29)\`. Its putative receiver is the terminal **256** original runes, with p55 unknown at positions 0–75 and the independently authenticated p56/p57 output at positions 76–255. We preselect the two historically printed seeds **3301/1033**, both additive signs, and both legitimate p56 registers (original ciphertext and solved prime-totient plaintext). We do not tune phase, key selection or page starts to fit English. The p57 known **95** clear runes provide a particularly decisive direct condition: a whole-terminal additive decoder must leave each unchanged, hence each of those 95 states must be **0 mod29**. Neither graph orbit satisfies that condition.

| Fixed source seed | Sign | Match solved p56 **/85** | Match clear p57 **/95** | Joint authentic known plaintext **/180** |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3301 | minus | **4** | **18** | **22** |
| 3301 | plus | **4** | **18** | **22** |
| 1033 | minus | **1** | **0** | **1** |
| 1033 | plus | **5** | **0** | **5** |

The direct consumer fails exact recovery of both authentic terminal registers. In particular, the 3301 orbit has **77 /95 nonzero** GP29 key states on clear p57 and the 1033 orbit has **95/95**. Positive controls plant an original GP29-native source text into the *same fixed* reversible arithmetic, then recover **every 180/180** symbol; thus this is an authentic source holdout rather than a cipher implementation mistake. A p55-only rim operand would bypass this holdout but has **no supplied instruction** for an independent mapping or cipher operation, and it cannot be rescued by treating this failed all-terminal model as proof of its different interpretation.

## Director decision

**Retire the source-seeded direct whole-terminal GP29 additive keystream.** The 256-byte functional graph itself is a new, reproducible representation of original p49–51 data; its historical-seed basins are observations worthy of remembering as *provisional numerical hints only*. Nothing in the printed manuscript or authenticated Cicada messages currently selects the byte pointer operation or supplies the next typed consumer that would predict authentic p55 plaintext. The complete 180-symbol holdout excludes a straightforward interpretation without requiring another key hunt. A future original-source pointer instruction could justify a separate investigation; additional graph path embellishments, seed tuning, and guessed 13-byte keys have no present causal route to plaintext.
