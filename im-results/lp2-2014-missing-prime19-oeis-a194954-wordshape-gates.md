# 2014 omitted prime 19: word-position test and source-derived direct-key gate

## Original clue and testable route

The [preserved 2014 signed Cicada stage11 message](../resources/historical/cicada2014-stage11-message.asc) displays prime-length blanks **2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 23, 29, 31, 37**, omitting **19**. The 5 is an internal indentation before the onion URL; the other ten values are trailing spaces. **Trailing whitespace is not authenticated** by the OpenPGP cleartext signature. The missing prime 19 is the Gematria Primus prime assigned to **ᚹ (W)**. The earlier [original W-transparent prime/totient decision](lp2-2014-missing-prime19-Wtransparent-original-cipher-gate.md) already proves its fixed p56-style transfer algebraically impossible by p0 rune6, irrespective of plaintext-word frequencies. This complementary test removes the need to assume that particular additive key, prime clock, or cipher family: it establishes a **key-independent plaintext-W positional ceiling even for a non-exclusive W bypass** under intact printed word boundaries.

If a plaintext **W always emits ciphertext W**, every original printed word beginning with plaintext W must also begin with ciphertext W. Thus the ciphertext has at least as many W-initial words as the plaintext, regardless of its secret state transitions or whether **other plaintext letters can also encrypt to W**. The same monotone necessary condition holds for terminal W and for W anywhere. This generalization matters: authentic solved LP2 p56 passes plaintext F unchanged while an encrypted non-F can also emit ciphertext F, a **non-exclusive transparent rune**. If **no other** plaintext symbol can encrypt to W, the preceding inequalities become equalities. A single-symbol change of the word-length register or use of encrypted separators can evade the bound. The original transcription's 55 encrypted runic pages p0–p55 (p50 contains no runes) match the canonical 12,956-rune ciphertext exactly. After joining visual line wraps **and the 32 genuine cross-page word continuations** and splitting only at marked punctuation/word-dot separators there are **2,896 complete words**. Splitting mechanically at page boundaries would create 32 artificial word groups and subtly bias terminal-W counts. Nine genuinely solved GP sections have **740** rune-word groups and **2,979** runes.

| Invariant | LP2 unknown ciphertext | Genuine solved GP plaintext |
|---|---:|---:|
| Initial W | 82 / 2,896 = **2.83%** | 79 / 740 = **10.68%** |
| Final W | 98 / 2,896 = **3.38%** | 8 / 740 = **1.08%** |
| All W | 401 / 12,956 = **3.10%** | 98 / 2,979 = **3.29%** |

Native GP control is not a prescribed plaintext target, so these tests measure how incompatible the source-derived *transparent W* hypothesis is with ordinary source-native prose, rather than a mathematical impossibility for every conceivable message. Exact two-sided Fisher tests give initial-W **p = 1.1566×10⁻¹⁶**, final-W **p = 0.00034776**, and all-position-W **p = 0.59949**. Thus the tempting overall W-frequency agreement conceals severe word-position disagreement. Three separately retained English prose controls support an English-like initial-W rate well above the ciphertext: Shakespeare **6.87%** (55,407 words), Gibran **6.78%** (15,733), and Emerson **6.44%** (77,932); their terminal-W rates are 1.22%, 0.99%, and 1.04% respectively. The independent **2,896-word contiguous-window control** tests the empirically strongest necessary condition without treating words as independent Bernoulli trials. Across every **140,387** adjacent 2,896-word window in the three historically appropriate prose sources, the fewest W-initial words are **158** (Shakespeare, 52,512 windows), **166** (Gibran, 12,838 windows), and **140** (Emerson, 75,037 windows). The original ciphertext allows **at most 82** initial-W plaintext words under *any* source-clock or other ciphertext-W collision pattern. None of these complete source-native text windows fits the ceiling. These overlapping windows are descriptive holdouts, not 140,387 independent statistical tests.

An exact GP28 W-excluding bijective substitution preserves all source W positions and passes the original-script test. A second positive plant implements the genuine p56-style **non-exclusive bypass** with W substituted for F: if plaintext is W, emit W without advancing the prime-totient stream; otherwise emit the GP29 shifted symbol and advance the prime. On all 2,979 genuinely solved GP plaintext runes it emits **189 ciphertext W** from **98 plaintext W**, including **91 additional W from non-W plaintext**. The resulting ciphertext has **99** W-initial words versus the source's **79**, and **32** W-final versus the source's **8**. The plant satisfies the monotone lower bound and demonstrates explicitly why the equality assumption would be too restrictive.

**Decision:** under the conditional assumptions of preserved complete-word segmentation and GP-native English, **even the non-exclusive p56-style transparent-W bypass is inconsistent with source-language W-initial frequencies**: it cannot output fewer initial ciphertext W than already exist in its plaintext. This rejects a much broader family than the preexisting fixed p56/φ prime-clock theorem. A different underlying language, encrypted/redefined word separators, characters not representing literal W, or a change in register before the W-bypass can evade the conditional inference. An arbitrary non-exclusive W output *alone* cannot evade the positional ceiling. No unknown ciphertext plaintext is recovered.

## Source-extrapolated A194954 keystream

The above eleven primes equal the start of [OEIS A194954](https://oeis.org/A194954), observed already in a [2015 community inventory](https://github.com/scream314/cicada3301/blob/master/hints_never_used.md#messagetxtasc-2014). It is a **possible** historically visible key stream, without a source-selected deciphering operation; the eleven-term match is not unique evidence of author intent.

The direct A194954-derived family was previously tested at original chapter boundaries with the authentic plaintext-F rule in [the 18 November recurrence decision](lp2-2014-a194954-recursive-prime-original-inverse.md), including the gap and binary-popcount streams. This 30-model extension adds page/global clocks, both additive signs and native-solved positive controls. It **corroborates an existing negative**, rather than identifying a new source-selected candidate cipher.

The first **12,956** terms terminate in a(12956)=**641057**. We tested exactly five recurrence-derived masks (a(n), totient(a(n)), prime gap, gap divided by bitcount(n−1), and bitcount(n)) with both GP29 additive signs, on three clock conventions (continuous, illustrated-chapter reset, page reset). These **30** fixed original-input direct inverses were checked against exact inverse round-trips and a 2,979-rune authentic native-GP plant.

Raw LP2 index of coincidence is **0.03447840**. The authentic solved GP source is **0.06138673**. No candidate has native-GP frequency structure or readable plaintext.
