# Key-free source-text fingerprint from original LP2 word lengths

**Scope and decision (11 November 2026).** Original LP2 word boundaries persist as a possible side channel regardless of the rune substitution/stream cipher, provided the encryption preserves word segmentation. We tested an independent route to *actual* plaintext: a source passage reused verbatim from an already available philosophical/literary text would have a long, highly distinctive sequence of Gematria-Primus rune word lengths. Neither the literary/language controls, the historically named book-code texts and the authenticated 2016 and March 2013 Cicada-signed messages produced a decisive match. **No unknown LP2 plaintext or key was recovered.**

## Original-input test and reproducibility

The runic-only 55 unsolved pages in `resources/lp2-transcription.txt`, joined across nonbreaking printed line/page wraps using the canonical word contract, contain exactly **2,896 words / 12,956 runes**. The tester `sims/lp2_wordlength_source_alignment_sft.cjs` counts a word's runes in the original ciphertext. For reference prose it converts Latin-letter words to approximate GP lengths by greedily recognizing the multigraphs `TH, NG, EO, OE, AE, IA, EA` as one rune each. It uses an exact six-word length seed and extends the alignment over at most 40 words allowing two rune-length discrepancies; the reported candidate lengths exclude the first third mismatch. Reference corpora are the repository's Emerson essays, Shakespeare excerpt, previously solved LP1 prose, Gibran's *The Prophet*, and the Latin Vulgate already encoded into one-GP-rune-per-character words. The Latin control is read as one complete base29 GP word per line, excluding header comments.

**Authentic solved-page control:** p57's unencrypted PARABLE and its 19-word body comprise 20 canonical rune words. Converting their independently known Latin transliteration yields **19/20 word lengths exactly**: the single difference is zero-based word 4, where the original rune spelling `TUNNELNG` has seven GP runes, while standard `TUNNELING` has eight. This establishes that a narrowly fuzzy source scan is necessary even for genuinely known text.

The first two fuzzy search maxima are at LP2 word 2210 against Emerson `FROM THINGS FROM SPACE FROM LIGHT FROM TIME FROM MAN BUT ONE WITH` and LP2 word 2585 against Shakespeare `THAT BE NOBLE HELP HIM YOUNG AND OLD CITIZENS DOWN WITH HIM DOWN WITH`, respectively. They contain two mismatches each and have **no cipher-independent evidence of plaintext identity**. The LP1 line summaries include editorial and duplicate solved material; therefore its negative is only a finite-file comparison.


**Original-boundary length profile:** all 2,896 unknown words have mean 4.47376 runes, 83 single-rune words (2.87%) and 448 two-rune words (15.47%). For the finite reference sets, corresponding GP-length means and two-rune fractions are Emerson 4.166/26.88%, Shakespeare 3.949/20.84%, Gibran 4.012/23.24%, solved LP1 prose approximately 4.13/22.35%, and Latin Vulgate 5.141/17.24%. In independently sampled (150-word stride) 2,896-word contiguous control windows, the two-rune proportion never falls below 24.65% in Emerson, 17.99% in Shakespeare, or 21.20% in Gibran, versus the unknown 15.47%. The unknown may contain different prose style/language or non-native word framing. These simple word-length differences do **not** establish that native GP English is impossible, and selecting a language after inspecting these distributions would require a held-out original-cipher consequence.


## Prior-author-selected historical-book gate (Agent 6, 11 November 2026)

This is a more causally motivated crib family than generic literary search. The [original 2013 signed book-code riddle and 2013 contemporary solve archive](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/tree/ade9a02889ef8acbc8cde8edbfd0a785c369f4c3/2013/001-232.jpg) identify Aleister Crowley's *Liber AL vel Legis* by its injunction to destroy the copy after reading. The **creator-selected** source is an external text used directly to solve an earlier Cicada book code, not a conjectured LP2 quotation. The separately signed 2012 second-chance image [selected a printed William Blake clipping](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/tree/ade9a02889ef8acbc8cde8edbfd0a785c369f4c3/2012/008-second-chance), specifically the "Voice of the Devil" passage, for another original historical book code. If LP2 reused one of these exact passages as unsolved plaintext, native word separators might preserve an identifiable 20-word or longer length signature independent of the encryption key.

The historically archived participant reference texts are pinned verbatim as `resources/external/2013-book-of-law-{I,II,III}.txt` and `resources/external/2012-marriage-of-heaven-and-hell-page.txt`. Their authorship as *literary works* is distinct from the independently signed Cicada messages selecting those works. These particular archived transcriptions are third-party copies, with possible OCR/copy differences. The source selection occurred before this LP2 comparison. The [new executable original-ciphertext SFT](../sims/lp2_historical_book_wordlength_sft.cjs) parses the canonical 2,896 rune words with `sims/lp2_word_pages.cjs`, removes verse numbering in the literary files, rejoins the Blake clipping's source-printed intra-word line hyphen, and counts 29-letter Gematria Primus word lengths, using `TH/NG/EO/OE/AE/IA/EA` as one rune each. It computes longest *exact* substring via dynamic programming, plus the prior fixed six-exact-word seed, 40-word cap and two-mismatch scan. No unknown ciphertext symbols are used in choosing the texts or conversion conventions.

The four archival files contain respectively **1,897, 1,968, 2,002 and 120** Latin-letter words. Across the combined **5,867** Crowley words the longest exact consecutive match with the original ciphertext is **8 words**, and the longest two-error match is **13 words**. The latter aligns original LP2 word **1,131** (physical p20) against chapter II source word **1,264**, beginning *"lift thee up nor shall they who cry aloud their folly that thou"*. Its 13 matching lengths contain two errors. There is no independent clue selecting this page/verse pairing, and the sequence is too short to identify a quotation. The Blake original clipping gives a maximum of **7** exact words and no ≥11-word two-error match. A known p57 textual control matches **19 of 20 words**; a different hidden **40-word** authentic-Crowley planted alignment with two length errors is recovered as **40/40** under this same scanner. The detector has demonstrable power for a verbatim 40-word source passage, while long genuine encrypted quotations could still be missed by non-preserved word boundaries or differing GP segmentation.

**Executed independent result:** [successful manual GitHub Actions run 43005379989](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43005379989), commit `dca63d9`, Node 22. Its log prints all four source chapter scores, source positions and both positive controls. The test recovers **no unknown plaintext, authentic key or LP2 cipher**; it is a finite negative for literal passage reuse from precisely these two historical book-code source works.

## Cicada-signed 2016 message: original-word-length crib (Agent 28, 11 November 2026)

**Finite decision: the exact 43-word signed message does not align distinctively with unknown LP2 words.** The original OutGuess payload from the 2016 \`4gq25.jpg\` clue is a preserved **1,136-byte** clear-signed ASCII file with SHA-256 \`fe89e77519dfaca998a257ed39c2861303029de2a49183db00eea0fda3a28010\`. Its OpenPGP signature was previously verified under repository key fingerprint \`6D854CD7933322A601C3286D181F01E57A35090F\` ([authenticated source record](../resources/cicada2016-4gq25.md)). The full signed body starts \`Hello.\` and ends \`Beware false paths. Verify OpenPGP 7A35090F.\`; word and paragraph boundaries are preserved in that file. Tokenizing exact source-body alphanumeric tokens and selecting alphabetic words yields **43** words. The signed numeric \`3301\` and mixed \`7A35090F\` are excluded as non-GP words; importantly \`7A35090F\` is **not** split into accidental \`A\` and \`F\` tokens. This choice is fixed before comparison.

The existing canonical **2,896-word / 12,956-rune** LP2 word parser is reused, including words continued across physical page turns. The existing historical crib detector recognizes the longest exact common word-length sequence and separately requires **six consecutive exact GP lengths before allowing two later mismatches, with a 40-word cap**. Latin words use the repository's frozen GP multigraph rules \`TH/NG/EO/OE/AE/IA/EA\`. The significance gate was fixed at **at least 20** consecutive source-aligned word lengths with at most two mismatches, plus independently selected placement or actual rune-level plaintext before accepting a true crib.

## What this changes and what it does not

The approach is independent of any encryption key or statistical GP29 decoder. It provides a lightweight, positive-controlled *crib detector* for a genuine source-reused passage, potentially enabling a known-plaintext attack even if ciphertext frequency is near uniform. Across the original five generic controls and the additional exact 2012/2013 creator-selected book-code corpora, the longest alignments remain far short of a compelling recovery. The finite negative does not rule out independently composed text, a missing source, extensive paraphrasing, other languages or GP orthography varying beyond two words per span. English word lengths cannot identify a ciphertext key by themselves.
