# Key-free source-text fingerprint from original LP2 word lengths

**Scope and decision (11 November 2026).** Original LP2 word boundaries persist as a possible side channel regardless of the rune substitution/stream cipher, provided the encryption preserves word segmentation. We tested an independent route to *actual* plaintext: a source passage reused verbatim from an already available philosophical/literary text would have a long, highly distinctive sequence of Gematria-Primus rune word lengths. Neither the literary/language controls, the historically named book-code texts and the authenticated 2016 and March 2013 Cicada-signed messages produced a decisive match. **No unknown LP2 plaintext or key was recovered.**

## Original-input test and reproducibility

The runic-only 55 unsolved pages in `resources/lp2-transcription.txt`, joined across nonbreaking printed line/page wraps using the canonical word contract, contain exactly **2,896 words / 12,956 runes**. The tester `sims/lp2_wordlength_source_alignment_sft.cjs` counts a word's runes in the original ciphertext. For reference prose it converts Latin-letter words to approximate GP lengths by greedily recognizing the source-attested multigraphs `ING, TH, NG, EO, OE, AE, IA, EA` as one rune each. It uses an exact six-word length seed and extends the alignment over at most 40 words allowing two rune-length discrepancies; the reported candidate lengths exclude the first third mismatch. Reference corpora are the repository's Emerson essays, Shakespeare excerpt, previously solved LP1 prose, Gibran's *The Prophet*, and the Latin Vulgate already encoded into one-GP-rune-per-character words. The Latin control is read as one complete base29 GP word per line, excluding header comments.

**Authentic solved-page control, corrected (Agent29, 11 November 2026):** p57's unencrypted PARABLE and its 19-word body comprise 20 canonical rune words. The rune ᛝ can represent **ING** as one unit, evidenced independently in solved LP1 `ᚦᛝᛋ` = THINGS and `ᚠᚩᛚᛚᚩᚹᛝ` = FOLLOWING as well as solved p57 TUNNELING. Recognizing `ING` before `NG` gives **20/20 exactly matched word lengths**, eliminating the previously reported false word-4 transliteration mismatch. Both NG and ING remain valid contextual spellings; an exact word-length crib still requires a matched textual source. The previous 19/20 claim and its argument for mandatory fuzzy scanning are superseded. The existing SFT asserts 20/20.

## One optional decorative rune per word: source-recovery inverse (Agent29, 12 November 2026)

**Result: finite candidate-source negative under a more permissive visible-word cipher.** The established p57 plaintext says to “shed our own circumferences”. An economical recoverable source-quotation hypothesis is that each unchanged original word boundary contains the source word's GP runes plus **zero or one independently inserted decorative rune**, perhaps at its edge. This is distinct from direct exact-length crib matching, and independent of how the cipher scrambles rune identities: the exact necessary word-length condition is `C_len = P_len` or `C_len = P_len+1`. The test grants the inserted rune at **any position** within a word and chooses the insert/no-insert bit independently at **every word**, making it strictly more permissive than a literal fixed leading/trailing one-rune null. It cannot rule out two-or-more nulls, unpreserved source word boundaries, or non-verbatim prose.

The SFT builds all six-word source-length fingerprints reachable by choosing a 0/1 null mask on six original words, then scans every word of each complete reference without choosing special original pages or quoted lines. Every candidate seed extends in both directions under the frozen per-word 0/1 condition. It ranks the longest exact *word-length-compatible* continuous source alignment, without decrypting or even looking at the original ciphertext glyph values. For each source and each tested encoding, replace 40 consecutive original lengths with a **known 40-word source passage with a deterministic mixed 0/1 insertion mask**, and check that the unchanged inference recovers its authentic position and every one of the 40 source words. ## What this changes and what it does not

The approach is independent of any encryption key or statistical GP29 decoder. It provides a lightweight, positive-controlled *crib detector* for a genuine source-reused passage, potentially enabling a known-plaintext attack even if ciphertext frequency is near uniform. Across the original five generic controls and the additional exact 2012/2013 creator-selected book-code corpora, the longest alignments remain far short of a compelling recovery. The finite negative does not rule out independently composed text, a missing source, extensive paraphrasing, other languages or GP orthography varying beyond two words per span.
