# Predecessor-conditioned GP29 substitution: exact nine-chapter concentration ceiling

**Scope and outcome (15 November 2026).** This tests one reversible, source-boundary-preserving stateful substitution model on **all 12,956 original unknown LP2 runes and 2,896 printed words**. Every current plaintext rune is substituted under a secret 29-symbol bijection selected by the **preceding published ciphertext rune**. The same 29 predecessor-specific bijections are reused within each of the **nine image-supported visual chapters**. Each chapter may choose 29 completely independent secret alphabets, for 261 arbitrary secret permutations across the whole manuscript. The first rune of every printed word is left unconstrained and omitted from the bound.

**Result:** At the original 10,060 eligible internal-word positions, any plaintext generated by this family has **at most 1,068 occurrences (10.6163%) of its most frequent rune**, **1,938 occurrences (19.2644%) of its two most frequent runes**, and **2,692 occurrences (26.7594%) of its three most frequent runes**. These are *exact necessary limits for all secret keys*, calculated without decoding guesses. Source-native English texts matched to the original complete word-length sequence repeatedly put the E rune alone at approximately 14.7% of the eligible positions, exceeding the one-rune cap. Therefore this keyed family cannot generate a GP29 English plaintext with that ordinary E frequency and the original ciphertext under the stated clock and boundaries. No unknown plaintext or key is recovered.

## Proof and original-source computation

For a chapter g, predecessor ciphertext rune a, and current ciphertext rune b, count original within-word transitions N(g,a,b). A keyed decoder assigns a 29-symbol permutation to each pair (g,a), so it merely relabels that row's 29 outgoing counts. For any selected set S of k plaintext runes, the row's occurrences of S cannot exceed the sum of its k largest counts. Summing these largest-k counts across all (g,a) is therefore an **exact key-independent upper bound** for the combined frequency of *any* k plaintext runes at the retained positions. This argument holds for every row permutation and requires no plaintext assumption.

For 29 globally reused predecessor alphabets, the original top-1/2/3 maxima are **578/1,086/1,582 of 10,060**. For the more permissive nine-chapter rekeying, they are **1,068/1,938/2,692 of 10,060**. A yet more permissive **physical-page-rekeyed** variant has maxima **2,396/4,135/5,624 of 10,028** eligible sites, because 32 internal sites cross physical page boundaries and are omitted. Its top-one ceiling of **23.8931%** does *not* exclude ordinary English E frequencies. In particular, do not transfer the nine-chapter exclusion to per-page or per-word rekeys.

The input comes from `resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json` and `resources/liber-primus/lp2-transcription.txt`, with original visual-chapter lengths **729, 1145, 1729, 1903, 1021, 1433, 1680, 3008, 308**. A word-internal position must have its previous rune in the same printed word and, when testing a chapter/page key, in the same partition. We make no inference about word-initial output-state handling.

## Language-positive and model-positive controls

Two independently sourced English registers, `resources/control/shakespeare_first300k.txt` and `resources/control/gibran_prophet_58585.txt`, are normalized with the project's GP29 transliteration and the authentic **ING→NG** and **IO→IA** single-rune conventions. From each register, 30 deterministic seeded complete-book controls sample words *of exactly the original 2,896 printed GP rune lengths*. At the original internal sites, E alone averages **14.76% for Shakespeare** and **14.73% for Gibran**; every one of the 60 controls exceeds the original 10.6163% ceiling. The sampling retains authentic original word-length inventory while varying source words; it is a finite English-style reference, not a claim about all possible plaintext.

For a **positive encryption control**, the SFT creates 261 independent secret 29-rune permutations, encrypts one full matched Shakespeare GP text with the *exact proposed predecessor-chapter model*, decrypts it completely, and verifies all 29 original-style majorization inequalities against the planted plaintext. This distinguishes a legitimate key-independent necessary condition from a broken decoder or self-fulfilling score. Its unknown-ciphertext input is unchanged.

## Causal interpretation and limit

This closes a useful, mathematically testable branch of **chapter-stationary predecessor-conditioned GP29 single-rune bijections** as an explanation of ordinary native-English ciphertext with authentic printed word boundaries. It is independent of prior word-position-indexed global alphabets and of additive ciphertext-feedback models: the 29 alphabets here depend directly on the preceding observed ciphertext rune and may all change at every one of the nine candidate visual chapters. The statistical argument depends on the *hypothesized ordinary English E concentration*, while the top-k cap is exact for every plaintext and secret key.

It **does not** exclude literal-English text with atypically low E, a changed spelling register, page/word/position rekeys, hidden state, non-bijective/homophonic encoding, inserted or deleted runes, changed printed segmentation, or mechanisms in which the previous ciphertext does not fully select the substitution alphabet. No original authored instruction selects this model. An author-selected independent state/key rule or recovered unknown-page plaintext remains the required next step. Additional predecessor-only key sweeps under the same nine-chapter and GP29 assumptions cannot improve this exact ceiling.
