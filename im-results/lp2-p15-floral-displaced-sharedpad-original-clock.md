# P15 cell offsets as a displaced shared-pad clock for the 16 floral pages

**Decision, 13 November 2026.** A source-motivated but author-unselected common-key hypothesis fails to resemble ordinary source-native GP29 English under the original p15 spiral schedule. Exactly aligned glyph pairs on pp40–55 retain markedly fewer native-English collisions even when an oracle chooses the best of **all 29 independent Caesar offsets for every pair of pages**. A row-major schedule leaves a small native-control overlap under this unusually permissive oracle. No unknown plaintext, authentic key or Cicada-selected cipher is recovered. This is a finite original-input diagnostic, not a universal exclusion of independently keyed pages, feedback, other alphabets or an additional transformation.

## Human solve-path premise and falsifiable model

Original physical p15 prints a four-by-four number square generated by a Fibonacci-indexed prime spiral beginning at the red 3299; original pp40–55 form a run of sixteen floral-framed physical pages. Their physical p50 contributes **zero runes** but still occupies the eleventh page/cell slot. These independently visible sets suggest, as a *testable hypothesis*, matching the sixteen p15 cells to the sixteen floral pages and using each printed value as the start of a common GP29 additive keystream. This is an analyst's bridge: the manuscript does not instruct the solver to pair those artifacts or to reuse a pad.

The literal printed square, in physical row-major order, is:

```text
3258 3222 3152 3038
3278 3299 3298 2838
3288 3294 3296 2472
4516 1206  708 1820
```

The spiral order begins at 3299 and follows the printed Fibonacci-prime construction:
`[3299,3298,3296,3294,3288,3278,3258,3222,3152,3038,2838,2472,1820,708,1206,4516]`.

For page a at within-page rune position i and page-specific start s(a), the proposed encryption is `C(a,i) = P(a,i) + K(s(a)+i) + b(a) (mod 29)`. Each b(a) may be an arbitrary hidden page Caesar shift. Align any two original runes for which `s(a)+i=s(b)+j`: their ciphertext difference is the plaintext difference plus the same pairwise offset `b(a)-b(b)`, independent of every unknown key symbol. A shared pad necessarily preserves the *histogram* of aligned pairwise differences up to rotation. As a generous upper bound, the experiment maximizes each page pair's 29 difference-bin counts separately, permitting mutually inconsistent page shifts that no single global decoder could achieve.

Eight exact schedules are frozen before inspecting their results: spiral or printed row-major order, forward or inverted-number clock, and an unwrapped absolute clock or period-3299 clock. The period-3299 option is exploratory because 3299 is explicitly printed red, rather than a derived keystream instruction. Word boundaries and page-continuation glyphs are retained exactly. A zero-rune p50 contributes no comparisons; deleting its page assignment would alter the source correspondence.

## Original-cipher measurements and native controls

For each schedule, each full 3,316-rune source-floral register is compared against **151** matching-length control windows from each of Shakespeare, Gibran and Emerson, using the repository's solved-LP1-calibrated GP spelling with the original single glyphs for ING and IO. The oracle statistic is the sum, over all aligned *page pairs*, of their largest 29 difference-bin counts.

The exact fixed-alignment equality count is **below every matched original-length control window** for all eight schedules, independent of the secret pad. The secret-page-shift oracle agrees for six of eight schedules; it is a very optimistic cipher-consistency bound and must not be interpreted as a recovered unknown Caesar offset. In the row-major alternatives, four control windows have oracle scores at most the original, weakening any all-clock rejection.

**Positive control:** for each of the eight schedules, the SFT encrypts an authentic GP-native English control with a deterministic shared keystream indexed at the same page-specific starts. With all page shifts zero, original plaintext fixed equality counts and oracle counts survive exactly; with independently nonzero Caesar offsets on every page, each original plaintext pairwise oracle also survives exactly. This validates that the invariant is a *necessary* condition for the precise hypothesized family. An original-cipher roundtrip without known plaintext would never authenticate a key.

## Interpretation and next decision

The p15-to-floral **cell→page** association is the major unsupported premise. Even accepting it, direct common additive pad reuse under the two visual read orders, both signed clocks, and both tested periods is unfavorable for ordinary GP-native English. This is independent of the earlier *same-physical-page-position* floral shared-pad negative in [lp2-floral-section-sharedpad-invariant.md](lp2-floral-section-sharedpad-invariant.md): that study fixes key starts equal, whereas this study fixes them at distinct p15 cell values.

Do not retune start offsets, page orders, word clocks or secret page shifts after this result merely to rescue a common pad. Reopen this path if an original source independently chooses a different clock, transformation or key role and yields a withheld original-cipher prediction. Prefer source-selected reversible operations or authentic external key recovery.
