## Source audit against the receiver contract

Issue #1623 requires a complete source-selected contract: source and sink, initial state/key provenance, exact reversible transition, clock and direction, and the radix consequence. The current source does not provide that contract.

### Source object and sink

The original pp49–51 table is real, and the 60 leading-zero cells are directly visible. The table does not label those cells as a mask, state, key, direction, opcode, or exception class. Its immediate context does not mark second-character parity.

No original caption, signed Stage11 wrapper, solved page, or authenticated delivery record says which unknown rune interval should consume the table. The empirical model aligns p50 cell0 to p0 rune0 and advances across p0–55, but the source-origin audit found no authored correspondence binding those two starts.

### Initial state and key provenance

The continuous phase-zero alignment is empirically preferred. The original p0 opening and pp49–51 table have no shared start mark, counter label, first-state instruction, or key identifier. No authenticated source supplies a secret state or key for the bit0 condition.

### Transition/update rule

The signed 2016 message gives genuine global guidance that Liber Primus's words are a map, meanings are a road, and **“their numbers are the direction.”** This licenses numerical directionality as a human clue.

It does not identify the pp49–51 table, the 60 leading-zero cells, the second printed character, or bit0. It does not say what two directions bit0 should choose, what state changes, whether a selected bit means hold, advance, branch, swap, or something else, or what reversible alphabet transform accompanies the branch.

Using the sentence to choose among those operations would introduce the free receiver menu #1623 was designed to prevent.

### Clock and direction

The correlation sharply prefers continuous global row-major traversal and outgoing/predecessor timing. Natural printed row-major order has source support.

The source does not independently instruct a 256-state counter over the whole unknown rune tape, phase-zero alignment to p0, wrap behavior, or continuous counting through page and chapter boundaries. The clock is therefore empirically selected rather than fully source-authenticated.

### Reversible encoding/decoding rule

No source-facing audit yields a finite reversible mapping from the bit state to GP29 plaintext. The most economical direct consumers already tested do not decrypt the original text. Adding another Boolean operation, state machine, cipher family, phase, reset, or key serialization would be a new hypothesis rather than completion of an authored instruction.

### Radix consequence

The shared leading-zero 60-cell class is stable under both documented numeral interpretations. The parity partition is not: **24/60 low cells change parity class** between conventional base60 and the documented drop-`f` base59 reading.

Conventional base60 bit0 is the stronger empirical predictor. Separate manuscript-internal DH work now uses both frozen readings in a p27→p50 arithmetic loop and materially favors base59 for its pointer traversal. That mechanism still does not globally authenticate the p50 numeral alphabet or select a bit0 receiver.

## Exact missing instruction

A creator-authenticated clue must bind the pp49–51 table to a named unknown-text sink and state clock, select the leading-zero/bit0 predicate under a fixed numeral alphabet, and specify the reversible state transition or alphabet operation commanded by each state.

Without that binding, the empirical bit state cannot distinguish among mutually incompatible reversible consumers. Plaintext scoring cannot supply the missing instruction.
