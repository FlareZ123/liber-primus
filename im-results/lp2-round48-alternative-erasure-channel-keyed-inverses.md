# Exact known-key decoders for two still-open Liber Primus output channels

**Decision — Agent6, 28 November 2026:** The independently proposed alternative hidden-output mechanisms have been converted into **working, source-independent finite-state decoders with the correct keystream supplied**. The new mod36 silent-symbol decoder recovers at least **97.62%** of surviving plaintext on each of 12 disjoint literary plants (mean **98.62%**); the GP29 post-filter deletion decoder recovers at least **87.98%** on each of 12 (mean **92.70%**). They beat their individual random wrong-key runs by large language-model margins. The original unknown-page keystream has *not* been recovered, and no authentic unknown-page plaintext has been identified. The mod36 score above covers only retained visible-emission plaintext; the full-path follow-up below recovers 95.02% mean complete-prefix LCS on six cross-author plants, with zero exact complete recoveries and a provably many-to-one hidden channel.

## Why this is a different route

The [Dukotah Round48 M1 independent pass (27 November)](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/liber-primus/analysis/round48/M1-INDEPENDENT-PASS.md) and its [complete experiment](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/liber-primus/analysis/round48/M1-leak-census/RESULTS.md) demonstrate that the original 86/12,955 adjacent equalities do **not** identify the fitted 0.813-repeat suppression rule. A strict-repeat filter, followed by either deletion of ≈18.7% of rune emissions, or a 36-symbol alphabet with seven nonprinting outputs, has virtually indistinguishable visible single-step statistics. The external [C11 public challenge](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/master/challenges/c11_alt_constructions.py) reports its older keyskip decoder recovering **0/40** known-key plants for each of the two alternatives. These are different ciphertext *channels*, with different inverse paths, even though their visible one-step statistics can coincide. T7's measured lag-one **doublet-gap** correlation +0.380 is still not explained by these mechanisms; this work does not claim otherwise.

The new decoders model the actual emission/suppression dynamics, not a shuffled candidate English paragraph. For each *visible* input rune they retain the cipher's most recent **emitted** symbol (even if that symbol was invisible), the consumed key-index state, and a rolling GP plaintext register. They enumerate 0–3 intervening invisible events, enumerate any intermediate rejected equal-cipher key draws, and emit only the rune characters genuinely printed in the ciphertext. A bounded Viterbi beam ranks those paths using a source-native **four-symbol** probabilistic model; the model's symbols are GP rune indices 0–28 plus an unprinted plaintext SPACE29 in between original English words. The key is supplied on controls and is never derived from the true plaintext in a test.

### 36-symbol *silent outputs*: exact state transducer

The new [silent-output inverse](../sims/lp2_alt36_silent_exact_latent_viterbi_sft.cjs) uses genuine 36-state ciphertext arithmetic `C=(P+K_j) mod36` with `P∈0..29` (29 native GP runes and plaintext SPACE29) and uniformly sampled `K_j∈0..35`. If the would-be output equals the previous emitted symbol, skip the key and retry with the same plaintext symbol. Outputs 29–35 remain in the cipher's state and consume the key, but print nothing; printed outputs are 0–28. Given a printed output c, its key-index j and previous emitted state, the inverse tests whether all skipped keys would have generated that previous same output. Between printed outputs it explicitly hypothesizes each hidden c∈29..35 and its plaintext p and key advancement, including key-rejection events. The control plants use the same fully specified original C11 channel family; exactness here refers to the *declared generative family*, not identified authorship.

**Fixed held-out test:** train on only the first 57% of each pinned Shakespeare, Gibran and Emerson donor word list (alphabet-normalization QU→CW, V→U, K/Q→C, Z→S, authentic ING/IO spellings). Plant 314-rune/spaced-symbol source windows from the untouched remaining 43%, strict-repeat encipher under a random uniform36 key and hide the symbols 29–35. Decoder beam400, max three hidden emits between prints, and one independently drawn wrong key. Every one of **12/12** fixed-seed controls recovers ≥85% of the retained printed plaintext under normalized longest common subsequence (LCS), with **97.62%–100%** exact recovery and mean **98.62%**. Wrong-key recoveries average **31.63%**; all true-key average per-observation language scores exceed paired wrong-key scores, minimum separation **2.682 nats/printed rune**. Exact true key-clock index recovered in **11/12**; the 12th differs by two consumed indices despite 98.58% rune recovery.

**Cross-author robustness, frozen decoder:** To remove even within-author training transfer, a second 12/12 run trains each model solely on the **other two** authors (Shakespeare withheld when testing Shakespeare, etc.), with the original plants, keys, model order, beam400 and max-hidden3 unchanged. Correct-key recoveries are **95.73%–100%**, mean **97.97%**, with **12/12** passing 85%; random wrong-key controls mean **31.58%**, and all true-key score margins exceed **2.638 nats/printed rune**. The result remains conditional on the correctly supplied key and C11-style in-sample style of GP29 English, but is not just memorization of a particular author's register. Reproduce by calling exported `experiment(raw,{beam:400,plants:12,crossAuthor:true})`.

**Independent external-author bridge:** In a third positive-control run, the receiver trained only on the original three donors and tested on **independent [Dukotah C11 development files](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/tree/master/liber-primus/data/keys/armada19) by John Stuart Mill (*On Liberty*) and Mary Shelley (*Frankenstein*)**, neither represented in model training. The same strict mod36 channel, beam400 and 0–3 invisible events, six fixed-seed ≈240–260-visible-rune plants yield **6/6** ≥85% recoveries; mean **99.19%**, minimum **97.55%**. Independent random wrong-key recoveries remain **31–33%**. This is substantially more out-of-register than the same-author and author-excluded controls, though not the challenge's sealed 40-plant official test. Invoke `experiment(raw,{beam:400,plants:6,external:true})` while passing additional raw strings `mill` and `shelley` fetched from those exact independent development files; the external texts are *test-only*, never incorporated into training.

### 29-symbol strict-repeat output followed by **deletion**

The new [exact hidden deletion receiver](../sims/lp2_alt29_delete_exact_latent_viterbi_sft.cjs) and [native hidden-word extension](../sims/lp2_alt29_delete_exact_latent_wordgap_sft.cjs) model uniform GP29 key, strict no-repeat on the *emitted* ciphertext state, then independent deletion of each emission with probability **0.187**. A deleted emission can consume plaintext, key and alter the cipher's previous-output state; it is not a missing key alone. The receiver enumerates all hidden plaintext rune values and 0–3 omitted emitted glyphs between visible glyphs, while reconstructing the exact rejection-rule key skips. The language prior optionally inserts unprinted plaintext word space without consuming key. The same first57%/last43% donor split and distinct true/wrong random key methodology applies. Its 12 fixed controls at beam500 recover **87.98%–95.40%**, average **92.70%**, versus average **34.69%** with random wrong keys. All 12 pass ≥85%, and all paired correct-key score margins exceed **1.077 nats/printed rune**. The receiver is a beam approximation to the exact transducer; max three consecutive hidden emitted symbols is an explicit boundary and can drop rare controls exceeding it.

[Machine-readable complete observed benchmark](lp2-round48-erasure-channel-knownkey-benchmark.json) includes all 24 true/wrong control LCS ratios, seed IDs, silent emission counts, language-score margins and key-clock evidence. Both executable decoders export `experiment(raw,options)`, require only Node standard libraries, and read pinned project resources. These are our own held-out controls, **not the official C11 40-plant checker or its 95th-percentile wrong-key score gate**. On current evidence, both families have an effective *known-key* inversion, not an authentication of either as LP2's actual cipher.

## Complete plaintext recovery and exact nonidentifiability (Agent11, 28 November)

The previous benchmark measures only plaintext GP runes corresponding to **visible** ciphertext emissions, excluding spaces and plaintext of hidden outputs. That is a legitimate retained-symbol score, not a lossless plaintext recovery. The [full-path adapter](../sims/lp2_alt36_full_plaintext_identifiability_sft.cjs) retains every latent plaintext symbol (including spaces and hidden-output events), its key index and emitted state. It uses the pinned original receiver blob `07ed9f331cff07655b775def9ec3e18ee7916cff`, same six first cross-author plants, beam400, three-hidden limit and unchanged scoring. The truth is truncated at the last printed output, since a wholly silent suffix is not evidenced by the observation. Results:

| Metric | Six controls |
| --- | ---: |
| Retained-rune normalized LCS | 97.94% mean |
| Full plaintext normalized LCS, including spaces and hidden outputs | 95.02% mean; 91.46–97.45% |
| Wrong-key full plaintext LCS | 32.38% mean |
| Exact full plaintext recovery | 0/6 |
| Exact full latent-event and visible ciphertext replay | 6/6 |
| Exact oracle-aligned unrestricted plaintext multiplicity | 2^104.14 to 2^157.07 |

The last row is a mathematical obstruction to treating this channel as a lossless cipher, even with the key and true event alignment supplied. Fix the real accepted key indices j_t, the observed visible symbols and the true print/silent mask. Let D_t(c) count paths ending at output c. For each candidate c (the fixed printed output or 29..35 for a silent event), compute p=(c−K[j_t]) mod36 and retain only p≤29. Sum D_{t−1}(a) over previous outputs a≠c such that every skipped key k between j_{t−1}+1 and j_t−1 satisfies (p+K[k]) mod36=a. Start with sentinel a=−1, count1 and j_{−1}=−1. Every surviving path is a distinct plaintext prefix because the fixed key and accepted clocks determine p from c. Exact BigInt counts therefore enumerate all compatible plaintext prefixes for this *oracle-fixed* alignment; additional clocks and suffixes can only increase ambiguity. The result is not an entropy estimate for English.

A linguistic collision is also possible: GP30 plaintext THE CAT IS RED and THE CAP IS RED (TH=2,E=18,space=29,C=5,A=24,T=16,P=13,I=10,S=15,R=4,D=23) under the same key `[34,19,9,34,16,16,13,33,29,16,6,29,25]` produces respectively `[0,1,2,3,4,32,6,7,8,9,10,11,12]` and `[0,1,2,3,4,29,6,7,8,9,10,11,12]`. Both satisfy strict no-repeat, have identical event clocks and mask, and print `[0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,11,12]`. This is a constructed existence proof, not an LP2 key or a frequency claim about real prose.

**Decision:** the model now has a usable approximate complete-text receiver and exact full-path replay, but it is intrinsically many-to-one. A claim of an authentic complete decryption under this architecture needs an independently recoverable redundancy/side-information mechanism, or a candid statement of remaining ambiguities; an LM MAP path and exact replay alone cannot certify hidden symbols. This does not exclude a deliberately lossy puzzle channel or ordinary English as a powerful prior. No original ciphertext was refitted, no key recovered, and no authoritative plaintext frontier changed.

[Raw outputs, full candidate and truth vectors, exact integer counts](lp2-alt36-full-plaintext-identifiability-output.json). The V8 core was executed; the Node CLI wrapper was not separately run. One Gibran plant contains Gutenberg boilerplate, so the benchmark is not six independently selected literary passages. The oracle multiplicities do not rely on the language model.

### Closed-red-seven augmented alphabet: source-derived hypothesis, unsuccessful

The eight red heading runes `M NG D IA TH X I M` begin and end with **M**, so exactly **seven distinct GP positions** are listed. Because a mod36 hidden-output model also needs seven unprinted states, we tested a falsifiable visual-alphabet hypothesis rather than treating this numerical match as an authored mechanism: insert one unprinted alphabet symbol immediately after each of those seven GP chart positions in increasing chart order `[TH,I,X,M,NG,D,IA]` (indices `[2,10,14,19,21,23,27]`), yielding exactly 29 visible and seven hidden states `[3,12,17,23,26,29,34]` in the 36-state ring. Native GP rune labels map to their visible 36-state positions; one hidden state maps plaintext SPACE29. Since the repeated heading's **M** is a plausible closure indicator, preselect the hidden symbol after M (space slot3 = 36-state position23). Key digits are the corresponding lifted rune positions, either repeated as all eight source glyphs or the seven distinct runes with final M marking loop closure. Also test the independent p56 prime-minus-one mod36 stream on the **heading-excluded** 299-rune recipient; this is only a source-derived candidate. The [exact augmented transducer](../sims/lp2_alt36_red7_insertion_alphabet_sft.cjs) models the forward rules and searches bounded inverse paths, includes invisible emissions and repeat-key rejections, and reads the *same original* p54 body and p55 as above.

- Eight-entry red cycle with M as hidden space: matched 266-printed positive control **99.08%** original-rune recovery, logscore **−2.071**; original **−5.225** and nonprose.
- Seven-entry closed red cycle with M as hidden space: matched positive **98.20%** recovery, score **−2.061**; original **−5.072**, nonprose.
- Prime-minus-one mod36 schedule with M hidden space: matched positive **98.51%** recovery, score **−2.079**; original **−5.045**, nonprose.
- As an **exploratory bounded family**, all seven candidate choices of the plaintext-space hidden symbol were also tested with fixed eight-red and prime keys, the complete 299-rune original, and no retuned model or key. Best original eight-red score was **−5.161** (hidden-space after NG), best prime score **−4.859** (hidden-space after D); every complete output is nonprose. No source cue selects those best slots, and their score advantage cannot justify one.

The seven-unique heading/hidden-state coincidence thus **does not supply an original key** under these specified many-to-one observation models. Retire the closed-seven augmented alphabet branch pending an independent instruction defining a nontrivial key/cipher recipient; never promote near-matching English fragments from the language-prior beam.

### Exact ambiguity with the actual tested source-derived key schedules (Critic, 28 November)

The augmented alphabet is also noninjective even when its **specific red7 or red8 cyclic key is supplied**, not merely for an arbitrary constructed key. Use GP30 plaintext indices `[2,18,29,5,24,16,29,10,15,29,4,18,23,29]` (THE CAT IS RED with trailing space) and replace only index3, C=5, by B=17 (THE BAT IS RED). Both are grammatical. Use the published lifted GP map, space slot3, zero initial phase, no IV. The seven-key cycle is `[22,25,28,33,2,16,11]`; the eight-key cycle appends 22. For each plaintext position i apply the strict forward rule `c=(lift(p)+K[j mod q]) mod36`, rejecting equality to the previous c; omit states in `{3,12,17,23,26,29,34}` and unlift visible values.

| Key cycle | Internal emissions for CAT | Internal emissions for BAT | Common printed GP indices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| red7 | 24,10,15,3,32,35,34,33,7,15,2,23,8,34 | same except event3 = 17 | 20,9,13,26,28,27,6,13,2,7 |
| red8 | 24,10,15,3,32,35,34,33,4,12,33,18,30,3 | same except event3 = 17 | 20,9,13,26,28,27,3,27,15,24 |

All 14 accepted indices are exactly 0..13 for both messages in both cycles; no repeat is rejected, the entire visible/silent mask agrees and the state agrees again immediately after the differing event. The two differing states 3 and 17 are both silent. Even disclosing the mask and all accepted clocks would not distinguish these words. Dropping the final space gives the same collision before the last printed symbol.

The GP29 strict-repeat-then-delete model has an analogous collision **under the exact tested prime-minus-one schedule**. Remove spaces from the two messages (C/B is index2); encode using `(p+prime_j−1) mod29` starting with prime2. Emissions are `[3,20,9,1,26,22,2,22,11,22]` and `[3,20,21,1,26,22,2,22,11,22]`. Both consume indices 0..9 without rejections. Delete event2 only in both (a positive-probability mask under independent deletion); both print `[3,20,1,26,22,2,22,11,22]`. Thus key and even exact deletion locations alone are insufficient. This is a constructive collision, not an estimate of English ambiguity frequencies or evidence that LP2 used either sender.

**Decision:** the inverse search is useful for candidate generation, not a reversible original-cipher mechanism. These examples preserve the successful retained-rune calibrations and original nonprose decisions. They invalidate the stronger claim that the augmented alphabet is entirely reversible, and show why a source-derived key schedule by itself cannot certify complete plaintext even for these exact variants. Retain the tools for conditional inference; require source-constrained redundancy or report unresolved alternatives before describing an exact decryption. Independently replayed with a direct forward implementation; no CI or language-model fitting was needed.

## Direct original-key decoding interface

The working core now accepts arbitrary supplied original/experimental rune sequences and **known key arrays** directly; this removes the positive-control harness as a prerequisite for future key discovery. Input is a JSON object `{"cipher":[0,1,...],"key":[5,13,...],"beam":600,"maxHidden":3}`, where `cipher` are visible canonical GP29 indices 0–28 and `key` entries are residues **0–35** for silent/mod36 or **0–28** for deletion/GP29. Supply at least as many key values as visible runes (normally considerably more because hidden emissions and rejected equal-output key draws consume extra indices). The user sets any genuinely source-derived clock origin by the key-array offset *before* calling; the recipient code must never pick the offset by reading an unknown original plaintext.

```sh
node sims/lp2_alt36_silent_exact_latent_viterbi_sft.cjs --input=/path/to/silent_candidate.json
node sims/lp2_alt29_delete_exact_latent_wordgap_sft.cjs --input=/path/to/deletion_candidate.json
```

Each returns the proposed **retained visible-emission plaintext GP rune sequence**, glyph string and transliteration, the consumed key index, approximate full-path log score, and explicit conditional status. The mod36 receiver does not retain hidden-output plaintext or spaces in its returned path (`advance` stores a node only when `printed && p<29`); the GP29 deletion receiver does not retain deleted plaintext or latent spaces (`deletedOne` preserves the previous path). Neither direct CLI returns the complete latent plaintext or enough events to independently replay it; use a full-path adapter for that purpose. The code checks alphabet/key bounds and rejects malformed input. The direct mode has been tested to **exactly reproduce** the earlier fixed original 299-rune red8 mod36 score **−5.6235500284** and 308-rune prime-minus-one GP29 deletion score **−4.1595207028** without any implanted plaintext. The ability to decode under a provided key is **not** evidence of that key's authenticity or the manuscript's actual sender.

## Decision and next causal requirement

These experiments **rescue two well-specified alternative channels from the previous “decoder fails even on known-key plants” obstacle**. The **silent-output** model is especially tractable (near-exact known-key recovery), so it deserves targeted work **only if a source-visible operand specifies a mod36 alphabet, a specific 36-symbol mapping or a small complete key-generator family**, with a separately justified method for resolving or explicitly reporting hidden-plaintext ambiguity. The 29-rune deletion model likewise requires an authentic key schedule beyond the already tried p56→terminal prime transfer, or a formal task that jointly learns the hidden clock and the key rather than scoring unconstrained strings. The low-doublet signature alone cannot tell which channel the creator used; the original T7 gap anomaly has not been accounted for. Do **not** generalize the conditional prime-key p54–55 negative to arbitrary Cicada keys or to all of LP2, and do **not** substitute this benchmark for the official C11 submission protocol.

Reproduce directly from the repo root:

```sh
node sims/lp2_alt29_delete_exact_latent_wordgap_sft.cjs --prime-original
node sims/lp2_alt29_delete_prime_literalF_sft.cjs --prime-plant
node sims/lp2_alt29_delete_prime_literalF_sft.cjs --prime-original
```

The benchmark JSON contains all 12 outputs each and their exact generator seeds. No CI or unbounded search required.

## Third construction: strict GP29 repeat filter plus latent piecewise-constant shift (Agent45, 28 November)

The external [C11 diagnostic](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/b271334d9718bf68187f7a1e6362731159c36a2a/challenges/c11_alt_constructions.py) leaves V-TWOLAYER unscored. Unlike deletion or silent emissions, its first-layer ciphertext is strictly nonrepeating, and every accepted symbol is printed after a second shift. The shift starts uniformly in GF29 and independently changes to one of the 28 other values with probability 0.186 before each emission. We implement all legal plaintext transitions with the known first-layer key and an approximate finite beam over accepted cursor, previous first-layer ciphertext and last three plaintext runes. The current printed rune determines the new shift; exact deterministic repeat rejection advances the first-layer key. The objective adds interpolated four-gram log probability and the exact second-layer shift transition log prior. Every returned path is forward replayed including key indices. No shift oracle enters the main decoder.

[Standalone decoder](../sims/lp2_alt29_two_layer_beam_sft.cjs) and [raw results including complete original candidate indices, shifts and accepted key indices](lp2-alt29-two-layer-beam-output.json). The first exploratory six samples used width 500, but the nearby sampling seeds selected overlapping donor windows; these are not independent performance evidence. The final width-1500 run uses separated seeds, two disjoint 240-rune windows per donor from the last 40% of Shakespeare, Gibran and Emerson, and trains only on the other two donors. Random first-layer keys, independent wrong keys, independent second-layer jumps. Six correct-key LCS recoveries are 77.5%, 85%, 90%, 85%, 89.17%, 82.92% (mean 84.93%); wrong-key mean 37.92%. Four of six reach 85% and all correct-key joint MAP scores beat all wrong-key scores in this tiny sample. Supplying the true second-layer shifts recovers 6/6 exactly; main decoder does not. This is neither the official C11 checker nor a reliable 80%-plant pass: different LM/scoring, six controls, and no external held-out seeds. The V8 core was run; standalone Node wrapper was not separately executed.

**Original test:** freeze the public p56 prime-minus-one sequence starting at prime 2 as the first-layer key, apply exactly this unmodified second-layer decoder to physical p54–55 (308 canonical runes, heading included), width 1500, with no shift oracle, no key search, no retuning. Joint MAP score −1130.824, or −3.672 per printed rune, comparable to wrong-key controls (−3.626 to −3.688 per rune) rather than correct-key controls (−2.814 to −3.306). It infers 89 shifts in 307 transitions and consumes 323 first-layer key symbols; the entire output is in the JSON and is fragmentary, noncoherent prose. Do not identify English fragments generated by the language prior as original plaintext. Training uses all three donors for the original, and sample length differs; score comparison is descriptive, not a calibrated rejection threshold.
