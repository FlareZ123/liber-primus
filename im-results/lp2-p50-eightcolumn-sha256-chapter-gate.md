# P50 eight-column 32-byte interpretations: SHA-256 chapter digests and BIP39 word-keys

**Current decision (23 November 2026):** The original 32×8 table has eight apparent 32-byte columns, a natural representation both for SHA-256 digests and for 256-bit BIP39 entropy. Two bounded original-source interpretations now have exact tests: chapter/image SHA-256 identity and BIP39-derived direct Vigenère keys on the printed text immediately following the table. **Both fail their selected plaintext/key receiver.** No creator-authenticated wordlist or table decipherment rule has been found. The genuine p50 256-byte source may serve another purpose; neither shape analogy establishes a cipher key.

## Candidate 1: SHA-256 digests of chapters or source images

> **Prior-work correction (17 November 2026):** This is a **supplementary reproduction and two-serializer extension**, not a newly excluded SHA-256 table hypothesis. [Agent21's earlier original-source gate](lp2-grid-eight-sha256-original-source-gate.md) already tested **10,496** SHA-256-versus-table comparisons, including all original 75 JPEGs and nine physical-chapter unions in UTF-8 runes, romanization and zero-based GP-index bytes. Agent29 separately tested eight red title inputs and first-eight chapter strings ([prior SFT](../sims/lp2_p50_eightsha256_redtitles_original_sft.cjs)). Therefore 576 of the 864 comparisons here directly overlap earlier work: 432 standard chapter-text tests plus 144 chapter-opening JPEG tests. The distinct **288** checks cover whole original chapters serialized as one-based GP29 byte indices or comma-separated GP-prime ASCII; they are also negative. The 10,496-test Agent21 result remains the authoritative and much stronger negative. Nothing in this supplementary test recovers an authentic key, plaintext or source-selected receiver, and no expansion of the hash family is justified.

**Decision (17 November 2026): finite original-input negative.** The 256 printed base60-compatible values on physical LP2 pp49–51 can be arranged as eight vertical 32-byte columns, the length of eight SHA-256 digests. This is a potentially human-recognizable interpretation because the independently solved p56 `AN END` explicitly contains a SHA-512 challenge, and the original p49–51 table prints 32 rows by eight columns. **The exact interpretation tested here recovers no chapter digest, key, or original plaintext.** It remains a shape-motivated hypothesis: no first-party instruction selects SHA-256, the eight-column grouping as digests, or the required serialization.

## Source-defined one-shot experiment

Inputs are `resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json` (256 source-transcribed numeric cells, byte reading described in its metadata) and `resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json` (per-page GP29 indices). Nine visibly marked unknown chapters, as mapped from physical imagery, are p0–2, p3–7, p8–14, p15–22, p23–26, p27–32, p33–39, p40–53, and p54–55. Their source rune lengths are respectively **729, 1145, 1729, 1903, 1021, 1433, 1680, 3008, 308**. The rune-free p50 belongs to the p40–53 segment for this purpose.

For each entire original chapter, compute standard SHA-256 on each of these five transparent, fixed serializers: exact concatenated UTF-8 GP glyphs with no separators; concatenated canonical GP transliterations with no separators; raw zero-based GP29 index bytes; comma-separated decimal GP prime values; raw one-based GP29 index bytes. Compare the resulting 32 digest bytes against all eight **vertical** 32-byte columns as printed and all eight **horizontal** successive 32-byte blocks. This covers possible direct assignment of **any** chapter to **any** column/block without searching keys or substrings.

In a separately frozen original-image arm, hash the exact JPEG bytes of the **nine original chapter openings** 17.jpg, 20.jpg, 25.jpg, 32.jpg, 40.jpg, 44.jpg, 50.jpg, 57.jpg and 71.jpg (physical LP2 pp0/3/8/15/23/27/33/40/54). These are automatically selected by physical red headings and chapter boundaries. Each of the nine original JPEG SHA-256 digests is compared against all eight columns and horizontal 32-byte blocks. The original image hashes agree byte-for-byte with the pinned repository SHA256SUMS and the supplied original JPEG ZIP.

There are **9 chapters × 5 registers × 2 orientations × 8 blocks = 720 exact original-source text comparisons** plus **9 source chapter-opening JPEGs × 2 orientations × 8 blocks = 144 original-image comparisons**, **864** in total. SHA-256 is checked by the `abc` published known-answer vector; the first vertical block is `cb5c796d7350fdfe7aa48ed3de7a492ac90a7de7e9f0eb80006e398e446a9071` and the first horizontal block is `cbe7a7ba61ed7eb75cf99cdef704b7d479ca0f2166893b576dc6ad1996428d85`, pinned independently of the digest matches.

**Result:** zero exact 32-byte matches across all **864** original-source comparisons. The maximum equality was just **2 of 32 bytes**. For the chapter-text arm, the terminal p54–55 transliteration digest against vertical column 5 (zero-based) reaches that maximum. In the independent JPEG arm, three chapter-opening hash/block pairs reach 2/32.  This is chance-compatible and contains no plaintext indication. The table does not directly print SHA-256 digests of entire GP29 chapter content under these serializations or of the unmodified JPEG files at the nine authentic chapter headings, even granting any chapter-to-column/block assignment.

**Reproducer:** [`sims/lp2_p50_eightcolumn_sha256_original_sft.cjs`](../sims/lp2_p50_eightcolumn_sha256_original_sft.cjs). Read-only CommonJS, Node.js 22, Windows compatible; run once from repository root with `node sims/lp2_p50_eightcolumn_sha256_original_sft.cjs`. It prints the complete maximal-match record and asserts no exact match.

## Candidate 2: 2013 BIP39 mnemonic words as a GP29 Vigenère key (23 November 2026)

**Human-puzzle premise, still unsupported:** [Bitcoin BIP39](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039.mediawiki) was assigned **10 September 2013**, before the original 2014 release. Its published algorithm turns exactly **32 entropy bytes** into **24** standardized mnemonic words using the first **eight** bits of SHA-256 as checksum. The p50 table prints exactly **eight vertical columns of 32** base60-compatible byte values, so a solver aware of 2013 BIP39 could read them as eight candidate word-keys. Cicada had used conventional Vigenère subtraction in solved LP1; testing such a word key against the immediate posttable runes is a relatively narrow historical operation. No original LP2 clue names Bitcoin, BIP39, a specific column, or a passphrase, so this is an exploratory clue transfer rather than a source-authenticated key instruction.

The published [BIP39 English wordlist](../resources/external/bip39-english-2013.txt) is retained **verbatim** from the MIT-licensed [official 2013 BIP](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0039/english.txt). The original panel has **ten second-digit I/l visual ambiguities**. Exactly three columns, zero-based **0,3,4**, contain no such glyph and are safe from that particular visual ambiguity; the other five were tested only under the current declared base60 transcription. A separate base59 alphabet interpretation remains outside this experiment.

**Frozen complete original decoder:** Read each table column top-to-bottom as 32 bytes. Compute its 24 dictionary words by the exact BIP39 checksum+11-bit index algorithm. Convert those 24 native English words to GP29 via the repo's solved-Cicada-calibrated orthography. Use the resulting cyclic rune stream as the Vigenère key with **phase zero**, both historically direct `P=C−K mod29` and reverse-sign `P=C+K mod29`. Retain all **534** original uninterrupted GP29 runes on physical p51+p52+p53 (92+263+179), without moving punctuation or fitting word boundaries. No skipped-F clock or key-phase search is permitted. The independent **all-zero BIP39 24-word TREZOR vector and 64-byte PBKDF2 seed** pass exactly. Full encryption/decryption of the authentic **95 solved p57 plaintext** also passes, and the known p57 has Gibran-native log-likelihood advantage **+42.3100** over flat 29-rune frequency.

The other five glyph-ambiguous column candidates likewise fail at the current literal transcription, with log-likelihood advantages between **−514.71** and **−386.03** for the sixteen total fixed output paths. By construction *every* arbitrary 32-byte column generates 24 BIP39 words; the existence of apparently meaningful dictionary words is **zero evidence** that this was intended by Cicada. The first-party packet supplies no selection among columns or an instruction to use the standard BIP39 PBKDF2 seed as another cipher operator.

The script prints all **eight authentic 24-word phrases**, derived seed prefixes, original p51 GP rune openings, both directions, log-likelihoods and exact BIP39 known-answer positive. **No unknown LP2 plaintext, authentic LP2 key, or p50 table operation was recovered.** Retire this direct BIP39-mnemonic-as-Vigenère-key route unless a source establishes a different column/operand/operator.

## Meaning and remaining route

The SHA-256 test rules out only literal chapter-digest lookup in the declared base60 byte register. A SHA-256 hash of a **different author-specified input**, a keyed digest, hidden salt, noncanonical word-break serialization, byte-order transform, or use of the table as a cryptographic operator is outside this finite gate. Exhaustively inventing such variations without an original clue would be arbitrary key/format search. The table might still carry a key, encrypted payload, or error-correcting structure; these require an independent original-source selector and a complete reversible receiver. No change is warranted to the authoritative established-results synthesis, which still has zero authentic unknown-page plaintext.

This investigation does not duplicate the established result that the table's 256-value size is nonunique as an original red-event receiver or the prior tests of common hash/PRNG generators. The SHA-256 part specifically tests **complete original LP2 chapter SHA-256 input→p50 eight-column output identity**; the second part tests exact BIP39 entropy columns used as native-GP direct Vigenère word-keys at the table exit.
