# LP2 large punctuation: 36 marks, three rigid templates and one black exception

**Evidence class:** original-image structure, independently annotated rune/ink source, and exact reproducible census. **No recovered LP2 plaintext, cryptographic key or destination.** This consolidates the former 35-red-motif inventory and the new complete two-ink examination; it supersedes any reading of the older red-only count as the entire large-punctuation count.

## Complete image census

The original LP2 images are archive JPEGs \`17.jpg\`–\`74.jpg\` (physical pages 0–57). On all 58 native 2400×3600 scans, the [red component classifier](../sims/red_marker_shapes.py) identifies **35 red large punctuation glyphs** in exactly three fixed dot occupancies. A complementary black-ink component classifier identifies exactly **one additional 13-dot glyph**.

**Geometry correction (11 November 2026):** the former 15×17 coordinate rounding merged adjacent alternating rows. Correct 4-pixel row/column clustering gives 13-dot 3×9, 10-dot 3×7 and 23-dot 5×9 templates. Event counts and locations are unchanged; the proposed LP1 11-dot to LP2 13-dot two-corner extension is withdrawn.

**New exception:** original archive \`24.jpg\`, physical LP2 **page 7**, contains a black 13-dot shape at image-local top-left approximately **(1692, 2233)**. Its corrected 3×9 row/column occupancy is identical to the red 13-dot shape. It appears at the end of the black-rune passage preceding a red heading near the bottom of the page. The red-only scanner omitted it because its ink is black, not because any dots were missing. The other two red 13-dot shapes on the same page occur on the following text line.

The red classifier uses RGB dominance (R>85, R>1.4G, R>1.4B, G<130), near-square connected components of width and height 7–12 pixels and area 55–120, then proximity grouping within 31 pixels followed by independent row/column centroid clustering at 4 pixels. The black scan uses the maximum RGB channel below 105, 6–12-pixel components of area 40–130, and the same 4-pixel row/column clustering with a cutoff of at least ten dots, excluding ordinary four-square black punctuation. Both reconstruct the original 13-dot grid identically. The grid records dot *occupancy*, not pixel-exact equivalence after JPEG compression.

## Pixel-exact stencil solid-square and alignment gate (13 November 2026, Agent 25)

**All 466 dots across the 36 original large-punctuation stencils are filled axis-aligned 9/10-pixel rectangles at the frozen original-ink segmentation threshold.** The complete 58-image scan independently finds 30 red 13-dot, four red 10-dot, one red 23-dot and the p7 black 13-dot marks. The **453 red plus 13 black** connected dot components each occupy exactly their bounding-box area. Their (width,height,area) triples are exclusively (9,9,81), (9,10,90), (10,9,90), or (10,10,100). In every stencil, dots sharing an original raster column have the same detected width and dots sharing a raster row have the same detected height. A planted one-pixel missing interior breaks this exact rectangle invariant. Across all **31** 13-dot stencils, including the black exception, the aligned positions differ by no more than **2 pixels horizontally** and **1 pixel vertically** for every corresponding dot. The same printed 13-cell pattern survives pixel snapping across original JPEGs.

### Fixed-pigment JPEG positive control: original colour differences are compression-driven

An original-RGB measurement takes each red dot's central pixels after removing a 2-pixel edge from its detected rectangle. Across all **453 red dot interiors**, red-channel means range **189.0000–194.4667** with mean **191.5683** and standard deviation **1.0078**, originally suggesting minor variable pigment or JPEG rounding. The new independent **source-exact forward-rendering experiment** reconstructs **all 35 red stencils** as plain white 256×256 crops containing only uniformly solid **RGB(192,0,0)** rectangles in the original extracted positions/sizes. It preserves each mark's original 16-pixel MCU phase, uses the original JPEG's two actual quantization tables and 4:2:0 subsampling (identical across all 35 source-bearing scans), compresses with Pillow/libjpeg and measures the same two-pixel-inset cores. The model has **no individually tuned dot colours, no cipher keys and no fitted per-mark terms**.

Of all **453** red dots, **437 (96.47%)** have original-vs-simulated mean values **identical within 0.01 in every RGB channel**. The single fixed source colour and compressor reproduce **95.0481%** of measured red-mean variance, **99.1792%** of green and **97.6031%** of blue; colour-channel residual RMS is **[0.22430, 0.04160, 0.12678]** in RGB order. Only **nine** dot means differ by more than **0.25** in *any* channel; original scans 20.jpg, 24.jpg, 40.jpg, 50.jpg and 70.jpg place every such outlier beside or inside the JPEG block footprint of a neighbouring coloured heading glyph. The largest red residual is **+3.9/255** at 50.jpg pixel (1141,1940), immediately beside red lettering. No visually separable per-dot pigment alphabet or independent bright/dim flag survives this forward source model. The deliberately planted one-dot **R+8/255** intensity change is detected by the same fixed-crop operation, providing a sensitivity control. This is substantially stronger than simply observing that the 453 red means have low variance.

The original **466** dots, including 13 black ones, also occupy only filled, aligned rectangles with no missing pixels. Together, these source-exact gates eliminate a high-contrast geometry or directly readable small-amplitude **red central-fill** code as an evidenced source of cipher escape bits. They do **not** prove a steganographic impossibility: changes inside the 16 noisy dot cores could be below resolution, and the present test does not examine JPEG DCT-coefficient channels outside red central-fill, positions of entire marks, altered black ink or other artwork. The repeated 13-dot 31-mark count therefore remains an ink/segmentation clue, **not an authenticated hidden key or sufficient sidecar for the 29→28 no-repeat hypothesis**. No plaintext or creator-selected inverse is obtained.

Reproduction: the original [geometry and 31-mark alignment SFT](../sims/lp2_red_punctuation_solid_dot_geometry_sft.py) retains the 466-dot area proof and one-pixel missing-dot plant. The distinct [source-JPEG forward pigment SFT](../sims/lp2_red_punctuation_uniform_jpeg_sft.py) processes unmodified archive ZIP or JPEG folder, asserts 437/453 exact RGB dot means, reproduces every outlier coordinate, checks identical JPEG quantization and includes the one-dot intensity plant. ## Shared floral-border ornamental circles (pp40–55) may encode 13 and 31

**Original-image observation, 10 November 2026 (Agent 24); corrected source scope 12 November 2026; interpretation explicitly provisional.** The same two mirrored five-circle ornaments occur on **all sixteen** original floral-bordered LP2 physical pages **p40–55** (archive `57.jpg`–`72.jpg`), where they flank the text column. On p55, they also flank the distinctive central tree. The right five circles are fully black. The left five circles are **three black disks plus two intentionally present faint gray ring outlines with white centers**. This difference persists in the original native 2400×3600 JPEGs; it is not produced by thresholding or omitted components. These ten circles are **part of a copied border, not special p55-only marks**. Original decoded RGB source crops at `(355,1460,440,1560)` on the left and `(1950,1460,2050,1560)` on the right each have **one identical byte hash across all sixteen images**; the full 500-pixel side strips are likewise identical ([complete source audit](lp2-floral-border-template-audit.md)). Any claim that the 13/31 reading selects p55 specifically requires a separate page-selecting clue.

Reading the five circle positions of each ornament in ordinary printed row order **top, middle-left, middle-right, bottom-left, bottom-right**, with black = 1 and white-centered ring = 0, gives:

| Ornament, common p40–55 image coordinates | Observed five-bit pattern | Binary integer |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Left (near x=367–424, y=1477–1540) | **01101** | **13** |
| Right (near x=1975–2032, y=1477–1541) | **11111** | **31** |

Under the same ordinary printed order, the filled positions are **2, 3, 5**, exactly the prime numbers among positions 1–5, while the two pale circumference-only positions are **1 and 4**. The resulting binary values **13** and **31** are themselves prime and are decimal digit reversals. Under the archived 2013 Cicada `NUMBER` rule, either input would return **`+`**, because both the input and its reversal are prime. This is a historically demonstrated operation that gives the left/right pair a second source-native interpretation beyond raw binary counting. The same pair now supplies two elementary arithmetic checks: **31−13=18**, independently reproducing the fold-mismatch fragment number, and the historical `+` response suggests the conditional read **13+31=44**. The **44th prime is 193**. Under the conventional base60 table, 193 occurs exactly at global cells **83 and 127**, separated by **44** cells and enclosing **43** cells. Exhausting every prime-valued table entry shows this is the **only** base60 pair whose occurrence distance equals its own one-based prime ordinal. Under the base59/f-skip fork, the two `3D` cells become 190, 193 moves to cells 61 and 234, and there are **zero** self-indexed prime-distance pairs. Thus the ornament arithmetic gives a conditional, source-derived radix60 discriminator. The weak link is semantic: the 2013 server used `+` as a classification response and did not explicitly command solvers to add the two later ornament values. The left ornament is therefore the canonical five-position prime-indicator mask **01101** without converting it to a decimal number. Folding against the all-filled right ornament makes the mismatch mask **10010**, the complementary non-prime mask, whose fixed binary value is **18**. Among the four row-order orientations already considered for the fold, only top-to-bottom with left-to-right order inside paired rows produces the prime-indicator mask. Reading the two hollow position labels in that same ordinary order gives **1,4 → 14**, returning to physical **p14**, where the signed-source word-map candidate selected the unique ciphertext word **FOLD**. This supplies a source-native orientation/self-loop check; concatenating 1 and 4 as a page number remains an interpretive step.

The pair **(13,31)** exactly matches *two previously independently recorded source facts*: the dominant large punctuation stencil contains **13 dots**, and the manuscript has **31 instances of that stencil across all 58 physical LP2 pages**, including one in black ink on p7. Those 31 marks divide the 13,136-rune corpus into 32 fragments, aligning numerically with the 32-row printed base-60 grid. This suggests a possible human-readable cue for attending to the 13-dot punctuation and counting every occurrence, rather than discovering that segmentation entirely through ciphertext fitting.

The [read-only original-image extractor](../sims/p55_binary_ornaments.py) verifies source SHA-256 `a80191592add93a86ed3868f9e4e6ba3944ec20c9ffccc7d3e221b461344beba`, tests **all ten fixed original-pixel centers** and their 9×9 center grayscale means, checks the two pale marks have actual gray annular outlines, computes both five-bit values, and independently counts 31 annotations. Black centers have mean grayscale **0.07–0.60** on the standard 0–255 scale, while the two pale centers have means **254.90** and **254.77** and more than 100 visible gray annular pixels each. The coordinates and thresholds are explicit; no guessed rune, image restoration or ciphertext score enters extraction.

### Fold-selected original pixel comparison: shared mirrored vines localize the two pale rings

**New source-constrained image finding (Agent 17, 11 November).** The independently described, 2016-signed *word-map candidate* selects physical p14 word1, the literal GP string **FOLD**. Recursively applying the same unchanged source-prime/page-word rule to FOLD selects **p55 word2, SB**, on the final unsolved LP2 page. This conditional word-address is the *only proposed p55 selector* in this chain: the folded ornamental vines and their pale-ring residuals are identical on p40–54. A human could fold the p55 page, but the ornament alone gives no reason to prefer it over the other fifteen floral pages. The numerical book-address operation is provisional and is **not** proof of the artist's intent.

An [original-image, no-key SFT](../sims/p55_fold_mirror_original_image_sft.py) checks the *byte-pinned source image* SHA256 `a80191592add93a86ed3868f9e4e6ba3944ec20c9ffccc7d3e221b461344beba`, measuring thresholded black pixels in **only the left ornamental vine** (downsampled source 1200×1800, threshold below 100, left x20–294/y330–1249). Mirror the source across the **exact printed page midpoint x600** and compare left art to reflected right art. The binary intersection-over-union is **0.9780403**; shifting that axis by only two image pixels gives at most **0.2351** and other tested shifts give still less. Thus these vines are not merely stylistically symmetric: **97.8% of the matched dark-pixel union is an exact reflected match** at the source page center, within this fixed crop.

The exact-center XOR contains **513 differing pixels** in **193** eight-connected groups. Its two largest connected regions (areas **103**, **99** at downsampled left coordinates approximately **(197,737)** and **(189,769)**) are the two independently noted **light-centered circles with gray rings**, located near original x394/y1474 and x378/y1538. Every remaining group contains at most nine thresholded pixels, compatible with JPEG-edge and raster antialiasing differences. This identifies a *localized, distinctive side-channel under a literal FOLD operation* rather than only a numerical coincidence.

The result passed [opt-in Python-image CI 42961016044](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42961016044). It links a pre-existing **FOLD→p55** word-address lead to a physically well-defined mirror operation and two prominent residual marks; the actual code/meaning of those marks remains unknown. As a separate exploratory numerical readout, the raw original circle bits `01101` and `11111` represent 13 and31 under an assumed top-down orientation, but no verified instruction uses their reflection or difference as a rune key. **No unknown ciphertext plaintext, mechanism, or recovered destination results.**
**Evidence boundary:** all sixteen original floral-bordered source images have the same 3+2 versus 5 asymmetry. The black/white-to-binary mapping and row-order reading were identified after noticing the 13/31 match, so their apparent numerical exactness has no calibrated discovery p-value. The circles could be decorative, and this observation does **not** authenticate Cicada's intended cipher, reveal any key or plaintext, or prove the 32-fragment/32-row numerical alignment is functional. The historically authentic black 13-dot exception remains vital if the hypothesis is pursued: counting only red stencils would give 30, breaking the proposed 31 target. A useful successor would extract a *second independent* image indication of the same bit convention, or a decoder that makes a previously unfitted original LP2 plaintext prediction under source-fixed states.

## Cryptanalytic interpretation and limit

The glyph designs act as **fixed punctuation classes**, including the black 13-dot exception. Their fixed row-count occupancy contradicts the idea that each 13-dot glyph carries an independently varying 13-bit bitmap payload. The corrected 13-dot stencil has nine alternating single/double-dot rows; the 10-dot stencil has seven. Reading the solved p56 10-dot mark with one-dot row=1/two-dot row=0 yields 1010101₂=85, matching its 85-rune page and terminal 13-dot offset, but no original instruction equates a punctuation glyph to page length. That same convention reads every identical 13-dot glyph as 101010101₂=341≡22 (mod29); deriving 170≡25 requires the additional, unselected opposite polarity. The two values equal the candidate p32 M7 fixed points, yet the repeated p53/p56 glyphs do not independently validate that mapping. This is a provisional numerical cross-link, not a source-calibrated selector, cipher inverse, or proof of M7's intended role. It does not fix an ordered eigenbasis, paired state, or schedule; #273's negative direct M7 inverse remains in force. See the cross-release report and binary-control SFT. Their *position, class, and colour* may still carry a bounded side channel. The exceptional black mark occurs at a strong visual transition, but no repeatable rule yet maps its colour or any heading to a cipher key.

The image finding leaves the general 55 encrypted rune-bearing pages unsolved. It is useful primarily because it prevents dropping a genuine document boundary when translating original images into a cipher segmentation.
