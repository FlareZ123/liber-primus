### Source-image crosslink: five-dot masks beside the 256-byte table

**Corrected source scope:** all sixteen border-decorated source images (`57.jpg`–`72.jpg`, physical LP2 pp40–55) reuse identical left/right **tree-ornament dot masks**, including the three numerical-table pages (`66.jpg`–`68.jpg`, pp49–51). The ornament is a section-wide border, not a table-specific motif. At original 2400×3600 resolution, each five-point ornament forms a pentagon. The five **left** positions contain three solid black dots and **two pale, hollow circles**; the five **right** positions are all solid black. Counting dark-center median values in an 11×11 pixel box produces exactly `[255,0,0,255,0]` grayscale levels on the left, versus `[0,0,0,0,0]` on the horizontally mirrored right, on **each of the sixteen original pages**. The complete leftmost/rightmost 520-pixel black masks, thresholded at grayscale <150, are identical across all sixteen JPEGs on both sides. These are sixteen uses of **one graphic template**, rather than independent drawings. A **new side-to-side mirror comparison** of that template distinguishes the two outline-only dots from the rest of the line art: thresholding the 520-pixel left border and the horizontally reflected right border at grayscale 150 produces **2,293** XOR ink pixels. Eight-connected components of the difference mask have largest areas **460 pixels** centred at **(396,1476)** and **432 pixels** centred at **(380,1540)**, exactly the pale top and lower-left dot centres; the next largest component is **32 pixels**. Across grayscale thresholds **60–240**, these are consistently the two largest components. This is physical evidence that two particular filled-dot counterparts were selectively rendered as hollow circles rather than an artefact of normal reflection. It strengthens the visual-mask observation while providing no independent evidence of an intended *numeric* reading or cipher key. [The source script](../sims/tree_dot_binary_source.py) now asserts the two large connected-component differences in each of the sixteen copies.

Reading the five dot positions from the unique highest point clockwise (top, upper-right, lower-right, lower-left, upper-left), and treating solid as `1` and hollow as `0`, gives source-visible masks **`01101₂ = 13` (left)** and **`11111₂ = 31` (right)**. These are the same integers as **13 dots per punctuation stencil** and **31 occurrences** in the independently annotated LP2 source. This remains a potential *source-to-source clue* for the 31-mark/32-row alignment discussed below, but the same ornament occurs on **thirteen pages without the numerical table**. Its presence next to the grid supplies no table-specific evidence; the 13/31 reading was selected retrospectively.

**Source-constrained traversal gate.** The two numbers in those dot masks also suggest candidate affine traversals of a 256-cell table. Both `i → (13i + 31) mod 256` and the recurrence `x(next) = (13 × x(current) + 31) mod 256`, started at zero, visit all 256 cells exactly once: 13 is odd, while the recurrence has full period because 31 is odd and 13−1 is divisible by four. Neither operation is literally printed as an instruction. The [original-event alignment test](../sims/dot13_31_grid_event_alignment.cjs) fixes these two traversals alongside natural and 32×8-transposed order, uses both `byte mod 29` and `floor(29 × byte / 256)`, and permits a completely unrestricted single secret 29-symbol output permutation that maximizes agreement between the 225 original red rune events and the table. It tests both the canonical table and the counterfactual five-cell variant reading, 16 variants total. The strongest, counterfactual-grid LCG/scaled interpretation, matches **50/225** red rune events; among **400 paired, inventory-preserving random table-position shuffles** with full family reselection, **84** produce a maximum at least as large (add-one tail **85/401 ≈ 0.212**). The canonical original grid peaks at 49/225 under the same LCG/scaled reading. Fully planted hidden 29-symbol bijections reach 225/225. This gives no positive evidence for a direct dot-selected event/grid relabeling, while leaving indirect key schedules, different framing and nonlinear interpretations open. No authentic LP2 plaintext is recovered.

**Evidence boundary:** the hollow/filled artwork and the stated clockwise decode are exact and reproducible in [the original-JPEG single-file check](../sims/tree_dot_binary_source.py). Its interpretation as an intentional `13/31` pointer is **retrospective and unconfirmed**. ### Five-dot left-shift register, 32-row de Bruijn traversal and black-marker phase anchor

**Exact combinatorial consequence of a visual hypothesis; no historical cipher selector or new plaintext recovered.** The original numerical-table JPEGs use a repeated five-dot tree ornament. The left filled-mask is `01101₂=13`, the right filled-mask `11111₂=31`. Taking the **two hollow positions** in the left drawing as active feedback taps gives `10010₂=18`; the all-solid right tree supplies seed 31. The explicit five-bit Fibonacci recurrence

`state = ((state << 1) & 31) | parity(state & 18)`

starts at 31 and visits **all 31 nonzero five-bit states exactly once** before returning to 31:

`31,30,28,25,19,6,13,26,20,9,18,4,8,16,1,2,5,10,21,11,23,14,29,27,22,12,24,17,3,7,15`.

Insert the unvisited state zero **between 16 and 1**, the only site that preserves four-bit overlaps for all cyclic successor pairs. The resulting 32-state binary de Bruijn row order is

`31,30,28,25,19,6,13,26,20,9,18,4,8,16,0,1,2,5,10,21,11,23,14,29,27,22,12,24,17,3,7,15`.

Every number 0–31 occurs exactly once, and every successive row ID, including final to first, shares four binary bits by a left-shift relation. This is a well-defined source-compatible traversal of the **32 rows** alongside the independently marked **31 thirteen-dot separators** in the text. The rule is a deliberately chosen interpretation, not a textual instruction in the artifact.

**Independent visual phase anchor:** Of all 31 original 13-dot marks, precisely **one is black** rather than red, the **eighth** (index 7), at physical LP2 p7 rune offset 194. A red mark follows at the exact same rune coordinate, making the intervening **fragment 8 empty**. A further source-constrained hypothesis identifies this visually distinctive empty fragment with the unique zero state. Rotating the above de Bruijn cycle until state 0 occupies row/fragment index 8 yields the fixed 32-row order

`13,26,20,9,18,4,8,16,0,1,2,5,10,21,11,23,14,29,27,22,12,24,17,3,7,15,31,30,28,25,19,6`.

This is a stronger independently constrained candidate than choosing row-zero phase to improve a guessed plaintext. The source does not actually specify that the black mark must represent zero or that table rows and rune fragments share this ordering. The two simultaneous marks have uncertain semantics. The new source clue **predicts a specific rotation** but does not yet authenticate the cipher.

## Thirteen-dot grid-row segmentation: exact all-key GP-E ceiling

### Independent DES-row key challenge: a finite source-derived candidate

The pairing above suggests one specific human-solvable mechanism worth actually inverting: eight *bytes* per numeric-grid row are naturally one **64-bit DES key**, and the 31 distinctive punctuation marks supply exactly 32 successive fragment boundaries. The manuscript itself does **not** name DES or a block mode, so this is a motivated and explicitly speculative cipher proposal. It is separate from the arbitrary static row-permutation capacity theorem below.

The [reversible original-input attack](../sims/fragment_grid_des_inverse.cjs) constructs the 32 true source fragments from the complete 13-dot stencil list, including the sole black mark and the two empty spans. The first **28** fragments contain **12,956 unsolved source runes**; 32 keys are the literal rows of [the original base-60 grid](../resources/liber-primus/base60-grid.json). For each corresponding 8-byte key, derive a page-position-independent stream using DES-CTR or DES-OFB, with either a zero or original 0–31 fragment index as the 64-bit initial counter/IV.
