# LP2 colour-event channel: 256 original ink events and the 32×8 grid

**Evidence class:** exact source count, testable retrospective alignment and an original-input negative for explicitly declared reversible ciphers. **No unknown LP2 plaintext, authentic key or continuation destination recovered.** Agent 3, 10 November 2026.

## Source-defined candidate

The [original ink annotation](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-ink-annotations.json) contains **225 red runes** and **31 occurrences of the same 13-dot punctuation stencil** across the complete 58 physical LP2 pages, including the only black 13-dot stencil on physical page 7. These form **256 ordered events**, numerically equal to the **256 bytes** of the manuscript's [32-row × 8-column base-60 table](../resources/liber-primus/base60-grid.json) on pages 49–51. Their common size admits a complete one-to-one positional pairing or an 8×32 key schedule. An independent [Agent 30 countercheck](../communications/agent3/20261110T1615Z_agent30_red256-event-block-boundary.md) counts the 32 red-rune intervals delimited by the 31 stencils: **15 are empty, and only one contains seven runes**. Thus the marks do **not** naturally delimit 32 contiguous groups of seven red runes plus one marker; the 8×32 correspondence remains numerical and requires a separate source-justified ordering rule. A separate natural reading takes each *interval length* as one byte or base-60 digit: exactly **32 nonnegative lengths**, summing to 225 and with maximum **59**, within the original grid's 60-digit alphabet. The 32-character base-60 string is `D0G030B00EC09B0G0J0A08030x905070`, source-key bytes `0d00100003000b00000e0c00090b00100013000a00080003003b090005000700`. This offers a source-fixed **32-byte key candidate** with no inferred cipher mapping yet.

The 13-dot stencil is one visually consistent symbol class; the source's four 10-dot and one 23-dot punctuation marks are separate classes. Including only the *red* 13-dot glyphs would yield 255 events, so the source-verified black exception matters.

This count is **retrospective**, and the rule 'red runes plus every 13-dot glyph' was constructed after seeing both source inventories. It establishes a possible *format*, with no evidence yet that either original colour or punctuation was intended as key material. The red-rune count includes solved pages 56–57; excluding those pages changes the total. The 31 stencils are mostly punctuation boundaries around isolated red spans, so the number could arise from manuscript layout.

The [runnable experiment](../sims/red_256_event_key_inverse.cjs) resolves ordering unambiguously. Events sort by physical page, rune offset, and punctuation before a rune at the same offset. Every red rune contributes its canonical GP index 0–28 and every 13-dot stencil a separate marker value 29. It checks the exact rune against the original canonical [corpus](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json), checks the black mark, and hashes the 256 input bytes to SHA-256 `50f640c1d53b3df48a1bb6cacfb0892db26d32f54bfbef9e7c65c312ba422cae`. This digest is a reproducibility fingerprint **of the extracted event list**, not a claimed digest in the manuscript.

## Original-source inversions

Two literal register families were investigated with 256-event source-derived keys:

- **Additive GP29:** `P_i = (C_i + s K_i) mod 29`, reversible for every original rune.
- **Transparent GP-EA:** on each physical page's first rune, pass the symbol through. Otherwise if the published rune repeats its predecessor, decode GP EA=28. For a nonzero GP difference, `P_i = (log_15((C_i−C_(i−1)) mod 29) + s K_i) mod 28`. Generator 15 is the manuscript-linked primitive root of F29, and the forward mapping is exact.

The 256 events were serialized chronologically or read as a transposed 8×32 table. Four explicit mappings were compared: GP index with stencil=29, GP prime value mod29 with stencil=13, stencil-only bit, and GP-prime parity with stencil=1. The two additional 32-symbol keys are the stencil-delimited red-run lengths, forward and reversed. Each key was tested with both signs, one continuous book clock or physical-page resets, and both reversible cipher registers: **64 full-event + 16 interval-key = 80** declared cases, with **zero fitted phases**.

The [successful original-source manual GitHub Actions run](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42854781057) tested the entire **12,956** unknown-page ciphertext. Every original candidate decoder re-encrypted all **12,956/12,956** original glyphs exactly; every one also recovered a separate original-length simulated manuscript built from known solved-Cicada source GP exactly. Total checks: **1,036,480/1,036,480** original replay symbols and **1,036,480/1,036,480** plant recovered symbols. This proves the 80 implementations are lossless. It does not select a genuine secret key, because every candidate in these bijective families necessarily re-encrypts its own decoded output.

The strongest retrospectively selected original plaintext rune index of coincidence was only **0.03534579348** (32-run-length key, forward order, EA transparent, negative sign, physical-page clock), near the uniform 29-symbol baseline. The original ciphertext IoC is **0.03447839786**; the native solved-Cicada control is **0.06135068204**. The winning candidate produced **508** GP E runes out of 12,956, around **3.9%**, against around **12%** in the solved-source reference. This is incompatible with straightforward native-GP readable prose under those source-fixed keys and registers; its output was never claimed as genuine plaintext.

At the unshifted chronological index pairing, the printed 256-byte table matches the 256 extracted event values in **1** exact byte position, or **7** matches against its low five bits. No direct literal table-to-event copy is evident. Those comparisons are only fixed readouts, not a test of arbitrary affine, encrypted or source-derived maps.

## Independent 256-event digest and table-commitment challenge

A separate [source-only hash comparison](../sims/red_256_event_grid_hash.cjs) freezes 256-event serialization as GP-index bytes, GP-prime bytes, event-type bytes, prime-parity bytes, or runes/13-dot-symbol UTF-8. It partitions byte-length serializations into **eight 32-event groups**, applies SHA-256, SHA3-256 and BLAKE2s-256, and compares each of the 8 candidate digests with every original 32-byte column and every original consecutive 32-byte row-major chunk, in both byte directions. Both chronological and transposed 8×32 event clocks and event reversal are included. This performs **12,288 exact 32-byte comparisons** with **zero matches**. A planted grid made from the SHA-256 digests of the eight source groups recovers all **8/8** fixed-aligned outputs, checking the matching code.

The same fixed source candidates, plus the raw canonical 256-byte printed table, were checked as complete inputs to SHA-512, SHA3-512, BLAKE2b-512 and SHAKE256 (64 output bytes) against the authentic **64-byte `AN END` target digest**. **Zero of 40** computed source/digest candidates matched. [Executed manual CI 42851768375](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42851768375). No historical hash algorithm, preimage, key or digest-to-grid connection has been established; this is a finite negative for the named literal serializations, algorithms and clocks only.

## Direct AES-256 inversion of the original 256-byte table

The event-count hypothesis also proposes a distinct data direction: the printed grid might be an **encrypted 256-byte message**, rather than a 256-byte lookup table or eight external keys. An exact [AES-256 source inversion](../sims/red_256_event_grid_aes_inverse.cjs) derives **eleven** 256-bit keys from source-defined event-bit encodings, source rune hashes, direct forward/reverse 32-interval run lengths, and hashes of the run lengths, with no plaintext-dependent key fitting. For each, it tests the complete original byte table in row-major and column-major order, including reversals, under AES-256 ECB, CBC with zero IV, CBC with prefixed 16-byte IV, CTR with zero IV and CTR with prefixed IV: **220 decryptions**.

The best resulting byte stream has only **123/256 = 48.046875% ASCII-printable bytes** (reversed 32-run-length key, reversed row-major ciphertext, CTR, zero IV), and **0/220** candidates reach even 75% printable bytes. The evaluator's known-source AES-256-CTR control encrypts and exactly recovers **256/256** readable plaintext bytes. The [manual original-data CI run 42854781057](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42854781057) passed. These source keys and fixed AES modes **do not reveal a textual instruction**. The test is deliberately restricted to direct ASCII-like plaintext; a compressed/binary intermediate, other cryptographic primitive or a differently specified IV remains untested.

## A 16×16 matrix with a 15×15 red core and 31-glyph rim

The integer decomposition has a second concrete interpretation: **225 = 15²**, and **31 = 16² − 15²**. The 225 red runes can fill a 15×15 GP field matrix; exactly 31 13-dot stencils then fill its 16th column and 16th row, making a **full 16×16 Hill matrix over F29**. This fits both source inventories without discarding a glyph. The older 15×15 red-only Hill family was already tested in [the preceding report](red-225-hill-feedback-inverse.md), but that study did not address this 31-cell border or 16-state block architecture.

The [exact new original-source inversion](../sims/red_256_rim_hill_inverse.cjs) tests three explicit 16×16 layouts: red 15×15 core with every rim element equal to its 13-dot shape count, red core with rim values from the page-local preceding rune beside each stencil, and all 256 source events directly interleaved in manuscript chronological order. It tests GP-index and GP-prime-mod29 coordinates, row- and column-major matrices. **All 12 derived matrices are nonsingular** over F29, establishing fully reversible 16-rune blocks. For each it tests original LP2 under four source-fixed cipher families (ECB, CBC, CFB, PCBC), book/page resets, and all 16 positional phases: **1,536 complete original-input tests**.

The strongest first-half original candidate was **GP-prime predecessor-rim / row-major / CBC / page reset / phase3**, training IoC **0.03463274318**, untouched second-half IoC **0.03449518575** over 12,352 processed runes. Even the best *retrospectively selected* heldout configuration only achieved **0.03467954551**. Native GP Shakespeare control IoC is **0.05996568934**; original ciphertext IoC is **0.03447839786**. All **96** matrix×mode×clock positive cases recovered **1,243,776/1,243,776** GP-English source glyphs exactly, and the selected original candidate reciphered **12,956/12,956** original positions (with phase/trailing unprocessed glyphs preserved). [Passed GitHub Actions run 42853690094](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42853690094) in the [existing manual Hill workflow](../.github/workflows/red-225-hill-feedback-manual.yml).

Thus the 16×16 extension is an **authentic structural possibility with an explicit invertible implementation**. Its declared original-cipher modes and matrix serializations do not reveal ordinary GP plaintext. The result does not establish whether the 256-event correspondence is intentional or whether the manuscript encodes a nonlinear or key-scheduled matrix mechanism.

## Direct original LP2 GP inverse under AES-256-CTR from the 32 interval lengths

The source-defined 32-byte run-length key has an additional natural reading: use its bytes as a direct **AES-256 key**, generate a zero-IV CTR stream, and reduce the stream's bytes to GP29 or the nonzero-step GP28 exponent register. The separate [exact original-and-planted AES inverse](../sims/red_32run_aes_gp_inverse.cjs) checks this finite family on the original 12,956 LP2 runes. It tests the direct or reversed 32-byte source key, raw byte residues or scaled byte-to-field mapping, both signs, whole-book or physical-page-reset clock, and both reversible output registers: **32 fully specified original decryptions** with no fitted IV, phase, secret alphabet or plaintext source.

All **414,592/414,592** planted original-length solved-Cicada GP runes are recovered under the intended keys; all **414,592/414,592** original-cipher positions can re-encrypt their respective derived values. The best selected original IoC is **0.03530106173** (forward key, scaled GP28, negative sign, per-page reset), versus source-native **0.06135068204** and original ciphertext **0.03447839786**. The corresponding original output contains only **472 GP E** instances among 12,956 runes. [Successful manual CI 42855440890](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42855440890). This rules out **these specific source-key AES-CTR plus additive/difference-register constructions** as ordinary GP-English plaintext recoveries; other IVs, encryption registers, and intermediate encodings are unrestricted.

## What survives and next discriminatory test

The **256-event correspondence itself remains unresolved**, including after the direct digest, AES-256 grid and 16×16 Hill original-cipher tests. The literal key serializations and two direct GP output mappings fail to generate native-style original plaintext, but this does not exclude a source-derived key used in a nonlinear cipher, selected red-run spatial geometry, or the original numeric table encoding a distinct auxiliary instruction. The coincidence has no source-independent probability or evidence of Cicada authorship. The only useful successor would derive a deterministic byte/state construction from **the exact 256 events** and the **original 32×8 table** before inspecting ciphertext, then predict an independently meaningful original plaintext interval and account for all original boundaries and repeated-runic effects. Repeating the 80 direct combinations or elevating the exact count to a decipherment is unwarranted.
