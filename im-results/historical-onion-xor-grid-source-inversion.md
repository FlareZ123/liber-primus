# Historical 256-byte hidden-service and LP2 grid key hypotheses

**Classification: bounded original-input negative for joint historical 256-byte source key interpretations. Agent 9's XOR/AES held-out study and Agent 45's exact polynomial-sharing exclusion, 10 November 2026. No original Liber Primus plaintext, authentic key, cipher mechanism, or destination was recovered.**

## Source material and independently frozen hypothesis

The public [historical byte-string compilation](https://github.com/DyrneCipher/Obsidian-Vault/blob/main/_archive/liber-primus/byte-strings) presents **four 512-hex-character strings**, each 256 bytes. It labels the first three as content of historical Cicada hidden services `cu343l33nqaekrnw.onion`, `fv7lyucmeozzd5j4.onion`, and `avowyfgl5lkzfj3n.onion`. Its fourth 256-byte value matches the repository's **canonical, uncorrected 32×8 LP2 base-60 grid in all 256 positions**. This exact-length coincidence motivates a narrowly specified key-source construction. The first three strings are preserved in [the supporting derived resource](../resources/cicada-hiddenservice-256-candidates.json). Their historical attribution and cryptographic relation to LP2 remain unverified; the 256-byte excerpts might simply be archival snapshots. Equal size alone is no evidence for genuine secret sharing.

The independent pnotisdev image-audited LP2-grid transcription gives **five corrections** to that canonical fourth source: zero-based row-major byte indices **45:107→81, 50:47→21, 165:18→47, 172:148→174, 246:198→224**. Both old and corrected grid sources are run; the original canonical bytes are preserved.

A finite family is assembled from exactly these four 256-byte inputs:

1. Form each of the **15 nonempty bitwise-XOR subsets** of the four source strings, byte-aligned and byte-for-byte.
2. For each 256-byte result, derive **eight vertical 32-byte keys** from its 32×8 row-major layout and **eight consecutive 32-byte keys**. There are **240 distinct source-derived keys**.
3. Treat each key as an AES-256-CTR or ChaCha20 all-zero-IV keystream, optionally reverse the 32 key bytes, map each output byte to GP index by `byte % 29` or `floor(29*byte/256)`, and test both signs of additive mod-29 encryption: **16 shared mode settings**.
4. Preserve eight manuscript rune sections, including the short bridge merged with its following section; fit the first **300 original runes of each section**. Choose the best candidate key from the 240 possibilities independently per section under each globally shared mode, maximizing recovered first-order GP index of coincidence. Freeze the single best mode and all eight selected keys.
5. Evaluate the **next 300 runes in each section** without refitting, and an **additional untouched 300-rune continuation in seven sections** with at least 900 runes; the shortest 729-rune section is excluded from the third block. Stream counters remain continuous through both boundaries.

There are 2,400 fitting runes, 2,400 second-block runes and 2,100 third-block runes. This model does not test nonlinear KDFs, nonaligned XOR, Shamir interpolation, unknown alphabet permutations, per-section key drift, other reset policies or an actual asymmetric secret-share recovery; none can be dismissed based on this negative.

## Planted GP-English detection control

An independently sourced Shakespeare text was GP-encoded and encrypted under eight **different, known 256-byte-source XOR key selections**, with AES-256-CTR, zero IV, forward key bytes and mod-29 additive encryption. A seeded 81% probability of replacing a would-be adjacent ciphertext doublet perturbed **231 symbols** in the 900-rune-per-section planted example. Running the *same* finite-family training algorithm without telling it the planted keys recovered **all 8/8 key identities** and the exact AES-CTR model, byte reducer, reversal and algebraic sign. The planted recovered symbol-distribution signal remained robust in both unseen intervals: **0.057753623** second-block and **0.060350374** third-block IoC.

This is a true discrimination control for the proposed no-key-pointer-drift source family. Its 81% direct overwrite channel is a perturbation; it does not preserve the overwritten rune information or implement an unknown-clock cipher. The control therefore does not prove the original LP2 mechanism or guarantee sensitivity to every plausible original encoder.

## Independently excluded 3-of-4 historical secret sharing

The same four public **256-byte sources** also support a more constrained key-construction hypothesis than arbitrary XOR or AES hashing: treat the hidden-service identifiers **2, 3, and 4** as share coordinates x=2, x=3, x=4 in an ordinary Shamir polynomial over GF(256), and interpret the LP2 256-byte table as a fourth share at **any** distinct, globally fixed field coordinate x. The grid's natural x=1 is included rather than singled out. A threshold of **3-of-4** implies that all 256 aligned byte positions independently lie on polynomials of degree at most two. The actual polynomial coefficients can vary arbitrarily across byte positions, so this is a necessary condition that requires **no guessed secret or ciphertext plaintext**. Two-of-four linear sharing is also excluded when degree-two consistency fails.

The [exact cross-source gate](../sims/onion256_shamir_cross_source_gate.cjs) tests the **complete 253 candidate grid coordinates** in GF(256) under each of the AES polynomial 0x11B and Reed–Solomon polynomial 0x11D, plus all **254 allowed GF(257) coordinates**. It uses both the published table and the independent five-cell-corrected version, each in four predeclared byte layouts (row-major, reversed row-major, column-major, reversed column-major): **6,080 original-source hypotheses**, each demanding 256 exact share-byte equalities.

**Executed original-source result: zero complete degree-two fits.** The maximum over all 6,080 tested coordinate/layout/field choices is only **6 correct byte positions of 256**; it arises in four equivalent GF256/0x11B reversed-row or column-major presentations with and without five-cell corrections. This excludes the entire stated alignment-consistent threshold-at-most-three polynomial sharing family independently of any candidate recovered LP2 plaintext.

**Native full-length positive:** six blind constructed 256-byte degree-two secret-share systems, with each of three fields and grid coordinate x=1 or 157, reconstruct **256/256 byte values** and uniquely identify the correct grid coordinate while the hidden polynomial coefficients vary independently per byte. This verifies that the original-source failures are detectable rather than a broken finite-field implementation. The test passed within the existing manual grid test suite in [successful GitHub Actions run 42820022422](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42820022422).

**Scope:** the onion-stage numbers are a natural but unproven choice of Shamir share coordinates. This does not exclude different source-byte offsets, unknown per-source encodings, arbitrary unrelated share coordinates, per-byte coordinates, nonlinear sharing or 4-of-4 **degree-three** interpolation. A degree-three polynomial can always pass through any four byte values, so apparent 4-of-4 reconstruction alone provides no authentication of a secret. The earlier 15-subset XOR and direct AES source-key negative above remains independently scoped. The secret-sharing exclusion introduces no recovered LP2 key or plaintext.

## Direct 4-of-4 historical interpolation as an original LP2 key

Failure of degree-two four-share consistency does not eliminate a **four-of-four threshold**. The exact remaining construction takes grid table coordinate x=1, service strings coordinates x=2,3,4, and **interpolates a separate cubic polynomial per byte**, reading each polynomial at x=0 as the candidate **256-byte secret**. Such a cubic necessarily exists for *any* four byte strings, so interpolation itself has **zero evidentiary power**; it must decrypt the actual manuscript to become meaningful.

The [original LP2 inversion script](../sims/onion256_shamir4_original_inverse.cjs) evaluates precisely two GF256 field conventions (irreducible polynomials 0x11B and 0x11D), canonical or five-cell-corrected 32×8 grid values, and four natural row/column/reversed orientations. These give **eight 256-byte source-derived secrets per grid version**, each split into eight vertical 32-byte keys or eight contiguous 32-byte blocks. For the corrected grid there are **128 candidate AES/ChaCha 32-byte keys**. All 16 shared cipher settings are tested (AES-256-CTR or ChaCha20, forward or reversed key, mod29 or scaled29 output mapping, positive or negative mod29 direction). Each of eight manuscript sections chooses its best key using **300 original fitting runes**; the global best model is frozen and checked on an **independent second 300-rune window in all eight sections**, then an untouched third 300-rune window in seven sections. A genuine single source-to-key construction would ordinarily select one consistent interpolated secret, whereas this search is more permissive by granting each section its own best key even across source variants.

**Source-original outcome:** The training-selected corrected-grid model scored **0.03774526** rune IoC in the 2,400 fitting runes, falling to **0.03457079** in the 2,400 untouched second-block runes and **0.03438127** in the 2,100 third-block runes (near uniform 1/29 = 0.03448276). Its eight selected keys derive from **different and mutually incompatible field/grid readings**. The original canonical-grid third block scored **0.03407867**, no improvement.

A **25-replicate fully refitted null** independently shuffles all four 256-byte inputs within themselves, reconstructs each four-of-four secret and reruns the entire key/mode/section search. Its mean third-block score was **0.03438140**, essentially identical to the authentic-source **0.03438127**; **11/25** source shuffles equaled or exceeded the real continuation, yielding an add-one upper tail of **12/26 = 0.46154**. No selected direct GP-English plaintext is present.

**Positive key-identification control:** Independently GP-tokenized Shakespeare text was encrypted into eight 900-rune sections under eight authentic keys from a *single source-secret variant*, using a known AES-256-CTR mode hidden from the blind search. The same 128-key/16-mode fitting routine recovered **8/8 correct keys**, the true model parameters and all **7,200/7,200** planted GP symbols via direct inverse. Its 300-rune heldout and third-block IoCs remained **0.05949** and **0.06142** respectively, much higher than the original source.

The original and plant tests passed [GitHub Actions run 42820730548](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42820730548). **No 4-of-4 source-derived key or unknown LP2 plaintext was recovered.** The original-source failure rejects this fixed byte-derived AES/ChaCha keystream family at the tested section starts, without disproving every possible way a cubic interpolant might be postprocessed, a different coordinate assignment, a different unobserved secret, or a nonlinear hidden-skip encoder. The absence of a field-polynomial source relationship and the failed third-block inversion together give no positive reason to prioritize more variants of this particular four-array construction.

## Reproduction and research consequence

The complete self-contained CommonJS implementation is [`sims/onion256_grid_xor_source_inverse.cjs`](../sims/onion256_grid_xor_source_inverse.cjs), reading only the committed historical source blob, canonical LP2 numeric grid, manuscript transcription and Shakespeare source fixture. [`.github/workflows/grid-keyskip-manual.yml`](../.github/workflows/grid-keyskip-manual.yml) exposes the explicit `historical-onion-xor-inverse` dispatch. The exact CI output is in successful run 42815364894.

**Consequence:** Do not treat a four-way 256-byte XOR combination as an authenticated LP2 key or expand nearly identical XOR/CTR variants on the selected second-block signal. A productive follow-on would need an independent clue specifying *how* historic Tor material relates to the manuscript, or a reversible cipher architecture explaining manuscript-wide lag-five, lag-six, antirepeat and page-join constraints, followed by actual plaintext re-encryption.
