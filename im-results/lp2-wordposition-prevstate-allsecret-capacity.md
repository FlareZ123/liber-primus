# Global word-position × previous-cipher all-secret GP29 gate

**Agent43, 14 November 2026. Classification:** a key-independent original-cipher necessary-condition exclusion for one entire changing-alphabet family, conditional on source-native GP29 English. **No unknown LP2 plaintext, authenticated creator key, deciphering rule or destination was recovered.** All numbers below are generated by [one standalone CommonJS source-input test](../sims/lp2_wordposition_prevstate_allsecret_sft.cjs), run successfully on [Windows Node22](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43644330556) with a further chapter-rekey extension at [the second run](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43644540687).

## Why test this state, and how it differs from prior gates

LP2 contains a pronounced shortage of immediately repeated ciphertext runes and a smaller but persistent *within-word* five-back repetition excess. The original five-dot motif printed on p7, p23 and p56 supplies a recognizable possible period-five clock, although no original sentence explicitly names a cipher. A plausible reversible generator might therefore change its substitution alphabet according to the **previous published ciphertext rune** and the **current printed word-relative position**. It could reset the predecessor at a word or page start.

Earlier [all-secret first-order work](lp2-firstorder-gp29-majorization-allkey.md) bounds a cipher alphabet indexed only by its predecessor; [five-phase work](lp2-lag5-fixedrune-boundary-alignment.md) bounds independently secret page×word-phase tables without the predecessor state. Neither state family includes the other. This experiment grants the attacker a strictly more expressive state than either standalone proposal: an arbitrary **29! secret bijection at every pair** of previous ciphertext rune and *exact position* within the current printed word, with no requirement that the five phases coincide. Thus every fixed five-phase rotation/affine/substitution of the same two public state variables is already contained.

This test measures a necessary property of that model; the motif does not authenticate the model or a key.

## Exact original-input theorem

Keep every original physical LP2 ciphertext rune on pp0–55, including all 32 printed cross-page word carries. This gives **12,956 GP29 runes in 2,896 printed words**; p50 has zero runes. Let the visible position within a word be zero-based **j**. Let **h** be the preceding published ciphertext GP29 value (0..28), or a distinct sentinel **29** when the chosen model resets. Give every state **s=(j,h)** its own arbitrary unknown permutation \(\pi_s\) of all 29 GP symbols:

\[
 C_i=\pi_{(j_i,h_i)}(P_i).
\]

The table is shared across the *entire* manuscript and is permitted to vary arbitrarily between different states, with no keyword, affine constraint, linearity, fitted English, or known initial alphabet. A word's public boundaries must be preserved and **j** is its complete local position, up to 13 on the longest word.

For each original state, count the 29 published ciphertext symbols and sort the counts in decreasing order \(n_{s,(1)}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s,(29)}\). Since an unknown bijection can only relabel symbols *within* a state, **every possible** decoded plaintext must satisfy, for each k,

\[
 \text{sum of its k most frequent rune counts}
 \leq \sum_s\sum_{r=1}^k n_{s,(r)}.
\]

This is a generous upper bound, since the independent state-wise optimum need not be simultaneously achievable. It is invariant to every possible secret permutation. Positive *native-GP plaintext* encryption under nontrivial state-dependent, invertible affine maps obeys the same bound and is exactly decoded **12,956/12,956**, controlling the argument against implementation errors.

Four plausible predecessor-reset policies are evaluated. Period-five uses \(j\bmod5\); **exact** uses independent states for every value of j and is the stronger all-secret family.

## Stronger exact one-rune-word constraint: a chapter-rekey lower bound

The original manuscript contains **83 complete single-rune printed words**. In source-calibrated English they would ordinarily be **A** or **I**; allow the archaic vocative **O** as a deliberately generous third choice. At a one-rune word, the within-word position is exactly j=0. For any *fixed epoch* and the same previous published ciphertext rune h, an invertible substitution table \(\pi_{(0,h)}\) can map **at most three distinct published ciphertext glyphs** into the three permissible plaintext letters. This yields a complete key-free necessary test over every original singleton site. It does not require an n-gram model, word dictionary, unknown cipher table or fitted decryption.

In the global carried-predecessor model, the 83 original singleton sites occupy **28 predecessor groups**. **Nine** groups contain more than three distinct published singleton glyphs (maximum **six**), with an aggregate **15 surplus distinct glyphs** beyond the maximum of three. Consequently this *entire* global all-secret cipher family is already excluded by a local word-grammar contradiction, independently of the global top-12 frequency theorem.
