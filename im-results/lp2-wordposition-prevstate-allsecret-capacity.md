# Global word-position × previous-cipher all-secret GP29 gate

**Agent43, 14 November 2026. Classification:** a key-independent original-cipher necessary-condition exclusion for one entire changing-alphabet family, conditional on source-native GP29 English. **No unknown LP2 plaintext, authenticated creator key, deciphering rule or destination was recovered.** All numbers below are generated by [one standalone CommonJS source-input test](../sims/lp2_wordposition_prevstate_allsecret_sft.cjs), run successfully on [Windows Node22](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43644330556) with a further chapter-rekey extension at [the second run](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43644540687).

## Why test this state, and how it differs from prior gates

LP2 contains a pronounced shortage of immediately repeated ciphertext runes and a smaller but persistent *within-word* five-back repetition excess. The original five-dot motif printed on p7, p23 and p56 supplies a recognizable possible period-five clock, although no original sentence explicitly names a cipher. A plausible reversible generator might therefore change its substitution alphabet according to the **previous published ciphertext rune** and the **current printed word-relative position**. It could reset the predecessor at a word or page start.

Earlier [all-secret first-order work](lp2-firstorder-gp29-majorization-allkey.md) bounds a cipher alphabet indexed only by its predecessor; [five-phase work](lp2-lag5-fixedrune-boundary-alignment.md) bounds independently secret page×word-phase tables without the predecessor state. Neither state family includes the other. This experiment grants the attacker a strictly more expressive state than either standalone proposal: an arbitrary **29! secret bijection at every pair** of previous ciphertext rune and *exact position* within the current printed word, with no requirement that the five phases coincide. Thus every fixed five-phase rotation/affine/substitution of the same two public state variables is already contained.

This test measures a necessary property of that model; the motif does not authenticate the model or a key.

## Exact original-input theorem

Keep every original physical LP2 ciphertext rune on pp0–55, including all 32 printed cross-page word carries. This gives **12,956 GP29 runes in 2,896 printed words**; p50 has zero runes. Let the visible position within a word be zero-based **j**. Let **h** be the preceding published ciphertext GP29 value (0..28), or a distinct sentinel **29** when the chosen model resets. Give every state **s=(j,h)** its own arbitrary unknown permutation \(\pi_s\) of all 29 GP symbols:

\[
 C_i=\pi_{(j_i,h_i)}(P_i).
\]

The table is shared across the *entire* manuscript and is permitted to vary arbitrarily between different states, with no keyword, affine constraint, linearity, fitted English, or known initial alphabet. A word's public boundaries must be preserved and **j** is its complete local position, up to 13 on the longest word.

For each original state, count the 29 published ciphertext symbols and sort the counts in decreasing order \(n_{s,(1)}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s,(29)}\). Since an unknown bijection can only relabel symbols *within* a state, **every possible** decoded plaintext must satisfy, for each k,

\[
 \text{sum of its k most frequent rune counts}
 \leq \sum_s\sum_{r=1}^k n_{s,(r)}.
\]

This is a generous upper bound, since the independent state-wise optimum need not be simultaneously achievable. It is invariant to every possible secret permutation. Positive *native-GP plaintext* encryption under nontrivial state-dependent, invertible affine maps obeys the same bound and is exactly decoded **12,956/12,956**, controlling the argument against implementation errors.

Four plausible predecessor-reset policies are evaluated. Period-five uses \(j\bmod5\); **exact** uses independent states for every value of j and is the stronger all-secret family.

| Predecessor clock across the whole manuscript | Five-phase bound, top 12 runes | Exact-j bound, top 12 | Exact-j bound, top 1 | Exact-j states |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Reset at every printed word | 7,942 | **8,459** | 1,175 | 309 |
| Carry through all words/pages | 8,248 | **8,978** | 1,283 | 338 |
| Reset at each original physical page | 8,271 | **9,001** | 1,291 | 346 |
| Reset at either word or page boundary | 7,956 | **8,475** | 1,183 | 317 |

The **9,001/12,956** most permissive exact-j top-twelve ceiling is a bound on plaintext *symbol concentration*, not an output of any guessed key or a derived original plaintext.

## Independent native-language calibration

Use the repository's source-calibrated GP29 encoder, which reproduces the independently solved LP1 spellings **ING** and **IO**. For each text corpus, sample **64 deterministic independent books of words** with exactly the same **2,896 source word lengths** as unknown LP2, preserving all 12,956 rune positions. A fixed xorshift32 seed family selects whole native English words separately by matching GP rune length. No runes or positions of the original ciphertext are changed.

| Native English reference | Reference corpus words | Matched control books | Minimum top-12 rune count | Maximum top-12 rune count |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Shakespeare | 55,407 | 64 | **9,921** | 10,142 |
| Gibran, *The Prophet* | 15,733 | 64 | **9,926** | 10,140 |
| Emerson essays | 77,932 | 64 | **9,996** | 10,157 |

**All 192/192** length-matched native GP-English books exceed the source's strongest global unknown-key ceiling by at least **920** occurrences among the twelve most frequent rune types. As a second witness, the same books have minimum counts of 1,500, 1,485 and 1,479 for their most common rune, versus **at most 1,291** for any stationary exact-j decoder. A separate Shakespeare-matched whole-book positive plant demonstrates exact inverse recovery under the source's published-cipher predecessor and word clocks; it preserves the necessary bound.

Consequently the globally shared two-variable, symbolwise, bijective GP29 predecessor-plus-word-position encoder is incompatible with the tested ordinary English register **for every secret alphabet table**, even if phase five is replaced by the maximum observed word-local position. This extends rather than repeats either predecessor-only or five-phase-only exclusion. It does not prove a universal language impossibility and it is not a key.

## How much extra state remains possible?

One additional author-visible **chapter rekey** can lift the bound sufficiently that an English-like source is no longer excluded by this invariant. For a single key-table replacement at each of the eight exact original chapter cuts, retain the previous published ciphertext across the cut and again permit all 29! secret tables per epoch/state:

| Cut after visual chapter | Global rune cut | Upper bound, top-12 count |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 (p0–2) | 729 | 9,275 |
| 2 (p3–7) | 1,874 | 9,714 |
| 3 (p8–14) | 3,603 | 10,000 |
| 4 (p15–22) | 5,506 | 10,140 |
| 5 (p23–26) | 6,527 | 10,163 |
| 6 (p27–32) | 7,960 | 10,142 |
| 7 (p33–39) | 9,640 | 9,969 |
| 8 (p40–53) | 12,648 | 9,110 |

This is a **necessary-condition ceiling only**. A value above a sampled plaintext's top-twelve total cannot establish a matching encoder, and none of the eight cuts is an independently authenticated rekey instruction. Still, a one-rekey at either bookend is already too weak for all 192 native controls, whereas a central-chapter rekey lifts this particular bound. A wholly independent table per visual chapter, secret word nonces, nonbijective/variable-length transport, plaintext-dependent state, longer history, alternate orthography, or changed word boundaries also fall outside the excluded family.

## Decision for the next attack

**Retire globally reused word-local five-phase (and even exact-j) predecessor-only bijections as native-English plaintext decoders.** A new candidate using the five-dot or lag-five clue needs source-selected additional state or rekeying, with an original observable prediction and complete inverse. A successful search for a source-specific chapter key might matter; freely adding independent keys to rescue the family would make it non-discriminating.
