# LP2 pp3–7 scrollwork: repeated frame without a source-selected spiral text traversal

**Original-geometry decision (Agent38 / #186, extended by Agent10 / #1148 and Agent5 / #1290, 16 November 2026):** The pp3–7 original source has an upper and a lower scroll on **each** side of the horizontally typeset rune block. Both sides mirror one another. The upper and lower forms contain several coiled tips and free tails; none is distinguished as the first terminal or the direction of reading. The shapes do not touch the rune lines or specify a raster-to-glyph sampling rule. The repeated frame offers no source-selected start, handedness, scope, grid or traversal from which to freeze a spiral transposition. Source-only reconstruction therefore stops **before** a cipher test: choosing an arbitrary spiral would select a key after seeing the target. The 217/261/263/196/208 page rune totals also rule out assuming a single unchanged row/column grid merely because the frame is identical. An independently sourced instruction could still give the frame a cryptographic role. No unknown ciphertext or key was solved.

## Original images, crop contract and controls

- Physical pp3–7 are the original archive JPEGs **20.jpg to 24.jpg**, all 2400×3600 RGB. The authentic solved-page layout controls use original **73.jpg (p56)** and **74.jpg (p57)**. All source JPEGs differ in whole-file SHA-256 and the p3–p7 text-body cropped pixels differ too: we are comparing distinct original pages.
- The **left** fixed frame-only crop is `(x0,y0,x1,y1)=(180,380,560,1500)` pixels. The **right** is `(1840,380,2180,1500)`. They cover the visible spiral strokes and isolated three-dot ornament of each margin. They avoid text/rune contents, so the comparison cannot accidentally equate identical ciphertext.
- Full decoded **RGB pixel arrays in both margin crops are identical** across original **21.jpg, 22.jpg, 23.jpg and 24.jpg**, corresponding to LP2 physical pp4–7. The original files and body crops are each different. Fixed RGB raster SHA-256 over the left and right crops: `81f1078ba68cb06a93cb6deef92544787e8e4869a24b8993bf306f7100c8a72c` and `0632d6e1b5a3f1248d77c09abf41029b4353b3ec22f8bb91751a1adc09042495`.
- **20.jpg (p3)** is not pixel-identical, but thresholding at grayscale intensity below100 gives **16,884 vs 16,923** dark pixels on the left and **16,879 vs 16,918** on the right against p4. Dark-pixel intersection-over-union is **0.9611903933** left and **0.9611791331** right. Under a fixed ±2-pixel morphological support comparison, **zero dark pixels on either page remain unmatched** in either margin. The same continuous strokes, curls, point tips and decorative dots survive.
- The solved p56/p57 images have **zero dark pixels** in those same isolated margin crops. This is a useful *different-layout* control, confirming that the pp3–7 common image is an actual template, rather than an artefact of the comparison method.
- The *body* control crop `(700,600,1700,1550)` has **five distinct decoded RGB hashes** across original pp3–7. The test therefore detects changed actual page artwork where changes exist.

**Historical positive control for altered repeated art:** the independently reproduced 2014 LP1 OutGuess instruction images 10–13 carry otherwise repeated *signed* ASCII-art frames whose source-visible border digits differ in order **3,3,0,1** ([original witness inventory](../resources/historical/lp1-outguess-2014-source-map.md)). That demonstrates a documented Cicada practice of deliberately modifying repeated picture-like material to signal a key. The pp3–7 physical scrolls lack the corresponding independently changed marks; the absence matters precisely because historical authors sometimes used such modifications.

## Extended lower-curl original-byte test (Agent10 / #1148)

The earlier audit verified upper scroll crops at original pixel bounds left `(180,380,560,1500)`, right `(1840,380,2180,1500)`. A separate raster test now covers the previously unaudited **lower scrolls**, with left `(60,1450,450,2600)` and right `(1950,1450,2340,2600)`. These are margin-only regions, disjoint from printed runes.

For each lower crop, the decoded RGB pixel arrays of source images **21.jpg–24.jpg (physical pp4–7) are exactly identical**. RGB SHA-256 values are left `b1eba33843a04034feb85d0e81777823084ba9b533c6961a24cc3ce4aa46842e` and right `fb18d22d35d3ac34a0fe3dbbdf1e16295d33b6ee73d82fe487935f6db14b5de2`. For **20.jpg (physical p3)** versus 21.jpg, thresholded (<100 gray) dark-pixel IoU is 0.9235383944 / 0.9234167894 left/right; the dark-pixel counts are 17,643/17,627 and 17,637/17,625 respectively. All dark pixels from either p3 or p4 mask lie within two pixels of the other mask, **zero unmatched in either direction**. Solved-image controls 73.jpg–74.jpg have no dark pixels in either lower margin crop. The SFT now asserts these facts for all four upper/lower margins.

A human trying to read the ornament as an instruction encounters multiple competing starts and ends on each side: upper and lower separate winding motifs, bilateral mirror reflections, center curl versus outer tail and both possible traversal directions. The source marks none. Runes are horizontal text lines with variable wrap, rather than cells on or in the scroll. No exact unambiguous mapping from ink path order to text rune indices exists in the printed artifact alone. **Decision:** reject the *source-selected spiral-readout hypothesis* at its selector gate; do not tune arbitrary path directions, offsets or page-specific grids against ciphertext statistics.

## Physically separate red paragraph initials and identical frame

The 16 November original-source revisit counted the illuminated red capital *components*, explicitly excluding 115-pixel-high red inline rune glyphs. Red-pixel connected components larger than 20,000 pixels recover **two** large capitals on physical p3 scan20, at source bounding rectangles (x,y,w,h)=**(601,669,149,496)** and **(601,2098,229,495)**; a third is on p6 scan23 at **(601,1721,149,497)**. Pages p4, p5 and p7 contain zero components at that giant-initial size, although p7 has an ordinary-size red terminal sentence. These positions show three typographically distinct paragraphs or subpassages in ordinary top-to-bottom source layout. The giant-cap scan was taken before any plaintext scoring and does not assign a reader direction or a page permutation.

A separate fixed continuous margin-only ROI, x10–400 and x2000–2390, y450–2800, produces **exactly the same full RGB pixels on pages p4–p7** on both sides, including upper and lower curl portions. Against p3, the unchanged black-form IoU is about **0.9384** at the separately frozen threshold110, consistent with the previous ±2-pixel registration of #1148. **No page-dependent scroll endpoint points to either giant initial.** An unaided solver already reads p3's two red-initiated blocks and p6's third from top to bottom. Choosing a nonstandard interleaving, reverse traversal, or an off-page starting point requires new instructions.

## What the original frame can and cannot encode

The image-source null is specific and strong: four separate original pages have precisely the same RGB bytes throughout both previously audited upper scrollwork regions (and now the lower pair). A hypothetical binary state from **changed endpoints, intentional chirality, altered dots, missing stems, spiral directions or an increasing number of ink components** cannot distinguish those four pages, and p3 differs only at tiny stroke-border pixels with no independent mark. Consequently the illustrations furnish **no unique sequence of five per-page directions or state seeds**, and there is nothing source-selected to hand to a reversible LP2 original-cipher decoder.

This is more discriminating than visually comparing thumbnail scrolls: it tests native pixels on original 2400×3600 JPEGs, explicitly includes a different-text control, and confirms the only nonidentical page stays within a two-pixel stroke outline. The fixed threshold and two-pixel support comparison were chosen for stroke equivalence before evaluating any unknown ciphertext; no language scoring, key/phase scanning, decorative-dot orientation enumeration or image steganography search was used.

**Bounded scope:** the experiment is about the four measured upper/lower original *margin scroll illustrations* and their gross strokes, not hidden JPEG bits, non-scroll artwork, other pages' illustrations, changes outside the measured crops, or code embedded in the unchanged shape itself. If a newly discovered original message explicitly assigns an operation to the stationary scroll shape, that would be a new route. Without such a cue the Director should retire this variation hypothesis instead of asking an agent to assign arbitrary page bits to near-identical curls.
