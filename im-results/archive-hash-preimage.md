### DUTA 2016 / DUTA-10K public-payload gate (updated 14 November 2026)

**Decision: the public DUTA-10K material located is not a source-dated raw HTTP response archive.** Keep the original DUTA crawl, the DUTA-10K release and later re-scraped corpora distinct.

- **Original DUTA (2016):** The 2017 EACL paper says the crawler fetched HTTP pages through Tor, explored to depth two, and concatenated all pages from each hidden-service domain into one HTML file. It reports 7,931 services (6,831 labeled) from May–July 2016. Footnote 1 says the dataset is available upon request to the first author; it is not a public per-response archive. [2017 EACL paper, pp. 35–37](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1004.pdf)
- **DUTA-10K (2017 extension):** The GVIS page publicly lists the 10,367-address dataset, but its endpoint was inaccessible in this run. More decisively, the 2023 RANLP paper states that, for legal reasons, DUTA-10K contains only manually tagged links and must be reconstructed by scraping the DarkWeb; its 571-page subsets were re-scraped and text-preprocessed. [2023 RANLP paper, §2.1.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.ranlp-1.102.pdf)
- **Public derived corpus:** The public [Choshen et al. 2019 repository](https://github.com/huji-nlp/cyber) contains `data.zip` (Git blob SHA `635a6834291138671912d49725f177d8373ea079`; repository README blob SHA `c167f2430953fc962d3db95ba63650dca0e8cab2`). Its README identifies `onion` and `onion_clean` as website-text documents, the latter cleaned. The 2023 paper explicitly identifies this repository as the source used to reconstruct DUTA-10K. This is a public re-scraped text corpus, not a dated original DUTA HTTP capture. The binary `data.zip` was pinned at the Git-object level but not downloaded in this run; no content-level checksum, raw response boundary, original capture timestamp or HTTP headers are claimed.
- **Private raw-HTML subset:** The 2025 *Snorkeling in Dark Waters* paper says its authors obtained a large subset of DUTA-10K raw HTML from the original dataset authors. This is a private-custody lead, not a public download. The paper does not establish that those files preserve individual response boundaries, original response headers or per-response timestamps; the 2017 collection's documented storage was concatenated per-domain HTML. Its separate Mimir crawler's `HTML` and `Timestamp` fields belong to Mimir's own crawl, not the DUTA subset. [2025 extended paper, §II-E and Table I](https://dspace.networks.imdea.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12761/1956/Snorkeling_in_dark_waters.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **Hash consequence:** neither the public links/labels nor the re-scraped website-text corpus is suitable for a byte-exact p56 comparison. A concatenated per-domain HTML file or a cleaned text export is not automatically the exact single-response body. No publicly obtainable DUTA source-dated body with URL, timestamp and transfer metadata was identified. The only remaining raw-HTML path requires owner approval under [#784](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/784); no private-custodian contact was made.

### Pre-LP2 2013 Tor crawl custody leads (added 15 November 2026)

Two documented 2013 Tor content crawls are additional, lower-priority custody leads for the p56 page target. They are not public page archives in the cited records, and neither is known to contain Cicada material.

**TNO / Spitters et al., December 2013.** The study reports 5,725 known hidden services, 1,481 with downloaded content, 2,196,410 downloaded page URLs, and 324,623 pages with usable content after preprocessing. The crawler downloaded HTML only; preprocessing filtered error/redirect pages, extracted text, and discarded texts shorter than 15 words. The paper does not establish whether original HTML or raw HTTP response bodies were retained. The TNO repository record exposes the publication, not a dataset download.

- Paper: https://gwern.net/doc/darknet-market/2014-spitters.pdf (DOI 10.1109/JISIC.2014.40)
- TNO record: https://repository.tno.nl/SingleDoc?docId=82829

**University of Luxembourg / Biryukov et al., February–April 2013.** The authors collected 39,824 hidden-service descriptors on 4 February 2013. They later attempted 8,153 HTTP(S) destinations; 7,114 ports were open and 6,579 destinations accepted a connection. After excluding short/error/duplicate material, 3,050 destinations remained for classification; the thesis describes 1,813 English hidden services classified into topics. Binary data were excluded. Neither the paper nor the ORBilu record provides a page-corpus download or confirms that original HTTP bodies were preserved.

- Paper: https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/17182/1/popularity-HS-2014.pdf (DOI 10.1109/ICDCSW.2014.20)
- Thesis with detailed collection and filtering: https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/21798/1/phdthesis-pustogarov.pdf, Section 4.4.2
- ORBilu record: https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/17182

**Causal value and priority.** Either corpus could help only if the p56 target page (or a relevant Cicada resource) existed by 2013. TNO is the stronger of these two because its crawl is later and larger; both remain below the 2016 EURECOM/Deusto and DUTA routes, which are temporally closer and have better-documented HTML/HTTP metadata. The exact target hash is not known to be present in either corpus. Extracted text alone may be inadequate for a raw-page digest.

**Next action:** after maintainer approval, ask TNO and the Luxembourg authors only whether the target host/page or digest is present and whether they retained the original URL, capture timestamp, raw HTML/HTTP body, headers, and body representation. A presence/absence result plus metadata is sufficient; do not request or redistribute the full corpus. No contact has been made. Do not treat these as recovered artifacts or as evidence of a Cicada connection.
