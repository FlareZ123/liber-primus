# Historical onion-page hash preimage: independently executed bounded regression

**Current assessment (14 November 2026):** eleven independently executed **bounded source-native test groups** report zero matches. A separate 65-case gate of 13 literal strings from the later, unverified 2019 onion/domain/video claim also found zero matches under five 512-bit digest families. Two source-derived serializations of the exact p50 dual-factor quotient—the 153-byte BASE29 big-endian integer and the 252-character BASE29 numeral string using the authenticated 2013 alphabet—miss the p56 target under the same five 512-bit families (0/10; conditional on base60). The eleven source-native groups are: **249** comparisons of frozen 2014 stage captures, URL representations and three direct onion-2 candidate forms, **232** of the 58 original archived LP2 JPEG page-byte streams under four named digests, **130** comparisons of the 17 original LP1 first-release JPEGs plus nine exact p05/p10 page/payload components under five 512-bit digest families (85 raw-page + 45 component checks), **58** additional raw-JPEG Whirlpool comparisons prompted by a dated 2016 public claim, **4** exact SHA-512 comparisons of the preserved 2016 signed JPEG, OutGuess payload, embedded signature packet and verified cleartext body, **340** original LP2 32×8 table serializations, **40** page-15 Fibonacci-square encodings, **12** raw hashes of the original 2014 stage11 sexagesimal source files, **36** historical-table/terminal-rune compositions, and **4,096** SHA-512-family comparisons of all **1,024 source-supported mixed readings** of the ten historically disputed sexagesimal table bytes. Counts are finite operations, **not** independent preimage discoveries; historical-corpus overlap with external audits is unquantified. The 2014 `56.bin` remains a derived rendering of the printed target hex, while the separately preserved `sexagesimal.bin` is a historical 256-byte table variant, not a missing page. The 2014 `59.txt`/`59.bin` lead is now traced to a 67-byte unsigned participant README added 11 May and deleted 25 May; all 15 reachable Stage11 commits and the advertised histories/trees of the two documented archive families were checked, with no 59 file and no independent 2014 signed attachment. GitHub's public API cannot enumerate dangling objects. No hash preimage or unknown page text was recovered. No original unknown LP2 text, key, algorithm, missing target-page bytes, or final destination recovered.

**2014–2016 archived-payload replay lane (14 November 2026).** The independent [Arquivo.pt CDX index](https://arquivo.pt/wayback/cdx/api-docs/v3) exposes original capture URL, timestamp, status, source WARC/ARC filename and base32 SHA-1 payload digest. An actual, positively indexed **27 April 2014** capture of `http://www.publico.pt/` (20140427170307; collection FAWP17; `IAH-20140427170152-00008-p12.arquivo.pt.arc.gz`) was retrieved through its documented `id_` raw replay endpoint. The **261,198 bytes returned by the `id_` replay** hash to **`URP2ID3HDROWNGCE6ZL7N5PHZ6Q56XSU` (SHA-1 base32)**, exactly matching that archive's CDX index record. This proves consistency between the archive's replayed payload and its indexed digest, and confirms that a dated 2014 body is retrievable. It does **not independently establish identity with the exact 2014 on-wire HTTP response or original unmodified entity body**: the CDX digest and replay may share the same archived or normalized payload. Thus the positive control validates archived-byte retrieval, **not historical wire-byte fidelity**, which must remain a separate requirement for any exact p56 preimage claim. This is a source collection distinct from the already audited 2014 Common Crawl and earlier Agent2 **2015** Arquivo/OnionCity index interval; it does not show that the separate unknown Cicada hash target was archived. A second contemporary `www.sapo.pt` CDX source control listed 2014 captures but its raw replay returned HTTP429 and supplies no second byte-level check.

The 2014–2016 Arquivo source-anchored screen tested (a) the documented manuscript distributor `ky2khlqdf7qdznac` under original `.onion.to` 2014 and 2015 root URLs and six alternate Tor2web gateway root spellings, and (b) **all six other original Cicada 2014 onion hostnames** from [the pinned historical archive manifest](../resources/cicada2014-archive-hash-manifest.json), with `http`/`https`, both `.onion.to` and `.onion.city`, source-fixed 2014–2016 host-root-prefix query ranges. **Unqueried raw-source custodian leads:** the May–June 2016 EURECOM/Deusto crawl and the May–July 2016 DUTA collection remain distinct, unqueried archival routes. DUTA should not be described as wholly non-public: the 2017 EACL paper says its original 7,931-service dataset is available upon request to the first author (`mnab@unileon.es`) and explains that collected pages were concatenated into per-domain HTML files. The DUTA-10K extension is publicly listed on the GVIS platform and later studies describe it as downloadable, but its GVIS endpoint was inaccessible in this run. The cleaned DUTA-10K text export strips HTML; a 2025 paper reports that its authors obtained a subset of raw HTML files from the original dataset authors. These routes may support a content/host presence query, but concatenated or cleaned HTML is not automatically a byte-exact HTTP response. Only an original response body with its URL, timestamp, and content/transfer metadata can establish an exact p56 hash candidate. A further, independently documented Australian TorCrawl ran 12 April–1 July 2016 under AFP authorisation: its paper abstract reports 232,792 pages / 7,651 Tor virtual domains, while its detailed results separately report 408,216 Tor URLs / 7,954 Tor virtual domains. Monash and publisher pages expose no downloadable raw-response/WARC archive or content lookup interface; the paper does not establish original on-wire response-byte retention. This is a private-custody pointer, not a public corpus or p56 candidate. The bounded public-source decision is recorded in [the 2014–2016 corpus gate](lp2-2014-2016-public-tor-corpus-gate.md). No target page bytes or digest match have been established.
### DUTA 2016 / DUTA-10K raw-HTML archive leads (updated 14 November 2026)

This is an **unqueried data-access route**, not a recovered page or a negative result. The original 2016 collection and its later DUTA-10K extension must be kept distinct.

- The original DUTA study reports a Tor crawler that fetched HTTP/port-80 HTML, explored to the second level, and collected **7,931 services during May–July 2016**. It says the collected pages for each domain were concatenated into one HTML file. The 2017 EACL paper states that the dataset is available upon request to its first author and lists **`mnab@unileon.es`** in the author block. [2017 EACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1004/)
- Public author information for Mhd Wesam Al-Nabki also lists **`wesam.alnabki@unileon.es`** in a later 2023 publication. [2023 Springer author information](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11042-023-15530-7)
- The DUTA-10K extension grew the corpus to **10,367 unique onion addresses**. It is publicly listed on the [GVIS DUTA-10K page](https://gvis.unileon.es/dataset/duta-darknet-usage-text-addresses-10k/), and later papers describe downloading it from that platform; the GVIS page/download endpoint was not accessible in this run.
- The DUTA-10K cleaned-text export strips HTML tags (the thesis describes using Lynx), so that export alone cannot verify a byte-level hash. [Al-Nabki thesis / DUTA-10K description](https://buleria.unileon.es/bitstream/handle/10612/11776/Tesis%20de%20Mhd%20Wesam.pdf?sequence=1)
- Crucially, the 2025 paper *Snorkeling in Dark Waters* states that its authors obtained access to a large subset of DUTA-10K **raw HTML files** from the original dataset authors. This makes them an additional custodian lead, but does not establish that their subset includes the 2016 Stage11 host or the relevant page. [Extended paper, §II-E](https://dspace.networks.imdea.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12761/1956/Snorkeling_in_dark_waters.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

The historical Stage11 archive path is `ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion`. Its 2014 repository-side README says `59.txt contains text from page 59` and gives `xxd -r -p 59.txt > 59.bin`; that README is not an authenticated Cicada message. The exact p56 target is:

`36367763ab73783c7af284446c59466b4cd653239a311cb7116d4618dee09a8425893dc7500b464fdaf1672d7bef5e891c6e2274568926a49fb4f45132c2a8b4`

The preserved [Stage11 `56.bin` artifact](https://github.com/micheloosterhof/cicada-2014/blob/master/stage11/56.bin) is **the 64 raw bytes of this digest itself**, not the preimage page: reconstructing its bytes gives the exact 128-hex target above, while SHA-512 over those 64 bytes is `97fa04efee9358e5d11b4186c1914057b1353562f67b7c6553fa78f22a36fc214cd253549bd8a775139cf9d600574cc829660b574c7991ab46aaafb07a8b9791`, not the target. Do not mistake `56.bin` for the page requested by p56.

The 2016–2017 window is temporally compatible with a v2 onion service still operating, but there is no evidence that this host was seeded, reachable, or present in DUTA. The crawler's depth and HTML-only focus also mean a linked non-HTML `59.txt` file may not have been retained.
