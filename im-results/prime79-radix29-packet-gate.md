## Exact candidate and why its source matters

The 55 rune-bearing unsolved LP2 pages have exactly **12,956 GP runes = 164 × 79**. The manuscript's page-15 spiral selects prime 79; the associated Euler totient is 78. This motivates a *lossless, fixed-size* model: represent each group of 78 base-29 plaintext symbols as an integer, then emit its 79-digit base-28 representation. Its published 29-symbol ciphertext can be generated by a predecessor-excluding walk with one of the 28 other rune symbols per step. Each entire block can be decoded as long as the previous published rune and the same rune ordering are known.

A new [exact source-original directed-cycle bound](prime79-binary-capacity-cycle.md) excludes any **independent unrestricted fixed-width binary integer** directly substituted for each 78-digit base29 input in this transport: widths ≤378 bits violate the observed packet-leading 3-cycle condition under every secret cyclic alphabet, whereas ≥379 bits cannot inject their entire domain into 29^78 possible values. An arbitrary fixed sorted alphabet is also excluded by its necessary leading-glyph bound. This is a narrower scope than all possible binary intermediates; variable-length or restricted encodings remain open.

The necessary numerical capacity bound is exact:

```text
0 ≤ integer < 29^78
integer = d0×28^78 + d1×28^77 + ... + d78
d0 ≤ floor((29^78−1)/28^78) = 15.
```

The test admits **any fixed permutation of the 29 rune glyphs**, forward or reversed digit order, sorted predecessor-exclusion rank or cyclic predecessor-exclusion rank, and an arbitrary underlying plaintext. It gives the cipher the benefit of the doubt at every observed repeated output by **discarding that entire 79-rune block**. It discards the initial ciphertext block because its predecessor is unknown. It does not infer plaintext or fit linguistic scoring.

