# Source-aligned 79/78 GP radix conversion: one viable original-cipher alignment

**Consolidated 10 November 2026 (Agents 22 and 23):** exact source-original necessary-condition searches exclude **157/158** global 79-rune packet origins and digit directions even against an arbitrary fixed secret cyclic rune order. **Only phase 35, forward digits, passes**, with 95 intact numerically valid packets for one explicitly recovered *candidate* alphabet. Its 7,410 decoded rune values have near-uniform frequencies and no readable plaintext. This is a conditional structural survivor of a hypothetical code, not a recovered original LP2 encryption key, plaintext, or destination. Selecting 79/78 because of known prime79 clues is post-selection.

## Exact candidate and why its source matters

The 55 rune-bearing unsolved LP2 pages have exactly **12,956 GP runes = 164 × 79**. The manuscript's page-15 spiral selects prime 79; the associated Euler totient is 78. This motivates a *lossless, fixed-size* model: represent each group of 78 base-29 plaintext symbols as an integer, then emit its 79-digit base-28 representation. Its published 29-symbol ciphertext can be generated by a predecessor-excluding walk with one of the 28 other rune symbols per step. Each entire block can be decoded as long as the previous published rune and the same rune ordering are known.

The necessary numerical capacity bound is exact:

```text
0 ≤ integer < 29^78
integer = d0×28^78 + d1×28^77 + ... + d78
d0 ≤ floor((29^78−1)/28^78) = 15.
```

The test admits **any fixed permutation of the 29 rune glyphs**, forward or reversed digit order, sorted predecessor-exclusion rank or cyclic predecessor-exclusion rank, and an arbitrary underlying plaintext. It gives the cipher the benefit of the doubt at every observed repeated output by **discarding that entire 79-rune block**. It discards the initial ciphertext block because its predecessor is unknown. It does not infer plaintext or fit linguistic scoring.

## Source measurements, exact necessary conditions and decision

At global 79-rune phase zero, **95 complete groups** are free of adjacent duplicate outputs. The public canonical GP ordering already yields invalid base29 codewords in **36/95** cyclic-forward, **33/95** cyclic-reversed, **39/95** sorted-forward, and **37/95** sorted-reversed cases.

For an **unknown fixed sorted alphabet order**, a leading rank ≤15 can arise from at most **17** of 29 possible rune labels. The 95 clean original groups contain **28 distinct leading symbols** in forward order and **29** in reverse order. Both orientations violate this necessary condition.

For an **unknown fixed cyclic alphabet order**, the first digit requires `1 ≤ (π(current)−π(previous)) mod29 ≤16` on every clean packet's leading ciphertext transition. A complete 29-symbol all-different constraint search, fixing the harmless rotation `π(rune0)=0`, propagated every required arc and exhaustively explored all remaining candidate permutations. The output was:

| Orientation | Clean blocks | Distinct constraint edges | Exact DFS nodes | Result |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Forward | 95 | 92 | 28,598 | **UNSAT** |
| Reversed | 95 | 90 | 6,164 | **UNSAT** |

These constraints are **necessary only**: the actual 79-digit values impose still more restrictions. Thus a proof of UNSAT at the leading-digit level eliminates the candidate without reconstructing whole blocks or a key. The search is complete for phase zero, with a one-million-node cap that **was not reached**. An interrupted or capped future run reports UNKNOWN and cannot support exclusion.

A clean packet is a necessary original-source observation. All 158 variants with a single unknown fixed *sorted* glyph order are already independently excluded by the 17-leading-glyph capacity argument.

For an arbitrary fixed **cyclic order π of all 29 runes**, each predecessor transition emits one radix-28 digit

## Exact GF79 geometric-position checksum exclusion

A separate possible 79-symbol code family would impose a block checksum in **GF79** while hiding a fixed rune-to-field mapping `f`. Consider every nonzero field multiplier `g=1,...,78` and every 79-rune block phase `phase=0,...,78`, with the source-indexed position weights `g^j mod79`, for `j=0,...,78`. Require one fixed, unknown checksum `b` common to all complete 79-rune blocks:

```text
sum(j=0..78) g^j * f(C[phase + 79*block + j]) = b (mod79).
```

For each `(g,phase)` build a 29-column matrix whose rows contain each rune's *weighted* occurrence sum in a block, then subtract the first row to remove the unknown `b`. The all-constant rune mapping is necessarily in the difference kernel, because the total sum of positional weights is the same for every block. Any nonconstant rune embedding, including an arbitrary injective hidden GP alphabet, would require a second independent kernel vector.

Exact finite-field elimination on the original LP2 source gives **rank28 and nullity1 in all 6,162 = 78×79 combinations**. Consequently, within this finite family **only constant `f` can yield the same additive checksum**. This rejects every nonconstant fixed rune-to-GF79 embedding for arbitrary constant checksum `b`, including the source-linked primitive root `g=53`, and all shifted global block origins. The unweighted case is `g=1`.

The [reproducer](../sims/prime79_glyph_checksum_rank.cjs) also creates 100 synthetic 79-rune packets using an unseen injective alphabet field mapping and exact checksum zero at **g=1 and g=53**; the same blind rank test returns nontrivial kernels in both. All original and positive-control assertions passed in the [successful source-original Actions run 42797361281](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42797361281), Node22, 10 November 2026. This is an **exact original-input necessary-condition test**, with no Monte Carlo significance assignment.

This conclusion is bounded to a fixed glyph map, a global packet clock, a geometric positional weight, and one shared checksum value. It does not constrain arbitrary non-geometric weights, position-dependent or changing glyph maps, per-section checksum resets, nonlinear codes, or variable-length encodings. The GF79 interpretation follows already examined prime79/totient78 clues, and no plaintext is recovered.

## What remains open

The combined exact proof concerns **fixed-sized 78→79 numeric packing** under a **single global fixed cyclic rune ordering** and a common global 79-rune packet clock, with entire repeat-containing packets conservatively erased. The naturally aligned phase zero is excluded. The all-phase fixed-sorted-order impossibility extends beyond natural alignment, while **157/158 global phase/direction cyclic-order cases have now been conclusively excluded by their combined one- and two-digit constraints**. Only **forward phase35** survives, and its known inverse yields near-uniform rune values. The proof does not exclude page-reset packing, state-dependent 29×28 row mappings, key-dependent changing alphabets, intentional transposition of runes, variable-length arithmetic/range coding, or extra escape/repair characters that change the block clock. Testing those requires a specific reversible encoder and evidence of a key or source-derived state, rather than expanding a generic language-score screen.

The equality 12,956=164×79 is an exact property of the source, but selecting it because of the known 79 clue is post-selection. The exclusion is mathematical for the stated model and **not** a claim about Cicada's intended mechanism. Further effort should seek a source-grounded stateful model that predicts untouched LP2 plaintext and forward reproduces the genuine ciphertext.
