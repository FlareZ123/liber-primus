## An exact invariant for a section-static word-start substitution

Suppose the first rune of each ciphertext word is generated by a **fixed arbitrary bijection of the plaintext initial rune within its section**:

    C(section,word,0) = permutation_section(P(section,word,0)).

The permutation can differ among sections and the remaining letters of each word can undergo other transformations. Equality of initial runes within one section is preserved exactly. The pooled within-section ordered initial-collision rate

    Q = [sum_sections sum_r n(section,r) * (n(section,r)-1)]
        / [sum_sections N(section) * (N(section)-1)]

must therefore equal the plaintext's Q under this entire family. The invariant needs no knowledge of the permutations and no assumption that they are affine or Caesar shifts.

The data are the nine unsolved $ segments 7–15 in 'resources/transcriptions/liber_primus_master.txt', containing **2,896 canonical words and 12,956 runes**. The runeless numeric page is preserved in the physical scan numbering. Printed lines and physical pages can bisect words; only lexical punctuation ends a word. Cross-checking with 'sims/lp2_word_pages.cjs' gives 55 rune-bearing unsolved pages, 2,896 words, 12,956 runes and **32 words crossing page breaks**. Note: the earlier '34 crossing words' in a broadcast and word-length report contradicts the current executable regression and should not be used as an invariant.

For an exact **known-plaintext reference**, we use the first runes of unenciphered LP1 segments 2, 4, 6 and LP2 PARABLE segment 17: 250 words. No enciphered segment is treated as plaintext.

### Controlled register-sensitivity experiment

We drew exactly the observed nine section sizes from the explicit distribution

    p_mix(r) = (1-epsilon) * p_ref(r) + epsilon / 29

where p_ref is the empirical 250-word known-plaintext initial-rune frequency vector. The experiment tests English-like word-initial profiles and progressively diluted alternatives; it does not assume this is the actual LP2 plaintext distribution. Each row comprises 5,000 independent random manuscripts. The generator seed is 0x354ae11. The lower-tail count records synthetic Q less than or equal to actual Q.

| Uniform mixture epsilon | Null Q mean | Null SD | Nulls at or below actual | Empirical one-sided p estimate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 0.067092 | 0.001430 | 0 / 5,000 | 0.000200 (Monte Carlo resolution) |
| 0.50 | 0.042624 | 0.000818 | 0 / 5,000 | 0.000200 (resolution) |
| 0.75 | 0.036523 | 0.000446 | 0 / 5,000 | 0.000200 (resolution) |
| 1.00 | 0.034476 | 0.000229 | 4,107 / 5,000 | 0.821436 |

The reference's unbiased observed Q=0.063325 is distinct from the 0.067092 expectation of independently resampling its empirical frequency vector; this difference is a finite-sample plug-in effect.

**Supported exclusion:** a bijective substitution fixed for each section's word initials, even if a different permutation is used for each section, cannot transform ordinary LP1-like initial-letter coincidences into those measured in LP2. The tested discrepancy persists when 75% of the English-like source distribution is replaced by uniform noise. Different language registers, mappings changed within sections, per-word keys, and encodings containing multiple or fewer than one plaintext letters per rune are outside the claim. This does not identify the actual cipher.

## Exact one-rune-word capacity for arbitrary secret alphabets

A separate **exact original-input exclusion** avoids any guessed English frequency profile. Of the **2,896** canonical whole ciphertext words, **83** contain exactly one rune, and those 83 words use **28 different GP symbols**. Suppose a genuine one-rune ciphertext word represents one genuine one-rune plaintext word from a fixed set of \(k\) symbols. For ordinary native GP-English, the permitted set is **A** and **I**, indexed 24 and 10. Give the cipher an *arbitrary, secret 29-symbol permutation* for each public key phase. The permutation may be independently chosen for the entire book, each of the eight nonempty \`$\` sections, or each original physical page. These freedoms favor the hypothesis.

Let \(n_{g,r}\) count the observed one-rune ciphertext words carrying rune \(r\) in key state \(g\). Order each state's 29 frequencies from largest to smallest. Because any bijection maps the \(k\) permitted plaintext symbols into **at most \(k\) distinct ciphertext symbols**, the exact maximum number of source words compatible with the model is

\[
 B_k=\sum_g\sum_{j=1}^k n_{g,(j)},\qquad
 \text{forced exceptions}=83-B_k.
\]

This bound is **sharp on the singleton evidence alone**: the unknown permutation for each state can always send the \(k\) allowed plaintext symbols to its \(k\) most common original ciphertext labels. No secret-table search, language model, or statistical null is required.

| Unknown-alphabet scope | Forced exceptions if native singleton alphabet has 2 symbols (A/I) | If 3 symbols | If 4 symbols | Minimum *additional* contiguous key epochs for exact A/I within the scope |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One book-wide 29-permutation | **73 / 83** | 68 | 63 | **40** |
| Independent permutation for each of eight nonempty source sections | **57 / 83** | 47 | 38 | **34** |
| Independent permutation on each physical page, with crossing words assigned to their start | **13 / 83** | 4 | 1 | **10** |

The 13 page-local A/I violations occur across **nine** original page indices **4, 10, 13, 16, 17, 19, 21, 28 and 30**. Within a scope, the minimum further rekeys are obtained by greedily partitioning the singleton outputs into consecutive epochs containing no more than two distinct glyphs. Rekeys may occur anywhere between two successive singleton words. Thus even independently secret page alphabets cannot explain the original native A/I singletons without more rekeys or some explicitly reversible source exception.

A more permissive model chooses one secret alphabet for each \`(scope, whole-word-ordinal mod P)\` state, where word ordinals reset at the stated scope. Exhaustive **original-source** necessary-condition testing at every period \(P=1,\ldots,256\) finds the *first* period with zero forced A/I exceptions as:

| Reset scope | First unexcluded word-key period | Exceptions at word period 23 | Exceptions at word period 29 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Book-wide | **130** | 40 | 27 |
| Each source section | **28** | 2 | 2 |
| Each physical page | **6** | 0 | 0 |

### Previous-ciphertext-keyed alphabets

A self-synchronizing architecture may select a book-, section-, or page-specific secret alphabet by the *preceding ciphertext rune*:

\[
 C_i=S_{g,C_{i-1}}(P_i),
\]

where every row is an entirely arbitrary, otherwise unknown bijective GP29 permutation. This is different from the whole-word-ordinal key clock above. Exclude the **one** original singleton word beginning at a physical page's first rune, since its preceding within-page ciphertext state requires an unknown IV. Exactly **82** original one-rune words then have observable previous-ciphertext states. The same sharp top-two capacity bound applies independently inside each state:

| Secret predecessor-alphabet reset scope | Eligible singleton words | Observed states | Forced A/I exceptions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Book-wide | 82 | 28 | **31** |
| Independently each of eight nonempty sections | 82 | 67 | **2** |
| Independently each physical page | 82 | 80 | **0** |

Thus book-global and section-global fixed predecessor-indexed arbitrary permutations cannot explain all the original native A/I singleton words. Page-specific rows escape this *singleton-only* condition because 80 state keys are observed at merely 82 singleton events; survival is not positive evidence of a cipher. The separate [full-corpus ciphertext-history collision study](previous-cipher-sbox-invariant.md) imposes additional language-conditioned constraints on page-specific rows.

The [same script](../sims/singleton_word_key_capacity.cjs) constructs three stateful, reversible, original-layout control encryptions with actual GP A/I singleton plaintext, random hidden previous-ciphertext-keyed 29-permutations, and a full **12,956/12,956** symbol inverse. All yield zero forced singleton exceptions. The original-source figures and these independent controls passed the [manual CI run 42856477400](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42856477400). This remains a conditional source-register impossibility theorem and recovers no actual LP2 plaintext.

Survival of the singleton condition is only a *necessary* condition; later periods can again fail as word-ordinal residues collide. An unconstrained ciphertext-conditioned state, a changing hidden key, inserted or removed runes, redefined word boundaries, different languages, and an intermediate non-native plaintext representation remain possible. In particular this does not establish that a length-130 key is present, recover any plaintext, or identify a viable cipher.

The on-original exact implementation is [\`sims/singleton_word_key_capacity.cjs\`](../sims/singleton_word_key_capacity.cjs). It preserves all **32 words crossing page boundaries** and verifies the independent shared whole-word parser. Three **12,956-rune reversible positive-control encryptions**, each with genuine planted A/I singletons and independent arbitrary keyed per-state permutations, fully invert and correctly return zero singleton exceptions. Its original and planted assertions passed the existing [manual word-start workflow, run **42854530210**](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42854530210). The separate [wordwise 29-to-28-radix nonce bound](wordwise-radix-nonce-capacity.md) concerns a different \(L-1\)-plaintext-runes-per-\(L\)-cipher-runes architecture; it should not be conflated with this same-length model.

## Interword repeated-phase test

A period-23 additive key on word initials would have C(word,0) = P(word,0) + K(word mod 23) (mod 29). Initials at word positions separated by 23 or 46 then share a key phase, so equality of ciphertext initials equals equality of plaintext initials. This remains true under arbitrary common additive page shifts. Ordinary English-like word initials predict elevated same-rune matches at these lags.

We scanned equality at lags 1–120 within the nine full sections and 1–50 in each rune-bearing page. The page model assigns the **complete** word to its starting page, using the shared parser. Both nulls independently shuffle the order of word initials **inside the same scope**, preserving each scope's rune inventory. A familywise p compares the full maximum over all searched lags; a 23+46 p compares the sum of the two fixed standardized scores. Z denominators use fixed-margin expectations and a binomial variance approximation; reported p-values come from actual permutations.

| Scope | Best scan lag, z | Familywise p | Lag 23 observed / expected, z, p | Lag 46 observed / expected, z, p | 23+46 p |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| Section-continuous (1,000 nulls) | 62, +2.421 | 0.6963 | 97 / 94.202, +0.293, 0.3766 | 85 / 87.767, -0.301, 0.6394 | 0.4855 |
| Page-resetting (2,000 nulls) | 45, +2.270 | 0.5077 | 59 / 56.564, +0.330, 0.4148 | 13 / 18.500, -1.301, 0.9355 | 0.7641 |

The section lag-23 sample contains 2,710 ordered pairs; the page-local sample contains 1,649. Neither lag-23 scan provides positive evidence of word-initial repeated-key phases.

**Planted positive control:** 100 synthetic copies retain the true word counts of each page. First letters are independently sampled from the 250-word known-plaintext reference, encrypted by an independent random 23-phase mod-29 Caesar key, and independently overwritten at 5% of sites as a proxy for downstream perturbations. At lag 23, mean page-local z=6.422, minimum 3.823. All 100 plants exceeded z=3, and 97 exceeded z=4. This establishes useful power for the **specified** period-23 planted model, though it does not validate sensitivity to arbitrary nonadditive keys, irregular word order, or more aggressive postprocessors.

## Arbitrary repeating alphabets: a phase-collision sieve

The preceding equal-lag test was motivated by an **additive** period-23 key. A stronger invariant holds for a key that assigns each word-position phase a different, completely arbitrary *bijective* 29-rune alphabet. The alphabet may vary independently from section to section or from page to page, while repeating after k words within that scope. For a fixed period k, two word initials belonging to the same phase are passed through the same bijection, so the number of equal-initial pairs **inside each phase** is invariant. This requires neither additive nor linear keying.

The new program [word_start_phase_permutation.cjs](../sims/word_start_phase_permutation.cjs) computes each scope's phase-conditioned collision rate and compares its excess against full within-scope shuffles. It scans **all periods 2–60**, preserving each scope's exact initial-rune frequencies. One-sided familywise calibration repeats the complete max-over-periods selection on every null. The input is the same 2,896 whole words, with page-boundary-spanning words assigned to their physical starting page. Source code fixes all random seeds.

| Key reset scope | Best observed period | Observed / expected ordered same-phase equality pairs | Best standardized score | Period-scan familywise p |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sections; 600 shuffled books | 9 | 5,002 / 4,771.74 | +3.393 | **0.328** |
| Physical rune-bearing pages; 1,000 shuffled books | 53 | 32 / 17.70 | +3.459 | **0.378** |

At the previously conjectured period **23**, the section-continuous result is 1,700 / 1,806.64 equality pairs (Q=0.0326333, z=-2.554, upper-tail p=0.972), while the page-reset result is 144 / 150.25 (Q=0.0328767, z=-0.519, p=0.651). The apparent isolated best periods 9 and 53 are not significant after correcting for the 59-way scan, and the page-local period 53 contains only 516 eligible ordered comparisons.

**Model-specific power:** 100 synthetic manuscripts retained the page word counts, sampled independent plaintext initials from the 250-word reference, then applied independent arbitrary random permutation alphabets to each word phase and each page at period 23, followed by 5% random overwrites. Their period-23 phase-collision score had z mean **10.064**, minimum **5.777**, with all 100 greater than z=4. The actual page-local period-23 score is z=-0.519. This planted test is genuinely substitution-invariant, and broadens the exclusion beyond Caesar and affine alphabets. It does not cover nonbijective per-symbol encoding, keys that change between successive occurrences of the same phase, or a plaintext initial distribution already close to uniform.

## Implication for further decryption

The previously observed **exploratory 23/46 word-length** phenomenon (see [word-length work](word_length_order_20261110.md)) has **no accompanying period-23 same-initial-rune signal**. This argues against attributing that length phenomenon to a simple fixed period-23 additive word-initial cipher. It leaves open word-length transposition, independent symbol mixing, and state-dependent key schedules.

The promising next hypothesis should jointly predict the near-uniform initial-rune collision rate, disappearance of adjacent English-like word-length dependence, and known extreme anti-doublet suppression. A credible model must then decrypt held-out original ciphertext, not merely reproduce these marginal statistics.
