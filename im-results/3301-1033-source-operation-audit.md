# 3301/1033: source roles and LP2 transfer decision

**Decision (11 November 2026): no single source-authenticated operation uses both 3301 and 1033 to select a unique LP2 interval.** There are real, distinct uses of each value. The only signed numeric operation with a direct LP2 algebraic consequence is the 2013 BASE29 example on 3301, which yields the p50 H factor. The 1033 BASE29 control does not divide p50; the original 2014 page-05 same-artifact rune-word-to-number reconstruction has already failed its proposed LP2 row-key transfer. No plaintext or key is recovered.

This audit distinguishes (a) signed/authenticated message content, (b) original puzzle artifacts that are not PGP-signed, and (c) participant/archive reconstructions. “Player-visible” does not mean “signed.” The archive manifest [1033-3301-historical-operator-chain.json](../resources/external/1033-3301-historical-operator-chain.json) explicitly identifies itself as a secondary reconstruction; it is useful for locating leads, not for upgrading them into authenticated Cicada instructions.

## Frozen operation tests against LP2

### BASE29: 3301 succeeds as one factor; 1033 is a negative control

The signed 2013 example fixes the operation and operand for 3301: BASE29(3301) = 3QO, whose coefficient vector gives H(X) = 3 + 26X + 24X². The existing [GF(29) grid-factor reproduction](2013-base29-gf29-grid-factor.md) shows H divides the original p50 256-byte table exactly; the independently source-derived p39 red triad supplies G(X) = 27 + 5X + 15X², and p50 is divisible by both. This is an exact structural relation, not yet a reversible cipher or plaintext.

Applying the same BASE29 conversion to 1033 gives 16I, with coefficient vectors [1,6,18] and reversed [18,6,1]. The existing original-grid test gives nonzero remainders [3,11] and [24,3], respectively. Thus the same BASE29-to-polynomial operation does **not** carry both constants into the p50 factorization. The 1033 input is present in the unsigned participant test vector; it is a useful control, not a second signed operand.

### Seven RAND bytes from the preserved March 2013 TCP session: page-key gate

The same unsigned participant transcript that records `BASE29 1033` and `DH 3301` also records four `RAND` requests: `RAND 3 → [151,207,64]`, `RAND 3 → [91,172,47]`, `RAND 0 → []`, and `RAND 1 → [179]`. The flattened output is the exact seven-byte sequence `[151,207,64,91,172,47,179]`. The signed protocol authenticates the command's byte semantics, but not these particular participant-generated values; they are not an organizer-selected key.

The p33–39 cuneiform interval has seven source-aligned segments, `p33[91:]` followed by p34–p39, of lengths `[123,261,271,238,228,228,240]` (1,589 runes total). The count match makes a one-byte-per-segment key or a period-seven key a plausible bounded human-solve hypothesis. It is not, by itself, evidence that this particular participant transcript is part of LP2.

The reproducible gate tests:
- `byte mod 29` as one constant shift per segment and as a continuous period-seven key, both signs, with and without transparent-F handling;
- each byte's conventional BASE29 representation (`56, 74, 26, 34, 5R, 1I, 65`) concatenated into a 14-symbol key, both as a global period-14 key and as a two-symbol page-local key, both signs;
- the known p56 `φ(prime)` stream with each byte interpreted either as a one-based prime ordinal or as a prime value advanced to the next prime, page-local, both signs, with and without transparent-F handling.

None yields coherent GP29 plaintext. Across the frozen candidates, aggregate IoC is `0.034213–0.036076`, essentially the original interval's `0.034350`; the 50-rune previews remain random-looking. All no-F candidates pass exact forward/inverse round-trip controls. F-bypass variants are explicitly heuristic decryptions: without known plaintext-F positions, they are not claimed to be invertible, because a non-F plaintext can in principle encrypt to F. [Reproducer](../sims/lp2_p33_39_cuneiform_2013_rand7_key_gate.cjs) · [completed RAND vector](../resources/external/2013-tcp-live-test-vectors.json) · [original participant log](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/blob/ade9a02889ef8acbc8cde8edbfd0a785c369f4c3/irc-logs/clientlog-1362290293-9.txt).

**Decision:** retire the direct page-shift, period-key, BASE29-expanded-key, and page-reset `φ(prime)` consumers of these seven bytes. The 7-byte/7-page correspondence is not a usable key selector on the tested paths; do not extend it into arbitrary PRNG/KDF searches without independent evidence that this exact participant session is part of the intended LP2 solve path.

### Small-prime RSA cycle-walk on original p33–39, p55 and both p50 representations (12 November 2026)

An additional source-constrained, independently executable interpretation uses **1033 and 3301 as RSA primes**, giving \(N=3,409,933\) and \(\varphi(N)=3,405,600\), and takes **e=65,537** directly from Cicada's archived 4096-bit RSA public key. The reused LP1 p5 moth/primes/totient illustration is visible on both LP2 p53 and p55. Physical p55 has **76 printed runes = 19 four-rune blocks**, with \(29^4=707,281<N\). Ordinary RSA modulo \(N\) does not preserve the four-rune domain; a narrowly defined **format-preserving cycle-walk** using repeated \(x^e\bmod N\) and its inverse, repeated \(x^d\bmod N\) until the state falls below \(29^4\), supplies a genuine bijection with no guessed secret parameters. The inverse exponent is **d=3,398,273**. The test grants both ordinary big-endian and reversed within-block GP rune numerals.

The original p55 input yields **no GP-language text**. Printed-direction decoding starts `SLNHEATHOEOIPYXWFAETOEAN...` and has IoC **0.02877193** (cycle steps per block range 1–11); reversed within-block decoding starts `XTRMXDJEAAENGODWYAEANGY...` and has IoC **0.03368421** (range 1–16), versus p55 raw IoC **0.03789474** and an authentic solved-p57 76-rune segment's **0.05964912**. Both original candidates re-encrypt all **76/76** runes; encrypting and decoding the solved-p57 native-GP **76-rune plant recovers all 76/76**. These results are a **finite rejection of this precise historical-key RSA model**. Re-encryption only checks the bijection, not the correctness of an inferred plaintext. A source-visible objection is that p55's **first rune is the `NG` carry of p54's final printed word**, so source page-local four-rune packing lacks an independent human instruction; its true 75-rune body cannot be partitioned into complete four-rune blocks. The LP1 moth also recurs on p53, weakening unique p55 selection. Neither Cicada's signed material nor the book prescribes this RSA modulus, block width or cycle-walk. [Full source-asserting CJS test and exact plant](../sims/lp2_p33_primepair_rsa_cyclewalk_p55_sft.cjs).

The same source pair gives another deterministic RSA reading for the manuscript's **256-cell pp49–51 number table**: conditionally parse the conventional printed base60 byte values and split the source-order stream into **128 contiguous 16-bit unsigned words**, which fit below the 22-bit RSA modulus. Apply the identical RSA private exponent with cycle-walking below \(2^{16}\), in each standard byte order. Both readings exactly replay all **256 original bytes** and each independently recovers a planted **256-byte ASCII** source under the reverse operation. The original results are random-like: **88/256** and **93/256** printable ASCII bytes, entropies **7.09458** and **7.16823 bits per byte**, with leading hex `0f9c5cb47eafa53d` and `f98d65a102e7ef9f`, respectively. Neither provides recognizable plaintext, a typed payload, a source-identified key, or a verified hash preimage. The explicit **16-bit packing and format-preserving walk remain interpretive choices** without an original instruction. Both the p55 and full-grid branches are implemented in the same CJS test.

A third and stronger cross-source RSA consumer first divides the unchanged **256 original grid bytes reduced modulo 29** by the two source-linked quadratics **2013 signed BASE29(3301)→3QO** and the **p39 red triple 27,5,15**. Their combined ascending-coefficient factor is **[23,21,11,17,12]**, and the exact GF29 quotient has **252 rune values = 63 complete four-rune blocks**, making it eligible for the same format-preserving RSA inverse with \(N=1033\times3301\). The original quotient has IoC **0.03633087**. Its unmodified printed-order RSA inverse has IoC **0.03367482** and starts `RDSOXCEOEOXJIAEACPWU...`; its within-block reversed inverse has IoC **0.03370644** and starts `EORNGPCAFEOECIAIWTH...`. Both re-encrypt all **252/252** original quotient runes and recover an independent **252-rune solved-LP2 language plant** exactly. Reversing the entire coefficient sequence merely reverses the complementary within-block interpretation, so there is no independent fourth reading. Neither output is GP-language plaintext. This is an especially bounded **two-source numeric factor → small-prime RSA** negative that relies on no fitted key, phase or English crib; the missing premise is the unauthenticated **RSA/cycle-walk opcode**. The exact SFT links both original sources and the test inputs, and no dependent cipher layers are justified by this failure.

**Most direct source-target test:** the original visual chapter physical **pp33–39 actually displays 1033 and 3301** and has exactly **1,680 GP runes = 420 four-rune blocks**, so it offers a much more defensible complete target than the remote p55 moth or later number grid. With the unchanged source primes, historical \(e=65537\), \(d=3,398,273\), and the identical format-preserving RSA cycle-walk, the original printed-order chapter decrypts with **IoC 0.03496200**, starting `LEAFBUBCXOEHBOEOEHOAEMSTIAEL...`. Reversing the GP digits within each block yields **IoC 0.03429195**, starting `JCAHSNGXOGEATHSEOABBGORFGWLM...`. Both versions re-encrypt **all 1,680 original symbols** and recover an independent **1,680-rune native solved-LP2 planted passage** under the reverse operation. Neither reveals plausible GP prose, and these values lie near random GP29. Thus even the *numerals' own original chapter* provides no plaintext under this precise RSA interpretation. RSA and the cycle-walk remain speculative authorial operations despite the source-selected modulus and chapter.

### Literal 2013 BASE29 operand as exponent over the DH modulus: two-rune modular-power FPE gate

The March participant transcript records `BASE29 1033 → 16I` and `DH 3301`. A literal but unverified role assignment is `e=1033`, `n=3301`. Since 3301 is prime, `φ(n)=3300` and the inverse exponent is `d=1297` (`1033·1297 ≡ 1 mod 3300`). This is distinct from the preceding test that treated 1033 and 3301 as RSA prime factors with Cicada's historical `e=65537`.

The GP29 alphabet gives a natural two-rune domain of `29²=841` values; `29³=24389 > 3301`, so two is the largest fixed block width that fits below the modulus. The full physical p33–p39 chapter has exactly 1,680 runes = 840 pairs. (The narrower p33[91:] prime-echo interval has 1,589 runes and is not pair-aligned.) I therefore tested a cycle-walked modular-power permutation on the full seven-page chapter: repeatedly apply `x ↦ x^1033 mod 3301` until the value is below 841; invert with exponent 1297. This is a mathematically valid format-preserving permutation on all 841 pair values, but the source does not state that 1033 is an exponent or prescribe cycle-walking.

The exhaustive control confirms both exponent directions invert exactly on all 841 states (maximum 20 cycle-walk steps). The four fixed readings—printed big-endian or reversed little-endian pairs, and decryption exponent 1297 or 1033—produce IoCs `0.03494072`, `0.03438697`, `0.03450396`, and `0.03470036`, against ciphertext IoC `0.03436002`. Their previews remain random-looking; no plaintext is recovered. [Reproducer](../sims/lp2_cuneiform_1033_exponent_3301_modulus_fpe_sft.cjs) · [2013 transcript vector](../resources/external/2013-tcp-live-test-vectors.json).

**Decision:** retire this literal `e=1033,n=3301`, two-rune cycle-walked modular-power assignment. It is a bounded negative, not a claim that all modular operations or other source roles have been excluded. The exact operand roles and cycle-walk rule are not authenticated by the historical protocol.

## Human solve-path assessment and decision

A contemporary solver could reasonably notice the 2013 signed BASE29 worked example on 3301, the LP2 p50 numeric table, and the p39 red structure. That route has a concrete algebraic consequence (H/G divisibility), so it remains worth retaining as a structural lead. The same solver could also notice the 1033/3301 reversed-prime pair in the archived 2013 services and later LP2 cuneiform; however, the service grammar is not in the signed TCP challenge, the ordinal normalization is not source-selected here, and the pair does not specify a reversible LP2 operation.

The two values do not form one common key, modulus pair, recurrence, or row-key rule in the preserved sources. **Source-boundary and diagonal certificate for Director #459:** the signed January 2013 text contains the worked `BASE29 3301 → 3QO` and a separate **DH modulus 23** example, while the unsigned March participant transcript records `BASE29 1033 → 16I` and `DH 3301` (in which 3301 is the DH modulus). The original 2014 square's five row sums and five column sums are all **1033**; its *both* principal diagonal sums are **1033**, and the sum of all 25 cells is **5165**. It has **no diagonal sum 3301**. Therefore the assignment premise claiming an authenticated 2013 `BASE29 1033, DH 3301` pair plus 2014 square diagonal 3301 is factually false. No source-authenticated 2013→2014 protocol-to-square operation remains to freeze or invert. 3301's signed BASE29 route and 1033's 2014 prime-sum/magic-square role are distinct. The 1033 BASE29 control, the source-selected 553-rune row transfer, and the paired-value LCG/page-walk candidates all fail to produce plaintext. **Close the combined-constant hypothesis as unsupported; keep the 3301→H/p50 and p39→G relationships as an intermediate structural candidate, not a solved cipher.**
