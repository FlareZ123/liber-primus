As a separately source-defined control, the seven-rune heading supplies exactly seven successive ring bytes, original addresses `[47,55,63,71,79,87,95]` and tokens `1G 1K 0p 1m 1M 15 0Z`.

## Possible source-to-solved-text route

The two three-byte labels admit a striking **different original-solved-page** reading, with original pages and text independent of the p50 data:

- **`LP3` under base60:** In the original 2014 LP1 booklet, physical **03.jpg** is the independently solved `WELCOME` passage. It includes the sentence `LIKE THE INSTAR, IT IS ONLY THROUGH GOING WITHIN THAT WE MAY EMERGE`. Original solved p57 begins `LIKE THE INSTAR TUNNELING TO THE SURFACE` and ends with `... AND EMERGE`. The exact uncommon opening `LIKE THE INSTAR` links these two original text objects without deriving new plaintext from ciphertext. The `LP3` → LP1 scan03 interpretation and this textual overlap were already reported in [Director Issue #2242](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/2242).
- **`KO2` under f-deleted base59:** LP1 has **two** independently solved `A KOAN` sections, physical scans **06.jpg** and **14.jpg**. On original LP1 page14, the *second* koan says literally `THE I IS THE VOICE OF THE CIRCUMFERENCE`. Solved p57's *next line* says `WE MUST SHED OUR OWN CIRCUMFERENCES`. Thus `KO2` can be read as **KO(an) 2**, a recognizable shortened locator to the second LP1 koan. The source itself never prints `KO2` as an abbreviation of `A KOAN`. This designation and its target are conjectures requiring a third, independent corroboration.
  
The two source-named texts occur in **different places of LP1**, and match **consecutive themes** of the p57 PARABLE. The exact original witnesses and solved transcriptions are [LP1/p57 reference text](../resources/liber-primus/lp1-plaintext-reference.txt) and the first-party JPEGs `03.jpg`, `14.jpg`, `74.jpg`. The earlier cross-section art studies and LP1 key-transfer negatives remain intact. No new key is implied simply because the solved sentences share words.

## Decisive qualifications before a plaintext claim

The new `KO2` string is **arithmetically linked to the already discovered `LP3`**, not an independent second random hit: the f-deletion reduces each of these three base60 ASCII bytes by one. Given the `LP3` token triple and that particular radix change, `KO2` follows automatically. There is no legitimate probability multiplication of two ASCII hits, no second blind prediction, and no evidence of two separately embedded source messages.

Both `LP3` and `KO2` require an **inferred** origin, clockwise direction, 13-dot indexing and ASCII interpretation; neither source explicitly orders these operations. `KO2` is further ambiguous: two 2014 *koans* is an interpretive expansion of those characters. The base60→base59 change is hypothesized from `SHED` and the unused lowercase f, but **there is no first-party instruction to delete that digit**. A source *could* have intended some other numeral register.

This new pointer is promising **only if it changes which source text or which historically demonstrated cipher operation is selected**. To test a causal next step, inspect original LP1 page14's **actual demonstrated decipherment mechanism** and ask whether its `I`/circumference clue prescribes a full, fixed GP29 inverse that can predict at least 40 consecutive previously unknown LP2 runes. A source-fixed outcome with coherent novel plaintext and exact forward re-encryption would justify escalation. In its absence, **do not** reopen already-failed literal p3 running keys, DIVINITY columnar transforms, OutGuess `LP3lRA#`, arbitrary 29-state alphabet searches or speculative successive radix switches. A direct dual locator, if genuinely authored, may be guidance to known **instructions** rather than a decryption key by itself.
