
Here `pi(n)` is the number of primes at most `n`, and `prime(k)` is the one-based kth prime. The manuscript separately supplies **29 GP runes** and a **16-cell Fibonacci-prime grid** on original p15. This is a multi-source arithmetic alignment, but no original instruction says to interpret HTML title and element ID as additive operands, as a 464-prime corpus split, or as a 16-by-29 key-table specification. 3301 predates LP2 and the GP rune chart, so their design chronology does not by itself prove a common purpose.

There is a simple retrospective selection caveat: for three-digit positive reversed pairs `abc` and `cba`, the condition that their sum is 464 gives exactly `133/331`, `232/232`, or `331/133`. The observed pair is the only nonidentical unordered pair, but we observed the numbers before selecting the equality. This is **not** a statistical significance test on all conceivable HTML coincidences.
