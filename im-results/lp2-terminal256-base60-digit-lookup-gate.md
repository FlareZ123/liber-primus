## Strongest model: completely unrestricted 256-byte stationary secret codebook

Suppose each printed base-60 numeral represents a byte `B` and there is an arbitrary secret `lookup[B] ∈ GF(29)`. Crucially, all **256** lookup entries are unrestricted, independent symbols; no key schedule, language model or cryptanalytic optimizer is assumed. If the same printed byte occurs at two known-inner cells, a stationary decoder requires **exactly the same plaintext GP29 rune** at both. This equality-invariant test allows all `29^256` possible secret codebooks simultaneously without enumerating them.

For every one of **512** inner-ring placements, source-identical bytes demand conflicting known plaintext. The most favorable deterministic first-observation assignment has **44 contradicting subsequent known positions**, with **127 distinct printed byte values** and first mismatch at known-text offset **24**, for ring orientations **[7,5,4]**. Contradiction counts range **44–53** and the exact histogram is emitted by the test. These are online assignment-witness conflict counts, **not** minimum Hamming repair distances or probability estimates.

A separate 180-rune planted control generated by an unrestricted hidden 256-entry byte codebook passes **180/180** on the same printed source bytes and ring geometry. Therefore no stationary global byte→GP29 substitution codebook can exactly explain p56–57 in this terminal arrangement. This subsumes *all* stationary digit-split lookup variants, whether their combination is additive, multiplicative, nonlinear or arbitrary, because each printed byte fixes its two digits.

The separate p56 and p57 physical pages use different known encipherment mechanisms. A global common byte→GP29 lookup across all 180 symbols therefore risks excluding a gratuitously shared key. A sharper known-plaintext test removes this assumption: **each nested ring gets its own independent, completely unknown 256-entry GP29 codebook**. Each ring may begin at **every one** of its actual physical sites, in either winding direction. The hypotheses are tested independently rather than requiring any coupling across the three rings:

## Additional exact model: hidden additive per-digit codebooks

A more structured historical reading uses both printed sexagesimal digits:

`P[r,c] = f(floor(B[r,c]/60)) + g(B[r,c] mod 60) mod 29`

The `f` table independently has **5** arbitrary GP entries and `g` has **60**; thus **65 freely secret entries**. Each known cell supplies a modular difference between a high-digit vertex and a low-digit vertex. Weighted-potential union-find detects inconsistent cycles without guessing any key. All **512** arrangements fail. The best deterministic pivot-order witness contains **106 inconsistent dependent constraints** among 180 observations, **60** independent equations, first mismatch **24**, orientations **[7,5,2]**. Worst pivot-order count is 119. A separate independent 180-rune planted `f+g` control is recovered with zero contradictions. These pivot-order witnesses are not minimum repair budgets.

The secret-digit family is therefore doubly rejected, since it is a subset of the general static-byte-codebook family. The all-byte equality obstruction is the stronger primary finding and does not even require the numeric order or radix once identical printed numeral pairs are recognized.

## Decision and limits

Retire **stationary identical-byte→identical-rune decoding of each known inner ring**, even with independent keys for each of the three rings and unrestricted arbitrary perimeter start, under this conditional p55/p56/p57 ring alignment. This includes additive digit maps and arbitrary table-byte substitutions. Do not add fitted word-specific exceptions, new phase states or secretly chosen inner-ring reassignments to rescue a falsified stationary model. This is not a manuscript-wide ciphertext exclusion: p55's first rune continues p54's last word, the 76+180 visual match is conjectural, and there is no source-selected stationary mapping. Context-dependent or keyed maps, different page/ring pairing, hidden numeral interpretations and other ciphers are unaffected.
