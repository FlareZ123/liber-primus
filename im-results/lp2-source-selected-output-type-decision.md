# LP2 output-type decision: no source-selected type for the unknown pages

**Decision (Agent9, 25 November 2026; Director #2833):** none of the five candidate output types presently has all three required elements: (1) an independent LP2 clue selecting the type, (2) an exact serialization or grammar selected before reading the unknown output, and (3) an independent validation signal. This completes the bounded type-selection question with a negative decision. It does **not** establish that the unknown plaintext is non-prose, prove that a typed payload is impossible, or recover plaintext, a key, an inverse, or a destination.

## Scope and decision rule

The target is physical LP2 p0–55: 55 rune-bearing pages, 12,956 GP29 runes and 2,896 complete printed groups; p50 is the numeric-only page in this span. Known p56 and p57 are positive controls, not substitutes for unknown-page plaintext. The original synthesis records p56 as 85 runes under the prime-minus-one/F-hold solution, p57 as 95 clear GP29 runes, and p49–51 as a 32×8 panel of 256 two-character cells with 161 distinct values. See [the authoritative state](../results/README.md).

A candidate type survives only if its LP2 selector fixes the representation and boundaries, its reversible grammar can be written without guessed delimiters or post-hoc format choices, and it predicts an independent control or target observation. Historical use by Cicada is a prior for what is possible, not evidence that the same transport is selected in LP2.

## What the historical controls actually demonstrate

The earlier stages establish that Cicada used several *different*, explicitly typed outputs:

- The PGP-signed [2012 RSA-stage message](../resources/historical/cicada2012-message-only-displayed.asc) explicitly calls the challenge ciphertext a **number**, says each participant receives a unique message and key, and instructs the solver to submit the decrypted number back to the same URL. This is a clear numeric serialization and server-side acceptance path.
- The signed 2013 artifacts include [whitespace-bearing steganographic material](../resources/historical/cicada2013-gematria-jpeg-outguess.asc) and signed text carrying onion addresses, e.g. [the ICMP-echo stage message](../resources/historical/cicada2013-second-onion-icmp-echo.asc). The preserved [client transcript](../resources/historical/cicada2013-clientlog-1362290293-9.txt) records typed `BASE29`, `KOAN`, `DH`, and `NEXT` exchanges with status/data responses; it is a participant-side log, not a PGP-signed message.
- The archived [2014 Stage 10 form](../resources/historical/cicada2014-stage10-original-form.html) specifies three named magic-square fields, an onion address, an upload endpoint and a public-key path. The signed [2014 Onion 5 artifact](../resources/historical/cicada2014-onion5-signed.asc) carries JPEG bytes as hexadecimal text; the signed [Stage 11 message](../resources/historical/cicada2014-stage11-message.asc) returns an onion address.
- The signed [2016 tree message](../resources/historical/cicada2016-4gq25-outguess.asc) says Liber Primus is the way and that its words, meanings and numbers guide the route, then warns the solver to verify the OpenPGP identity. This is the strongest LP2-specific semantic instruction, but it selects navigation/authenticity—not a prose, instruction-language, BASE29, or binary serialization.

These controls prove that a human solver could reasonably consider several data types. They do not authorize choosing one by analogy. In particular, the public-mirror [2013 Wisdom/Folly binary note](../resources/historical/cicada2013-wisdom-folly.md) explicitly records that the binary files' function and LP2 role are unverified; it is not a positive key/transport control.

## Strongest typed positive control—and its limit

The exact 2013 `761.MP3` ID3 TXXX object is the strongest structured-payload precedent because it contains the Parable and a checksum-like product of its clause prime sums. Its original bytes, ID3v2.3 frame layout, and parser are already pinned; the existing decision recovers the known 88-rune p57 body and its cuts. This is a real typed text/checksum validation of **p57**. It does not contain a second unknown-page checksum, identify an unknown target, or specify a producer/consumer transform from LP2 ciphertext to that object. See [the completed 2013 structured-operand decision](lp2-2013-instar-structured-operand-decision.md). Do not rerun the MP3 parser or reinterpret its field offsets as a new key.

Likewise, the p56 digest is exactly 128 hexadecimal characters, but its hash algorithm is not named and the external page bytes are unavailable. Without a canonical byte serialization and a target, arbitrary hash/file-format searches have no discriminating test. The [signed-path antecedent gate](lp2-2016-signed-path-antecedent-gate.md) and [multilingual source gate](lp2-native-welsh-multilingual-source-gate.md) also find no independent first-party selector for a different language or external textual register.

## Decision and next research fork

No output type is currently justified for the 56 unknown pages. The evidence does not support the stronger claim that all unknown plaintext must be ordinary prose; nor does it support an instruction language, a BASE29 number stream, or a binary/file payload. The manuscript may also mix representations, as its rune pages and numeric p50 panel already demonstrate.

Do **not** run generic entropy, compression, file-signature, format, encoding, or language-model sweeps to choose a type after seeing the output. Reopen this question only when a new original LP2 clue fixes (a) the target type, (b) its exact serialization and boundaries, and (c) an independent validation signal. Until then, the causal next step is source-selected key/state recovery, not another output-format menu.

**Result:** #2833's completion criterion is met by the compact decision that no candidate type is presently justified. No new original-cipher computation was warranted by the available evidence; no unknown plaintext, key, inverse, or destination is claimed.
