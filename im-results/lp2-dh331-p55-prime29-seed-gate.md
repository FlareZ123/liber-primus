## Hypothesis

Since \(108=109-1\), and 109 is the 29th prime, a source-constrained receiver worth one fixed test was to start the already established p56 prime-predecessor cipher at prime ordinal 29 and apply it to p55.

This is a conditional bridge, not an authenticated DH interpretation: the upstream report explicitly notes that the proposed \(B=133\) is outside the subgroup generated by \(g=16\pmod{331}\), so this is not conventional two-party DH.

## Frozen test

- p55 contains 76 runes; its first source rune is GP29 index 21, transliteration `NG`.
- The 29th prime is 109, so the proposed first p56-style key is \(109-1=108\), or 21 modulo 29.
- The p56 inverse is subtractive and treats plaintext F (index 0) as transparent and non-consuming.
- The first p55 inverse would therefore be \((21-108)\bmod29=0\), forcing plaintext F at a ciphertext position whose observed ciphertext is NG (index 21).
- Under the p56 transparency rule, plaintext F must be transmitted as ciphertext F. The observed non-F symbol cannot be the encryption of F. There are consequently **zero legal inverse paths**, with the contradiction at the first rune.
