# DH output 108 as a p56 prime-totient cursor for p55 — bounded negative

## Hypothesis

The provisional p0/2014-HTML/p50 assembly in `im-results/lp2-dh331-red13-p50-two-anchor-original-gate.md` yields a one-sided DH output \(K=108\). Since \(108=109-1\), and 109 is the 29th prime, test one source-constrained receiver: start the already established p56 prime-predecessor cipher at prime ordinal 29 and apply it to p55.

This is a conditional bridge, not an authenticated DH interpretation: the upstream report notes that the proposed \(B=133\) is outside the subgroup generated by \(g=16\pmod{331}\), so this is not conventional two-party DH.

## Frozen tests

p55 has 76 runes. Its first rune is an incoming carry from the preceding page; the next 75 runes form the page's new body. The p56 inverse is subtractive and treats plaintext F (GP29 index 0) as transparent and non-consuming.

The proposed first key is \(109-1=108\), or 21 modulo 29. Two natural starts both fail:

1. **Reset at the first p55 rune:** \(C_0=21\) (NG), \(K_0=108\equiv21\pmod{29}\), so the inverse forces \(P_0=(21-108)\bmod29=0\) (F). But plaintext F must be transmitted as ciphertext F; the observed ciphertext is NG. No legal inverse state exists at the first rune.
2. **Skip the incoming carry and reset at the first complete word:** the first three body ciphertext indices are \([25,19,10]\). The first three keys are \([108,112,126]\), or \([21,25,10]\pmod{29}\). The inverse forces plaintext \([4,23,0]\) = [A, OE, F]. The third observed ciphertext is index 10 (I), not F, so the F-transparent rule again makes the inverse illegal. The dynamic inverse has one state before this third body rune and zero afterward.

## Decision and limits

Reject the frozen **prime-29 start** both at the page boundary and after skipping the single incoming carry. This does not reject the upstream numerical assembly, nor a page-continuous schedule whose prior cursor is independently specified. No alternative cursor or cipher direction was searched. No plaintext was recovered.
