# Arbitrary outer rune alphabets with a drifting key pointer

**Status (Agent 9, 10 November 2026): demonstrated arbitrary-alphabet and hidden-skip inversion components, including independently held-out shared-alphabet recovery across eight separately keyed sections. No Liber Primus plaintext or key has been recovered.** This exploratory work belongs in `im-results/`; it does not establish LP2's actual encryption mechanism.

## Mechanism and exact optimization

Consider a 29-rune ciphertext of the form `C_i = sigma((P_i + K_{j(i)}) mod 29)`, where `sigma` is any unknown permutation of the 29 rune symbols and `j(i)` is the key draw that produced observed ciphertext rune `i`. If the key positions are known and a plaintext unigram probability `q_i(p)` is available, the log likelihood for each hypothesized inverse cipher label `g(c) = sigma^-1(c)` is

```
score(g) = SUM_i log q_i((g(C_i) - K_{j(i)}) mod 29).
W[c,t] = SUM_{i:C_i=c} log q_i((t - K_{j(i)}) mod 29).
maximize SUM_c W[c,g(c)] subject to g being a permutation.
```

That is an **exact maximum-weight 29 x 29 bipartite assignment**, solved by the Hungarian algorithm in `O(29N + 29^3)`. It optimizes over **all 29! outer permutations**. The optimality theorem applies to this conditional unigram likelihood and a known key-position assignment, not to a joint search over unknown keys, skips, word grammar or cipher families.

`sims/outer_permutation_assignment.cjs` implements the assignment and independent regression tests. `sims/outer_permutation_word_context.cjs` conditions `q_i` on the preserved word length and the position of each rune in its word. The synthetic English-to-GP encoder is a documented approximation, calibrated using 193,920 separate Shakespeare rune symbols, not a claim about the missing LP2 text.

## Why the skip alignment matters

Under a proposed soft anti-doublet filter, a repeated ciphertext candidate may be rejected and an extra key draw consumed without advancing plaintext. An incorrectly aligned key silently collapses the alphabet-recovery signal. A 3,000-symbol filtered plant incurred 95 extra key draws; fitting a fixed `j(i)=i` recovered **0/29** correct rune labels, while supplying the exact draw positions recovered **29/29**. The analogous 1,500-rune word-conditioned plant recovered 29/29 with known alignment, but only 2/29 without alignment.

A strict one-retry Viterbi algorithm for **known** outer alphabet in `sims/outer_permutation_alignment.cjs` tests the deterministic condition `g(C_(i-1))-g(C_i) = K_(j-1)-K_j (mod 29)` at a rejected draw, and scores plaintext rune likelihoods along each path. On a 1,200-symbol filtered plant, the known-alphabet Viterbi inferred the correct key position at 1,114 positions, with the correct final key-pointer drift of 24. The result is a maximum-likelihood path for the specified model, not a decoding of LP2.

`sims/outer_permutation_context_alignment.cjs` alternates a word-conditioned Hungarian alphabet fit and a skip-aware Viterbi path. Its first illustrative plant had no skips until output position 220. Starting from only a 60-rune prefix, the initial alphabet fit recovered 13/29 labels; one alternating update recovered 29/29 and the same 1,114 aligned positions as the known-alphabet oracle. The unusually late first skip makes this particular plant favorable.

The **50-independent-plant** study in `sims/outer_permutation_context_power.cjs` used fresh random hidden alphabets and independent mod-29 key streams, the same 81% collision rejection, widths 60/100/160/300, and up to five alternating optimization updates. The initial prefix-only algorithm recovered the complete 29-symbol alphabet in **26/50** trials; the true pad outscored one independently drawn wrong pad in **41/50** trials, and median correct key-position alignment was **89.58%** (CI [42744668097](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42744668097)).

The improved algorithm enumerated the location of **one early rejected draw** at positions 1 through W-1 for prefixes W=60 or 100; it retained up to four distinct top-ranked alphabet assignments alongside the no-skip candidate and then ran the same alternating solver. This increased full-alphabet recovery to **37/50**, with the true pad ahead of one matched wrong pad in **47/50** trials; median key-position accuracy increased to **98.42%** and median true-minus-wrong score margin was **+2,367.83 log units** (CI [42745114710](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42745114710)). This is a useful measured improvement, though **13/50** planted keys still escaped complete alphabet recovery. The experiment compares each true key with just one independently sampled wrong key and **cannot** establish a family-wise detection threshold over thousands or millions of candidate secrets.

Reproduction: [`sims/test_outer_permutation.cjs`](../sims/test_outer_permutation.cjs), [`sims/outer_permutation_context_power.cjs`](../sims/outer_permutation_context_power.cjs), and opt-in [`outer-permutation.yml`](../.github/workflows/outer-permutation.yml). Synthetic tests use fixed public seeds and report all parameters. The planted tests validate a hypothetical English-like GP plaintext and one particular collision filter; they do not imply those assumptions hold for LP2.

## Actual LP2 input and bounded candidate scan

`sims/outer_permutation_sha_probe.cjs` parsed **12,956 runes** and **2,896 word units** from the shared LP2 transcription and tested 27 historically motivated password phrases under SHA256(seed||counter_BE) and SHA256(counter_LE||seed), both key signs, with an arbitrary learned outer alphabet. It examined the first 200 and 350 runes and compared each best-of-108 candidate with 4,000 randomized wrong-key scores.

- Window 200: best `2014-01-05`/suffix-BE/sign+, score -676.17; 90/4,000 nulls scored at least as high (raw tail p=0.0227, Bonferroni bound 1).
- Window 350: best `THE WORDS ARE THE MAP`/suffix-BE/sign-, score -1323.40; 60/4,000 nulls scored at least as high (raw p=0.01525, Bonferroni bound 1).

**Neither is a candidate key or decryption.** Crucially, the 350-rune plant with a genuine SHA key and 81% rejection-sampled extra key draws scored **p=0.467** against 1,000 wrong SHA keys, with only three correct rune labels. Thus this direct candidate screen has insufficient sensitivity to rule out skip-based cipher mechanisms. Its well-powered scope is narrower: with zero extra draws the same 350-rune plant recovered 29/29 symbols, beating all 1,000 wrong keys (p<0.001); rewriting ten repeat collisions *without consuming extra key draws* also recovered 29/29 and beat all 1,000 wrong keys (p<0.001). Actual LP2 supplied no signal for those bounded, aligned-key alternatives.

### Joint skip-and-alphabet scan on the real manuscript

The strengthened [`sims/outer_permutation_real_skip_scan.cjs`](../sims/outer_permutation_real_skip_scan.cjs) searched the first **1,200 genuine LP2 runes**. For each of the same 108 named-seed, digest-layout and sign combinations, it maximized over arbitrary outer rune permutations, 12 prefix-width and initial-skip-hypothesis groups, and up to five word-contextual Hungarian/Viterbi updates. The key remained one continuous SHA-256 counter-mode stream beginning at offset zero, with at most one rejected key draw per output rune in the strict filter. It then ran **250 independent random SHA counter seeds through the same full search**, so control scores include the benefit of alphabet and skip fitting.

The best named option was `THE KEY IS ALL AROUND YOU` with suffix-BE counter and positive sign: log score **-5284.147**, first-skip hypothesis at output position 3, inferred final drift 13. The random-key control median was **-5382.025**, and the **random maximum was -5279.289**, higher than the best named candidate. **Three of 250 controls** scored at least as high, giving the prespecified corrected empirical raw upper-tail **p=(3+1)/(250+1)=0.01594 per selected comparison**. The nominal Bonferroni bound over 108 named variants is **1**. The next-best `CICADA` candidate scored -5285.733. None constitutes credible plaintext or a recovered key.

A successful workflow is [run 42745382206](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42745382206). A null result over these 108 strings and two hash layouts is evidence only about this bounded construction and tested candidate list; it is **not** a general exclusion of SHA-256 keystreams, arbitrary alphabets or all key-skip processes.

## Shared global alphabet across eight differently keyed sections: genuine held-out test

The earlier single-window solver sometimes fails when its first hidden skipped draw occurs near the beginning. A distinct, potentially valuable hypothesis is that **different manuscript sections use different keystreams but share one fixed unknown outer rune permutation**. Such a shared mapping can accumulate evidence from short unskipped prefixes of *all* sections, even when the cipher pointer quickly drifts independently in each section.

[`sims/outer_permutation_pooled_sections.cjs`](../sims/outer_permutation_pooled_sections.cjs) implements a joint estimator. The eight input streams are the literal, ordered 32-byte columns of the locally canonical 256-byte base-60 grid (before a later independent five-cell correction), each interpreted as an AES-256-CTR key with all-zero 128-bit IV; key runes are negative ciphertext-pad bytes modulo 29. Each section consumes its own independent key pointer. Twenty initialization alphabets include joint 12/20/30/50-rune unskipped prefixes, plus per-section 60-rune zero/one-skip alternatives. The loose-skip forward/backward HMM computes exact posterior key-offset occupancies under its stated approximation: extra draw independently with probability 0.81/29. Their pooled 29×29 expected likelihood matrix is optimized by the Hungarian assignment, then the result is polished with the existing **strict collision-conditioned Viterbi** and pooled assignment. This forward/backward EM is exact for its stated *auxiliary* skip model; it is a heuristic initializer for the collision-conditioned source mechanism.

A six-plant independent benchmark with **eight separately encrypted 240-rune segments and one new random outer permutation per plant** recovered the complete inverse alphabet in **6/6 trials**. Cipher-key draw alignment ranged from **99.1% to 100%** across trials, using the actual eight grid-derived AES key streams as the known planted key family. This contrasts sharply with the isolated 240-rune planted failure reported in `sims/grid_column_unknown_sigma.cjs`, and establishes that pooling provides a viable joint-inversion path when the global-sigma and supplied-key assumptions are true. [Opt-in CI run](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42763016426).

An independent continuation test is implemented in [`sims/outer_permutation_pooled_holdout.cjs`](../sims/outer_permutation_pooled_holdout.cjs). It fits the shared alphabet and eight hidden key offsets using **only the first 240 runes in each section** (1,920 training runes), freezes the alphabet and each inferred pointer, and decodes the **next 240 runes per section** (a different 1,920 rune positions) using those fixed choices. Ten new English-GP/rejection-filtered plants with new independently randomized alphabets yielded:

- **10/10 exact 29-symbol alphabet recoveries** on the fitted prefix, each with its own random sigma;
- held-out plaintext recovery from **77.97% to 99.90%**, mean **93.22%** over all 10 trials;
- held-out GP-English per-rune log scores **−3.1934 to −2.5356**.

The real LP2 test uses the identical eight source keys, fixed section-to-column order, AES-CTR with zero IV, negative-byte-mod29 output map and independent section resets. Its fitted training score is **−4.6024 per rune**; the untouched continuation score is **−5.06964 per rune**, and the displayed rune previews are noise-like. Fifty matched runs replace each literal 32-byte grid column with an independently shuffled order of the *same* bytes while re-optimizing all eight sections and the unknown sigma; null held-out mean **−5.12798**, maximum **−4.97045**, and **7/50 outranked the observed real score** (corrected empirical upper-tail **p = 8/51 = 0.15686**). The fitted-window null upper-tail was **12/51 = 0.23529**. No evidence supports this **specific fixed-order source-key model** over its shuffled-key null, and no LP2 plaintext or true key has been recovered. [Successful held-out run](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42763325067).

**Corrected-source check (Agent 9, 10 November 2026).** The independently audited grid differs at **45, 50, 165, 172, 246** (zero-based row-major), changing four of the eight AES-256 keys. The opt-in corrected-grid mode of `sims/outer_permutation_unknown_column_assignment.cjs` reruns the same eight-section unknown-assignment/global-29-permutation/hidden-skip inverse, with the same frozen 180-rune original continuation. The **corrected original** fit score is **−4.512161/rune**; its held-out score is **−5.227647/rune**, worse than the earlier transcription's original held-out **−5.192346/rune**. Four new corrected-source GP-English plants all retrieve the exact **8/8 assignments** and **29/29 alphabet labels**, with held-out plaintext accuracy **85.4–94.7%**. This directly checks the four source keys previously affected by ambiguous case readings. **There is still no recovered original plaintext or key.** The tested overlay is described jointly with the direct-input results in [grid-section-column-keys.md](grid-section-column-keys.md), and [successful CI 42812635906](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42812635906) preserves the output. No new shuffled-key null was run for this corrected joint solver, so its standalone likelihood is not a calibrated p-value.

**Limits:** no search over unknown column assignment, other cipher modes, reverse-byte orders, IVs, non-English plaintext registers or alternate anti-repeat operations was performed in this pooled test. A 50-replicate shuffled-byte null is an explicitly bounded comparison. The excellent planted recovery establishes sensitivity under the exact proposed forward generator and a shared global sigma; it does not demonstrate that the original LP2 cipher has that architecture. Further finite-key expansions must provide an independently heldout alphabet/key match rather than promote the fitted score or target-selected plaintext fragments.

## Unknown section-to-grid-column assignment: joint 8×8 + 29×29 inference and a decisive held-out miss

[`sims/outer_permutation_unknown_column_assignment.cjs`](../sims/outer_permutation_unknown_column_assignment.cjs) generalizes the pooled-sigma solver by **treating which of the eight literal base-60 columns encrypts each of eight LP2 text sections as unknown**. Conditional on a proposed inverse sigma, it runs strict hidden-skip Viterbi for every section/key pairing, producing an **8×8 score matrix**. A Hungarian 8×8 assignment selects the best one-to-one column mapping. Conditional on that mapping and each section's inferred hidden key index, a second, globally optimal **29×29 Hungarian** step recovers the output rune permutation under independent word-position priors. Up to six alternating updates are initialized from 128 short pairwise alphabet fits and shortlisted by an 80-rune all-section assignment, with a 180-rune fit and independent 180-rune continuation per section. Conditional exactness of both assignment steps does not make this overall joint search globally optimal; it is a nonconvex alternating method.

In four **new planted** synthetic controls, the cipher used a fresh random outer permutation, independent collision-triggered key skips, eight literal AES256CTR key-column streams with zero IV and negative byte mod29, and a **random one-to-one section-to-column permutation**. All four runs recovered the exact **8/8 key-column assignments and 29/29 rune alphabet labels**, using *only the first 180 runes of each section* (1,440 training runes). On the held-out next 180 runes per section (another 1,440 runes), the recovered plaintext accuracy was **99.65%, 67.5%, 94.72%, and 99.65%**. The low control still demonstrates a valid alphabet and assignment but exhibits inferred key-pointer drift errors; absent a fully recovered drift path, strict word reading requires caution. Successful CI [42763847446](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42763847446).

With the original LP2 ciphertext, the best fitted source column mapping was **[6,2,3,0,1,7,4,5]** (zero-based grid columns), log likelihood **−4.504986/rune** on training, but **−5.192346/rune** on independently untouched continuation. The resulting rune sequence is noise-like. As a matched null, [`sims/outer_column_assignment_null.cjs`](../sims/outer_column_assignment_null.cjs) independently shuffles the 32-byte ordering within each of the eight original source grid columns, then repeats **all** 128 initial inverse-alphabet hypotheses, 8×8 assignment, 29×29 refinement and frozen 180-rune continuation for **60** synthetic source-key controls. A total of **49/60 controls outscored the original source grid on the held-out interval**, giving empirical upper-tail **p=(49+1)/(60+1)=0.81967**. The null heldout mean was **−5.13394/rune**, range **−5.30009 to −4.96631/rune**, and training-window upper-tail p was **22/61=0.36066**. [Successful matched-null CI](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/42764202291).

**Decision:** under the explicitly bounded fixed AES256CTR, zero-IV, negative mod29, global-sigma, independent-advance, one-to-one source column model, the real source-grid order has no observable predictive advantage after all nuisance parameters are fitted. Strong planted recovery makes this a sensitive test **for that source-key family**. It cannot exclude other deterministic IVs, hidden transformations of the grid, ChaCha, another source key, secret pre-processing of words or a different filter model. No original plaintext, exact recovered key, or authenticated destination has emerged, so do not promote this family or the fitted column mapping as a clue.

## What would materially advance decryption

The pooled eight-section experiments above have solved the **synthetic global-alphabet, multiple-independent-key and unknown-column-assignment** variants for their stated grid-key plants, though isolated early-skip starts remain unreliable. The next hard problem for the real manuscript is to derive candidate keys and section-to-key assignments from independent evidence, or infer additional information that constrains a unique decrypting architecture. Exact inversion of an unchanged original ciphertext interval and prediction on an unfitted interval remain mandatory. Before claiming a real-key hit, use a matched null for the full multi-hypothesis search, recover legible plaintext, and verify an unchanged key and alphabet on held-out pages. A valid breakthrough needs independent recovery across material not used for key or alphabet selection.

Agent 20 independently developed an exact phase-marginalized HMM for mod-29 arithmetic-ramp keys ([`sims/skip_phase_hmm.cjs`](../sims/skip_phase_hmm.cjs)). We coordinated to keep arbitrary **outer ciphertext alphabet** search distinct from arbitrary **key-state schedule** search. Both can benefit from position-conditioned language priors, and neither currently decodes the actual LP2 manuscript.
