# M7's 2,896-rune pole orbit as one key rune per original word

**Agent 10, 11 November 2026. Conditional source-led inverse, original-cipher negative with a reversible native-GP plant.** No new plaintext, authentic key or final destination.

## A concrete candidate suggested by the existing numerical correspondence

The independently documented [p32 M7 to p50 fragment ranking](p50-fragment-projective-rank-crosslink.md) uses the red M7 projectivity, the p50 grid's uniquely all-distinct second column, and chronological 13-dot intervals. Its seven-fragment pole orbit at rank 7 has exactly **2,896 runes**, the same as the canonical **2,896 complete words** in unknown LP2 pp0–55. The other five projective orbits sum to **10,240 runes = 40×256**, matching the size of the p50 table's full byte layout repeated 40 times.

That cross-artifact numerical equality suggests a candidate **one key rune for each printed word**. Unlike using the p50 table as a blunt repeating pad, the independently solved p56 totient-of-prime cipher supplies a variable within-word operation. For an individual source-derived key index k and within-word rune position j starting at zero, the two simplest explicit transfer rules are:

1. **Prime-index seed:** shift by \(\varphi(p_{k+j+1})=p_{k+j+1}-1\).
2. **Totient plus rune seed:** shift by \(\varphi(p_{j+1})+k=p_{j+1}-1+k\).

Here \(p_i\) is the i-th positive prime, and all rune shifts are reduced mod 29. The direction is chosen by the *known* solved-p56 inverse: subtract the shift. Two ways to read the 2,896 source key runes are the exact M7 orbit order from its pole and original chronological fragment order. These give **four frozen candidates** without searching phases, keys, numeric alphabets, orientation families or language-fit parameters.

**Weakest assumption:** the M7/p50 projective rank assignment and the resulting 2,896 correspondence are provisional and post hoc; no source clue states that the pole orbit must key LP2 words. A source key rune may actually be part of the ciphertext it decrypts, making a naive all-rune self-keyed transform non-invertible. The following model handles that issue explicitly.

## Lossless sidecar architecture and input contract

Preserve all **2,896 pole-orbit rune positions unchanged** as a transparent sidecar in the original full 13,136-rune book (which includes 180 solved pp56–57 runes). Of those, **2,891 sidecar positions** fall in the 12,956 unknown runes and five lie in the solved region. Thus exactly **10,065 unknown-page runes** are subject to the candidate totient transformation; 2,891 are intact source-visible key material. For original word i, apply the shift generated by its associated key rune to each **non-sidecar** rune at its ordinary original within-word offset j. The key positions stay unchanged. Decryption and encryption are both deterministic and mutually invertible, including when the key is extracted from the ciphertext itself, because the sidecar is held constant.

The [reproduction SFT](../sims/p50_m7_pole_word_seed_phi_sft.cjs) builds the projective rank map directly from original-scan transcription, the 31 thirteen-dot marks, 256 table values and M7 formula. It asserts the original seven fragment IDs **[6,2,23,24,18,5,29]**, the source-aligned 2,896 key positions, exactly 2,891 unknown sidecar runes, 10,065 remaining unknown ciphertext runes and 2,896 complete words. It compares the unknown payload alone against a GP English trigram model trained on the shared Gibran control; the sidecar is excluded from both decoded and raw language scores.

The projective cycle starts at pole rank 7 and ends at fragment #29, comprising the **five original runes of the solved p56 AN END heading**. The last-five-word test does not exclude a different p56 source-selected mode, but supplies an independent direct original-cipher consequence that the present architecture fails.

## Research decision

The M7 pole orbit's numerical match with the 2,896 original words does **not**, under the two source-typical prime-seed rules, define the missing plaintext decoder. This finite result rules out only the four stated word-aligned φ-prime sidecar inverses, with the original visible key runes retained as ciphertext-sidecar. A scheme that moves key positions, chooses an unknown word-key alignment, introduces nonlinear state or ignores the source's order remains logically possible, but those additions have no independent selector here.

This test complements the earlier [direct period-256 p50 additive-pad negative](p50-fragment-projective-rank-crosslink.md#direct-256-byte-additive-pad-test--negative) and the [p15 181×16 word-order negative](lp2-p15-spiral-181word-transposition.md). The 2,896 equality itself remains a retrospective arithmetic observation, not an authenticated cryptographic operation.
