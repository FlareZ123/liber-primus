# Original solved p56 SHA-512 digest as a direct GP29 key for encrypted p55

**Classification: source-fixed plaintext-producing transfer, negative on original ciphertext.** The original solved p56 text says that pilgrims should locate the deep-web page whose 128-hex-digit SHA-512 digest is printed. This supplies literal source-derived key material adjacent to the preceding **76-rune unsolved p55**, whose printed enclosure includes one incoming one-rune fragment and **16 complete words**. The original 2013 signed Cicada TCP challenge also taught BASE29 conversion. The source itself does not instruct using the hash as a cipher key; that is the weakest hypothesis.

We tested four fixed and physically natural GP29 additive key registers on p55, with the key clock beginning at the first original p55 rune: **64 digest bytes mod29** (cycled to cover 76 symbols), **128 printed hex nibbles**, **105 high-first base29 digits** of the complete 512-bit integer, and the same 105 digits low-first. Both additive signs are inverted, totaling eight exact original-cipher candidates. No offset, extra key, reset, word-fitting or new hash function is introduced. The original digest begins 36367763ab73783c and ends 9fb4f45132c2a8b4.

All eight full 76-rune output indexes of coincidence are between **0.0291228 and 0.0452632**. Crucially, none has even one complete four-rune-or-longer word in the repo's fixed **29,060-form GP29-normalized TextBlob English lexicon** under the **original preserved word boundaries**. The total native dictionary matches are **[1,1,2,0,2,2,1,0]**, entirely short forms. Low-first-base29 addition raises IoC to 0.0452632 but has zero dictionary matches and no recognizable prose: a single marginal concentration value is not evidence of correct decoding.

**Independent native-language coverage check:** The same unmodified lexicon, GP token table and printed word parser recognize **16/20** complete words on the real clear p57, including **8/12** original words with at least four GP runes. The four unmatched long p57 forms include PARABLE, INSTAR, TUNNELNG and CIRCUMFERENCES; native ING/IO variants and rare proper nouns limit English-dictionary coverage. Thus the zero-long-match p55 observation is evidence against these exact decoders, with a measured lexicon sensitivity limitation rather than a universal impossibility theorem.

Each of eight inverses exact-forward-reencrypts the untouched original p55 ciphertext. A native-dictionary positive, assembled into the **exact original 17 printed source word lengths**, is independently masked under every digest scheme. All eight original inverses then recover **76/76 planted GP runes** and **16/16 complete English words**. This validates that the fixed words/clock can reveal plaintext if the hypothesis is true.

**Decision:** the p56 printed digest does **not** directly decode p55 into native English GP29 under these four no-tuning representations and both additive signs. The digest remains an original, explicit *target fingerprint*; a historical original key-derivation instruction is missing. This finite exclusion does not cover hash preimage recovery, extra cryptographic layers, unknown salt, or a differently keyed cipher.
