# P8 Pythagorean/Berggren-tree proposal: original-source selector gate

**Decision (22 November 2026, Agent 2; Director #2297; cross-span transfer updated by Agent45, #2301):** The 243-black-rune count makes a depth-five ternary-tree model arithmetically possible, but **no original illustration or authenticated instruction selects one 243-rune value sequence or a reversible cipher operation**. The proposed 3–4–5 roots depend on taking a five-rune *page fragment* from p9; the authentic six-rune complete word crossing p8→p9 instead has GP29 product **2**. This does not disprove an intentionally segmented 3–4–5 mnemonic, but it makes the segment-extraction rule an extra, unprovided instruction. No unknown LP2 plaintext, authentic key, or historical inverse is established.

## Exact original operands, with scope

The authoritative page mapping is resources/liber-primus/README.md: physical LP2 p8 is original 25.jpg, p9 is 26.jpg, p10 is 27.jpg. The source registers are resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json, lp2-transcription.txt, and lp2-ink-annotations.json.

- P8 prints 255 GP29 runes. Its red 12-rune heading contains **two complete printed words** of 4 and 8 runes: GP0 [14,10,14,19] and [1,14,19,5,2,13,3,17]. Their products modulo29 are respectively **4** and **3**. The 243 black runes include the last black p8 rune **EO** (GP0=12).
- The original page boundary is the printed tail ᛇ/ followed by a page separator and ᚣᚹᚫᚷᛄ. There is **no word separator** between the last p8 rune and the five p9 runes. The five p9-only runes have GP0 [26,7,25,6,11], product **5**. The authentic *whole six-rune word* has GP0 [12,26,7,25,6,11], product **2**. These are two distinguishable operands and must not be conflated.
- Another exact link exists: 4×3 mod29 = 12, the carried rune's GP0. Because the inverse of 12 is 17 modulo29, 2×17 mod29=5. Thus the 3–4–5 reading is equivalent to dividing the complete-word product2 by the carried GP12, an operation not explicitly stated by the original printing. A solver must choose fragment extraction over the printed whole-word grammar. **Relevant independent numeric echo:** the original last complete red word on preceding p7 is ᚷᛠᛗ, GP0 [6,28,19], whose product is **2**. The authentic six-rune p8→p9 word product2 therefore also matches the p7 terminal red-word product2 and the *already-known* 2013 DH shared K2; the separated page9 fragment product5 instead resembles the historical DH generator g5. This is a potentially useful source-local pair of interpretations, reported nonclaimingly in [#2267](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/2267). Both matches are retrospective; neither alone selects a decryption operation or establishes which reading the creator intended.
- At depth five, each ternary Berggren-tree parent has three primitive Pythagorean children, so 3⁵=243, matching the source black-rune count. This is a **matching count**, not a decipherment. The red heading count12 and carried GP12 also match the triangle's perimeter 3+4+5=12 after this choice of reading.

The historical Berggren/Barning/Hall matrices on (3,4,5) produce the first-level triples (5,12,13), (21,20,29), (15,8,17). Even granting this unprinted program, **the manuscript does not place the three matrix operations into one uniquely readable child order**. All six possible global orders produce 243 valid, distinct depth-five triples, with six **different** ordered 243-symbol streams when their hypotenuses are reduced modulo29. This is an explicit branch-order ambiguity, independent of language scores. Choosing either leg, perimeter, full-triple serialization, forward/reverse order, or feedback adds further options. The illustration specifies none of them.

## Additional p8 original-red algebra: a source-visible reciprocal but no ciphertext receiver

A newly noticed exact relationship sharpens the **non-tree** candidate source operation and deserves to be separated from the Berggren claim. The 12 original red GP0 indices split at the two **genuine printed word cuts** as [14,10,14,19] | [1,14,19,5,2,13,3,17]. The first entire word sums to **57**; the *first seven indices* of the second eight-rune word also sum to **57**. Its final original red glyph **B** has GP0 **17**, which is the unique modular inverse of the full red-header product **12** modulo29: 12×17≡1. The complete red-header product12 equals its 12-rune length and the carried final p8 GP0=12.

The reciprocal has an exact arithmetic continuation across the **true printed six-rune word**: its original GP29 product is **2**, and multiplying by the red-terminal inverse17 gives **5**, precisely the GP product of the five-rune p9 page fragment. The preceding p7 last red complete word separately has product **2**. Thus the local source contains the numeric tuple header product12; inverse17; complete crossing word2; page9-only residue5. This is a **reproducible source-local operand relation**, potentially more relevant to a human solver than extrapolating a mathematical Pythagorean tree from naturalistic artwork. It is not an independently chosen GP29 cipher/key/output map: the 7+1 partition of the printed eight-rune red word, the choice of sums versus products, and interpreting a page-fragment quotient as an instruction are **retrospective**.

One bounded specificity calculation holds the actual 12 original red glyph occurrences and exact 4|8 printed word lengths fixed. Among the 12×C(11,4)=3,960 uniformly labelled placements into first four, next seven and final one position, exactly **15** have both a final reciprocal GP17 and the two matching partial sums modulo29. The conditional exact rate is **15/3960 = 0.0037878788**. This is a conditional glyph-shuffle rate with a selected arithmetic predicate, **not** an author-intent p-value or a familywise significance estimate. The committed SFT derives these 15 cases without ciphertext tuning.

**Actionable next decision:** Seek an independent first-party instruction for whether the modular reciprocal17 acts on GP29 ciphertext values or page fragments and exactly how that operation produces a *new source-selected plaintext receiver*. The reciprocal exists, but a full reversible decryption requires a value-changing operation, clock/reset rule, and genuine unknown-page linguistic output. Generic GP29 multiplicative scaling preserves glyph-frequency flatness and does not suffice. Avoid promoting this numeric circuit to plaintext or recombining it speculatively with already-failed tree pads.

## Manuscript-wide red-terminal reciprocal control and independent carry transfer (Agent45, 22 November)

The p8 inverse17 is not isolated in the original ink register. Applying one **fixed** predicate to every full red-rune span on the 58 physical pages, namely \(P=\\prod_i r_i\\pmod{29}\), \(P\\ne0\), and \(r_{last}=P^{-1}\\pmod{29}\), yields exact hits **p7 red closing 14-rune span, p8 red opening 12-rune span, and p27 red opening 19-rune span**:

| Printed red span | Complete-word GP0 products | Whole-span product P | Last rune GP0 | P × last mod 29 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| p7, offset 194, 14 runes | 11, 26, 8, 15, 2 | 26 | 19 | 1 |
| p8, offset 0, 12 runes | 4, 3 | 12 | 17 | 1 |
| p27, offset 0, 19 runes | 13, 14, 12 | 9 | 13 | 1 |

There are **18** annotated red spans in p0–p57, **11** with nonzero GP0 product and only **four** whose *inventory* even contains their product's inverse (p7, p8, p27, p56). The solved p56 red five-rune span has product7 and index25 somewhere in its span but ends in index4: it **does not** satisfy the rule. Under independent random reordering of glyphs *within each original red span*, leaving every span's original length, inventory and product fixed, the exact distribution of the number of reciprocal-terminal successes has expectation **0.4600251** and tail Pr(three or more) **0.00469925**. Eligible final-position probabilities are p7 1/14, p8 1/12, p27 2/19, p56 1/5. This *selected-predicate conditional permutation diagnostic* is **not** a multiple-hypothesis-corrected chance of creator intent. It does justify preserving the three-locus relationship as a candidate source-visible register, rather than presenting p8 as a uniquely reciprocal red heading.

**A narrower exact mathematical reading of p8:** Its red whole-word GP0 products **4** and **3** multiply to **12**. The actual terminal red B17 is **17=−12 mod29**, and both **12²≡17²≡−1 (mod29)**. Thus they are the **two** square roots of −1 in the published 29-symbol field. The original p8 carried final black rune is also GP0 **12**; the p7 final red complete word and p8→p9 complete word both have product **2**, while the p9-only continuation has product **5**, giving **12×5≡2 (mod29)**. This is the explicit identity behind **29=2²+5²**, a Gaussian-integer/sum-of-two-squares explanation of why 12 and17 are inverses. It is **not** a distinct new decipherment, and the 5 remains a page fragment rather than a printed word. The superficially attractive 3–4–5 from the two *whole* p8 red products and the p9 *fragment* is an alternative retrospective interpretation, not an authored instruction.

A strictly frozen, reversible candidate directly suggested by the order-four root of −1 is the four-phase multiplier \(P_j=C_j\\,12^j\\bmod29\) (or its inverse \(12^{-j}\)), with phase0 at the beginning of an intact original target. On the full p8 black 243 runes, p9 268 runes and p27 black 215 runes, **forward** four-cycle original ciphertext IoC29 values are respectively **0.03435, 0.03410, 0.03604**; **reverse** values are **0.03554, 0.03511, 0.03595**. The fully legible solved p57 original GP text has IoC29 **0.06271**. Neither decoded p8/p9/p27 stream begins with GP-readable English. This bounded failure retires this *direct* i-rotation receiver under the stated zero phase; it says nothing about a separately authenticated nonlinear state machine, different keyed phase, or arithmetic used only for navigation. A cycle of a given order can also be inferred from any four-phase multiplier, so its discovery is not a unique creator-selected operation.

The independent **original page-break receiver** does **not** carry that candidate into a nontrivial instruction. Of 32 genuine complete words spanning unsolved physical-page boundaries, p8→p9 is [12,26,7,25,6,11], product2, first rune12, remaining page9 fragment product5. Its identity \(2\\times 12^{-1}\\equiv5\\pmod{29}\) holds *by definition* for any such word with nonzero first-rune factor. P27's genuine p27→p28 crossing word [9,13,0,13,5] has product **zero** and a zero-containing receiver; the red reciprocal13 can only yield zero, not a GP29-invertible new state or an independently legible word. P7's closing red span ends in a printed period and is **not** a p7→p8 carry. Thus the two other reciprocal loci do **not** independently predict the same p8→p9 quotient operation as a useful cipher instruction.

The printed first-party markup distinguishes opening red heading (p8/p27), closing red stanza (p7), intact complete words, page-only fragments, and actual punctuation. Neither the 2013 DH example nor the manuscript provides a common instruction to multiply the **last glyph of a red heading** by the **whole heading product**, then choose one of the non-equivalent following word/fragment operands as GP29 ciphertext. A fixed nonzero multiplication on a 243-rune receiver is a value permutation and cannot by itself alter the original source's GP29 index of coincidence. As a proposed *direct stand-alone decipherment*, the reciprocal model currently has no independently selected state clock, output law, or recovered plaintext. Reopen only for a first-party rule predicting a fresh intact original receiver and a substantive decryption; otherwise regard p8→p9 division as cancellation, and keep the three red coincidences as **exploratory**, not a key.

## Exact original-cipher obstruction to direct 243-leaf symbol readout

The ambiguity can be narrowed further **without a guessed GP29 alphabet or reading order**. A direct reading of one Berggren numeric leaf coordinate as one p8 black rune, even if both 29-symbol alphabets are related by an **arbitrary secret 29! permutation**, must preserve the sorted 29-class symbol-frequency histogram. Permuting all 243 leaf positions, reversing the traversal, or choosing any of the six globally ordered Berggren child permutations does not change this condition.

For the **exact original 243 p8 black runes** (including the final carried EO/GP12), sorted frequency bins begin **[15,14,12,12,11,11,10,10,...]**. The fully enumerated standard depth-five primitive Berggren leaves have these first sorted-bin counts when the indicated numeric output is reduced modulo29:

| Leaf output | First sorted GP29 frequency bins | Direct original p8 readout under any secret fixed rune alphabet? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First leg a | [20,11,11,11,11,10,...] | Impossible |
| Second leg b | [13,13,13,11,11,10,...] | Impossible |
| Hypotenuse c | [15,15,12,12,11,10,...] | Impossible |
| Perimeter a+b+c | [19,14,12,12,11,11,...] | Impossible |

The exhaustive 29-bin comparisons fail for every candidate. For the closest hypotenuse candidate, two bins each contain 15 leaves while the original has only one 15-count bin and one 14-count bin. **No secret substitution, stem-side order, global branch ordering, or permutation of all243 positions can repair this exact multiset mismatch.** This is stronger than having to guess which of six orders was intended and directly excludes treating those simple leaf quantities as the original p8 ciphertext under a single value-labeling alphabet. It does **not** exclude using the leaves as an evolving **key**, applying an additional value-changing cipher after leaf generation, choosing a different coordinate function, or encoding a 243-rune intermediate that need not equal the p8 black glyphs. Those possibilities still lack an authored selector.

The original-source SFT now asserts all four no-monoalphabetic-inverse gates as strict inequalities, alongside the whole-word and branch-order checks.

## Original image and historical-operation decision

Original p8 25.jpg and p10 27.jpg show the same irregular, naturalistic botanical stem reused beside the prose, rather than a five-tier regular ternary graph with 243 individually displayed leaves. In 25.jpg a large red initial ᛉ/X resembles a three-pronged fork, but is also the first canonical red rune (GP0=14), not a separately drawn cipher operator. There are no matrix labels, distinct leaf numbers, branch reading arrows, or marked depth-five paths. This comparison agrees with the independently established repeated stem and page-parity evidence in [closed #2294](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/2294) and its [bitmap report](../im-results/lp2-p8-p14-stem-side-parity-source-gate.md). The artwork could still allude to trees, but it does not physically encode a uniquely ordered Berggren key.

Prior [#2267 source decisions](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/2267) already tested direct Berggren hypotenuse/perimeter additive keys on untouched p8/p9–14, along with strict transparent-F and related feedback receivers; none recovered plaintext. Do **not** rerun or tune these rejected families using free branch, phase, sign, orientation, output or reset choices. The p8→p9 arithmetic also retrospectively echoes an already-known 2013 DH tuple, but supplies no original GP29 key schedule.

Any future ternary-tree cipher proposal needs a first-party visible instruction fixing **all** of: (1) five-rune fragment rather than six-rune whole word, (2) ordered child operations, (3) exact GP29-changing output and key/state law, (4) genuine p8→p9 carry preservation, and (5) one independently untouched original receiver yielding continuous linguistic plaintext. Reencryption alone cannot distinguish wrong bijective keys.
