## First decision: what could the five numbers mean?

**A. Five *successive complete intermediate states* of one fixed-length GP29 message.** This is impossible under every composition of the historically demonstrated length-preserving GP29 operations (Atbash, Caesar, fixed or keyed rune substitution/Vigenère, prime/totient, and bare rune transposition): the complete successive output states have **different rune counts** 139,91,98,96,129. Every length-preserving bijection/ordinary transposition conserves the number of GP runes. One could posit deletions, insertions, variable-width encoding, or five unrelated messages, but none is selected by these red numerals or the unchanged cuneiform header. This is a structural exclusion of the *literal-stage-output* model.

**B. Five independent ciphertext paragraphs, each using the 1–5 label to select a separate stationary substitution.** For each 29-symbol letter permutation, even if its entire alphabet is secret, the exact equality-multiplicity (and hence IoC) in that paragraph is invariant. The five low original IoCs above are therefore incompatible with fixed-bijection decoding into ordinary native solved-LP1 GP prose. The record also includes a complete positive with a secretly permuted 29-symbol alphabet and proves its exact IoC invariance. This strengthens earlier tests of five individually named historic substitutions, without trying a new set of unsourced offsets.

**C. Five procedural *instructions* selecting operations for another message.** This remains possible in principle, but every item has 91–139 undeciphered GP runes. In a true human-executable program, a solver would first need a specified first-item decoder or an independently readable instruction. The original printed red numbers encode stage order; they do not identify names like Atbash, DH, BASE29, XOR, OutGuess or a historical cryptographic tool. Genuine old stages used typed inputs and output checks: original OutGuess takes a JPEG; OpenPuff takes an MP3 and passwords; signed BASE29 takes a numeral; Stage04 Vigenère took a specifically clued prior plaintext key. Bare red 1..5 cannot substitute for such parameters or change an image/byte operation into a GP29 operation. The repeated cuneiform [17,13]/[55,1] supplies numeric 1033/3301 but no instruction choosing one of these consumers. Prior #985 and #1044 already closed the untyped numeral-to-cipher transfer and five historical static-key attempts; these are *inherited*, not re-executed.
