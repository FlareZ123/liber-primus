# Original LP2 256-cell grid: exact AES expanded-key and round-state gate

**Decision (12 November 2026, Agent6):** Under the explicitly conditional base60-byte interpretation and physical page-major 32×8 reading of the original pp49–51 grid, **no contiguous complete standard AES-128, AES-192 or AES-256 expanded key, and no four-state sequence consistent with one standard AES round-key schedule, occurs**. All source-input and positive-control assertions passed in [manual GitHub Actions run 43162504439](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43162504439). This retires these two precise byte-format consumers; it supplies **no LP2 plaintext, key, decipherment operation or final destination**.

## Source, motivating premise and scope

Original physical pages **49–51**, scans `resources/liber-primus/66.jpg`, `67.jpg`, `68.jpg`, print **10+13+9 rows of eight two-character symbols**. The original-image-adjudicated row-major tokens and conditional digit alphabet `0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwx` are stored in [source-first table](../resources/liber-primus/base60-grid.json). Their byte interpretation is `60*digit(left)+digit(right)` and yields 256 bytes, SHA-256 `3b9b07d9a26e6d55c432d94d2661fdff3c2b348daed06821f2bdb23184a4b290`. The text-to-byte conversion and all 256 original table byte entries are verified by the reproducer. The 60-symbol alphabet and even the byte interpretation **remain unproven as authorial instructions**; base59 also parses the printed cells. Source order is forward physical page/row/column order. A reverse-stream test has no original-image selector and is intentionally outside this decision.

Why test AES? A 256-byte serialized table could contain a known-algorithm key schedule or 16 intermediate 16-byte AES round states. **AES is a hypothesis**, not an independently authenticated LP2 consumer. The test is motivated by standard cryptographic structure, not by any claim that the artwork explicitly names AES. The relevant original standard is [NIST FIPS 197, §5.2 key expansion and §5.1 cipher](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.FIPS.197-upd1).

## Complete exact expanded-key windows

For `Nk = 4,6,8` (AES-128/192/256), enumerate every consecutive forward window long enough to hold the **complete** FIPS 197 schedule of 44,52,60 four-byte words, respectively. Treat its first `4*Nk` bytes as the candidate raw key and compute all subsequent words using `RotWord`, `SubWord`, `Rcon`, and the AES-256 middle-word S-box case. Demand byte-for-byte equality of the full window.

| Format | Required bytes | All offsets | Exact full-window matches | Most matching bytes / required | Best offset | Longest matching prefix |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| AES-128 | 176 | 81 | **0** | 19/176 | 57 | 16 |
| AES-192 | 208 | 49 | **0** | 26/208 | 9 | 25 |
| AES-256 | 240 | 17 | **0** | 36/240 | 9 | 32 |

The AES-128 and AES-256 longest prefixes correspond exactly to their seed key bytes. The AES-192 25-byte prefix includes a single accidental matching generated byte. This is an exact exhaustive finite exclusion of the stated serialization, rather than a significance-based language test.

## Original four-state / three-round-key consistency

A stronger second necessary condition asks whether any **four consecutive 16-byte state blocks** are consecutive AES *post-round* states, with ordinary FIPS `SubBytes → ShiftRows → MixColumns → AddRoundKey` for internal rounds and omission of MixColumns at the final round. For each pair of observed states and candidate absolute AES round number, the demanded round-key bytes are uniquely recovered by XOR with the independently computed unkeyed round transformation. Three adjacent demanded round keys form **12 consecutive schedule words**, with at least 8, 6 or 4 exact recurrence checks for AES-128, 192 or 256. Each candidate must satisfy every mandatory byte equation for one internally consistent FIPS expanded key.

In addition to physically aligned 16-byte states (two printed rows), the script tests every shifted 16-byte partition **0–15** as an explicitly broader diagnostic, all available block starts and each allowable three-round phase. It does *not* fit independent round keys or vary the source data.

| Format | Four-state / three-key candidates | Exact consistent sequences | Minimum contradictory recurrence bytes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| AES-128 | 1,544 | **0** | 30 |
| AES-192 | 1,930 | **0** | 22 |
| AES-256 | 2,316 | **0** | 14 |

This rejects only contiguous *standard post-round state* serialization. It does not exclude a table of independent AES blocks, AES-encrypted data, an unspecified secret serialization, an unconventional round order, or use of a separate key not displayed in the table.

## Complete reproducibility and positive controls

[Single runnable CommonJS test](../sims/lp2_grid_aes_schedule_round_state_sft.cjs) computes the S-box from the GF(2^8) multiplicative inverse and the standard affine transform, implements all three key expansions, standard AES round transforms and exact schedule equation checks. Every NIST `00112233445566778899aabbccddeeff` ECB vector was independently checked against Node's OpenSSL AES implementation and matched its published full ciphertext: AES-128 `69c4e0d86a7b0430d8cdb78070b4c55a`, AES-192 `dda97ca4864cdfe06eaf70a0ec0d7191`, AES-256 `8ea2b7ca516745bfeafc49904b496089`. Correct independent key-schedule prefixes, exact derived key bytes and deliberately embedded full-key-schedule and four-state traces were detected under **all three** formats.

The manually dispatched [workflow](../.github/workflows/lp2-grid-aes-schedule.yml) runs Node 22, with no push or pull-request trigger. CI run [43162504439](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43162504439) completed successfully and prints every original score and all positive gates.

## Research decision

No AES standard key, original plaintext or decipherment consumer was recovered. This exhausts only **AES expanded-key and consecutive AES post-round-state readings of the conditional forward base60 stream**. Further AES experiments require a separately sourced original instruction selecting an alternative serialization or AES role. The 256-cell object remains a genuine source artifact with unresolved cryptographic use; seek a different authentic operation/key selection rather than a tunable new AES hypothesis.
