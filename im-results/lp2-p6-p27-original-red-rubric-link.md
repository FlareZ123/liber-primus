One simple human-visible *navigation* rule is to treat the uniquely isolated **M** as a request to find a new chapter whose first red rune is **M**. Among the nine source-visible unknown-page chapter starts **p0, p3, p8, p15, p23, p27, p33, p40, p54**, their first original runes spell **S L X F U M D F A**. Thus **exactly one** start matches M: physical **p27**, the page immediately preceding the independently analyzed p28 DH construction. The p27 original scan **44.jpg** indeed shows an **oversized red M**, then a 19-rune red rubric `[3,12,4]` followed by a red 13-dot mark. The red heading's **length19** also equals the GP index of M and the public DH value `B=19`. **Its full 19-rune sum is exactly 305 ≡15 (mod29)**, which matches the independently known DH private exponent `b=15`. Thus this single rubric can retrospectively be read as a two-field `(B=19,b=15)` state checksum; this choice of field assignment was **not** specified by a signed instruction or selected before seeing the DH state. Neither identity by itself selects a cipher.

**Independent source-field agreement:** the **first black word immediately after the p27 rubric** is p27 local19–25 and also has **seven runes**, indices `[9,8,6,1,27,8,1]`, numeric sum **60 ≡2 (mod29)**. ## What this changes, and what it does **not** establish

The **M→M** locator is an executable original-image *page-choice hypothesis* with a unique recipient in the nine printed starts. The newly found **p27 red length19 / red GP-sum15 / immediately following black GP-sum2** triple reproduces the independently known DH `(B,b,K)=(19,15,2)` numerically, but the choice to assign those three field roles followed discovery of the matching numbers; a genuine cipher would need a first-party role grammar rather than postselection. It is stronger than a numerical sum chosen without a receiver because the operand is a visibly isolated red rune and its comparison class is the manuscript's established chapter-opening rubric. The repeated `(7,2)` post-rubric fingerprint adds a concrete independent source field, worth preserving for an authored state/receiver search. It should motivate examining whether a real contemporary reader would follow this literal glyph match from p6 to the p27–28 DH state, rather than continuing to tune the p30–42 fitted key shells.

**Critical provenance and selection caveat:** the relation was **noticed after inspecting the corpus**, including p27. It was **not** a genuinely blind prediction, and neither the probability of observing one duplicated signature among 16 spans nor the human-puzzle probability has been established. GP0-residue **2** was recognized as K after the DH packet was already known; the red singleton was interpreted as a page pointer after knowing the p27 chapter-initial. The signed 2016 source, previous p6 layout reports and original p27 image do **not** say **match M to a chapter, sum the next word, or use it as a key**. Other possible readings of the same p6 suffix length7 include an omitted two-word prelude `SEEK AND` (GP lengths4+3), or a seven-rune terminal target `OPENPGP`. Both make retrospective numeric fits and **neither is selected by the page layout alone**.

Therefore **do not promote this to an authentic cipher opcode, receiver-mode prediction, full-page inverse, or plaintext recovery**. A successor must first find an authored operation typing the red singleton as a locator and the recipient seven-rune black word as a key, checksum, arity or ciphertext receiver. It must state the full directed mapping and a genuinely independent held-out cipher output before any new target-word comparison. The original p6 initial Fehu F and p27 initial M do not by themselves authorize F deletion, alphabet reordering, subtraction or a key schedule.
