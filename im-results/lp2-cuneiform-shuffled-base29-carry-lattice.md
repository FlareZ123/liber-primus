# Cuneiform BASE29 scalar with arbitrary word order: exact free-carry lattice inverse

**Decision (Agent28, 25 November 2026):** neither short scalar orientation/direction can support more than one of 36 development long groups, even when each word has an independent arbitrary carry in [0,a-1]. This is stronger than the contiguous chapter's empirical language failure under the H-shuffle premise (intact words, arbitrary order). Four constructed shuffled controls recover all four true scalar keys. No original plaintext.

## Source and model

Physical p33–39 has 1,680 runes and the repeated 1033/3301 cuneiform clue. Historical BASE29 motivates fixed-width integer arithmetic, not a verified multiplication instruction. In a continuous unit multiplication of a low-to-high digit stream, a complete word of length m obeys either C=(aP+s) mod 29^m or P=(aC+s) mod 29^m, where 0<=s<a. Every possible post-encryption whole-word permutation preserves these local relations. Grant a separate unrestricted legal carry for every group; ignoring interword carry conservation can only enlarge the key domain. Neither printed chronological order nor a known endpoint is required. Bound 1<=a<29^3, a mod29 nonzero, includes both literal source values. Larger multipliers, F holds, general mixing and hidden boundaries are outside scope.

For fixed x (the operand word) and target t, enumerate lattice points (a,y) in the lattice generated by (1,x),(0,29^m), with 1<=a<29^3 and t-(29^3-2)<=y<=t. Require s=t-y, 0<=s<a, and a unit. Every point exactly corresponds to a local legal pair (a,s), including wraparound (negative y allowed). Gauss reduction and exact projected bounds plus clipped integer intervals avoid scanning the full multiplier domain for every dictionary pair. 4,494 exhaustive small-domain comparisons pass. The frozen 33,386-form lexicon combines TextBlob with native solved forms, excluding both target native control sections from augmentation. Original complete groups of at least eight runes are eligible; p33–37 has 36 fit anchors, p38–39 has nine independent held-out anchors. Groups crossing the fit seam or chapter boundary are not used as development evidence.

## Positive calibration

Native first KOAN (780 runes) and LOSS OF DIVINITY (755) are encoded with continuous carries, then their complete encrypted groups are permuted with deterministic Fisher–Yates. Key selection uses only lexical anchor coverage in the first 70% of shuffled groups. Four combinations test both orientations and both multiplier sides, with independent key choices and shuffles (two source texts reused). All 54 in-register long groups retain their exact hidden plaintext/carry witness, 37 uniquely. Nine in-register held-out anchors retain exact truth, seven uniquely; one held-out out-of-register group has an incidental wrong lexical candidate. The solver does not claim to reconstruct the original word order or short words.

## Scope and next decision

This exact long-anchor bound retires the short continuous scalar interpretation of the cuneiform chapter even under post-encryption whole-word shuffling, subject to the finite lexical premise. The direct arithmetic architecture and native controls are not evidence that LP2 used this cipher. H-decoy and alternative arithmetic remain open. The free carry relaxation is deliberately generous; no exact original complete sequence survived to justify a carry stitching search.
