# LP2 sequential JPEG AC-coefficient LSB scan

**Classification:** bounded negative for the tested unkeyed, sequential JSteg-style coefficient-LSB channel. No LP2 plaintext, key, or hidden file was recovered. This does **not** exclude encrypted payloads, keyed/randomized coefficient methods, or other image channels.

## Why this test was worth running

LP1 JPEG 05 and JPEG 10 contain genuine image-level payload structures. LP2 has since had its DHT/DQT, APP metadata, and appended-byte channels audited, and all 58 images have been screened under default-key OutGuess. Those tests leave the quantized DCT coefficients themselves as a distinct, still-open source channel. This experiment tests one concrete, low-complexity member of that family rather than launching a generic steganography sweep: sequential replacement of AC-coefficient least-significant bits, skipping zero- and unit-magnitude coefficients (the JSteg-style rule).

## Exact extraction model

Input: all 58 original LP2 JPEGs, resources/liber-primus/17.jpg through 74.jpg.

The C reproducer uses libjpeg's jpeg_read_coefficients to read the original quantized DCT arrays directly. It does not decode to pixels and re-encode for extraction. For each block it:

1. skips the DC coefficient;
2. visits the 63 AC coefficients in standard JPEG zigzag order;
3. skips coefficients with absolute magnitude 0 or 1;
4. emits abs(coefficient) & 1, packed most-significant-bit first.

Two traversals are implemented: component-major row/block order and MCU-interleaved order. LP2 contains 35 three-component 2×2-subsampled RGB images and 23 one-component grayscale images. The two traversals therefore differ for some RGB streams, while preserving the same eligible-coefficient count.

The reproducer also includes a coefficient-domain planting mode. It changes an eligible coefficient by at most one, preserving its sign, to encode a supplied payload, writes the modified coefficient arrays back to JPEG, and permits an end-to-end extraction check.

The signature scan included common archive, image, document, compression, executable, SQLite and PGP headers, plus the strings CICADA and LIBER. No page stream or physical-page-order concatenation produced a recognizable file header or sustained readable plaintext. The high entropy is consistent with natural coefficient parity; it would also be consistent with an encrypted or compressed payload, so it is not evidence of absence by itself.

A repeated-prefix artifact is explained by the image template rather than treated as payload evidence. In component-major order, the longest shared page-stream prefix is 2,187 bytes (image files 54 and 56). The top 672 raster rows of image files 51–56 are pixel-identical; their common crop SHA-256 is 6397f6cabfd1ae9aecf68f8146dc65abbe3115a0b36df864d7b2a63e8a63aaa8. These files correspond to physical LP2 pages 34–39. The shared stream prefix is therefore expected from the repeated header artwork.

A secondary LSB-pair histogram statistic is recorded by the C tool, but it is not used as a classifier: its baseline varies substantially with page artwork. On the two planted-control images, a 32-KB payload reduces the full pair statistic from 32,545.6 to 19,858.9 (RGB image 17) and from 37,630.2 to 23,872.7 (grayscale image 33), while the unmodified original pages span 10,035.7–40,090.4. That overlap makes a universal threshold unjustified.

## Positive controls

Deterministic payloads were generated by concatenating SHA-256 blocks of label || counter_be64, with labels LP2-JSTEG-POSITIVE-{n}. For example, the 4-KB control is reproducible with:

~~~python
import hashlib
from pathlib import Path
n = 4096
label = f"LP2-JSTEG-POSITIVE-{n}".encode()
payload = b"".join(hashlib.sha256(label + i.to_bytes(8, "big")).digest()
                  for i in range((n + 31) // 32))[:n]
Path("/tmp/payload.bin").write_bytes(payload)
~~~

Payload sizes of 32, 512, 4,096 and 32,768 bytes were embedded into original RGB image 17 and original grayscale image 33 using component-major traversal. Every payload was recovered byte-for-byte after writing and rereading the JPEG. A further 4,096-byte payload was recovered exactly under both component-major and MCU-interleaved planting/extraction on both image types.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, with libjpeg-turbo 2.1.5 or a compatible libjpeg development package:

~~~sh
cc -O2 -Wall -Wextra sims/jpeg_jsteg_coeff_scan.c -ljpeg -o /tmp/jpeg_jsteg_coeff_scan
mkdir -p /tmp/lp2-jsteg
/tmp/jpeg_jsteg_coeff_scan extract resources/liber-primus /tmp/lp2-jsteg 17 74 > /tmp/lp2-jsteg-coefficients.csv
python3 sims/jpeg_jsteg_stream_analyze.py /tmp/lp2-jsteg --csv /tmp/lp2-jsteg-streams.csv
~~~

The extractor writes PAGE_component.bin and PAGE_mcu.bin for each image. The analyzer reports per-page entropy, printable fractions, stream hashes, known signatures at all eight bit alignments, and repeated prefixes. To reproduce a positive control, generate a deterministic payload as described above and call:

~~~sh
/tmp/jpeg_jsteg_coeff_scan plant resources/liber-primus/17.jpg /tmp/jsteg-positive.jpg /tmp/payload.bin component
~~~

## Interpretation and next boundary

This retires the specific **unkeyed, sequential AC-LSB cleartext/file-payload** hypothesis under the declared coefficient traversal and skip rule. It does not establish that the DCT domain is clean. A keyed or pseudorandom placement, F5/JPHide-style method, encrypted/compressed payload, a different coefficient statistic, or pixel/color-channel modulation remains outside this test. Do not repeat this exact sequential extraction without a new clue that specifies a different ordering, component subset, or payload framing.
