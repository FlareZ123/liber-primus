# Original LP2 word-private GP29 feedback: exact lexical recovery frontier

## Motivated, key-independent inversion question

The unchanged original LP2 p0–55 ciphertext has **12,956 runes in 2,896 complete printed words**, including **32 words crossing physical page boundaries**. Only **86 same-rune adjacent pairs** appear across the manuscript, versus the roughly 445 expected under independent GP29 drawing. This motivates a feedback mechanism that could suppress adjacent matches. Ask directly whether a whole original English word could be recovered even when its encryption parameters are independent of all other words. The model below grants so much freedom to the hypothetical encoder that recovered long lexical words would be candidates worth following, but no model is claimed as Cicada's original.

All tests preserve exact printed word boundaries and **give each printed word its own unrestricted secret parameters**, plus arbitrary plaintext for its first rune. Work over `GF(29)`; `P_j` denotes native-GP plaintext, `C_j` the unchanged source rune index, and `j` the position within its complete printed word. A stable lexicon of **29,157** TextBlob source words is converted to the standard solved-Cicada GP29 transliteration (normalizing QU/V/K/Z and ING/IO digraphs); no unknown LP2 words enter its construction. Source-native solved-p57 GP rune words are separately encrypted and recovered as unconditional implementation controls.

## Exact, nested candidate mechanisms and original results

1. **One secret multiplicative slope per word**: `C_j−C_(j−1)=a(P_j−C_(j−1))` for every `j≥1`, `a∈1…28`. Enumerate **all 28 slopes** per original word and grant any first plaintext rune. Among **559 original words of length≥7**, **zero** has any GP native English word in the source 29,157-word lexicon, while **48** native-GP planted English words are recovered at all tested slopes. A 250-sample source-length-matched low-doublet random baseline averages **0.072** accidental long matches. This is a strong original lexicon exclusion for the exact word-private slope architecture. 2. **Every secret affine slope plus intercept per word**: `C_j−C_(j−1)=a(P_j−C_(j−1))+b`, `a∈1…28, b∈0…28`, still an arbitrary first plaintext rune. An exact two-point finite-field recovery checks **all 812 keys for every word** without guessing any. Original: **3/559** words of length≥7 match an English-lexicon GP candidate, all at length7; **0/343** original words of length≥8 match. The low-doublet 24-trial random baseline averages **1.17** length≥7 matches, maximum4; observed3 gives upper tail **0.12**. All **225 authentic solved-p57 native GP planted tests** pass. [Source exact SFT](../sims/lp2_wordprivate_affine_feedback_exact_nativelex_sft.cjs) · [Node22 PASS 45025887265](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/45025887265).

3. **Every invertible three-coefficient predecessor affine transducer with independent key per word**: `C_j=aP_j+bC_(j−1)+d`, where **all 28×29×29=23,548** `a≠0,b,d` options per word are admitted with an arbitrary first plaintext rune. Exact three-point elimination, with exhaustive degenerate-branch handling, checks the entire family without optimizing a score. Original **216** length7 words give27 lexicon hits, **156** length8 words give just two, and **all 187 original words length≥9 give zero English lexicon matches**. For the 343 original words length≥8, two matched words are the unrelated `vacantly` at p24 word1327 and `nicholas` at p35 word1940, both explainable by chance. The 42 source-length/low-doublet random baselines average **0.95** length≥8 matches and reach3; empirical `P(null≥observed)` **0.233**. Native p57 real-rune positive tests **288/288** pass. [Complete three-coefficient source gate](../sims/lp2_wordprivate_threecoef_feedback_exact_nativelex_sft.cjs) · [Windows Node22 PASS 45026054568](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/45026054568).

A candidate match is a necessary lexical consistency condition, **never a decryption claim**. The observed three-coefficient matches supply no coherent adjacent original words, no shared author-specified cipher instruction and no excess over source-matched random controls. The tests assume unchanged word boundaries, a GP29-native lexical plaintext matching the finite independent English list and a *fixed* set of field coefficients **within each word**. They do **not** exclude every possible English text, any two-previous-rune state law, nonlinear or word-position-dependent update, alternate GP representation, altered word segmentation, or binary message. The original's sparse adjacent equalities motivate the experiment but do not establish this cipher architecture.

## Decision toward plaintext

The unrestricted one-step **word-private linear GP29 predecessor** family, including all 23,548 keys independently per original word, has **no evidence of authentic GP29-native prose** and cannot recover any of the 187 source words of length≥9 as known English dictionary entries. Long original words are valuable because a genuine cipher of this type would retain their suffix under an exact, testable arithmetic inverse. Next serious attempts require **an independently supplied operation or stronger state model** rather than extracting ordinary words through a per-word first-order affine recipe. Preserve the full exact source tests and independent solved-rune positives for any later mechanism that claims to invert this architecture.
