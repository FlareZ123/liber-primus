# The source-p51 three-step fiveback echo rules out a fixed plaintext-marker copy rule in native prose

**Agent23, 15 November 2026, Director task #1101.** The original LP2 p51 has an eight-rune complete printed word with a **three-rune suffix identical to its three-rune prefix**, five places later. This yields an exact, secret-key-independent obstruction to a tempting finite-state decipherment architecture: an encoder that always emits the fifth-previous ciphertext rune whenever it sees one fixed plaintext marker. All four source-native English registers tested contain **zero** eight-GP-rune words whose last three GP runes are identical. This is a bounded **negative for that architecture under ordinary native English**, not decrypted original plaintext.

## The original discriminating word

The canonical source word number **2722** (zero-based among 2,896 full words) begins at global rune index **12157**, physical LP2 **p51** rune offset **43**. It is precisely:

```text
source GP:   ᛋ  ᛞ  ᛝ  ᚷ  ᛚ  ᛋ  ᛞ  ᛝ
GP numbers: 15 23 21  6 20 15 23 21
word offset: 0  1  2  3  4  5  6  7
```

At `j=5,6,7`, `C_j=C_(j-5)` **three consecutive times**; on each occasion `C_(j-1)≠C_(j-5)`, so neither the predecessor-repeat target nor a fifth-repeat target is ambiguous. There is no page-wrap artifact: this is an original complete eight-rune word entirely on physical p51. The entry is independently checked against canonical `lp2-corpus.json` in the SFT.

Consider a fully reversible online GP29 cipher whose state includes the predecessor `C_(j-1)` and word-local `C_(j-5)`, and whose author-chosen plain rune `M5` is the unique plaintext symbol mapped to the fifth-back ciphertext rune when the two state targets differ. All other 28 plaintext symbols may be assigned using arbitrary, potentially secret, state-dependent bijections; a separate fixed marker may emit the preceding ciphertext symbol. Unique inverse images force

```text
P_5 = P_6 = P_7 = M5.
```

This implication survives *every* choice of `M5`, all secret tables, all parameters on other letters, previous-symbol rules, and page keys. It needs no corpus-wide statistics or cipher key fitting. It only assumes faithful printed-word boundaries, an invariant single-rune fifth-back marker, and one-to-one symbol mapping at each state.

## Native-language exclusion and genuine reversible control

GP source spelling follows the pinned LP1 orthography, including `ING` and `IO` ligatures and historical standardizations. A deterministic full-language sweep found:

| Source | Eight-rune words | With three identical terminal runes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 29,157-word English frequency lexicon | **4,331** | **0** |
| Gibran corpus | **453** | **0** |
| Shakespeare corpus | **2,328** | **0** |
| Emerson corpus | **2,944** | **0** |

Both the original eight-rune exception and these four zero counts are frozen as SFT assertions/outputs. Notably, nonordinary labels such as Roman numerals can contain triple identical GP symbols elsewhere in a word, so this is a *position-specific* statement, not an absolute prohibition of triple runes in all texts.

To show the implication belongs to an **actual reversible cipher**, the SFT implements a full 29-output-permutation encoder. It assigns arbitrary key-dependent outputs to other plaintext symbols, reserves one plaintext symbol for the preceding ciphertext, and reserves `M5` for `C_(j-5)` whenever distinct. It uses a deterministic collision remedy when the reserved targets coincide. Encrypted GP-native Gibran control words, exactly matched to all 2,896 original word lengths, recover **12,956/12,956** source symbols with the true table and have **zero** eight-rune words with the original exact three-event signature. A deliberately synthetic eight-rune plaintext ending with `M5 M5 M5` produces precisely the original three fifth-back equality positions, showing that the test is a valid true-positive for this model.

## Research decision

**Retire the fixed-single-plaintext-marker fifth-back copy cipher for ordinary GP English**, rather than allocating more keys to a family that necessarily forces an unattested eight-rune English word ending in three identical GP letters. The original fiveback echo may still be generated by a phase-coherent plaintext repeat, ciphertext copy instruction of variable length, other finite-memory feedback, or by chance. Specifically, a five-phase per-word bijection would force `P_{0..2}=P_{5..7}`, not `P_5=P_6=P_7`; that is a distinct model and is already independently constrained by the full-book secret-phase alphabet evidence.

This result does **not** identify the plaintext word, key, final destination or valid cipher. It changes the candidate-mechanism choice at a concrete original page and preserves one author-plausible negative without reopening all lag-five theory.
