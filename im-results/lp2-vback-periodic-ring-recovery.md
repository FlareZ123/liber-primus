# V-BACK periodic-key recovery by uninterrupted-window nominations

External Round47 [C9 encoder](https://github.com/Dukotah/cicada3301/blob/fed5473f6ea8180c7a5bfcf1bc19b746db38f9eb/challenges/c9_rejection_loops.py) defines backward cursor rejection: on a proposed repeat, while cursor>0, decrement cursor with probability supp and retry the same plaintext. Otherwise emit and increment. Unlike ordinary rejection, monotone-clock decoding misses valid paths. This is a modern exploratory variant, not a first-party clue.

For each fixed key cursor j, an ordinary emission is possible. A backward chain has forced plaintext p=C[i-1]-K[j] and can continue only while rejected digits equal K[j]. Enumerate every legal stopping point, including accepted repeats and the zero-cursor boundary. Use a finite width 64 development / 256 continuation beam over cursor, phase and last three plaintext runes. Replay every event. Supp=1-.664/3.448, GP29 order-four language model fitted on Shakespeare/Emerson.

Unknown key nomination: take length64/96 windows, stride16, ignore backward steps locally. For every q=8 or 13, construct bigram pair factors by key phase. Condition on first key digit and solve exact cyclic max-sum; retain three highest candidates among the best paths for each first/last digit pair (not globally exact top3 in case multiple paths share endpoint digits). Deduplicate rotations, sort by nomination multiplicity and best window score; top20 only. Decode first480 runes jointly with unknown starting phase, freeze best key/phase/cursor/context before evaluating suffix. This does not rely on knowing which windows contain rejections or their positions.

20 independent Gibran controls (12 q8, 8 q13, lengths729–976) recover **20/20 hidden cyclic keys**, including a target ranked eighth at nomination. Frozen continuation **7449/7450 exact runes**, all 20 controls >=85% positional. Control suffix language scores >=-2.139 nats/rune; wrong-key full scores <=-6.230. Short terminal-matched controls (lengths299/308, last100 held out) recover only16/24 keys,18/24 positional passes: weaker short-scope power.

Original: nine visual chapters, both q8/q13; terminal chapter also after omitting the nine red heading runes. Fit first480 (short: L-100). All20 fitted keys fail frozen continuation, best held-out -6.248667; best fit -5.323533. Each long-scope winner nominated by one window only. No coherent passage or authenticated key. Bounded recovery-screen failure, not an all-key proof and not a sealed external C9 submission. No source selects V-BACK, q8/q13 transfer is historical-key-length motivated.

## Exact nonperiodic prime-minus-one V-BACK inverse (Agent23, 25 November)

This tests a different key family from the periodic-key nomination above: the signed prime-minus-one schedule from authentic solved p56, independent start ordinal 0–3301, ordinary or native literal-F holds, backward rejection with probability 0.80744 (calibrated from observed repeat suppression). The model is exploratory: p56 establishes the prime schedule and F holds, not V-BACK. The new SFT replays all 85 authentic p56 runes at suppression zero and consumes exactly 84 keys.

The exact DP state is (absolute next-key cursor, previous symbol consumed/held). A consumed previous plaintext is determined by the previous ciphertext and key at cursor−1; a held previous symbol is F. On a rejection chain the plaintext is forced by the first rejected key and predecessor ciphertext; enumerate all legal stopping points until the first different emitted value or the zero-cursor boundary. No beam or pruning. Bigram model uses training portions of Gibran, Shakespeare, Emerson and solved native GP sections excluding p56. Heldout donor slices supply the six controls. Freeze the best first-two-thirds path's origin and terminal state, then recover the remaining third without refitting the origin. Both signs and clocks included. Controls use three distinct starting ordinals (one shared within each pair of clock variants) and distinct rejection histories. All six origins recovered; withheld 3607/3612 runes exact, suffix scores −2.75249 to −2.57271 per rune.

Original nine chapters, four variants each: four F variants impossible before the cut; 13 more selected F prefix winners cannot continue; 19 legal frozen continuations (18 ordinary, one F) score −5.34712 to −4.59893, all far below controls. No coherent plaintext. An independent whole-chapter run allowing **all** origins and F paths for both signs finds 11/18 infeasible, including both signs on p3–7, p8–14, p15–22, p33–39, p40–53. Seven globally legal F variants have whole-span optimal scores −5.10970 to −4.68552. Thus even changing the selected origin after looking at the suffix cannot make this exact F mechanism fit all chapters. The ordinary clock remains a bounded predictive negative, not an information-theoretic impossibility.
