# Full-book LP2 GP29 pair-order capacity: exact repeated sites, original inversion, and missing key

The strongest construction below is a single uninterrupted source-defined GP29 codeword that preserves every exact repeated-rune position. ## Original inputs and plausible human clue

The untouched canonical LP2 unknown corpus has **12,956 rune identities**, **2,896 complete printed words** (including 32 true page-spanning words), and **86 adjacent-equal GP29 rune transitions** in the entire continuous tape. The manuscript's genuine 29-symbol Gematria Primus alphabet is independent of English words. The January 2016 signed Cicada message says its **words** are the map and its **numbers** are the direction; it could motivate a word-number direction channel, but **does not specify a consecutive-word pairing, use of lengths, rank code, reset policy, or key**.

The source itself supplies one low-tech binary choice: take each *consecutive pair of complete printed words*, preserving its two observed lengths. If they differ, the pair could be printed longer-first or shorter-first, carrying **one visible direction bit** while maintaining both authentic lengths and the total 12,956 runes. If the two lengths tie, the pair carries zero bits. On the complete unknown book there are exactly **1,448 pairs**, of which **1,242** have unequal lengths. This uses genuine printed positions and requires no hidden sidecar. It is a *hypothesis about an encoder*, not evidence that actual words were reordered or that a historical player would have chosen those pairs.

## Strongest exact model: one global codeword fixing all 86 observed repeat sites

Let N=12,956, E=86 and u=1,242. Define a **fixed binary mask** at all N−1 rune transitions, taking its repeat sites *exactly* from the observed original ciphertext. A codeword contains one arbitrary initial rune (29 possibilities), a chosen successor distinct from its predecessor at each of the other N−1−E=12,869 positions (28 possibilities each), and a binary orientation choice at each of the u unequal-length printed word pairs. At the 86 fixed-repeat positions the rune is forced, and every pair of word lengths stays fixed as an unordered pair.

The resulting original-mask codebook and unrestricted GP29 source vocabulary are exactly

\[
M=29\cdot28^{12,869}\cdot2^{1,242},
\qquad Q=29^{12,956}.
\]

The repeated-site constraints remove precisely

\[
12,955\log_2(29/28)+86\log_2(28)
=1069.2932999184\ \text{bits}
\]

of source coding capacity. Thus **1,070 bits suffice** for an unrestricted GP29 injection, while the untouched authentic pair orientations provide **1,242 bits**. The whole-book original-source capacity surplus is **log₂(M/Q)=172.7067000816 bits**, checked with exact BigInt inequalities. No *punctuation* marker, page break, red heading, or source typography is asserted to reset cipher state.

**Critical original-word-order null:** keeping *exactly the original 2,896 word lengths* and all fourteen authentic length-category counts fixed, but drawing an otherwise **uniform random permutation of those lengths**, the expected number of unequal adjacent pairs is **1,234.969948**, with analytically derived standard deviation **12.874058**. The actual original has **1,242**, only **+0.5461 standard deviations** from that conditional null. The expectation uses probability of equal pair **Σcₗ(cₗ−1)/(2896·2895)=0.1471202** and the variance uses the corresponding disjoint-two-pair without-replacement covariance. The original source's apparent 1,242-bit carrier size is therefore **ordinary under a trivial non-authored length-order model**. The actual direction bits themselves are also balanced: **636 longer-first, 606 shorter-first** among the 1,242 unequal pairs, a two-sided binomial-fair-sign normal score of only **0.851 standard deviations** (exact conditional fair-orientation binomial **p=0.410586**). In the resulting bit tape there are **604 runs**, versus **621.638** expected and SD **17.604** under a uniformly permuted binary sequence conditioned on the exact 636/606 signs (**z=−1.002**), also ordinary. Neither the sign balance nor the simplest serial structure selects an authored bitstream. Thus the orientation bit frequencies likewise offer no independent source-authorship signal. More strongly, the fixed-E mask model needs at least **1,070** unequal pairs, versus null mean **1,234.97** and variance **165.74136**. Applying the distribution-free one-sided **Cantelli inequality** to the exact without-replacement mean and variance gives probability **at least 0.9939468** that an entirely random rearrangement of these *same real printed lengths* already has enough unequal-pair bits to meet the GP29 capacity inequality. This is a conservative bound, **not an exact permutation tail probability**. Hence the capacity success is expected for almost every random length order conditional on authentic source histogram, and cannot meaningfully select a decipherment operation by itself. Its numerical sufficiency for encoding an unrestricted GP29 source is *not evidence that Cicada deliberately set these length directions as data*. This is stronger scientific caution than merely observing that an arbitrary codebook can fit the manuscript.

This is a **conditional mathematical existence result**. It chooses the original ciphertext's equality mask and printed pair-length inventory as the particular admissible output-register shape. There are **C(12,955,86)** possible 86-site masks (approximately **2^741.32984**), and this test conditions on the one already observed in the original ciphertext. Its sites are directly readable from that output, but no independent Cicada command tells an encoder to choose that particular 86-site template or use its orientation bits; that large post-observation template freedom further weakens any claim of identified cipher design. An actual sender could choose such a template and an actual receiver could read it from the printed output, but the manuscript supplies **no authenticated instruction selecting this template as an algorithm**.
