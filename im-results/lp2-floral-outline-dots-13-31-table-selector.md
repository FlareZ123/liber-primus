# The original floral five-dot masks read 13 and 31

**Agent24, 16 November 2026. Evidence class: new image-original source observation; conditional inter-artifact selector, not a decryption.** The original 16 floral LP2 plates, physical p40–55 (JPEG scans 57–72), contain two five-circle motifs. Their mirror-registered shapes are the same, but **two circles in the left motif have pale outlines instead of black interiors**, whereas all five on the right are filled. The image's natural three-row layout yields **01101₂ = 13** on the left and **11111₂ = 31** on the right. Independently, the 58 original LP2 pages have **31 visible thirteen-dot punctuation marks** (30 red and one black), and their 31 ordered events define 32 source intervals, equal to the **32 printed rows of eight tokens** in the pp49–51 numerical table. This gives a concrete possible authored *selector* for thirteen-dot punctuation as a distinct cipher/framing class, stronger than choosing it solely by counting coincidences. It does not specify the transformation applied to a fragment or table row, and no new plaintext or creator key is recovered.

## What the unchanged source actually shows

The visual source is the original 2400×3600 JPEG `resources/liber-primus/57.jpg`, SHA-256 `3f5af995b39f94f268d4aa94825a10f2429f4a3affa1921a7f627e6d1bfcfb69`. The same two ornamental-dot patches recur **pixel-identically** on all sixteen original p40–55 JPEGs, scans57–72, despite distinct centre-page inscription. They are one static signal, **not sixteen independently varying keys**.

A five-point ornament sits in each outer margin at original y≈1476–1540. The exact five left-ornament dot centres are frozen below in their natural three tiers (upper one, middle left/right, lower left/right); each right coordinate is the horizontal reflection `x'=2399−x`. The two lower dots have slightly different actual y coordinates, so **row grouping** matters; sorting all five by y alone gives a different ordering and is not the depicted 1+2+2 row convention.

The two left hollow cores each have **zero** such dark pixels; filled cores have 286–289. Reading 0=hollow, 1=filled in visual rows gives exactly `01101` and `11111`. No interpolation, image registration, pattern search, language model, OCR or adjustable threshold is used. A synthetic in-memory fill of the two left hollow cores flips the readout to `11111` and the original readout remains unchanged.

Across the full matched left/right exterior crops (source left x20..599, right x1800..2379 mirrored, y500..3099), thresholding at luminance120 leaves 2,262 dark-pixel disagreements. Eight-connected mismatch components have precisely **two dominant sites**: 436 pixels at approximately (385,1464) and 404 pixels at (369,1528), both overlapping the two hollow left dots. Every other connected difference is below 50 pixels (small mirror/raster artifacts). This comparison is between **left and right borders of one original image**, and is distinct from the prior independently established *between-page* identity of each border. The numeral mask is observable without the component analysis.

The independent original ink annotations [`lp2-ink-annotations.json`](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-ink-annotations.json) record 30 red thirteen-dot marks and one black thirteen-dot mark; one special p7 black and red pair shares the same rune offset but appears on separate printed lines. Consequently there are **31 ordered mark events** even though two occur at the same rune index. Exactly **32 intervals in event order** arise if every mark is treated as a delimiter; one may be zero-runed. The [original numerical grid](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json) has **32 rows × 8 cells**, containing 256 printed tokens. The separately counted **225 original red rune symbols + 31 thirteen-dot events = 256** also matches this table size. Those three counts were known before this image discovery.

## Causal interpretation and strongest rival

**Possible human route:** a solver notices that a nearly mirrored floral border treats exactly two of five circles differently, reads its naturally grouped binary forms as **13 and 31**, identifies **31 thirteen-dot author-made marks** in the full manuscript, then partitions its text at precisely those events. The resulting **32 fragments** align by count to the table's **32 original rows of eight cells**. The graphic would thus *select the mark class and framing unit*, while the separate printed table supplies potential per-fragment operands. This is a concrete input-selection hypothesis, not a complete decoding rule.

**Rivals / limitations:** the pale dots could be a decorative asymmetry or stock-art property. The binary convention, reading direction, interpreting white as zero, counting mark events across *both* LP2 releases or only LP2, and aligning 32 text intervals with 32 grid rows are **inferred**, not authored instructions. The value31 on the right follows automatically from an all-filled five-dot motif; that match is less independent than 13 on the altered left. The exact `13/31` numerical match is post-observation and has no discovery-adjusted probability. The original art does not say whether table bytes are conventional base60, how to map eight cells to runes, whether an empty interval consumes a row, what to do with punctuation, or whether the source order is linear. These omissions block a complete reversible consumer.

**Prior bounded inversions:** the repository already tested the **31 thirteen-dot → 32 fragments → 32 grid rows** geometry with direct repeated row keys, RC4 and a GP28 predecessor-exclusion rank, and none yielded native plaintext; see [fragment-wise RC4×GP28 source inversion](fragment-gridrow-rc4-rank28-inverse.md) and its [complete SFT](../sims/fragment_gridrow_rc4_rank28_sft.cjs). This new artwork observation cannot undo those negatives. It only improves the *source motivation* for the same alignment and encourages a distinct, independently selected nonadditive eight-cell consumer if a further instruction is found. Repeating arbitrary pad families with this label would be unjustified.

## Reproducer and decision

[Standalone original-image Python SFT](../sims/lp2_floral_13_31_outline_mask_source_sft.py) uses Pillow and NumPy against all original scans57–72 plus the canonical annotation and table JSON. It asserts the exact scan57 byte hash, all ten source dot states, the mirror disagreement count, identical patches across sixteen plates, in-memory mutation positive, 30+1 marks, 225 red runes and 32×8 cells. It performs **zero ciphertext decryption searches**. The source assertion values and source hashes were first evaluated directly on the supplied original 75-JPEG archive.

**Research decision:** preserve the novel static **13/31** floral contrast and the conditional mark/grid selector. Do **not** promote it to proven authorial intent, recovered key or authentic plaintext. The next worthwhile cryptanalytic step requires an original-authored instruction identifying an eight-cell row consumer and its output register, after which the unaltered 32-fragment ciphertext provides a frozen full-input test, with genuine known plaintext and held-out pages for discrimination.
