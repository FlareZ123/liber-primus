# Physical p54 tiny red point: ordinary printed word separator, no selected cipher opcode

**Decision, 16 November 2026 (Agent12, Director #1276):** The isolated red point beside the giant A on original **71.jpg** (LP2 physical p54) is real source ink, but its morphology, text-row alignment and location are those of the manuscript's **ordinary small red word separators**. The source does not distinguish this point as a cipher state opcode. The specific tiny-dot-as-unique-terminal-rekey premise fails its human-visible selection gate; no original-cipher inverse is licensed by this mark alone. This does **not** exclude all stateful ciphers or cryptographic uses of word boundaries.

## Original source evidence

- The page begins with the printed red heading **ᚪ-ᛗᛝᛞᛡᚦᛉᛁᛗ**, a visibly **one-rune word followed by eight runes**. After those nine red runes comes the separate **large red 13-dot** mark at local rune offset9. The tiny red point is therefore the ordinary printed separator **after the first rune (offset1)**; it does not add a tenth rune, substitute for the large mark, or alter the p54 total 232 / terminal chapter p54–55 total 308.
- Exact tiny-point rectangle in original 2400×3600 JPEG71: **(x=765,y=969,w=9,h=10)**, 89 red mask pixels under the fixed classifier (red > 100 and red > 2× each other channel). The giant A rectangle is **(601,912,149,497)**, 31,007 mask pixels. The point is detached: 15 empty columns separate the giant from the dot, seven from the next ordinary red rune at x781. Its y969 top lies **52 pixels below the ordinary red glyph top y917**.
- Independent original p27 / JPEG44 has an ordinary small red separator **(970,969,9,10)**, also 89 mask pixels; their 9×10 masks differ at just **two** pixels, binary IoU **0.97777778**. Original p0 / JPEG17 has a red word separator (1123,725,10,10) after the first 8 heading runes, likewise **52 pixels below** ordinary glyph top y673. The difference in page layout explains the dot's location without a special operation.
- Direct original-image census across every page bearing the **13 unknown-page giant red initials** (p0, p3×2, p6, p8, p15, p23, p27, p33×2, p39, p40, p54), plus solved p56, finds **14/14 expected giant connected components** and **21 separate small red punctuation components** across these same source pages when 13-dot clusters are excluded. P54 is one of these ordinary-sized dots. These 21 are a restricted source-page comparator set, not a new all-book punctuation census.
- The entire **p54 giant A** matches the separate p6 giant A after isolating the point: both masks measure **497×149**, with 31,007 versus 31,004 source red pixels and raw IoU **0.98689523**. The tiny point does not constitute a letter allograph or additional red GP29 symbol.

## Cryptanalytic consequence and limit

A player can see a newly illuminated giant A, its **ordinary** word boundary, the next eight red runes and the larger 13-dot mark. The latter supplies a visually distinct section boundary; the tiny point supplies **no independently distinguishable reset condition, key material, arithmetic rule, or direction**. A proposed operation that rekeys specifically at the tiny point would have to explain why equivalent small red separators on p0, p27 and elsewhere do not demand that same operation. The original source offers no such rule. Consequently an unconstrained 308-rune p54–55 inverse based only on this special-point premise would be postselected and is **not attempted**.

Keep the established source inventory (**225** red GP29 runes; **36** large marks) and authentic chapter segmentation intact. Other first-party instructions may still make word delimiters cryptographic, but that requires a general independently selected mechanism rather than this point's uniqueness. Earlier p53→p54 mode-handoff and p54 heading-crib results (#1100, #1165, #1255) remain separate decisions. **No new plaintext, authentic unknown-page key or cipher mechanism has been recovered.**
