# Pp36–38 numbered source-square inverses: original 5×5 and 7×7 keys

**Research decision, updated 22 November 2026.** The original LP2 chapter visibly prints **1033 and 3301**, directly matching the authenticated 2014 Stage10 magic-square puzzle. Stage10 actually supplied **three** squares, **order 5 sum 3301**, **order 7 sum 1033**, and **order 5 sum 1033**. The five numbered LP2 passages on pp36–38 contain 553 GP29 runes. Three independently bounded operation families are now tested: a five-entry matrix across the 91 columns where all five passages are present, a complete seven-entry matrix over the exactly divisible **553 = 7×79** runes, and literal single-cell Stage10 order-five additive shifts per complete printed word. The last family admits a stronger all-secret adjacent-rune invariant. The original inputs and deterministic algebraic inverses produce no native GP29 prose. Across all 122 intact numbered words there is exactly one within-word adjacent-equal rune, which every additive per-word shift must preserve regardless of its secret keys. No authentic key or unknown plaintext is recovered.

## Original-input hypothesis and the serious rival

The original printed unit lengths are **139, 91, 98, 96, 129** GP29 runes, a total of 553. Numbered units and chapter cuneiform numerals were visible to a 2014 solver; earlier LP1 pages supplied a square with row/column sum 1033 and a second with sum 3301. Both original matrices remain invertible modulo 29, with determinants **3** and **10** respectively. It is therefore affordable to ask whether these pairs of original-source objects jointly prescribe a block-Hill cipher, without inventing independent 125-cell keys. The strongest rival is much simpler: the header restates the Cicada 3301 motif and five labels simply enumerate five sayings, while the keys to those sayings reside elsewhere.

**Critical physical restriction:** the aligned five-column prefix lasts only **91 rounds = 455 rune values**, since the second numbered passage ends there. The actual remaining source positions are **48/0/7/5/38 = 98** runes. They are retained unchanged as ciphertext in this test. A full 553-rune Hill decoder would require a further creator-supplied rule for those unequal tails; no such source instruction is identified. Fitting padding or choosing new row counts from output would invalidate this gate.

## All-secret wordwise bijection: exact doublet obstruction and two independent conditional nulls (Agent26, 22 November 2026)

This is a **stronger, independent decision** for a family suggested by the historically real Stage10 source squares. A five-by-five matrix contains five literal rows, and the LP2 chapter prints five red-labelled passages. An affordable first-opcode interpretation would use each original red passage label to select one source matrix row, choose the matrix column by printed word ordinal modulo five, and apply the resulting cell as a **constant GP29 Caesar shift within that word**. The alternative source order-five matrix has sum1033 rather than3301. In either source square, the creator would still need to select modulo29, word-rank repetition, shift sign, and plaintext register: the Stage10 HTML form itself merely requests the numeric square and a Tor-service address. The following structural result therefore makes a broader statement without trusting any of those unsupplied key values.

The original text contains **122 complete printed words** in the five units, divided **30/20/25/19/28** and containing **139/91/98/96/129** runes. The p37→p38 physical seam crosses a printed word; the original scanner strips its line-wrap slash and correctly counts its six runes. There are **431 adjacent-symbol positions within printed words** and exactly **ONE** identical-adjacent-rune event in the entire 553-rune enclosure. The counts in each original numbered unit are **1/0/0/0/0**. This is an exact source observation, rather than an English model or result of key fitting. The single actual printed exception is p36 first numbered paragraph **word27** (zero-based), the three-rune ciphertext word **`ᚱᛉᛉ`** at original physical p36 rune offsets227–229. Its consecutive `ᛉᛉ` at offsets228–229 are visually identical in original archive **53.jpg**, confirming that the numerical rarity is present in the author's image rather than caused by a missing transcribed delimiter or a mistaken letter.

For any word-wise **fixed bijection of the 29-rune alphabet** `C_j=π_w(P_j)`, identical adjacent plaintext symbols map to identical ciphertext symbols, and distinct adjacent plaintext symbols remain distinct. Thus the observed single within-word adjacent equality is preserved by **every choice of an independent secret 29! permutation on each of the 122 words**, encompassing all `29^122` source-independent Caesar key schedules, every per-word invertible affine mapping, and arbitrary word-local monoalphabetic substitution. ## Decision and next hypothesis

The original cuneiform numbers and the three 2014 Stage10 squares are legitimate independent prompts for these two tests, but **neither direct five-lane 5×5 mixing on 455 core runes nor complete source 7×7 mixing on all 553 runes deciphers the numbered passages**. The five-lane interpretation requires an additional typed operation for 98 unmatched rune positions; the seven-dimensional interpretation avoids that tail problem, while still failing native-language recovery. A plausible next advance needs independent evidence selecting a different cipher operation or a genuine source key.
