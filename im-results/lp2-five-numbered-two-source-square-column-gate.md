# Pp36–38 numbered source-square inverses: original 5×5 and 7×7 keys

**Research decision, 22 November 2026.** The original LP2 chapter visibly prints **1033 and 3301**, directly matching the authenticated 2014 Stage10 magic-square puzzle. Stage10 actually supplied **three** squares, **order 5 sum 3301**, **order 7 sum 1033**, and **order 5 sum 1033**. The five numbered LP2 passages on pp36–38 contain 553 GP29 runes. Two independent natural operations are therefore affordable: a five-entry matrix across the 91 columns with all five numbered passages present, and a complete seven-entry matrix over the exactly divisible **553 = 7×79** runes. The original inputs, both historical square types, and their natural algebraic inverses produce no native GP29 prose. No key or unknown plaintext is recovered.

## Original-input hypothesis and the serious rival

The original printed unit lengths are **139, 91, 98, 96, 129** GP29 runes, a total of 553. Numbered units and chapter cuneiform numerals were visible to a 2014 solver; earlier LP1 pages supplied a square with row/column sum 1033 and a second with sum 3301. Both original matrices remain invertible modulo 29, with determinants **3** and **10** respectively. It is therefore affordable to ask whether these pairs of original-source objects jointly prescribe a block-Hill cipher, without inventing independent 125-cell keys. The strongest rival is much simpler: the header restates the Cicada 3301 motif and five labels simply enumerate five sayings, while the keys to those sayings reside elsewhere.

**Critical physical restriction:** the aligned five-column prefix lasts only **91 rounds = 455 rune values**, since the second numbered passage ends there. The actual remaining source positions are **48/0/7/5/38 = 98** runes. They are retained unchanged as ciphertext in this test. A full 553-rune Hill decoder would require a further creator-supplied rule for those unequal tails; no such source instruction is identified. Fitting padding or choosing new row counts from output would invalidate this gate.

## Frozen finite inverse

Use precisely the original LP1 square-sum-1033 and square-sum-3301 numeric matrices, reduce entries modulo 29, preserve the literal column order 1–5 and the 91 original GP-index vectors, with no unknown key. The bounded model family includes the matrix, its inverse, transpose and transpose-inverse for each visible square, plus both original-square compositions and the four simple sign/orientation combinations of each order. Every output is replayed into the source input under the algebraically inverse matrix. Each version is checked on an *independent*, authentic 455-rune solved-LP1 GP29 plant with the same shape, yielding exactly **455/455** original plant values after full forward/inverse. All original 553 source positions remain accounted for.

These are rejection diagnostics on the explicit family, without choosing an English fragment as the key.

## The complete 553-rune order-seven square test

The original signed Stage10 HTML form explicitly requests an **"Order 7 Square, 1033"** alongside both order-five squares. Its 7×7 source matrix in `resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt` has all rows and columns summing to 1033 and **determinant 2 modulo 29**, so a full reversible Hill transform is well-defined. The five original numbered units sum to exactly 553 runes, or 79 seven-rune blocks, and require **zero padding** when treated as a seven-block serial input. A seven-by-79 row framing also consumes all 553 runes, without loss.

One independently frozen gate compares the two source-native orderings (literal passage serial and the label-selected one-rune 1→5 interleave), two source-derived keys (original values modulo29 and original cell totients modulo29), four standard orientations (K, inverse, transpose, transpose-inverse) and the two unpadded seven-rune framing conventions, yielding **32** complete original transforms. Every output admits the exact inverse, and independently solved native-LP1 553-rune text can be planted and recovered through every transformation.

The best **entire 553-rune** output under a bigram model trained on disjoint solved-LP1 material is **−0.52862880 nats/rune**, obtained from the literal **raw 7×7 K**, adjacent-block framing, after the numbered one-rune interleave. The most favorable selected 79-rune lane among all 32 variants scores only **−0.26604522 nats/rune**, still below native original text. The positive solved-LP1 553-rune control is **+0.60010483**, and known clear p57 scores **+0.71748878**. Every whole-stream score and every best 79-rune lane score is negative; no coherent contiguous GP native prose or independently sourced unknown plaintext emerges.

This exact seven-by-79 framing was not covered by earlier p33-only 7×7 matrix experiments or the distinct 7×79 finite-field DFT experiment; those source-led negatives remain separate. ## Decision and next hypothesis

The original cuneiform numbers and the three 2014 Stage10 squares are legitimate independent prompts for these two tests, but **neither direct five-lane 5×5 mixing on 455 core runes nor complete source 7×7 mixing on all 553 runes deciphers the numbered passages**. The five-lane interpretation requires an additional typed operation for 98 unmatched rune positions; the seven-dimensional interpretation avoids that tail problem, while still failing native-language recovery. A plausible next advance needs independent evidence selecting a different cipher operation or a genuine source key.
