# Pp36–38 numbered five lanes versus the original 1033/3301 source squares

**Research decision, 22 November 2026.** A potentially human-observable connection exists between the two sexagesimal numbers **1033 and 3301** visibly printed as the p33–p39 chapter heading, their matching authentic LP1 five-by-five magic squares, and the five physically numbered passages in pp36–38. The natural algebraic test is to align one GP29 rune from each numbered lane as a five-entry vector and apply either period-visible five-by-five matrix over the 29-rune field. On the original 91 *complete* columns, this does **not** recover native GP prose, under either single square or the two historical squares composed in source order. No key or plaintext is established.

## Original-input hypothesis and the serious rival

The original printed unit lengths are **139, 91, 98, 96, 129** GP29 runes, a total of 553. Numbered units and chapter cuneiform numerals were visible to a 2014 solver; earlier LP1 pages supplied a square with row/column sum 1033 and a second with sum 3301. Both original matrices remain invertible modulo 29, with determinants **3** and **10** respectively. It is therefore affordable to ask whether these pairs of original-source objects jointly prescribe a block-Hill cipher, without inventing independent 125-cell keys. The strongest rival is much simpler: the header restates the Cicada 3301 motif and five labels simply enumerate five sayings, while the keys to those sayings reside elsewhere.

**Critical physical restriction:** the aligned five-column prefix lasts only **91 rounds = 455 rune values**, since the second numbered passage ends there. The actual remaining source positions are **48/0/7/5/38 = 98** runes. They are retained unchanged as ciphertext in this test. A full 553-rune Hill decoder would require a further creator-supplied rule for those unequal tails; no such source instruction is identified. Fitting padding or choosing new row counts from output would invalidate this gate.

## Frozen finite inverse

Use precisely the original LP1 square-sum-1033 and square-sum-3301 numeric matrices, reduce entries modulo 29, preserve the literal column order 1–5 and the 91 original GP-index vectors, with no unknown key. The bounded model family includes the matrix, its inverse, transpose and transpose-inverse for each visible square, plus both original-square compositions and the four simple sign/orientation combinations of each order. Every output is replayed into the source input under the algebraically inverse matrix. Each version is checked on an *independent*, authentic 455-rune solved-LP1 GP29 plant with the same shape, yielding exactly **455/455** original plant values after full forward/inverse. All original 553 source positions remain accounted for.

Across the 16 original variants, the **best individual 91-rune lane** by the frozen native-LP1 bigram model is still only **−0.39212772 nats/rune** (3301 transpose-inverse, lane two); the highest whole 455-rune score is likewise negative, approximately **−0.55672 nats/rune**. By contrast the exact native 455-rune solved LP1 plant is **+0.58250**, a separate 553-rune solved LP1 sequence is **+0.60010**, and authentic solved LP2 p57 is **+0.71749** under the same held-out model. The best candidate's combined 455-rune GP29 IoC is **0.0341966**; all other candidates also remain in the ciphertext-like 0.0335–0.0353 band. These are rejection diagnostics on the explicit family, without choosing an English fragment as the key.

## Decision and next hypothesis

The original cuneiform numbers and the two published 5×5 matrices are legitimate independent prompts for this test, but **direct cross-lane GP29 Hill mixing does not decipher the five numbered passages**. A complete physical interpretation would additionally need a typed operation for the 98 unmatched rune positions. A plausible next advance therefore requires independent evidence choosing a cipher operation or a genuine source key, rather than another post-selected orientation.
