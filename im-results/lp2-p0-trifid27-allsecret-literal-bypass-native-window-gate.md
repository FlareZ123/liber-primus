## A finite source-relevant architecture

The book's first complete visual chapter has **729 rune glyphs across physical p0–p2 (262/266/201)**, suggestive of **27²**. A classical three-dimensional **Trifid** cube has **27** cells, while Gematria Primus contains **29** distinct runes. One elementary way to use Trifid is to pass two reserved rune identities through unchanged, applying any secret 27-cell cube permutation to the remaining 27. A source-specific previous test used **F (GP index0)** and **EA (GP index28)** as its two literal control runes, taking the natural consecutive interior GP indices1..27 as its cube alphabet; it tested sixteen fixed cube/period/clock options ([prior p0 source gate](lp2-p0-instar-trifid27-source-gate.md)). Neither the source images nor authentic Cicada messages *instruct* Trifid or identify literal bypass runes. This is a bounded architectural test.

A genuine F/EA literal passthrough makes **two exact predictions that are independent of every possible cube key, period, direction, secret alphabet order, transposition within cube blocks and page clock**: the original ciphertext's counts of F and EA must equal those of its original plaintext. These counts survive both the forward and inverse reversible operations. An additive operation outside the cube, symbol-name relabeling that changes the two controls, hidden source rewriting, or a different plaintext language lies outside the tested class.

## Allow the two transparent identities to be entirely unknown

For perspective, each GP27 selection has exactly two control runes, and the 29-symbol inventory yields **406** unordered candidate pairs. The SFT uses the identical original GP29 histogram and checks all406 source-count pairs independently of any cube permutation. An identity pair survives this finite corpus *if at least one reference 729-rune window matches both original counts*. This permissive condition ignores all cryptographic constraints of the 27-symbol core, so it cannot create false exclusions of a genuine literal-bypass plaintext within the fixed controls.

- Canonical GP spelling: **81/406** pairs have a match in the union of three sources, hence **325/406** have none.
- Source-calibrated ING/IO spelling: **93/406** pairs have a match, hence **313/406** have none.
- **F/EA is excluded under both**. Other pairs are not automatically authenticated when their count condition passes.

These counts describe the *fixed literary registers only*. Shorter or longer opening sections, different prose, foreign languages, replacement alphabets, further cipher layers and nonliteral bypasses can have different symbol frequencies.

## Entire-manuscript, all-pair word-grammar gate

The p0 count sieve is intentionally incomplete: a hidden-key 27-cube could transparently pass through *some other two rune identities*. The original manuscript provides a stronger, qualitatively independent constraint before trying any secret keys.

The authoritative `resources/lp2-transcription.txt`, reconciled glyph-for-glyph with the canonical GP29 source, contains **2,896 complete printed ciphertext words** in the 55 rune-bearing unknown pages and **83 complete one-rune words**. These 83 words use **28 of the 29 GP rune identities** at least once; the *only rune never printed as a standalone word is C*, GP index5. If the cipher preserves original printed word breaks and writes conventional native-GP English, the only possible plain one-rune words are **A**, **I**, and archaic interjection **O**, GP indices24,10,3. A literal passthrough rune appearing alone in ciphertext must remain the same standalone plaintext rune. Therefore the 83 source singleton words immediately exclude **all except four possible transparent rune identities: O, C, I, A**. There are just **six unordered bypass pairs** satisfying this purely exact, full-book constraint:

`O/C, O/I, O/A, C/I, C/A, I/A`.

This singleton elimination does **not** require a statistical plaintext scorer, any secret Trifid cube, a period, an English dictionary beyond the allowed one-letter-word inventory, or a frequency fit. It does require that the printed `-` and period boundaries represent actual plaintext word breaks. If the ciphertext words are decoys or the plaintext is non-English, the implication fails.

Next intersect these **six preregistered-by-source singleton-compatible pairs** with the already enumerated native-GP p0–p2 **729-rune pair-frequency** census:

| Source-native writer | Of six singleton-compatible pairs passing p0–p2 exact frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Canonical GP transliteration | **None** |
| Calibrated ING/IO GP writer | **C/I only**, in 85 Gibran reference windows |

Thus **C/I** is the *only* identity pair still compatible with any original singleton and opening-chapter count screen across either native writer. It is a permissive survivor, not evidence that Cicada selected C and I.

A further unavoidable property of that pair is readable *without guessing its key*: when ciphertext word ends in literally transparent **I**, the plaintext must also end with rune **I**. In the original 2,896 printed words, **96 words longer than one GP rune end with literal I**; another **91** longer words end with C. Our fixed independent native-English sources have the following **maximum** count of long words ending I across **every** complete **2,896-word sliding window**, after source-calibrated GP encoding:

| Native-GP source | Complete 2,896-word windows | Highest count of long-word-final I | Original LP2 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gibran, *The Prophet* | 9,677 | **0** | 96 |
| Shakespeare | 52,512 | **62** | 96 |
| Emerson | 75,037 | **7** | 96 |

None of the **137,226** word-count-matched control windows reaches the original's 96 long-word-final I positions. A secret cube cannot change any of these suffix events if I is a passthrough glyph. Consequently **C/I**, the sole remaining pair under the earlier exact singleton and native-frequency screens, also fails the source-native English word-position screen on the full original manuscript.

**Combined original-input decision:** Of 406 possible GP27 two-rune literal bypass pairs, **400 fail the original singleton requirement outright** for normal GP-English word semantics; five of the remaining six have zero exact opening-count support across the fixed 1,173,158 729-rune reference windows, and the sixth C/I has no whole-book long-I-suffix support across 137,226 source word windows. Hence **zero of406 pairs remain compatible with all three necessary constraints under the explicitly tested conventional native-English corpora**. This rejects the entire two-literal-reserved-rune keyed-GP27 model as a *direct* explanation of this original manuscript under the stated source-register and printed-word hypotheses. It does **not** exclude artificially I-final prose, foreign-language inflections such as Latin, a cipher that changes word breaks, unprinted symbolic control runes, or a full29 changing-output mechanism.

Applying forward and inverse over the 729 authentic original glyphs also recovers every glyph, validating that the tested literal-bypass model is reversible. **Research decision:** Retire whole-manuscript 27-cell Trifid with any *two fixed GP glyphs left literally unchanged* as a direct native-English explanation under the already calibrated literary registers and authentic printed word-breaks. This is a complete **all-secret 406-pair necessary-condition screen**, including the earlier F/EA model.
