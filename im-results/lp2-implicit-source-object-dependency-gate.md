# Implicit source-object dependencies: exact mixed-cell and digest controls (#3158)

**Decision:** a source-local structural anchor can select an operation without explicit instruction syntax, but no new unknown LP2 object was found that supplies the indispensable same-coordinate value or typed output constraint and an independently specified receiver. This is a targeted **upstream selector failure**, not a general impossibility theorem. The following retrospective positive controls sharpen the distinction from #3152's negative verbal grammar. Neither is blind; both solutions were already known.

## Frozen rule and comparison

An implicit dependency graph must provide (1) an original operand or named primitive, (2) a separate original structural relation fixing the receiver or expected output type before full decode, and (3) an independent continuation/check that is not the anchor used to choose the operation. The anchor need not uniquely identify all parameters; any missing parameters remain missing. Do not use an output-selected phrase as independent evidence or promote a general "numeric object" to a particular cipher key.

### LP1 p5: mixed source types in the same geometric object

The original scan05 prints the named primes/totient statement and a 5×5 mixed rune/decimal square. Its 180-degree symmetry is visibly supported by the existing decimal pairs (272/272, 138/138, 131/131, 151/151, 18/18). Two central-row opposite pairs bridge types before arithmetic: printed 226 opposite MOBIUS, printed 245 opposite OBSCURA. Among the finite primitive transformations suggested by the chart and named functions:

| Candidate rune-word value | MOBIUS | OBSCURA |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rune count | 6 | 7 |
| Sum GP indices (0-based) | 65 | 69 |
| Sum GP primes | **226** | **245** |
| Sum totients of GP primes | 220 | 238 |
| Totient of sum of GP primes | 112 | 168 |

The two exact mixed pairs select prime-sum among these five; no other square cells were needed to choose it. Replace all 13 word cells (12 printed decimal cells) with GP prime sums: every row, column and both diagonals sum to 1033. Independently all 25 cells match the third magic square recovered from the authentic earlier MP3/OpenPuff object (resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt). The mapping is not a unique mathematical function among arbitrary functions, but there is a genuine cross-type shared-coordinate witness and independent exact confirmation.

### LP2 p56: typed adjacent digest anchor, independent of full prose

Original scan73 places a literal 128-character hexadecimal string between two rune passages. The first 45 native runes precede the string; the final two printed words have lengths 6+2, spanning the physical line break. The hex object can motivate hash/digest terminology without guessing unrelated prose. The earlier p5 explicitly names primes and totient, and the known LP1 controls establish transparent F as a possible clock. This is a retrospective finite test of a possible human route, not evidence that solvers historically used it.

Use four fixed primitive/sign combinations (add/subtract consecutive primes or their totients, starting at prime2 on the page), all 32 visible-F masks, and the fixed type vocabulary HASHES/HASHED/DIGEST/CIPHER × TO/AS/IS/OF (16 two-word candidates of exact 6+2 widths). Only negative totient and the phrase HASHES TO match the eight anchor runes: 16 of 128 parameter/mask paths reach and match the anchor, all share the same no-hold interpretation of visible F at35 and the same complete 45-rune decoded prefix. Twelve reach the full page under the literal-F rule. The independent prefix outside the eight anchor runes exactly reads the known 37-rune text before HASHES TO. After the digest the typed object does not select among the 12 legal masks; the known readable continuation identifies hold56. Therefore the original *object role* can narrow a finite operation library without a full-prose score, but is not sufficient for all state choices, and the start/sign family is itself hypothesized from solved context. No claim of unique general derivation.

## Transfer gate on original unknown-page objects

The strongest local numeric comparator is original p15 scan32: red-origin 4×4 board with a verified Fibonacci-prime formula. Its actual source-adjacent red title is two words of lengths 4+5, separated from the second rubric by the entire numeric board and infinity sign. It has no mixed rune/decimal counterpart at corresponding geometric cells (unlike p5). The already decoded board supplies its type and complete arithmetic but no exact textual output or consumer. The two literal LP1 numeric-square rubrics KNOW THIS are 4+3 native runes; neither they nor the single nine-rune word FIBONACCI match the printed 4+5 pattern. The latter crib and its continuation were already rejected in [the prior title gate](lp2-p15-fibonacci-title-crib-closed-loop-gate.md). Do not reopen it by selecting words from language scores.

For original p49–51's 256 two-character panel, all 256 cells share the *same* untyped pair-token representation; there is no known second representation in homologous positions, no fixed radix/codec, and no independently fixed adjacent plaintext phrase naming an output object. Its exact dimensions alone do not provide either dependency (see [prior radix gate](lp2-p50-radix-codebook-overlap-source-gate.md)). This is a contrast case, not an unused new object/receiver or another numerical-consumer sweep. The original p33–39 cuneiform pair has an established numerical reading, not a same-coordinate mapping to neighboring ciphertext words. Existing authentic transfer audits already establish the seven LP1 units and the absence of a target/key/reset mapping.

**Missing indispensable edge:** an unknown LP2 object independently specifies the receiving text positions *and* a concrete value/type constraint that distinguishes a particular operation before reading the candidate output. A general family resemblance (square, moth, 128 digits, 1033, prime, direction) is not that edge. No previously unused unknown object meeting the graph was located. Do not treat the positive retrospective controls as a new receiver selector or rescan their previously retired numeric consumers. No unknown LP2 plaintext or key.
