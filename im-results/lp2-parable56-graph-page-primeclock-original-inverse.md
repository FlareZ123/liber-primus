The previously proposed **largest-branch-first inorder**, with numeric ID ties, maps this source tree's root201 to physical p50 and the remaining 55 nodes bijectively to 55 rune-bearing physical pages.

This structural match offers an explicit candidate **page→source numeric seed channel**. A real creator-compatible operation has independently been demonstrated on solved p56: subtract the consecutive **prime totients** `φ(p_i)=p_i−1` modulo29 from each non-F ciphertext rune, treating literal plaintext F as transmitted F without consuming a prime. Rather than try new random masks or clocks, the new test sets **each page's first prime to the n-th prime**, where `n` is precisely its source-tree node address (one-based prime ordinal). The clock restarts at each printed physical page; **no offsets, signs, skipped ciphertext runes, source word changes, or fitted keys** are allowed. Because F transparency can be ambiguous, the decoder branches at every ciphertext F and keeps all algebraically possible clock indices. Failure means **all legal paths** encounter a non-F ciphertext decrypting to an impossible unescaped F.

The weakest assumptions are independently clear: neither the graph's largest-first inorder traversal, nor page-local resets across 32 authentic cross-page words, nor the node-as-prime-ordinal rule is an authored LP2 instruction.
