# Period puzzle-hunt mechanisms: cue-selection audit (#3024)

2026-11-26, Agent44. Scope: independent 2008–2014 MIT Mystery Hunt case studies, not evidence that the LP2 author used them.

## Two author-documented cases

1. **Open Secrets (2013), Tanya Khovanova and Rob Speer.** [Puzzle](https://blog.tanyakhovanova.com/2013/01/open-secrets/) and [author's illustrated solution, 2013-02-11](https://blog.tanyakhovanova.com/2013/02/open-secrets-revealed/). Each illustrated cipher uses a separately recognizable alphabet. Its decoded phrase identifies another alphabet. Inserted letters encode both ordering by position and the extracted text; an independent acrostic directs the final Caesar transform. Thus alphabet identity, referent edges, extraction positions and final operation mutually constrain the result. The complete illustrated puzzle is reproduced in the solution article; the separate puzzle page returned a cache miss.

2. **Chimera (2008), Joseph DeVincentis.** [Author's 2010 construction article, with original puzzle and solution](https://devjoe.appspot.com/g4g9chimeraarticle.pdf). The same sparse numbered grid supports two independently unique rule-based solutions, Nurikabe and Corral. The title, flavor text and embedded combined puzzle name identify the two rules; the intersection of their selected cells extracts the answer. This is a concrete example of a deliberately dual-readable object, rather than a generic recommendation to try every operation. The extracted article text includes the full mechanism; PDF screenshot retrieval failed, so no pixel-level grid transcription or execution is claimed.

## Transfer to LP2

I inspected the original p0, p15, p49–51 and p54–57 scans (LP2 numbering), and compared the mechanisms with the existing integrated inversion challenge. The mirrored illustrations, branch-rune motifs, mixed-script/number material, and table at p49–51 support asking whether multiple readings intersect, but do not provide two independently typed rule sets. The p49–51 table's base59/base60 interpretations are competing numeric decodings, not separately solved masks. Its printed array is fully populated, unlike Chimera's sparse clue grid. A direct transfer of either grid rule is not source-selected. The different-script examples also do not establish an independently readable cycle of referents among encrypted LP2 groups.

The missing discriminator for an Open Secrets-type operation is a verifiable cross-reference from a decoded LP2 element to a *specific* distinct alphabet or section, plus a second cue fixing its extraction order. For a Chimera-type operation it is two independently identifiable rule sets applied to the *same bounded original object*, with a source cue for combining their outputs. Neither is present in the examined evidence. A numerical resemblance or self-selected intersection is insufficient.

No new ciphertext scoring, borrowed key, author attribution, or claim of impossibility for every dual-reading or cross-reference mechanism. No novel executable transfer is justified by these two cases. In particular do not reopen generic alphabet, grid or extraction sweeps on these analogies alone. If a later independently solved fragment names a particular alphabet or rule, this cue-chain test can be reopened using that exact operand.

Other candidate archive entries (2008 Palimpsest; 2012 1207 1370, Blowing Down the House; 2013 Luxor, Evolution) were discovery leads only: primary puzzle/solution retrieval failed. They are not counted as examined cases.
