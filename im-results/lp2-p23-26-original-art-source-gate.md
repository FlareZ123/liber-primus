# LP2 physical pp23–26: original-art clue-to-decoder gate

**Decision for Director Issue #531, 12 November 2026.** The original pp23–26 source provides a four-page, **1,021-GP-rune** visually bounded chapter. The five-dot figure and exactly **1,005 post-heading runes = 201 complete quintets**, independently noted by Agent43 on #531, form a real source-delimited five-unit candidate. Direct quintet folds, historical five-share operations, exhaustive modern/classical Baconian decoding, and a key-independent five-lane common-pad test have been completed. None recovers plaintext or selects a complete original-cipher inverse. No source specifies a complete original-cipher inverse or key. The most specific candidate suggested by the illustrations is a left/right or top/bottom reversal, perhaps routed by the five dots above the opening, but neither a traversal order nor a substitution/keystream is printed. The exactly repeated side art prevents treating each illustrated page as an independently supplied, unique *visible* per-page mask. **No unknown plaintext, key or destination was recovered.**

A stronger **source-grouped, exact, language-independent code check** tests for a fixed linear or affine five-symbol parity equation over the authentic GP29 index field. For all **201** source-ordered quintets, the 201×5 original matrix has GF(29) rank **5**, and its 201×6 matrix with a constant 1 column has rank **6**. Reinterpreting the original body as five contiguous 201-rune rows, then taking one rune from each row, also has ranks **5** and **6**. Thus **no nonzero, fixed affine relation** \(a_0C_0+\cdots+a_4C_4=b\pmod{29}\) holds for every one of the 201 groups under either natural, zero-offset source layout. A positive plant setting its fifth rune to the sum of the first four instead has rank **4** (affine rank **5**), so the test recognizes an authentic linear checksum when present. This precisely excludes a static, public **linear parity/rune-checksum** for these two locked five-position layouts; it does not rule out nonlinear, keyed or variable-state five-symbol coding.

Importantly, reducing five symbols to one through a sum is many-to-one. Without an independent rule specifying the four redundant or random symbols, such a fold cannot satisfy the complete reversible original-cipher requirement. A high language score alone would not identify a correct forward encoder.

## Five 201-rune lanes: exact shared-additive-pad obstruction

The five black dots and 1,005-rune post-red16 body also select a **five-lane common-key hypothesis**, which has a more immediate path to plaintext than another arbitrary quintet fold: if five independently native-GP plaintext passages were enciphered with the *same 201-position additive pad*, corresponding ciphertext symbols would expose plaintext relationships through their pairwise differences, without knowing a single key value. The naturally selected layouts are (a) columns of the 201 chronological quintets and (b) five consecutive 201-rune stretches. Both preserve all 1,005 original GP29 symbols and the source-fixed heading boundary.

For five 201-rune lanes \(C_{a,j}\), let \(h_{ab}(d)\) count positions where \(C_{a,j}-C_{b,j}\equiv d\pmod{29}\). Any shared unknown additive pad \(K_j\) cancels exactly. With a further arbitrary secret **constant Caesar offset per lane**, the histogram shifts cyclically, so its maximum value is unchanged. Define \(T=\sum_{0\le a<b<5}\max_{d=0}^{28}h_{ab}(d)\). This is an **all-pad and all-lane-Caesar invariant**. The upper-bound interpretation is deliberately generous: each of the ten lane pairs may choose its best shift separately, even when no five consistent lane offsets produce all ten maxima simultaneously. Without independent lane shifts, the more direct count is \(E=\sum_{a<b}h_{ab}(0)\). There are exactly \(10\cdot201=2,010\) aligned lane-pair positions.

| Original-input lane arrangement | Direct equalities E/2,010 | Independent-pair shift upper bound T |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One rune from each chronological five-rune group | **55/2,010 = 0.02736** | **126** |
| Five successive 201-rune blocks | **60/2,010 = 0.02985** | **122** |

For native controls, the same source's Gematria Primus transliteration rule maps pinned English references to GP29. Split them into **nonoverlapping** 1,005-rune source windows and five contiguous 201-rune plaintext lanes without language-score tuning. The source lengths and window counts are fixed by the external reference files.

| Reference language control | Disjoint windows | Minimum direct E | Minimum all-shifts T | Windows with T≤126 | Windows with T≤122 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

| Emerson, *Essays* | 323 | 100 | 125 | 1 | 0 |

The native common-pad **positive control** masks five 201-symbol Gibran lanes with an independently generated 201-symbol pad and five secret per-lane Caesar offsets \([0,7,13,23,28]\); every GP rune is recovered exactly when the planted key is supplied, and the fully unknown-key statistic \(T=144\) is exactly preserved. Thus the original ciphertext falls below **601/602** native controls for the five-quintet-position layout and below **602/602** for the five-block layout, despite granting every source pair an independently optimized unknown offset. The finite add-one lower-tail frequencies are 2/603 and 1/603 respectively; these are *observed sample comparisons*, not proof about every possible English text or a universally calibrated probabilistic claim. The uniform-looking original pair differences and zero-phase equalities independently argue against a shared 201-rune pad for ordinary GP-native prose.

The [runnable source-native CommonJS SFT](../sims/lp2_p23_26_five_lane_sharedpad_sft.cjs) freezes both layouts, asserts the exact original counts, tests three reference corpora and performs the planted full-lossless positive control. This **retires only** the otherwise attractive five-strand *shared additive GP29 pad* architecture, even when each strand has its own unknown constant shift. It leaves distinct pads, ciphertext/plaintext feedback, nonadditive substitution, alternative alphabet/registers, reordered symbols, and non-native-language plaintext unresolved. The five-dot artwork still supplies no instruction that a common additive pad is intended; its use here is a motivated falsifiable hypothesis, not a recovered mechanism.

## Five-dot Baconian hypothesis: exhaustive all-secret key search

A human solver encountering five black dots and a source-delimited **201×5** body might try a Bacon cipher: assign each of the **29 original GP rune identities** an unknown fixed 0/1 value, read each consecutive quintet as a five-bit integer, and interpret that number as an alphabetic character. That creates a concrete, potentially plaintext-producing operation with only **29 secret bits**, much smaller than a free 201-position pad. Crucially, the *entire admissible keyspace can be enumerated exactly* by rejecting the forbidden letter codes **before** using an English score. Both printed quintet order and a literal mirrored quintet (suggested, though not selected, by the bilateral page decorations) are source-natural. Both original and reversed 201-group order are scored.

### Modern A=0 through Z=25 (26-character) readout

For a source quintet \((g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3,g_4)\), let \(x[g]\in\{0,1\}\) be the one permanent unknown bit for that rune. The decoded code is \(16x[g_0]+8x[g_1]+4x[g_2]+2x[g_3]+x[g_4]\). The code is invalid under literal A=0,...,Z=25 exactly when the first **two** bits and either the **third or fourth** bit are all one. Consequently, each of the 201 groups yields the two forbidden triples \(\{g_0,g_1,g_2\}\) and \(\{g_0,g_1,g_3\}\), interpreted as bitmask hyperedges (including naturally collapsed two-rune constraints when symbols repeat). The original source has **375 distinct forbidden hyperedges** in printed quintet orientation and **385** in mirrored orientation.

Rather than fit a few arbitrary rune-to-bit mappings, the [complete original-source CJS SFT](../sims/lp2_p23_26_bacon_exhaustive_sft.cjs) recursively enumerates every 29-bit assignment that avoids **all** forbidden hyperedges. Search-tree pruning is exact because a completed all-one forbidden edge can never be repaired by assigning additional bits. It counts and scores every surviving key without an omitted heuristic branch:

| Locked orientation | Exact valid 29-bit keys | Maximum one-bit rune identities | Candidate forward/reversed texts |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Printed five-symbol groups | **90,836** | 10 | 181,672 |
| Mirror each printed group | **73,362** | 10 | 146,724 |
| **Total** | **164,198** | | **328,396** |

The exhaustive output-readout uses a normalized alphabetic **quadgram model trained from the pinned Gibran, Emerson, and Shakespeare control texts**, excluding a 201-letter mid-book holdout from each. For printed and mirrored quintets, respectively, the *best across every key and either source-natural passage direction* scores **−2964.920** and **−2940.749**. The three untouched native-English 201-letter holdouts score **−1827.994**, **−1836.508**, and **−1848.795** under the same model. The best original outputs remain visibly nonlinguistic; the highest-scoring mirrored readout begins `UETFDAKSEADAQYSQAEADBESAQGAUBJUISABDHIQADIEZFAEACARESAKQYU...`. A positive encoder draws rune identities from two secret bit classes to carry the withheld Gibran passage through all **201 groups**. The planted GP output is exactly reversible, and the exhaustive search finds that same native-English string as its highest-scoring decoded passage. The negative therefore comes from actual source constraints, not failure of the decoder or ranking implementation.

It fits an independent, heldout-excluding 26-letter quadgram model per source, then enumerates and scores **every same** 90,836+73,362 secret bit-key assignment under both paragraph directions. The four original search orientations and every admissible secret 29-bit map are tested again, so this is an explicit bounded decoder negative beyond the first three English references. These scores are evidence against **this fixed 26-character codebook interpretation of native English**, and the exact key count is unconditional under the fixed binary-mask model. An alternate 26-letter labeling, a second cipher, word segmentation, a changing bit assignment, or plaintext in another language/register are distinct hypotheses.

### Historically classical Bacon alphabet (24 characters)

The older Bacon system combines **I/J** and **U/V**, with the literal 24-character alphabet `ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPQRSTUWXYZ` and codes **0..23**. This historical variant has a *stronger* all-key source obstruction: any output code ≥24 means the **first two encoded rune bits are simultaneously one**. No five-bit mapping respecting a stationary 29-rune bit key may allow that on any of the 201 original groups. The resulting original source forbids **159 distinct pairs** in printed orientation or **158** in mirrored orientation. All feasible keys are exactly enumerable:

| Locked orientation | All admissible 29-bit keys | Maximum one-bit rune identities | Minimum occurrences of output A |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Printed quintets | **2,574** | 8 | **40 of 201** |
| Mirrored quintets | **2,650** | 7 | **45 of 201** |
| **Total** | **5,224** | | |

The minimum-A result is an **exact exhaustive lower bound**, valid against *every* unknown stationary rune-to-bit assignment that produces legal historical Bacon letters. Reversing the group order does not change it. For a reference comparison the [historical 24-letter exact SFT](../sims/lp2_p23_26_bacon24_exhaustive_sft.cjs) normalizes J→I and V→U and counts A in **3,164 nonoverlapping 201-letter** source windows: Gibran **330** windows, maximum A-count **28**; Emerson **1,693**, maximum **31**; Shakespeare **1,141**, maximum **28**. **Zero of 3,164** reference windows reach **40** instances of A, whereas every allowable original-source Bacon key forces at least 40, or 45 under quintet mirroring. This is a decisive key-independent frequency obstruction for ordinary 24-letter native prose in the sampled register. A global letter permutation can rename A, but the independent all-symbol concentration test below also constrains that possibility.

**Independent non-modern-language controls:** Two additional sources already pinned in the repository are the Latin Vulgate New Testament (original word-separated GP29 numeral stream) and corrected Old English *Beowulf* (GP29 indices serialized with its own 29-character alphabet). Decode each original GP29 source symbol through the canonical rune transliterations, merge J→I and V→U, and take disjoint 201-letter text windows. Latin yields **208,680** normalized letters, **1,038** full windows, maximum **29 A**. Old English yields **52,805** normalized letters, **262** full windows, maximum **31 A**. Thus **0/1,300** Latin/Old English controls reach the exact original Bacon minimum 40 A. Together with 3,164 English controls, **0/4,464** pinned controls over three related historical-language registers reach 40 A. The historical 24-letter SFT reproduces both source serializations and asserts every count.

**Stronger all-secret *letter-permutation-invariant* exclusion:** A hidden **global 24-letter permutation** could change which named output letter is A but cannot change the maximum count of any letter in the 201-letter decoded string. The exact enumerator checks that **every** stationary binary map valid in classical Bacon has some symbol appearing **≥40** times in the printed orientation or **≥45** times with each quintet mirrored, without using an English model. In the same 4,464 disjoint English/Latin/Old-English reference windows, only **10/4,464** have *any* letter occurring 40 or more times (**Emerson 3, Vulgate 5, Beowulf 2; Gibran 0, Shakespeare 0**), and **0/4,464** contain any letter 45 or more times (the maximum per source is Gibran 38, Emerson 42, Shakespeare 39, Vulgate 44, Beowulf 40). This closes the earlier loophole of a *single global output-symbol permutation* for the quintet-mirrored historical Bacon model under the finite sampled native registers, and makes the printed orientation extremely atypical even under arbitrary renaming. It remains a finite reference-language exclusion rather than a universal ban on all postprocessing or all conceivable prose. The exact [historical Bacon SFT](../sims/lp2_p23_26_bacon24_exhaustive_sft.cjs) asserts these full-alphabet comparisons as well as the named-A counts. This strengthens only the bounded original-plaintext register comparison; it does not exclude other languages or a post-Bacon permutation, and the two old-language controls are transliterations rather than original manuscripts in 24-letter Bacon spelling.

For completeness, the exhaustive 24-letter quadgram scores for the best original print/mirror keys are **−2841.525 / −2802.549**, compared with independent heldout native-GP-orthographic Latinized 24-letter scores **−1824.799 / −1830.806 / −1845.329**. An original-length classical Bacon *positive plant* also recovers all **201** heldout source letters byte-for-byte and wins the exact all-key search. Thus both the historical codebook and modern A–Z extension are actually invertible when the requisite bit key exists.

### Independent finite known-quotation crib exclusion

The earlier [stationary Baconian crib scanner](../sims/lp2_p23_26_bacon_crib_scan_sft.cjs) takes a complementary all-key approach: each candidate letter from a purported 201-letter source quotation forces the five associated rune-bit assignments, immediately rejecting contradictions. It searched every 201-letter window in the pinned Shakespeare (229,448 normalized letters), Emerson (340,374), Gibran (66,521), and Blake (2,170) texts, in four source-picture-compatible orders: printed, quintet-mirrored, reversed group order, and both reversed. Every one of these 16 source/order combinations failed even the first 12 decoded letters (60 original runes); a positively planted homophone Bacon text passes. This remains a finite quotation-reuse negative, while the new exhaustive key census covers all legal **26-character** and classical **24-character** Bacon keys whether or not the presumed plaintext quotes those sources.

**Decoder decision:** The striking five-dot / 201×5 pairing motivates an exact two-class Bacon attack, and its complete keyspaces were tested without secret-key guessing or fit-dependent pruning. No plaintext or runic bit key is recovered from either conventional alphabet. The two-class model is retired as an original-source native-English decoder in the declared orientations. The page artwork does **not** specify which rune shapes belong to bit class one, and it does not authenticate Bacon as the intended operation. New work would require an independent source rule selecting a different symbol register, variable bit clock, or original decoding instruction; arbitrary relabeling/bit-fitting alone does not create a new finding.

## The bounded clue→mechanism decision

The **five dots** plus the padding-free 201×5 body make a naturally selected *group size* of five, but do not select a five-column rail/route transposition, a Bacon/5-bit representation, a particular five-rune arithmetic operator, a constellation reading, a keyed inverse, or a specific GP29 numeric key. The **bilaterally symmetrical repeated winged-insect** could invite reversal or alternating read directions, yet does not specify which runes to rearrange, the original plaintext alphabet or a substitution. A pure permutation of rune positions leaves all unigram counts exactly unchanged and therefore cannot by itself reconstruct ordinary GP29-transcribed English from this chapter's nearly uniform ciphertext distribution. An additional unspecified changing-key cipher would be needed.

As a finite source-native calibration, the p23 red 16-rune heading was read under the historically solved **GP29 Atbash**, **Atbash+3**, **DIVINITY Vigenère subtraction**, the historical LP1 **FIRFUMFERENFE** working key, and the solved-p56 **prime-totient subtraction**, all phase-zero and with **no inserted skips**. Their respective GP transliterations are `IA-R-NG-W-OE-X-C-OE-E-J-E-T-EO-J-E-TH`, `U-W-A-I-AE-B-H-AE-NG-X-NG-M-S-X-NG-C`, `W-X-F-J-Y-R-W-N-T-W-O-TH-W-W-D-F`, `U-X-O-NG-C-A-D-B-G-EA-U-EO-IA-B-F-OE`, and `F-OE-O-S-AE-TH-W-B-B-E-N-C-C-R-OE-O`. None contains a recognizable English title or authentic next instruction. These **title-only negative transfers** are not complete-chapter exclusions and do not exhaust historically unknown per-page keys.

**Strongest alternative:** the dots and marginal art are chapter typography and illustration only, with the key or readable instructions outside the chapter or concealed in an unrecovered channel. Nothing in the source currently separates that explanation from a cryptographic use of the art.

**Director consequence:** preserve the source-delimited 201×5 body as a candidate input for a future independently selected five-rune block operation, but do not create another unconstrained five-rail/five-bit/mirror-cipher search branch from this artwork alone. Invest in independent source evidence that fixes an operation, key and exact source positions; a new original-page clue would justify reopening. This no-selector result is bounded to pp23–26 and its immediate visual boundaries, and should remain supporting evidence rather than promotion to decrypted results.
