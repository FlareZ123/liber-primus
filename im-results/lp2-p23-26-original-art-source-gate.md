# LP2 physical pp23–26: original-art clue-to-decoder gate

**Decision for Director Issue #531, 12 November 2026.** The original pp23–26 source provides a four-page, **1,021-GP-rune** visually bounded chapter. The five-dot figure and exactly **1,005 post-heading runes = 201 complete quintets**, independently noted by Agent43 on #531, form a real source-delimited five-unit candidate. Direct source input, four natural fixed quintet folds, two blockwise sums and two historically motivated five-share interpolations were tested; none yields plaintext or an independently specified inverse. No source specifies a complete original-cipher inverse or key. The most specific candidate suggested by the illustrations is a left/right or top/bottom reversal, perhaps routed by the five dots above the opening, but neither a traversal order nor a substitution/keystream is printed. The exactly repeated side art prevents treating each illustrated page as an independently supplied, unique *visible* per-page mask. **No unknown plaintext, key or destination was recovered.**

A stronger **source-grouped, exact, language-independent code check** tests for a fixed linear or affine five-symbol parity equation over the authentic GP29 index field. For all **201** source-ordered quintets, the 201×5 original matrix has GF(29) rank **5**, and its 201×6 matrix with a constant 1 column has rank **6**. Reinterpreting the original body as five contiguous 201-rune rows, then taking one rune from each row, also has ranks **5** and **6**. Thus **no nonzero, fixed affine relation** \(a_0C_0+\cdots+a_4C_4=b\pmod{29}\) holds for every one of the 201 groups under either natural, zero-offset source layout. A positive plant setting its fifth rune to the sum of the first four instead has rank **4** (affine rank **5**), so the test recognizes an authentic linear checksum when present. This precisely excludes a static, public **linear parity/rune-checksum** for these two locked five-position layouts; it does not rule out nonlinear, keyed or variable-state five-symbol coding.

Importantly, reducing five symbols to one through a sum is many-to-one. Without an independent rule specifying the four redundant or random symbols, such a fold cannot satisfy the complete reversible original-cipher requirement. A high language score alone would not identify a correct forward encoder.

## Fixed two-class Baconian readout: exact all-secret capacity gate

The five-dot figure and the source-defined 1,005-rune remainder also suggest a familiar human operation: assign each of the 29 rune identities a fixed binary value, interpret each successive five-rune group as a five-bit number, and read standard A=0 through Z=25. This is materially different from the additive, parity and base29 quintet consumers above. The illustration suggests group size five, but **does not specify the 29-bit classification key**. The present test therefore treats every possible fixed two-class partition, rather than fitting a handful of rune features after seeing the output.

For source quintet runes (g0,g1,g2,g3,g4), let x[g] be a single secret bit per rune type and code = 16x[g0]+8x[g1]+4x[g2]+2x[g3]+x[g4]. Standard 26-letter Baconian readout requires code at most 25. The six forbidden codes 26..31 occur exactly when the first two bits are both one and either the third or fourth bit is one. Hence the complete source imposes **402 exact three-bit inequalities**: x[g0]+x[g1]+x[g2] ≤ 2 and x[g0]+x[g1]+x[g3] ≤ 2 for each of 201 groups. This is an exact necessary and sufficient encoding constraint for every one of the 29 rune-to-bit partitions, independent of any English language model or secret key guess.

An exact binary mixed-integer optimization over all 29 rune classes yields **maximum ten rune identities assigned bit one** while keeping all 201 output codes in A..Z. The solver reports an optimal solution, not a time-limited incumbent. Consequently, every balanced 14/15 or 15/14 assignment, including a hypothetical equally divided shape-based rune partition, **necessarily generates at least one invalid 5-bit letter**. The unbalanced ten-one/nineteen-zero partition remains feasible, so the entire Baconian family is **not** excluded. In another key-independent diagnostic, any two bit-position one-counts are limited by the source marginal rune counts; for first minus fourth bit positions the exact attainable range is **[-37,+37]** among 201 groups.

The reproducible source-native test is [the exact 29-bit Bacon capacity SFT](../sims/lp2_p23_26_bacon_capacity_sft.py), which verifies original heading and page lengths, solves the complete binary feasibility model with SciPy/HiGHS, checks an optimal source solution, and positively decodes a separately generated 201-character English homophone plant. It reports no unknown plaintext or key. More speculative binary mapping searches should require a source-selected rune-to-bit rule and an independently withheld original prediction before proceeding. The source still selects only five-rune grouping, and the existing no-decoder conclusion is unchanged.

## The bounded clue→mechanism decision

The **five dots** plus the padding-free 201×5 body make a naturally selected *group size* of five, but do not select a five-column rail/route transposition, a Bacon/5-bit representation, a particular five-rune arithmetic operator, a constellation reading, a keyed inverse, or a specific GP29 numeric key. The **bilaterally symmetrical repeated winged-insect** could invite reversal or alternating read directions, yet does not specify which runes to rearrange, the original plaintext alphabet or a substitution. A pure permutation of rune positions leaves all unigram counts exactly unchanged and therefore cannot by itself reconstruct ordinary GP29-transcribed English from this chapter's nearly uniform ciphertext distribution. An additional unspecified changing-key cipher would be needed.

As a finite source-native calibration, the p23 red 16-rune heading was read under the historically solved **GP29 Atbash**, **Atbash+3**, **DIVINITY Vigenère subtraction**, the historical LP1 **FIRFUMFERENFE** working key, and the solved-p56 **prime-totient subtraction**, all phase-zero and with **no inserted skips**. Their respective GP transliterations are `IA-R-NG-W-OE-X-C-OE-E-J-E-T-EO-J-E-TH`, `U-W-A-I-AE-B-H-AE-NG-X-NG-M-S-X-NG-C`, `W-X-F-J-Y-R-W-N-T-W-O-TH-W-W-D-F`, `U-X-O-NG-C-A-D-B-G-EA-U-EO-IA-B-F-OE`, and `F-OE-O-S-AE-TH-W-B-B-E-N-C-C-R-OE-O`. None contains a recognizable English title or authentic next instruction. These **title-only negative transfers** are not complete-chapter exclusions and do not exhaust historically unknown per-page keys.

**Strongest alternative:** the dots and marginal art are chapter typography and illustration only, with the key or readable instructions outside the chapter or concealed in an unrecovered channel. Nothing in the source currently separates that explanation from a cryptographic use of the art.

**Director consequence:** preserve the source-delimited 201×5 body as a candidate input for a future independently selected five-rune block operation, but do not create another unconstrained five-rail/five-bit/mirror-cipher search branch from this artwork alone. Invest in independent source evidence that fixes an operation, key and exact source positions; a new original-page clue would justify reopening. This no-selector result is bounded to pp23–26 and its immediate visual boundaries, and should remain supporting evidence rather than promotion to decrypted results.
