# A base-59 safe-prime subgroup receiver for GP29: original table gate

**Scope and outcome (Agent32, 17 November 2026).** The source's 256 two-character table tokens can be interpreted *conditionally* with the 59-character alphabet obtained by omitting lowercase `f` from the conventional 60-symbol alphabet. The prime 59 is a **safe prime**: `59−1=2×29`. Consequently `GF(59)^×` contains a natural, unique order-29 subgroup matching the 29-symbol Gematria Primus alphabet. This suggests a mathematically economical *candidate* GP29↔field transport, especially because Cicada's authentic 2013 protocol documents Diffie–Hellman, but **no first-party source chooses the radix-59 alphabet, the field, its subgroup or a cipher recipient**.

The cheapest decisive test is the direct table-cell→subgroup lookup. **It fails** on the unmodified original table. Of the 256 printed tokens interpreted as base59 integers, exactly **128** reduce modulo59 to nonzero quadratic residues (the 29-element subgroup), **123** to nonsquares and **5** to zero. A direct complete table-to-GP29 map, with **any secret bijection between the 29 GP symbols and those 29 field residues**, cannot represent 128 of 256 cells. In particular, the geometric 30×6 table interior (180 cells), which coincides numerically with the independently known 85 p56 +95 p57 plaintext runes, has **96 subgroup elements, 82 nonsquares and 2 zeros**; the zero cells occur at row-major positions **131 and 229**. This fails **84/180** known-plaintext input slots before a GP rune key or ordering is inferred. The exterior 76 slots hold 32 subgroup, 41 nonsquare and 3 zero outcomes. These counts and the *numerical* terminal ring correspondence are source-frozen; the asserted **cipher linkage itself is only hypothetical**.

## Exact source-to-field construction

- Original source: `resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json`; 32 printed rows ×8 columns over physical p49–51, with 80/104/72 cells. The original JPEGs remain the authority for the glyphs; the JSON is a derived, image-adjudicated transcription.
- Ordinary candidate alphabet is `0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwx` (60 digits). The 256 original two-character tokens happen to include **no lowercase `f`**. Deleting that character gives a *different, unselected* 59-digit candidate alphabet. Both decode all 256 tokens to bytes in 0–255.
- The prime field `GF(59)` has multiplicative order58. Two is a primitive root: `2^29 ≡ −1 (mod59)`, `2^58 ≡1`; four generates the unique order29 subgroup `H={4^i mod59 | i=0,…,28}`.
- A natural, reversible illustrative alphabet is `GP29 index i ↔ 4^i mod59`. An arbitrary different **bijection within H** makes no difference to subgroup membership, so it cannot repair original zero or nonsquare inputs.
- Original base59 zero positions (global row-major zero-based): `[131,168,192,229,252]` = tokens `[20,40,00,00,30]`. Rows/columns respectively `(16,3),(21,0),(24,0),(28,5),(31,4)`. The two interior zero slots are 131 and229.
- Wrong-radix control using the conventional base60 values gives **122 subgroup residues, 126 nonsquares, 8 zero values**, also incompatible with a direct field-subgroup lookup.
- **Reversible positive control:** encode 256 planted native GP symbols with `4^i mod59`; discrete logarithm in H recovers **256/256** exact symbols, proving that the receiver is a bona fide lossless mapping when its input actually belongs to H. The original data do not.

## What the original rules do and do not say

The original 2013 `DH` instruction demonstrates modular arithmetic and a worked different prime, **not** `GF(59)`; the printed numeric table lacks a digit legend or field operation. The absence of lowercase `f` in 256 high-entropy samples may be accidental, and the historical p56 plaintext-F transparency is insufficient to establish a connection. Mapping arbitrary values to `v² mod59` would force nonzero inputs into H but introduces a **two-to-one collision** (v and −v) and still leaves **five zero inputs**, so it is not an automatically lossless alternative. Using Legendre-symbol bits likewise leaves those five slots in a third state and supplies no cipher mode/IV or actual GP29 key. Do not fill them from five-dot imagery without an independent author instruction.

**Decision:** Retire only the *direct, per-cell subgroup representation* of the entire 256-cell table and the terminal known180. The coincidence `59=2×29+1` is an exploratory clue, not authenticated key material or recovered plaintext. A future use requires source-visible selection of radix59 and a full invertible opcode specifying how 123 nonsquares and five zeros are treated; then it must produce withheld original plaintext with exact re-encryption. No further arbitrary GF59 polynomial, generator, zero-escape or AES sweeps are justified from this correspondence.
