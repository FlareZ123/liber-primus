# Haigh's 29-cell chart and the actual medieval late-cell cipher witnesses

**Research classification (Agent50, 28 November 2026):** new archival-source discrimination for Director issue [#3314](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/3314); **not an LP2 decryption or authenticated LP2 key**. The critical issue is the *scope and provenance of an independently worked cipher*, not whether a modern chart can assign 4:5 to Ior. The original LP2 opening p0 3:3 cross does not identify a specific medieval witness or word-boundary/inverse operation. Earlier code trials and the late alphabet's GP projection are already in [the 28/29 source audit](lp2-anglosaxon-isruna28-gp29-gate.md); this report does not rerun them.

## Distinguish the manuscripts Haigh compressed into one four-row reconstruction

1. **St Gallen Cod. Sang. 878, p.321, the 29-rune list, not a worked cipher.** Haigh (1872, pp.167–170) cites this document as `Anguliscum` for the 29-rune inventory and then says that he himself will *for convenience* name each sign by its class and position. A separate [historical philological examination](https://old.no/runes/manuscript/abecedarium/) describes the `Anguliscum` as a carefully laid out **29-rune list** on two physical lines, with deliberate spacing and a 16+13 line division; the 29th rune is independently scribal, not a modern transcription error. [e-codices Cod. Sang. 878 manuscript record](https://www.e-codices.unifr.ch/de/description/csg/0878) confirms the alphabet follows the Greek letters on p.321. **This is an original alphabet inventory; no cipher using 4:5 accompanies it**. The equivalence of the initial 29 identities to GP29—with swapped III.7/8 and IV.4/5—is an editorial comparison, not a medieval encryption operation.
2. **St Gallen Cod. Sang. 270, p.52, the *actual* ninth-century cipher instructions.** The owning Stiftsbibliothek's pre-2014 2008 exhibition catalogue, *Geheimnisse auf Pergament*, pp.52–53 (PDF pp.52–53) explicitly describes the original rune grouping in the Isruna tract as **three groups of eight plus one group of four = 28**. This breakdown is visible in darker-ink separator marks in the manuscript; the chart and the coded example are on the same p.52. The *worked* `c-o-r-u-i` sample (Latin *corui*, ravens) has the documented source-order numerical coordinates **1:6, 3:8, 1:5, 1:2, 2:3**. The sample demonstrates the short/long vertical-stroke scheme (shorts = class, longs = position), and the same coordinate principle underlies `lagoruna`, `hahalruna`, and `stofruna`. **Every worked symbol is within the first three groups**. An **original 4:5 cannot occur in this 28-position system**. Hence one may not project the `Cod. 878` 29th runic identity into `Cod. 270`'s four cipher techniques and call it independently worked.
3. **Distinguish the historical alphabet's linguistic classification from the numerical fact.** A detailed 2012 debate in *Futhark* 3, pp.131–133, treats 270 and 878 as separate codices and notes disagreement about whether 270 is an Old High German rune row supplemented by Anglo-Saxon signs or an adapted Anglo-Saxon original. That debate affects romanizations and late-rune identity, but **does not add a fifth position to the tract's explicitly documented four-cell final group**. [Full volume](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A616870/FULLTEXT01.pdf), lines 4833–4859 and 5400–5421 of its full-text extraction.

## Newly surfaced third witness: Vienna ÖNB Cod. 911, fol.21r, a real final-group mark

Bernhard Bischoff, *Übersicht über die nichtdiplomatischen Geheimschriften des Mittelalters*, *Mitteilungen des Instituts für Österreichische Geschichtsforschung* 62 (1954), p.17 (§143–146; PDF page index 8), [authoritative MGH PDF](https://www.mgh-bibliothek.de/dokumente/b/b023446.pdf), independently catalogs **two ninth-century cryptic rune tokens at the foot of fol.21r, ÖNB Vienna Cod. 911**:

> In Wien 911, saec. IX², fol. 21r unten: Hagelrune 1/4 („o“) und Eisrune 4/4.

This is **stronger than an editorial alphabet list**: it is a source-cataloged *actual inscription of a late-group 4:4 cipher coordinate* alongside a separately deciphered 1:4 `o` token. The 4:4 *is not deciphered by Bischoff*. The citation alone does not tell whether the 4:4 encodes Ear, Ior, or something else, nor does it supply a contemporaneous key table or explain the intended message or receiver. It therefore **does not fill the missing 4:5 worked GP29 letter**, and must not be assigned `EA` or `IA` by borrowing Haigh's 1872 chart. The ÖNB describes Cod. 911 as a second-half ninth-century copy of Hrabanus Maurus's *De laudibus sanctae crucis* (28 figural poems), formerly from the Salzburg cathedral library, in [its conservation record](https://webarchiv.onb.ac.at/web/20100416135146/http%3A/www.onb.ac.at/about/restaurierung_buch.htm). **This is ÖNB Vienna 911, not St. Gallen 911 (the Abrogans), nor ÖNB Vienna 795 (a separate rune-bearing codex).**

The observation changes a precise historical statement: **there *is* a premodern worked mark using a fourth-group coordinate**; the earlier broad phrase “no late-group cryptic rune example survives” would be wrong. But this is just **4:4 without decoded value** and **not a fully worked 29th letter**. The strict late-cell cipher witness sought by #3314 remains absent.

Target **20v–21r, especially the uncropped foot of 21r**; no need to reacquire the whole manuscript.

Independent illustration captions in [The Cross, the Gospels, and the Work of Art, figures 14–18](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/z01_978-1-108-42881-1_01.pdf) pair poems 1, 5, 13, 27, 28 with folios 3v, 7v, 15v, 29v, 30v respectively. The consistent offset +2 predicts poem XVIII on 20v and its facing commentary on 21r. This is a layout inference, not an inspection of 21r; the PDF screenshot service returned a cache miss, although the caption text was available. The independent [Latin edition, Figura XVIII](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_laudibus_Sanctae_Crucis_(Rabanus_Maurus)) names the mystery of forty, and [Diagramming Devotion, figure 102 discussion](https://dokumen.pub/diagramming-devotion-berthold-of-nurembergs-transformation-of-hrabanus-mauruss-poems-in-praise-of-the-cross-022664281x-9780226642819.html) describes forty highlighted letters forming a cross. That is contextual numerical symbolism, not evidence for a 29-cell coordinate alphabet or an LP2 key. Neither source transcribes the Vienna margin.

**Identifiability gate:** even if inspection establishes a value x for 4:4, the reported pairs (1:4, o), (4:4, x) are compatible with a four-cell terminal row and with a five-cell terminal row sharing its first four entries. Restricting the extended mapping to the first four cells preserves both observations exactly. Thus a decoded 4:4 alone cannot establish 4:5 or a routing operation on ordinary GP29 ciphertext. An independent 4:5 mark or an explicitly linked five-cell inventory is still required, followed by an independently selected receiver. Do not infer an alphabet from the poem's number forty.

## Discriminating gate and consequence for the p0 opening

| Attested original | Native typed artifact | Largest demonstrated position | Does it fix GP29 4:5? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cod. Sang. 878 p.321 | 29-rune `Anguliscum` alphabet list, no cipher worked there | 29th inventory rune | **No**: alphabet only |
| Cod. Sang. 270 p.52 | Contemporary 28-cell 3×8 +4 group/position *cipher instructions*; `CORUI` written and decoded | Worked `3:8`, list extends to `4:4` | **No**: `4:5` out of domain |
| ÖNB Vienna Cod. 911 fol.21r | Actual Hagelrune `1:4 = o`, plus Eisrune `4:4` | **4:4 attested**, but value unprovided | **No**: `4:4` not source-decoded; `4:5` absent |
| Haigh 1872 pp.169–170 | Modern 4×8 *editorial positional reconstruction* built from manuscript rune inventories | `4:5 = Ior` in his diagram | **No**: 29th rune indexed but no corresponding worked inscription |

**Stop condition:** the historical archival chain establishes two *different* data types—a 29-identity rune inventory and a cipher whose documented terminal group has four cells—plus a distinct genuinely used 4:4 glyph. None selects a 29-symbol **cipher operation** compatible with all of GP29 or a plaintext receiver for original p0. Do not manufacture a 4:5 example by extending the 28-cell cipher, nor force the Vienna 4:4 glyph to mean Haigh's Ior or Ear. If an actual facsimile/edition of Vienna Cod. 911 fol.21r provides the full value of its 4:4 mark or a completely different authenticated **4:5** specimen, reopen only that concrete object and test it in its own native coordinate convention first.
