### Source-native floral berry rule: a historically selected geometric operator, still no plaintext

A 16 February 2016 23:52–23:56 conversation in the original later log (L298219–34) explicitly identifies the **berries on the page-margin plants** and their **clockwise five-site pattern \`ABAAB\`**, giving the address list \`floor((5*N−1)/3)\` as a formula for one color. For N=1,2,3,… that enumerates sites **1,3,4,6,8,9,11,13,14,…**, yielding five-period word \`ABAAB\`. The original p55 left dots physically read **filled/hollow/filled/filled/hollow** clockwise starting at the leftmost dot, exactly that word; starting from the upper hollow dot instead yields hollow-indicator bits \`10010\`. The direct five-rune GP29→25-bit exploratory packet of p55's 75-rune body independently has first-five overflow bits \`10010\`, followed by five zero bits corresponding to the **all-filled right five-dot group**. The [existing terminal packet investigation](lp2-terminal-quintet-overflow-channel.md) now incorporates this original 2016 identification.

This is **new historical witness evidence for a finite original visible pattern**, and provides a viable human-noticed selector if a subsequent source gives a consumer. The same 2016 participant was actually describing **XOR and byte-addition attempts on OutGuess data** and supplied no signed instruction to read 75 GP29 runes, select 24-bit bytes, choose an F exception or treat the dots as ciphertext-overflow metadata. The floral border is the same source template on all pages p40–55. Moreover, the entire previously decoded 45-byte candidate lacks known plaintext, recognized file header or stable checksum, and the 72 source-derived RC4 direct consumers fail. The authentic clue→operation→plaintext bridge remains absent. A direct 15×25 full-bit reading of p55's same 15 original quintets also produces no discernible textual/graphic raster.
