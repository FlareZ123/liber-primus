The original-image frame census establishes an exact ornate bilateral outer frame on **every page p40–p55**, the final 16 unsolved pages, with the next solved pages p56–p57 outside that frame. Separately, solved p56 authenticates a concrete GP29 cipher immediately after the framed section: non-F plaintext consumes the successive key `prime[k]-1 mod29`; plaintext F (GP index 0) prints as literal F and **does not consume** a prime.

A direct section-scale interpretation had not been closed by the existing mirror tests: reflect the **allocation of the known prime key across the whole framed block**. Read the unchanged p40–p55 ciphertext from its terminal rune backward, start at the authentic source-analogue prime index 0, and apply the p56 inverse. Returning the resulting plaintext to natural page order is equivalent to assigning the first prime key to the final consuming rune, the second key to the preceding consuming rune, and so on.

This is distinct from the existing page-pair `+/-` mirror combiner, word-reversal receiver, clause-local reversed-prime blocks, and terminal-only p54–p55 backward-prime origin sweep. It adds no key, offset, page choice, language score, or fitted reset.

## Exact F-hold inverse

For traversal ciphertext rune `C` and consumed-prime count `u`, let

`K_u = (prime[u]-1) mod29`.

For the authentic sign, a consuming inverse proposes

`P = C-K_u mod29`.

A consuming branch with `P=F=0` is forbidden: the real p56 encoder would have emitted that plaintext F literally and held the prime cursor, so a **nonzero** ciphertext cannot represent that event. When `C=0`, the exact inverse also preserves the literal-F hold branch. States with the same consumed count are future-equivalent for reachability.

The primary test fixes the authentic p56 sign. A single mirror-sign sensitivity test also tries `P=C+K_u`; it is reported separately and is not a claim that the frame specifies sign inversion.
