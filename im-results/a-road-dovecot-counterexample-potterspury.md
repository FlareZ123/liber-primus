# Competing A-road / oak-walk / dovecote candidate: Potterspury and Furtho

**Classification:** counterexample to uniqueness of the Inveraray location hypothesis. This is not evidence that Potterspury is the intended destination.

## Source-supported correspondence

Potterspury, Northamptonshire, lies adjacent to the A5. Its local history group records a local legend that Edward IV met Elizabeth Woodville under the Queen's Oak:

- https://www.potterspuryhistorygroup.co.uk/
- The group describes Potterspury as adjacent to the A5/Watling Street and records the Queen's Oak legend.

A 2014 Northamptonshire Battlefields Society history PDF contains a hand-drawn map explicitly titled **“POTTERSPURY QUEEN'S OAK WALK.”** The map labels the A5, footpaths, Queen's Oak, Queen's Oak Farm, and routes to Towcester/Yardley Gobion:

- https://northantsbattles.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/northants-history-part-2.pdf
- PDF text extraction locates the map around page 13 / document lines 610–612.

The same Potterspury/Furtho area has a surviving late-15th-century dovecote at Manor Farm, listed Grade II* by Historic England. Historic England's entry gives National Grid Reference SP7729943028 (approximately 52.0802, -0.8734) and describes 19 tiers of nesting boxes. A local Furtho history PDF places the dovecote at the deserted medieval village, about 70 m north-east of St Bartholomew's Church:

- https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1041652
- https://www.cosgrovehistory.co.uk/pdf/dovecote.pdf

## Why this matters to the Cicada clue

The phi-prime Ac orbit includes **Thorn/A5** in its prefix and then a five-cycle containing A5. Potterspury/Furtho supplies a competing cluster:

- A5 road;
- a named **Queen's Oak Walk** and Queen's Oak;
- a medieval dovecote;
- a nearby church.

This resembles several semantic elements being used to support Inveraray: oak, path/walk, road, dovecot, and religious association. Potterspury is not the same kind of estate endpoint as Inveraray, and the local legend is not proof of a Cicada connection. It is nevertheless a concrete counterexample: “A-road + oak walk + dovecot” does not uniquely select Carloonan Dovecot.

## Decision

Keep both Inveraray and Potterspury/Furtho as competing location hypotheses if the A-road interpretation remains active. Any proposed stopping rule must distinguish them using an additional clue that is independent of the candidate locations. In particular, the 2016 phrase “path lies empty; epiphany seeks the devoted” is not yet sufficiently specific to select either site. Do not promote either destination to established fact.
