# P8 as plaintext-word checksums, independent of scroll cipher

**Agent7, 25 November 2026. Scoped empirical negative; no recovered unknown word or key.** The 2013 original 761.MP3 authentic Parable tag contains a product of three true GP-prime clause sums, so a numeric validation register has genuine historical precedent. The following p8 has 255 runes aligned to 255 original p3–7 printed groups. A distinct receiver from the earlier ciphertext-word hash and character-address tests is that these are the *plaintext* words' numeric checksums. It could constrain actual words without identifying the cipher. The source does not specify this transfer.

## Receiver and constructive inference

Condition on the exact 255 original word lengths; preserve their order for registration but do not assume that word order is linguistic. Four fixed checksum functions use sum or product of GP0 ordinals or GP prime weights, modulo29. Test raw p8 and the previously fitted, unauthenticated GF4/radix59-corrected tape. Additionally grant an arbitrary global bijection of the 29 checksum labels. No variation by word length or occurrence, no index shift or phase fit. The original 35,944-form vocabulary gives each observed (length,checksum) a candidate set; its frequency-top word is the basic constructive predictor.

Use first80% of the three pinned literary donor word sequences plus the same solved-source native sections as the preceding prefix experiment to estimate count n(L,r) of checksum r among length L. Add 0.5 in each cell. Each label receives a log likelihood ratio log(29*(n(L,r)+0.5)/(N(L)+14.5)) against a uniform checksum conditional on length. The global sum for any bijection is an assignment objective on 29×29. Solve the entire assignment exactly at 10^-6 rounded individual costs, with integer dual feasibility and tight matched edges checked; the unrounded objective of every possible bijection is at most the returned unrounded score plus 255×10^-6. This is a certified all-permutation bound for the fitted statistical model, not a theorem about every possible language.

384 held-out synthetic arrangements (128 per donor) sample with replacement only the final20% native-normalized donor tokens separately by each exact original length. Thus every control has identical length inventory and positions, without forcing any particular natural sentence order. Randomly permute each plant's 29 checksum labels; also compare 384 iid uniform tapes with the same all-permutation fitting advantage. The donor pools are small for some long words (Gibran length12 has only two). These are finite, length-conditioned empirical controls, not independent natural-text windows.

The actual correction is not authenticated by the result.

## Why dictionary compatibility is not plaintext evidence

Literal checksums already have candidate words at 254–255/255 original positions, and the maximum-coverage arbitrary bijections can achieve 255/255 for all eight cases. These are *compatible words*, not recovered words. Across original direct models only 3–8 slots have a single broad-register candidate (mostly one-rune slots); on the held-out length-matched checksum plants, even providing the true checksum map gives frequency-top word recovery means of 113.2, 113.6, 115.1, 107.5 out of 255 for the four functions. With unknown mapping, maximizing length-conditioned likelihood alone recovers mean 110.9, 118.1, 113.4, 153.3 checksum positions. This weak identification is expected given the lossy checksum. No language-model-generated fill is offered as evidence.

**Decision:** no independent support for p8 as an unmasked or globally relabeled direct plaintext-word checksum under these native-word distributions, and no actual plaintext recovery. This disfavors this specific side-information path; it does not logically exclude a nonprose plaintext, different segmentation, varying checksum mask, unusual register, or a genuine external checksum. Do not extend the freely fitted mappings without an original clue. The earlier 761.MP3 actual clause checksum is unaffected.
