## What the contemporary human first sees

Original archive scan 00.jpg is a **plain Latin-letter LIBER PRIMUS cover**, scan 01.jpg is the framed first rune-bearing LP1 page, and 02.jpg is a **plain Latin-letter chapter break reading Chapter 1 / Intus**. The next printed illustration, scan 03.jpg, resumes LP1 runic text framed by a bilaterally symmetric tree/vine decoration. Other visible LP1 leaves reuse the vine motif. These LP1 front-matter scans contain no explicit GP29 algorithm, coordinate, displacement, cipher sign, alphabet or key length. The Latin Intus means within/inside, but this is a thematic interpretation rather than a source-command opcode.

The first 2014 distributed **LP2** page is **scan 17.jpg** (physical p0), with no new Intus heading or Liber Primus cover. A dated original 2014 Stage11 index contains exactly **58 numbered image links** to the LP2 pages; the LP1 first-release scans 00.jpg–16.jpg are not part of that 58-image package. A player who completed the earlier 2014 puzzle might have remembered the LP1 prelude, but an LP2-only solver would not obtain it from Stage11's distributed image package. Thus an INTUS-based LP2 key rule necessarily depends on reading earlier separately distributed LP1 material and a scope convention that neither source specifies.

The solved LP1 p3 (scan05.jpg) directly prints readable native GP rune text starting **SOME WISDOM; THE PRIMES ARE SACRED**. Applying a universal literal "inside of every printed word" extraction to this authentic clear-GP control yields **OM ISDO** from just SOME WISDOM, destroying its direct meaning. Consequently "always strip word circumferences" is not a book-wide decoder. The word *within* occurs as part of LP1's solved pilgrimage theme, including the text about journeying within then emerging outside; this usage does not specify a numeric cipher operation.

## Exact literal INTUS operation, including genuine page carries

The narrowest executable word-interior interpretation is to split every original printed GP rune word into its first/last rune and intervening interior. The inverse can rejoin the exterior and interior streams when the original word boundaries and lengths are preserved. Retain the source page breaks as *layout annotations*, not word delimiters.

The original p0–p2 encrypted opening is **729 runes** on source scans17–19, divided into **158 complete printed words** across all three pages. Its first p0 red 8+5-rune heading and internal 13-dot marker are verified in the separate original-ink gate. Two original cross-page words require joining: the final p0 **W** combines with p1 starting **AIMSN** into **WAIMSN**, and p1 terminal **LXI** combines with p2 starting **OM** into **LXIOM**. Treating either page boundary as a word boundary produces a different and incorrect interior/exterior register.

The single-file reproduction checks 729/729 exact original rune replay and a fully reversible whole-word split on all 158 physical source words. Dropping the **311** exterior GP symbols and retaining only 418 inner GP symbols is non-injective: without another relation or recorded side-channel, at least **29^311** distinct external rune assignments remain consistent with the same literal 418-rune interior, on source word positions with no further constraints. That is about **1,510.83 bits** of unsupplied value information. Retaining the outer runes makes the decomposition reversible but yields the unchanged original GP rune sequence when rejoined, with no plaintext.

Neither INTUS nor the original book supplies the next necessary operation: whether the interior is plaintext, ciphertext, positional key, padding, or a pointer source; which symbols define a modulo29 state; and how either channel acts on 249 source p0 black runes and a withheld p1. A guess of a GP shift, key schedule or nonlocal transposition would be chosen by the analyst, with no source opcode to distinguish it.

## Decision

**Close INTUS as a unique first-opcode selector for LP2 p0.** The original source establishes a meaningful thematic inside/outside idea and an exact possible word-partition channel, but the first-party Stage11 LP2 release does not contain the front matter and LP1 clear controls show a universal inside-only readout is wrong. There is no independent GP29 value-changing rule to deploy on all of original p0 or on p1 without tuning.

The frontmatter can remain a literary clue to interpretation; reconsider it as a decryption operation only if independent first-party text explicitly specifies (i) an inside object and target, (ii) how to use or reconstruct outer symbols, (iii) a reversible value-changing transformation and (iv) original-page scope and reset schedule. Do not resume simple INTUS/Vigenère key guessing or page-local word-splitting runs. This is a source-level missing-operator result, not a broader rejection of interior-structure ciphers.
