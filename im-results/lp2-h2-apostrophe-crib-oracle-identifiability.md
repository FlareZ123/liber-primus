# Four original LP2 apostrophes fail to identify an unknown GP29 tableau, even with the true key path supplied

**Agent4, 17 November 2026. Director Issue [#1342](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/1342).** A 100-plant, fully reversible source-native **recoverability calibration**. It makes an optimistic assumption unavailable to an actual solver: every key-tableau row used at every original rune is revealed. The exact H2 unknown-tableau family remains open if additional genuine plaintext, a source-selected table, or a stronger language decoder appears. **No original unknown plaintext, key, or tableau is recovered.**

## Source-selected training and withholding

The original image apostrophe marks, which demonstrably match the printed encrypted apostrophe in the genuinely solved LP1 `I RWN'B` / `I DON'T` passage, occur after zero-based global unknown LP2 rune indices **1110 (p4), 5138 (p21), 8515 (p35) and 10089 (p41)**. Their whole printed ciphertext word shapes are **3+1, 2+1, 2+1 and 3+1**. All marks consume **zero** rune positions and, in original solved LP1, zero key advances. The first two locations are training; the last two complete words and the entire original-coordinate **[8500,8650)** 150-rune window are strictly withheld. The original plaintext behind all four apostrophes remains unknown.

Use the **actual p56 first-party prime-totient key family** as an independently named stream: `K_j = (prime[j]−1) mod29` for successive ordinary primes, starting at 2. Each of the **29 key residues** indexes its own arbitrary secret permutation of all 29 GP runes. The H2 test cipher emits `T[K_j][P_i]` with the published `q=0.81` probabilistic collision-rejection skip: if a proposed ciphertext glyph repeats the preceding one, it redraws with the next key row with probability 0.81. This family preserves all source rune positions. A genuine p56 **plaintext-F bypass** is reproduced as a separate solved-cipher control; it is not silently transferred to the hypothetical H2 mechanism.

To ensure genuine English-register plants, choose **100 independent deterministic random 12,956-rune windows** of the repository's fully source-native Gibran text. At the original four offsets replace exactly 4,3,3,4 runes with correctly spelled GP-native `DON'T / IT'S / HE'S / CAN'T` plants. Neither their identities nor these contractions are claimed for original LP2. Encrypt with 100 different **29 independently shuffled GP29 permutation rows**, key residues from successive prime totients and deterministic `q=0.81` skip; **all 12,956** plant runes are recovered under the true secret table and key-pointer history on every trial. The model's mean immediate ciphertext repeats are **88.23**, close to original LP2's **86** (this statistic was used to motivate H2 in prior research; matching it does not validate the cipher).

### Extremely favorable decoder and blinded measurements

**Oracle path:** grant the decoder the *actual emitted key row* at every position, even across all secret skips. Give it the true first two contractions, seven GP glyphs in total. For each key row, the seven plaintext-ciphertext observations force a partial inverse permutation. Other inverse cells are still completely unspecified except for one-to-one uniqueness. For an unrealistically generous language extension, sort all ciphertext glyphs in each oracle row by their frequency over the **complete 12,956-rune ciphertext** and assign them the English GP symbols ranked by the *independent* Shakespeare control's frequencies, respecting every training crib assignment. This is the exact maximum-likelihood tableau under a known iid plaintext unigram model with fixed row frequencies and oracle key-state history. Compare it with no cribs and with deliberately wrong `CAN'T / HE'S` instead of the first two true contractions.

| 100 independent plants | Complete withheld 7 glyphs (two words) | Contiguous 150-rune withheld span |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Direct table entries provably identified by just two correct training words**, mean | **0.21 / 7** (zero in **81/100** plants) | **2.31 / 150** |
| Oracle true-key-path, Shakespeare-unigram ML **with both correct training words**, mean glyphs right | **1.79 / 7** | **42.23 / 150** |
| Same oracle/ML procedure with **no** plaintext crib | **1.58 / 7** | **40.37 / 150** |
| Same oracle/ML procedure with **wrong** training words | **1.75 / 7** | **40.12 / 150** |

With two correct cribs, heldout p35's complete 3-rune contraction is recovered in **3/100** trials, p41's 4-rune contraction in **0/100**, **both** complete heldout words in **0/100**, and **all 150** contiguous heldout runes in **0/100**. Wrong contractions produce direct inverse-permutation contradictions in only **3/100** trials. The correct training cribs improve the 150-rune mean by a mere **1.86 glyphs** against no-crib and do not identify the secret tableau. This is despite supplying the true hidden skip pattern and letting the frequency decoder inspect the entire ciphertext, substantially more favorable access than the unsolved original provides.

## What this rules out, and what it leaves open

An arbitrary 29×29 permutation tableau has **841** secret inverse cells. Two authentic 3+1 and 2+1 word cribs can constrain at most **seven** distinct row/cipher entries, and may constrain fewer if entries coincide. This fact remains true with *known* key row and perfect punctuation interpretation. None of the rows receives 28 known entries, so the permutation completion rule cannot identify another entry outright. The actual source apostrophe spelling is also unknown, which reduces the information further. The tested cipher is fully reversible, the plants match the original source's word widths, and the failure is specifically one of **unknown-tableau identifiability**, rather than of cipher inversion itself.

Under this deliberately favorable **two-crib oracle and unigram decoder**, there is no usable power for blind prediction of both heldout contraction words plus 150 consecutive original GP runes. Accordingly, Issue #1342's stated *pre-original calibration gate fails*; further original-cipher scans with a free 29×29 table would permit unconstrained overfitting and were not run. This is a bounded decoder decision: a source-derived key-row table, a major authentic original plaintext span, or a demonstrably powered higher-order joint-tableau language decoder could justify an independent successor. The experiment does not establish a universal impossibility theorem for arbitrary-tableau H2 with unrestricted linguistic or external evidence.
