## Inversion and independent inputs

The LP2 pages 49–51 numeric grid is **32 rows × 8 columns = eight 32-byte values**. Eight major ciphertext sections begin with individually red-marked rune headings, with original physical page/heading-start pairs **(0,0), (3,0), (8,0), (15,9), (23,0), (27,0), (33,91), (40,0)**. The original sources are [numeric grid](../resources/liber-primus/base60-grid.json), [colored rune positions](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-ink-annotations.json), and [canonical ciphertext](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-corpus.json). In this hypothesis the visible red title is a standard cryptographic salt or HMAC message for its corresponding section; an arbitrary permutation of the eight printed grid columns into the eight sections is unknown and fitted exactly.

For each grid column K and title T, form a 32-byte stream-cipher key from one of exactly three public standard derivations:

1. SHA-256(K || T)
2. HMAC-SHA256(key=K, message=T)
3. HKDF-SHA256(input=K, salt=T, info=empty, output=32 bytes)

Use either UTF-8 of the literal original rune glyphs, or the corresponding 0–28 GP integer bytes, as T. Decrypt with standard AES-256-CTR or ChaCha20, either a zero 128-bit IV or SHA-256(T)'s first 16 bytes. Convert generated bytes to 29-symbol additive keys via byte modulo 29 or floor(byte×29/256), and test both additive signs. This gives **3 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 96 global mechanisms**, each allowing all **8! distinct column assignments** through exact subset dynamic programming. This is a finite, testable model; the artifact does not assert that the publisher chose SHA/HMAC/HKDF, AES or ChaCha.
