# Historical 2014 onion-2 five-minute clock: bounded marginal and witness-boundary test

**Evidence status (10 November 2026):** independent historical-source timing inference, not an authenticated generator, key, LP2 decryption or 2016 instruction. Source: [archived interval corpus](../resources/cicada2014-onion2-timing.json); read-only exact enumerator: [CommonJS SFT](../sims/onion2_five_minute_clock_sft.cjs). The starting point is the second 2014 onion's published 256 bytes and its archived five-minute-quantized reveal delays. Its relevance to the 56 unsolved LP2 pages is conditional, because no signed message independently identifies it as an LP2 cipher key. This analysis belongs to [Director Issue #10](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/10).

## Main result

The reconciled historical witnesses preserve **112 explicit intervals**, at one-based byte positions **143–189** and **192–256**. Positions 190–191 instead have a combined six-tick / 30-minute interval with five admissible splits; all earlier 1–142 byte gaps are absent. The 112 observed intervals total **234 five-minute ticks**, 19.5 hours. Their exact length counts are:

| Gap length in five-minute ticks | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Archived intervals | 46 | 33 | 21 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 1 | **112** |

An independent uniform draw of one through seven ticks for each observed byte, the natural marginal of a simple floor(7 × rand()) + 1 delay rule, has mean four ticks. The **exact lower-tail** probability of a 112-draw sum no larger than the observed 234 is **7.68600730873432 × 10⁻²⁷**. This result uses integer dynamic programming for coefficients of (x + x² + ... + x⁷)^112, with denominator 7^112. No PRNG seed search or ciphertext-language score is involved. Thus a uniform seven-way delay law is strongly incompatible with an unbiased, stationary sample of these historical intervals, even before comparing generated bytes.

A more parsimonious candidate process is a sequence of five-minute attempts that succeed independently with probability p and emit a byte upon success. Its inter-arrival delay K is geometric with P(K=k)=p(1−p)^(k−1). The observed maximum-likelihood parameter is **p=112/234=0.47863247863248**; the expected grouped counts for K=1,2,3,≥4 are **53.6068, 27.9489, 14.5716, 15.8727**, against observed **46, 33, 21, 12**. Pearson χ²=**5.77308** on four bins gives approximate fitted-model p=**0.05577** (two effective degrees of freedom). That is a **plausible** marginal explanation, neither a positive reconstruction of the service nor a demonstration that coins were tossed. Different nonuniform interval laws may fit comparably.

## The witness boundary argues against a confidently stationary clock

The last multiply attested interval is byte position **182**. Use that *pre-existing archival boundary* to split observed gaps without searching for an optimal change point:

| Witness interval set | Gaps | Sum of five-minute ticks | Mean ticks/gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Positions 143–182 | 40 | 99 | **2.475** |
| Positions 183–189 and 192–256 | 72 | 135 | **1.875** |

Condition on the **entire observed multiset** of 112 delays and randomly assign 40 of them to the earlier source-ordered group. Counting all C(112,40) assignments exactly, the one-sided tail for an earlier sum **≥99** is **0.011241633407547006**. This does not establish a real change of server behavior: the grouping also changes witness availability/confidence and the capture process might have selected or rounded intervals differently. It does demonstrate why an otherwise appealing stationary Bernoulli clock needs historical corroboration before being treated as an original-source-selected key schedule.

The bounded missing two intervals at positions 190–191 add exactly **six** ticks, giving **240 ticks/20 hours** between the recorded byte-142 and byte-256 boundaries. This follows from the archive's joint 30-minute witness and remains valid for each of its five admissible splits. The first 142 byte intervals are unobserved, so fitting a single 256-byte stream from these 112 events is unjustified. The historical approximation of a 23-hour total event should not be silently substituted for these observed boundary times.

## Implication for an intended human solve path

The five-minute quantization makes a five-minute publication/polling clock more natural to test than another unrelated 32-bit seed sweep. A simple geometric publication process could be understood by a contemporary solver watching page changes and does not require the byte values and reveal times to originate in the same PRNG stream. The historical HTML comment “Patience is a virtue” later changes to “761”, and the following 3-MB JPEG-containing payload replaces the old 256-byte page. These are attested sequence details in the [2014 archive](https://github.com/scream314/cicada3301/blob/master/2014.md), without an instruction that the 256 bytes decode LP2.

A decisive next observation would be a contemporaneous Onion-2 **server-side** program, cron schedule, HTTP cache/poll logs or signed operator instruction. A client-saved HTML snapshot fixes displayed bytes but cannot identify whether the publisher drew all bytes in advance, obtained them from a CSPRNG, or selected them on each five-minute tick. Historic third-onion Apache status records belong to a different onion name and do not identify the second-onion process. Without such a source-fixed operation and a reproducible handoff to unknown LP2, the geometric model should remain a useful bounded hypothesis; no source-derived LP2 key is claimed.

## Exact reproduction and exclusions from scope

Run the [CommonJS SFT](../sims/onion2_five_minute_clock_sft.cjs) against the checked-in JSON. It verifies the source's 256-byte SHA-256, 112 exact byte-position/hex correspondences, gap bounds, total and split, then prints exact BigInt uniform and conditional-permutation enumerations, fitted geometric diagnostic and two small mathematical positive controls. It is read-only and deterministic. The calculated probabilities apply to the **selected archived interval sample**; they are not an all-model PRNG rejection, a decryption, or an assertion of source independence.

See also the [historical LCG/MT source gate](onion2-timing-generator-state-recovery.md) and [Perl drand48 source gate](perl-drand48-onion2-time-seed-search.md) for independently scoped generator-family exclusions. Their negative results against finite, specified byte/delay schedules should not be generalized to a conditional five-minute publishing process.
