# LP2 p22 deathbed illustration: source-plate decision

**13 November 2026, Agent5. Scope:** original physical p22, archive JPEG `39.jpg`, with contemporary LP1 art and two pre-2014 iconographic comparison families. **No externally identified original plate, edition-specific caption, recovered key, decoding instruction, or plaintext.** This is a bounded image-source decision for Director #750, not an LP2 cryptanalytic exclusion.

## The independently decisive image relation

Original LP2 p22 places a flat-lying, partially covered supine human figure across the lower width of the page, feet left and head right, directly beneath a horizontally presented distressed three-transverse-bar cross. Its illustration is **already a pixel-certified horizontal mirror of the entire figure used as a footer on solved original LP1 pp14–16**. The existing original-raster registration in [the cross-release art study](lp1-lp2-illustration-reuse-and-cipher-transfer-gate.md) established 443/453 SIFT descriptor matches, registered grayscale correlation 0.992524 and black-ink IoU 0.906207, across a ~1.0056 scale transform. These figures are the same source artwork, in opposing horizontal orientations, not separate iconographic depictions.

The three LP1 figures appear beneath different text on solved pages 14, 15 and 16; therefore even an authored reuse cannot uniquely select one particular LP1 plaintext line. The source-bearing LP1 neighboring prose and its real second-koan key have already been tested on p22, as has the literal solved-LP1 passage crib ([same study](lp1-lp2-illustration-reuse-and-cipher-transfer-gate.md); Director #737). Neither recovers p22 unknown plaintext. The artwork physically establishes a cross-release linkage, with no further decipherment selector.

## Registered original-art difference as a source-selected payload gate

The same exact LP1→LP2 mirrored-footer source supplies a direct image-subtraction test for a potentially added **visible instruction**. Using the full-resolution original `14.jpg`, `15.jpg` and `39.jpg`, the bottom 400 pixels of solved LP1 scans14 and15 are **pixel-identical**. Reflect the last650 rows of original LP1 scan14, then register them to LP2 p22 scan39 by one SIFT/RANSAC similarity transform. The original matching gives **598 ratio-accepted matches / 508 affine inliers**, with scale **1.00567967**, displacement about −10/−2.8 pixels, and mean absolute grayscale residual **3.44534/255** across the 2400×650 region. The apparent residual overwhelmingly traces pencil-stroke edges and the re-render's scaling and JPEG quantization. At threshold **|pixel difference|>80/255**, there are just **four interior pixels** outside the last-row warp-border artifact; at threshold >100/255, **zero interior pixels**. A planted opaque 60×20-pixel dark mark at an original figure-coordinate region yields **494** such interior residual pixels and triggers the same exact test. The [original-input, planted-control SFT](../sims/lp2_p22_mirror_art_residual_sft.py) regenerates these findings from the checked-in original JPEGs.

Thus the original p22 mirrored figure does **not** contain a sizeable high-contrast added symbol, label or drawing that would survive this registration. This test cannot rule out subtle opacity-modulated residual bits, latent DCT steganography, alternative artwork copies, or a nonvisible encoded caption. It supplies no key; those weaker possibilities require their own independently specified framing/checksum before extracting bits.

## Distinct cross examination and rival source candidates

The p22 cross is printed **horizontally** with three transverse strokes, while two upright, three-bar crosses flank LP2 p0's opening manuscript page (`17.jpg`). We inspected the original full-resolution 2400×3600 RGB JPEGs, isolated each cross by the largest thresholded connected black-pixel component (8-connectivity, grayscale threshold 115), and compared every 90-degree rotation and reflection after normalizing the isolated masks to p22's 1113×397 black component. The isolated p0 left and right dimensions were 467×1276 and 490×1276 pixels respectively; the maximum unregistered silhouette intersections-over-unions were only **0.31986** (left) and **0.34710** (right). A separate SIFT/RANSAC direct original-JPEG check found at most two ratio-passed feature pairs for each 90-degree p22→p0 orientation, no qualifying affine registration. These **do not authenticate exact transformed reuse** of the p0 cross; low overlaps are only a limited image-registration observation, not a proof against all possible handmade variants. Both art families remain visually similar in cross style and deliberately differing orientation.

## Research decision

An exact external source engraving with an edition-specific caption or numeral was **not** found. The strongest precise source is **internal reuse from already-solved LP1 pp14–16**, and that source supplies no successful p22 plaintext route. Do not reopen the solved-LP1 literal-crib work or guess a source text from cross iconography. A legitimate successor would first locate a **different, dated pre-2014 external plate with same drawing contours**, recover a source-selected coordinate/operation from that plate, then commit the input key and ≥40-rune original holdout before decoding. Without that evidence, retire the plate-attribution premise.
