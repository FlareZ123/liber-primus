# Source-native GP29 word-length crib retrieval on original LP2 ciphertext

**Classification:** reusable source-text crib search and bounded negative. This does not recover unknown plaintext, identify a key, or establish that the unknown pages quote any particular literary source.

## Why this route could reach plaintext

The original cipher pages retain printed word delimiters. The solved LP2 physical p57 provides an unusually strong calibration: the entire 95-rune, 20-word original lineation agrees word by word with the known orthographic English passage once the historical GP29 multi-letter units are contracted. Consequently a consecutive **word-length signature** can be searched against an external text in its *actual GP29 rune lengths* without knowing a cipher key, selecting a substitution cipher, or modifying any unknown ciphertext. A sufficiently long, independently sourced exact match would be a concrete candidate plaintext crib, suitable for a frozen original-cipher attack. It would still require cipher verification.

## Exact original-input screen

[Single-file CommonJS reproducer](../sims/lp2_original_wordlength_crib_sft.cjs) parses the canonical 2014 [original LP2 rune transcription](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-transcription.txt), ignoring line wrapping and preserving words that cross page turns. The source has **2,896 words / 12,956 runes** on physical pp0–55, including the absent-rune p50; the GP29 spelling clock uses the established orthographic units **ING, TH, NG, EO, OE, AE, IA, EA** from the [2013 source table](../results/2013-original-gp29-source-map.md), with the remaining Latin letters as single runes. The independent [solved-p57 reference](../resources/liber-primus/lp1-plaintext-reference.txt) matches **20/20 original word lengths and all 95 source runes**; the control text is never added to the unknown-page search corpus.

A seven-word length-signature index retrieves matches from the six unchanged source files now in the repository; every candidate is then extended to its maximal exact consecutive original-word match. The three literary word counts include Gutenberg metadata and are deliberately left untrimmed, giving a larger, more permissive candidate set. The other three files preserve the exact Crowley chapters used in the [2013 Cicada 232.jpg book-code reconstruction](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/blob/main/2013/001-232.jpg/README.md) and by its [executable book-code script](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/blob/main/2013/001-232.jpg/book-code.py). This is an independent historically used source, not a guessed author or a key. Local resource copies retain their upstream bytes.

### Six-source near-quotation gate (two substitutions in fifteen words)

The exact same original-rune lengths were compared with **all six source texts**, including the three independently authenticated Cicada-used Crowley chapters, under one additional finite tolerance: a *15-word contiguous passage* may change at most **two** GP-word lengths. Split the 15 words into three consecutive five-word blocks. Any pair of passages differing at at most two positions must agree on at least one complete five-word block, so indexing all original/source five-block matches and checking each resulting aligned passage is exhaustive for this Hamming-distance bound. There are **12,074 / 43,818 / 36,747** distinct candidate start pairs for Gibran / Emerson / Shakespeare and **1,683 / 1,453 / 1,470** for historical chapters I / II / III. Every source yields **zero passages with at most two mismatches**. The same routine positively identifies the genuine solved-p57 passage. This excludes only near-verbatim passages with stable word count and at most two altered orthographic word lengths, not insertions, deletions, translations, paraphrases, or other books.

### Solved LP1 prose as a candidate unknown-page crib (Agent 32, 12 November 2026)

A contemporary solver already had the decoded LP1 manuscript, making repeated passages of its own prose a more specific candidate source than arbitrary literary works. The existing original-source word-length program now includes the editor-normalized [solved LP1 reference](../resources/liber-primus/lp1-plaintext-reference.txt). Solved physical LP1 pages 1–16 yield **744 uppercase words**, after omitting bracketed editorial continuation placeholders. Source headings and repeated reference passages remain in the input, making this a permissive search. Both sides retain exactly the same GP29 multi-letter spelling rules and original unsolved LP2 word breaks.

Only **three** LP1-to-original-LP2 matches reach seven consecutive GP word-length equalities; **zero reach eight**. The three length-only seven-word alignments begin on LP2 **p5** against `TO DESTROY ALL THAT YOU OWN AN`, **p25** against `BE TRUE TEST THE KNOWLEDGE FIND YOUR`, and **p33** against `THREE BEHAVIORS WHICH CAUSE THE LOSS OF`. A separate exact 15-word test allowing up to two altered word lengths per quote examines **582** candidate alignments (complete three-by-five-word seed cover); **zero** survive, and the closest candidates have four different word lengths. The same 20-word solved-p57 control still matches completely. These short coincidences do not recover rune plaintext or a cipher key.

This specifically excludes a verbatim eight-word LP1 reuse, and a fifteen-word LP1 near-quotation with at most two changed rune word-lengths, **if original unknown-page word separators and this reference's historical spelling are preserved**. It leaves paraphrases, substantially altered wording, different segmentation and other plaintext origins unresolved. The updated [same-file CommonJS SFT](../sims/lp2_original_wordlength_crib_sft.cjs) tests the original complete corpus and the 744-word reference directly.

### Contemporaneous signed Cicada messages as exact cribs (Agent 32, 12 November 2026)

An additional source-constrained test uses **nine preserved 2012–2017 Cicada OpenPGP-signed cleartext messages**, including the original 2012 Blake chorus in the MIDI email, the 2013 forest/sky instructions, the 2014 Stage11 welcome, the 2016 authenticated OutGuess instruction, and the 2017 false-paths warning. These messages are a plausible preexisting crib source because the creator explicitly gave them to players. The nine preserved cleartext portions contain **242 total uppercase word tokens** (repeated passages included), of which 85 belong to the 2012 email and 45 to the 2016 message. Only original signed cleartext before the `BEGIN PGP SIGNATURE` marker is scanned; the 2012 MIDI email's separate encrypted OpenPGP attachment is explicitly excluded. This avoids treating base64 armor as prose. Input runs are separately aligned per source, not concatenated across messages.

Under the same unmodified original LP2 2,896 printed words and authentic GP29 orthographic multi-letter counting, **none of the nine messages shares seven consecutive exact word lengths** with unknown LP2. The maximum is six consecutive word lengths, occurring once for the 2012 Blake passage (`BUT ACTS NOT FOR EVERY THING`, LP2 p41) and twice for the 2016 signed message (`ARE THE DIRECTION SEEK AND YOU`, p34; `ARE THE MAP THEIR MEANING IS`, p37). These are length-only coincidences and do not decode any original runes. The nine exact signed message scans are now asserted in the expanded [existing CommonJS SFT](../sims/lp2_original_wordlength_crib_sft.cjs), with the solved-p57 native positive unchanged. This excludes verbatim seven-word quotation from these particular preserved messages under unaltered word boundaries and GP spelling, while other quotations and altered segmentation remain possible.

### Converting a length match into an executable key candidate

The reproducer now exports **encodeGP** and **keyFromCrib**. For a matched source phrase it converts the proposed plaintext words into original 2013 GP29 symbol indices (including documented K/C, Z/S, V/U and NG/ING spelling aliases and the attested solved-LP1 QU→KW spelling) and aligns each rune to the original printed ciphertext. It yields the exact **candidate additive key stream** K[i] = C[i] − P[i] (mod 29). Unsupported standalone Q, incompatible transcription, or unequal total rune count returns no key. This is a direct way to carry a newly discovered long source quote into an independent key-reuse or cipher-mechanism prediction without retroactively choosing the plaintext.

The **real solved-p57 control** yields all **95 original GP symbols** exactly and hence the **95-symbol zero keystream** from source P=C. The committed SFT was executed against the nine actual source files in a JS runtime with Node-standard-library shims, and every assertion passed, including the 15-word exhaustive near-quotation screen on all six sources. Neither the short matches found here nor a putative recovered key stream alone would authenticate an unknown-page decryption.

## Research decision

The three pinned literary texts and the original 2013 Cicada book-code source do **not** supply an exact ten-word unknown-page plaintext crib or a fifteen-word near-verbatim crib with up to two substituted word lengths under standard source-native GP orthography and preserved word boundaries. This is a useful bounded exclusion of **verbatim borrowing** in these *particular* editions. It says nothing about a paraphrase, non-English prose, changed segmentation, spelling differences, a different literary source, or a cipher that modified word delimiters. No plaintext, key, or mechanism is claimed.

**Actionable use:** index any genuinely source-motivated historical text with the same search routine, then require a long source-original match and cross-validate the resulting proposed plaintext with a complete reversible cipher, preferably on an independently withheld passage. Do not treat a short length match as plaintext or open an unrestricted corpus crawl absent a source lead.
