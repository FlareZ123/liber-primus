## Exactly which entire secret family is covered

For each printed item independently, split the actual GP ciphertext from its first rune into nonoverlapping pairs (x,y). Permit **every invertible 2×2 matrix** over GF(29) as its decryption matrix, and two independent arbitrary additive output constants:

\[
P_{2i} = ax + by + u \pmod{29},\qquad
P_{2i+1}=cx+dy+v \pmod{29},\qquad ad-bc\ne0.
\]

There are exactly **682,080** choices of invertible matrices (29²−1)(29²−29), each combined with **29²=841** arbitrary output-offset pairs. The offsets can differ across units. We avoid enumerating the offset combinations by proving a stronger **upper bound that permits even arbitrary label reassignment of one output parity lane**.

For a fixed matrix, let A be the 29-entry frequency histogram of the first decoded output coordinate *before* the additive offset, and B the same for the second. Let A↓ and B↓ be those 29 counters sorted descending. Any pair of additive constants merely shifts/relables A and B. By the rearrangement inequality, for all allowed offsets,

\[
\sum_z (A_z + B_{z+\delta})^2
\ \le\ \sum_z A_z^2 + \sum_z B_z^2
   +2\sum_{z=0}^{28}A^\downarrow_z B^\downarrow_z.
\]

For n decoded even-pair rune positions, subtract n and divide by n(n−1) to obtain a **rigorous maximum possible GP29 index of coincidence (IoC)**. For each original input, enumerate all 682,080 invertible matrices, take their individual sorted upper bounds, then take the greatest. This proves one bound simultaneously for every secret affine key and every additive offset. The sort-bound deliberately allows more than the actual legal cyclic GF29 offsets and can only be overgenerous; it cannot falsely eliminate an authentic plaintext generated by this family.
