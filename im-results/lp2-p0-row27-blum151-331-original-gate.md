## Exact all-seed BBS period is at most twenty

Let \`p=151\`, \`q=331\`, \`N=pq\`. A normal BBS state is \`x0=s² mod N\`, followed by \`x_(t+1)=x_t² mod N\`. The quadratic residue groups have orders

\`\`\`text
|QR(151)| = (151-1)/2 = 75
|QR(331)| = (331-1)/2 = 165
2^20 mod 75 = 1
2^20 mod 165 = 1.
\`\`\`

Consequently, **for every seed** and both components under the Chinese remainder theorem, \`x_(t+20)=x_t\`. The maximum BBS state period is **20**, irrespective of the starting key. Tested source-visible seeds 13 (large dots), 315 and347 (red word sums), 3301 (signature) yield periods20,20,20,20; the candidate difference32 yields period2. Exact initial squaring and inverse square-root positive tests pass for the full cycles.

This is much stronger than a finite seed sweep. Any one-byte-per-state deterministic BBS output has **at most 20 distinct byte values**. A conventional parity-bit BBS stream emits at most **five distinct bytes** when eight consecutive bits are packed per byte, because its 20-bit sequence repeats. The actual original numeral table contains **161 distinct bytes in 256 positions**. Therefore **no such unreset BBS stream generated by these factors is the original 256-byte table**, even granting an unknown seed and arbitrary deterministic one-state-to-byte map.

A short-cycle BBS generator used as a direct page-continuous GP29 pad is likewise an unlikely original English receiver. ## Direct Blum–Goldwasser binary framing also fails under the source-coherent radix60

Blum–Goldwasser adds a BBS keystream to a binary message and appends the terminal BBS state \`x_(t+1)\`. That state is necessarily a **quadratic residue modulo both p and q**, independent of the plaintext and seed. Because \`N=49,981 <2^16\`, the natural two-byte terminal candidate is the table's original last two bytes. Under radix60, the last two printed tokens \`39 1K\` decode as **189, 80**, giving:

| Radix60 terminal reading | Candidate integer | QR mod151 | QR mod331 |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Big-endian | **48,464** | Yes | **No** |
| Little-endian | **20,669** | **No** | **No** |

Both variants are mathematically invalid as a terminal BBS state, and thus **cannot be a classic, source-coherent radix60 Blum–Goldwasser trailer** under modulus 151×331. No language scoring or guessed plaintext is involved.

The printed alphabet also admits a previously discussed alternate **radix59** parsing with the unused lowercase *f* removed. Its last two bytes become **186,79**: big-endian **47,695** is not QR modulo151, while little-endian **20,410** *is* QR modulo both factors. This is a **necessary condition only**; the compatible orientation has no authenticated framing rule and no recovered plaintext. Crucially, radix59 changes row27's *2V* from **151 to149**. Since \`149 ≡1 (mod4)\`, the very same heading→table selector can no longer supply a Blum-prime pair. Separately printed LP1 **151** could be reused with the alternate numeral radix, but doing so would introduce a **different** selector, and would not rescue a direct twenty-state table emission.

## Cryptanalytic decision
