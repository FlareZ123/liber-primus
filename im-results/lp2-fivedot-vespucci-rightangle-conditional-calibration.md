## Historical figure and numeric-variant gate

The [physical 1503–1504 *Mundus novus* catalogue](https://www.themorgan.org/printed-books/176346), folio 3a, records **one three-star illustration** with four lines of numeral-five-like marks and **one separate six-star illustration**, with the name Canopus. The [1903 Rostock-edition facsimile](https://archive.org/details/mundusnovus00vesp) contains the same distinct three- and six-star depictions (original-text scan also [here](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/Mundus_novus%3B_%28IA_mundusnovus00vesp%29.pdf)). It is an early printed source *available in principle* to a Cicada player, but is **not** a Cicada-authenticated reference.

For the *specific Rostock edition* reproduced in that facsimile, the astronomical sentence supplies **9½**, **12½**, then literal Latin **"triginta novem" = 39** degrees. A [modern English rendering](https://www.guidingamerica.org/praeconium/mundus-novus-amerigo-vespucci) instead gives **32** for the last quantity. Thus the proposed 32-versus-39 key parameter is edition/transcription dependent. Taking **39** for this identified edition freezes that ambiguity, but the book does not define a mapping from those arc measurements to the five LP2 x/y centroids.

The author's three-star orthogonal triangle plus “two others” in prose is a possible *word-level* analogy; it does not identify **which two** of the separate six drawn stars to retain, where to place them, how to handle the first diagram's intervening marks, or how to project the resulting five to p56. Any choice of the missing two, arbitrary rotation or separately fitted sky projection would violate the preselected-five-point test. **Consequently no unique five-point original-source registration exists in the frozen two historical figures**, so the original-input plaintext stage is *not* unlocked.
