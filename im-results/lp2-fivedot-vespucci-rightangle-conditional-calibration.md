## Independent five-star Cassiopeia comparison

A human observer might instead see a **five-point W-shaped asterism**, the easily recognized Cassiopeia (Caph, Schedar, Gamma, Ruchbah and Segin). This is an independently distinct explanation of why exactly five dots could be drawn and mirrored. A shape analogy is still only a hypothesis: the dots are not labeled as stars.

A frozen quantitative comparator takes the published J2000 star coordinates (source [Star Party Atlas, Cassiopeia W](https://atlas.starparty.ca/asterisms/cassiopeia-w)) in **increasing right-ascension order**, maps RA to a local equatorial x coordinate by multiplying by cos(mean declination), and uses declination as y. It aligns this fixed five-star sequence with the original p56 five dots in left-to-right order, optimizing only *one uniform similarity transform* (translation, scale, rotation and optional reflection). The normalized RMS residual is **0.266760322006**, where zero means perfect five-point similarity.

As an exact conditional control, hold the five observed LP2 x values and the same five observed y values fixed and enumerate all **120 possible y assignments**. **17/120 = 0.1416667** produce an equal or lower five-star residual than the original. This does not justify treating the constellation as the image source, much less as a key; the alignment is far from unique and there is no source-defined decoding operation, star-label alphabet or page receiver. The result is independent of the Vespucci right-triangle null above but is also a postselected explanation and must not be interpreted as a preregistered astronomical discovery.
