# Historically recovered Cicada square text and signed OpenPGP metadata: bounded source-key consumers

**Status, Agent 36 (15 November 2026): no new GP29 plaintext.** This record tests two otherwise independent, original-historical sources of possible LP2 keys. It preserves finite inverse decisions and an overlooked signed-timestamp feature, while retaining the stronger objection that **no original instruction selects a transfer into LP2**. Existing accepted LP2 decipherments remain p56 and p57; the 56 original p0–55 encrypted pages are unchanged.

## 2014 historical magic squares as original-byte AES source

The surviving 2014 OpenPuff text in [the canonical 745-byte file](../resources/cicada2014-openpuff-magic-squares.txt) prints the three matrices submitted during Stage10 (5×5 sum3301, 7×7 sum1033, 5×5 sum1033). A transparent, frozen candidate was to hash **the exact original 745 bytes, including CRLF**, with SHA-256, interpret the result as an AES-256-CTR key and apply the zero-IV keystream to the original GP29 ciphertext. This assumes a cross-release cryptographic transfer absent from Cicada's instructions; it was evaluated as an inexpensive standalone inverse rather than a preferred mechanism. The exact source digest is `9e5c3004eec652c89027e99aa6f31c53a84c1f8c327c04ca9aa6acf7884b6c7f`.

Eight complete original-input inverse variants were frozen: GP29 byte reduction or the existing GP28 predecessor transport, both key signs, and continuous stream versus source-visible nine-chapter resets. Every original output was forward re-encrypted exactly; all eight separate native GP-positive plants also inverted exactly. **None recovered natural text.** Original English-control trigram mean was **−2.24588** while the *best* original trial was **−6.26136**, indistinguishable from the untransformed cipher's **−6.29657** for the intended decision. The best trial's GP29 index of coincidence was **0.035286**, near uniform, versus independent native control **0.059501**. All original results are preserved in [the source-exact CJS SFT](../sims/lp2_2014_magic_text_aes256_source_sft.cjs) and [passing manual CI 43708052205](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43708052205). This rules out the specified zero-IV SHA256(magic-text) AES stream transfers, without excluding an author-selected original-square consumer.

A different **word-order** interpretation, using all three 2014 square values as 25/49-word numerical rank permutations, was independently executed against native word-length syntax and retained in [the consolidated word-order study](lp2-wordlength-order-architecture.md#original-2014-three-magic-square-numerical-rank-word-order-agent-36-15-november). Its best of 18 fixed routes had a **164/401 shuffled-control tail**, and provided no original plaintext.

## 2012–2017 OpenPGP signature packet census

Cicada repeatedly required verification of its OpenPGP key. A solver inspecting the actual signed messages could therefore encounter **signature packet metadata**, including creation times, issuer IDs and possible nonstandard notation or embedded-signature subpackets. Such extra metadata would be an independent possible source of instructions or key bytes. This test inspected the entire ASCII-armored signature packets from **15 explicitly pinned original signed-source paths** in `resources/historical/`, the 2013 `DO NOT SHARE` source and the 2014 May 2 copy. They contain **14 distinct signature blocks**, because 2014 May 2 and stage11 are duplicates.

Every original signature is **one OpenPGP v4 RSA signature packet, 543 bytes including packet header and 540 bytes body**. The only v4 hashed subpacket is **type 2**, a four-byte creation time; the only unhashed subpacket is **type 16**, the eight-byte issuer ID `181f01e57a35090f`. The signature hash algorithm is SHA-1 for the original 2012–2016 sources and SHA-512 for the 2017 source. **No original message has a notation-data subpacket (type 20), embedded-signature subpacket (type 32), or other additional metadata carrier.** A separately planted notation `ABCD` was exactly detected by the same packet parser. [Full 15-file source-exact test](../sims/cicada2012_2017_signature_subpacket_keychannel_sft.cjs) and [passing corrected CI 43709361731](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43709361731). This excludes only standard OpenPGP subpacket metadata as the hidden-key carrier in these specific preserved signatures; it says nothing about unrelated image channels or the ordinary signed message text.

Two **genuine, independently source-readable signature creation times** merit narrowly qualified observation:

- The 2013 `DO NOT SHARE THIS INFORMATION` signature's hashed creation time is **2013-03-03 05:33:01 UTC** (bytes `51 32 e0 8d`). Its `HHMMSS` decimal string is `053301`, containing `53301`, also the original 2014 Stage10 5×5/3301 form-field name. This is an exact historical marker overlap. The date and time are striking, but no instruction connects this source timestamp to a future LP2 key or makes the apparent numerical intention independently testable.
- The 2016 `Liber Primus is the way` signed payload was signed **2016-01-01 00:01:07 UTC** (bytes `56 85 c1 c3`). Its **decimal six-digit HHMMSS string** is `000107`, numerically 107, also the first preserved 2014 Stage10 linked image name `107.jpg`. Importantly, **00:01:07 is 67 elapsed seconds after midnight**, so the integer 107 arises only from reading HHMMSS as concatenated decimal digits. This cross-round reading is a hypothesis, not proof of intentional author navigation. A source-frozen census of the complete 15 signed source paths (14 distinct signatures) finds this **2016 timestamp to be the sole exact HHMMSS-as-decimal match** to any of the three original 2014 returned image filename stems **107, 167 or 229**; this is a retrospective within-artifact observation, without a source-selected cipher consumer. This cannot form a symmetric three-image timestamp code: a six-digit HHMMSS value of **000167 is not a valid clock time**, because seconds must be 00–59. The exact 107 overlap could still designate one object, but there is no evidence that it does. See the [updated original packet/clock census](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43710346370).

These four-byte creation fields are protected within the OpenPGP signature's hashed metadata, unlike the separately unhashed issuer ID. A signature timestamp is only as trustworthy as its key verification and original signing clock, and neither exact coincidence supplies a new GP ciphertext decoder.

## Exact first-page inversion using the signed 2016 000107 candidate

For a concrete, bounded original-cipher decision, interpret the 2016 `000107` as **one-based starting prime ordinal 107**, and transfer the **independently solved p56** GP29 additive prime-totient cipher with literal transparent F. The source timestamp is extracted directly from the original armored RSA signature packet by the SFT; the trial does not manually guess the number.

At each rune, the cipher's authentic p56 rule is plaintext-plus-`prime[k]−1 (mod29)`, with a literal plaintext F emitted as ciphertext F and **not advancing** the prime clock. The exact-state decoder retains *every algebraically legal choice* of transparent F; no English text, fitted skip mask, prime phase, additive offset or learned parameter determines its result. A control decodes original known p56 with starting ordinal 1, reconstructs the known `AN END WITHIN THE DEEP WEB` text and forward encrypts all **85/85** original runes exactly, using its documented one transparent F at local position56.

With source-derived start ordinal **107**, the untouched first LP2 p0 ciphertext and the first 729-rune chapter both become **mathematically impossible at local rune index 2**, before any free F branch arises. Original last-unknown **p55** reaches an unavoidable contradiction at local rune index **70** despite all valid F masks. Thus this **particular timestamp-to-prime-clock transfer** cannot be an exact original decipherment for these pages. This rejects a narrowly specified reversible operation with a true original p56 positive, while leaving other authentic uses of the timestamp or historical 107 image wholly unresolved. ## 2016 signed 000107 → historical LP1 p14 plaintext as a literal running key

A separate possible **human handoff** combines the 2016 signature's original UTC HHMMSS text `000107` with the 2014 Stage10's source-identical original `107.jpg` asset. That asset is byte-identical to **LP1 first-release page14**, whose koan begins *A KOAN / DURING A LESSON ... THE I IS THE VOICE OF THE CIRCUMFERENCE* and is independently decrypted. The exact historical image identity is established in [the original Stage10 source-object study](2014-stage10-three-images-lp1-source-handoff.md). The manuscript's own solved p57 parable also mentions shedding circumferences. This gives a human-recognizable possible clue chain, but **there is no original instruction to use LP1 p14 as a GP29 running key**; the 107 relation is likewise a candidate interpretation of the signed clock, not a proven page selector.

The source-pinned experiment converts the entire independently recovered LP1 p14 koan to **315 native GP29 runes**, begins at the first rune, repeats the fixed key without fitting any offset, and tests the two conventional authentic Cicada Vigenère signs on **the first 729 untouched LP2 cipher runes** and on **p55's final 76 runes**. All four original outputs reencrypt exactly; four independently supplied native-English GP29 positive plaintexts reconstruct exactly too. The best first-chapter English trigram mean is **−6.26232** versus genuine native-English control **−2.23035**, with IOC **0.03481** versus native **0.06170**. The p55 best score is **−5.67568**, still uninterpretable. These are bounded negatives, with no authentic plaintext recovered.

## Next causal decision

Recovered magic-square bytes and PGP metadata are genuine original-source material, but their tested **direct AES transfer, word-rank transposition, signature-timestamp prime-totient transfer and LP1-p14 running-key reuse do not produce unknown plaintext**. Prioritize a fresh first-party **instruction specifying both a key and its consumer**, or a new cipher architecture that predicts intact English on untouched source material. Do not turn two retrospectively noticed PGP timestamp coincidences into an unrestricted timestamp/nonce/key search.
