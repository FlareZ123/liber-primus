## The two original-facing clues

The 2013 Cicada telnet command `primes` is preserved as a participant-era transcript in [the 2013 first-onion historical repository](https://github.com/neuroretransmit/cicada/blob/main/2013/004-first-onion/README.md#primes). The printed sequence goes directly from **71 to 1229**, omitting every prime **73 through 1223**, and then continues with 1231,1237,1249,1259 etc. The printed list also uses double spaces after 29 and 3257. The preserved transcript is not a signed direct source or live server binary; it could contain historical capture errors. A separate [archived 2013 CicadaOS `prime_echo` script](../resources/historical/cicada2013-os-original-prime-echo.sh) emits **all 464 primes from 2 to 3301**, explicitly including every omitted telnet prime 73–1223. The ISO script and the first-onion telnet service are **different original-era interfaces**. The complete ISO list confirms a reproducible source for the proposed missing-prime vector, but neither invalidates the telnet report nor authenticates the missing range as a deliberately selected puzzle key. The earliest separately identified wiki witness is 2016, and the precise historical limitations are documented in [the first-onion source audit](2013-cicadaos-telnet-prime-gap-source-audit.md). The **exact omitted prime ranks** are one-based **21 through 200 inclusive**, hence **180** integers, with original order completely determined.

The 2014 Liber Primus source contains a **32×8 two-character numerical table** spanning LP2 pages 49,50,51 (original JPEGs 66–68). Under the existing source-derived byte interpretation in [the preserved grid resource](../resources/liber-primus/lp2-grid-256.json), the 256 table cells partition into **76 one-cell-thick outside-rim cells** and precisely **30×6 = 180 interior cells**. The source artifact fixes this printed geometry; using its 180 interior cells requires a selected one-cell boundary but no ciphertext-derived tuning.

The independent 2013 omission count equals the 2014 inner-table count **exactly**. This is a potentially useful human-visible way to select a *specific 180-entry prime tape* for the non-rim part of the 2014 table, distinct from unconstrained keyword hunting. The first prime of the gap, 73, was in the original 29 GP29 prime-weight alphabet (GP rune L, zero-based rune index 20), and 1229 also appears as an independently verified sum of a 2013 Instar Parable clause. Those further correspondences are contextual only; they do not specify a decoder. The omission could be historical output truncation or an unrelated 2013 staging choice.

## Frozen affordable direct consumer

To avoid creating a new unlimited prime-key search, the first trial uses the two most natural table/prime byte relations without adjusting their order to match the ciphertext:

1. Read the **180 interior bytes in ordinary printed row-major order**, row1–30, column1–6, from the 32×8 source table.
2. Read precisely the **180 omitted primes in strictly ascending order**, rank21–200.
3. Treat the prime's low eight bits as a possible byte mask. Test fixed inverses: **(table−prime) mod256**, **table XOR (prime mod256)** and the inverse sign **(table+prime) mod256**. Each is reversible byte by byte and needs no guessed parameters.
4. For each result, measure direct ASCII-readable byte fraction and GP29 low-residue index of coincidence. Compare against native text, without trying byte rotations, reverse primes, row reshuffles, free phase, or flexible text fits.

## Additional source-fixed invertible consumers

The 180 omitted primes are all odd, so every prime has a unique multiplicative inverse modulo **256**. This is an algebraic feature of the source-selected prime tape and provides a distinct lossless candidate byte cipher from the previous additive/XOR trials. Apply each prime in ordinary ascending rank order to its row-major 30×6 original table-interior byte; the plausible decryption is `plaintextByte = tableByte × prime⁻¹ mod 256`. Test its inverse orientation, multiplication by the prime, without changing rows, columns, phases, or prime order. The untouched interior contains **73/180 printable ASCII bytes**; the prime-multiplication output contains **68/180**; the division output contains only **64/180**, with a longest contiguous printable run of **3**. Encryption of each candidate decrypt is exact because the primes are invertible. Neither direct output is an ASCII plaintext message.

An independent, already-known GP target provides a stronger original-artifact control for the corresponding reversible **GF(29)** operation: multiply or divide each interior byte's residue by its assigned prime residue, then compare the final **95** positions with the **95 actual clear GP29 runes of p57**. With p56 occupying the preceding 85 slots, the raw original table residues match **2/95** clear runes, multiplication matches **0/95**, and division matches **1/95**. The natural direct multiplicative prime-key transfer therefore fails the only completely clear held-out part of the 180-glyph terminal register. This is restricted to the fixed reading and operation and cannot exclude position-dependent or externally keyed operations.

The **180 omitted ordinal slots** also specify a finite interpolation question rather than a symbol-by-symbol pad. Over the ordinary prime field **GF(257)**, assign the 180 row-major table-interior values to evaluation coordinates **21–200**, matching the missing prime ranks. Interpolate the unique polynomial of degree at most **179** and evaluate it at all **76 complementary coordinates** 1–20 and 201–256. ## Decision

Retain the count alignment as a **conditional, separately sourced key-tape hypothesis**, not as an established cryptographic relationship. Direct row-major additive, XOR and multiplicative consumers fail to reveal plaintext; the 95-rune clear control excludes the tested multiplicative GP29 transfer, and exact GF257 interpolation fails the 76-cell rim test under every cyclic perimeter orientation. These negatives do not rule out a source-selected transposition, a different payload representation, or an independently defined nonlinear operation involving the **76-cell rim**. A successor investigation is worthwhile only if an original image mark, authentic 2013 instruction, or dated contemporaneous solver account independently selects the prime-to-grid permutation or an explicit operator; this is the missing ingredient. This route is stronger than arbitrary number play in that **all 180 input primes are chosen by a concrete original-era omission and the 180 table positions by 2014 artifact geometry**, before any ciphertext scoring.
