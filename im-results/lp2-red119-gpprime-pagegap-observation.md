## An original-artifact navigation hypothesis

On the actual JPEGs, p33 ends with a cuneiform-style emblem reproduced at the top of p39; p39 is the final page before the next chapter begins at p40. Thus the possible manuscript reading is **p3 red triad → p33 chapter → the repeated p39 emblem and red triad → p40 next chapter**. The exact source motifs make this route more discriminating than an arbitrary lookup using these two numbers. Nothing on the source pages explicitly tells a player to apply *prime-index-to-page-number*, and the p33→p39 leg is the natural reading order of a chapter with a repeated emblem. No ciphertext decipherment emerges at p33 or p40 by the pointer operation alone.

Both triad sums are prime, and their difference **173−137=36** equals the source page gap **39−3=36**. Their decimal digits are anagrams, although 173 is not the literal reverse of137 (which is731). Subtracting the respective **one-based physical page ordinals** 4 and40 from the two sums gives **133** in both cases. These page-ordinal corrections are a possible checksum, not a proven original author rule.
