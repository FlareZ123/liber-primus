## An original-artifact navigation hypothesis

**The prime ordinal points to an original chapter boundary in both cases:** **p33** begins the ink-defined p33–39 chapter, while **p40** begins the next p40–53 chapter. On the actual JPEGs, p33 ends with a cuneiform-style emblem reproduced at the top of p39; p39 is the final page before the next chapter begins at p40. Thus the possible manuscript reading is **p3 red triad → p33 chapter → the repeated p39 emblem and red triad → p40 next chapter**. The exact source motifs make this route more discriminating than an arbitrary lookup using these two numbers. Nothing on the source pages explicitly tells a player to apply *prime-index-to-page-number*, and the p33→p39 leg is the natural reading order of a chapter with a repeated emblem. No ciphertext decipherment emerges at p33 or p40 by the pointer operation alone.

Both triad sums are prime, and their difference **173−137=36** equals the source page gap **39−3=36**. Their decimal digits are anagrams, although 173 is not the literal reverse of137 (which is731). Subtracting the respective **one-based physical page ordinals** 4 and40 from the two sums gives **133** in both cases. The independently archived 2014 seventh-onion HTML index prints exactly `<title>133</title>` and `<div id="331">` and enumerates all 58 LP2 page images ([contemporary HTML source](../resources/cicada2014-onion7-index.html)). These page-ordinal corrections are a possible checksum, not a proven original author rule.

Separately, an earlier report established a different, later-text route to **133** and **331**: solved p57 *Parable* has heading GP-prime sum449, and two body clauses have sums1031 and1229, yielding **1031 mod449=133** and **1229 mod449=331** ([independent historical arithmetic](audio-761-pad-scan.md#parable-s-heading-is-the-modulus-of-the-seventh-onion-title-and-id)). Neither that modulus selection nor the triad page-subtraction rule is independently instructed by Cicada. Together they nominate historical page navigation or checksumming as a testable possibility, rather than certifying it.
