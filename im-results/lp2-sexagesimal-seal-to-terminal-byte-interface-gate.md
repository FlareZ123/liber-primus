# Issue #833: Babylonian seal versus alphanumeric 256-cell table

**Decision (Agent48, 14 November 2026):** The original sources support a shared *possible radix 60*, but fail the proposed **same literal glyph/digit alphabet** precondition. A byte-alphabet conversion is plausible and historically conventional, yet no pre-2014 Cicada-authored instruction selects the intervening transliteration or a table→GP29 decoding operator. **Do not fit the 180 known terminal runes or spend CI on further rings under this incomplete interface.** This is a source-selector negative, not a no-go theorem about every use of the table.

## Actual source-facing operations

1. The repeated graphic in physical p33–39 (`resources/liber-primus/50.jpg` through `56.jpg`) uses cuneiform-style additive groups of tens and units. Historical standard reading fixes two positional pairs **(17,13)** and **(55,1)**, resulting in **1033** and **3301** as two-place integer values in base 60. The grouped *wedges* are the symbols actually printed. Exact upper stamps are pixel-identical across p34–39 (closed #714); a historical numeral comparator and lack of independent cipher operation are documented in `im-results/issue344-cuneiform-historical-template-gate.md` and closed #815.
2. The distinct printed object in physical p49–51 (`66.jpg`–`68.jpg`) has **10+13+9=32** rows, eight Latin-alphanumeric two-character tokens each. Under the conventional fixed ordered alphabet `0123456789ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwx`, all **256** tokens are valid and give candidate base60 values **0..255**, with **161 distinct values**. High digits are **0/1/2/3/4** in counts **60/58/65/56/17**, respectively; second characters cover 59 of 60 digits, with lowercase `f` (41) absent. The leftmost tokens `3N 3p 2l` give bytes **203, 231, 167**. The maximum is printed `4F`, yielding **255**. This is internally compatible with a byte encoding; it does not authenticate a specific consumer.
3. Writing the seal's two place-value pairs in the *table's distinct* Latin alphabet would require **`HD` for 1033** and **`t1` for 3301**. Neither appears in the table, and neither could appear as a single candidate **byte** because their first digits are 17 and 55, not 0–4. The seal visibly prints wedge counts, never the ordered Latin alphabet. Equating these two original glyph systems requires an added transliteration instruction; equating their numerals with the 256 table bytes requires a further framing, key, or reduction rule.
4. The exact Latin digit order is independently attested in the **2007 MIRACL manual** (`resources/external/miracl-2007-base60-alphabet.md`), which establishes period plausibility without Cicada authorship. Cicada's **signed 2013 TCP-server protocol** explicitly specifies `BASE29` (not BASE60) and has no rule carrying two cuneiform sexagesimal values into a 256-byte GP cipher (`resources/external/2013-tcp-server-signed-source.md`). The authentic 2014 Stage11 distributor HTML supplies image links but no operation. This bounded first-party/source review found **no author-selected** direction, mapping of byte values to 29 runes, cipher family, clock, key, or handling of F.

## Consequence and guardrails

The original p55(76) + known p56(85) + clear p57(95) totals 256, and the table's four nested rings have lengths 76/68/60/52. These independent source counts can motivate further research, but already-closed [terminal known-180 experiments](lp2-terminal256-knowninterior-ring-gate.md) establish strong negative gates for many unselected ring consumers. This task's **preconditions stop before such a test**: a shared mathematical base is insufficient to claim a shared literal glyph inventory, a selected serialization, or a source-provided reversible operation.

Lowercase `f` missing from one table digit slot does not by itself authenticate transfer of the historically solved **plaintext rune F** bypass into this different character system. Both base59 and base60 have period-plausible alternatives in the source corpus; a Latin-ASCII base60 reading remains a useful *working assumption*. No p56 matched rune and no p55 candidate plaintext has been recovered here.

**Reproducer:** `node sims/lp2_sexagesimal_byte_interface_sft.cjs` reads the existing 32×8 source-derived table, asserts every numeric fact above and prints the original seal's independently established readings. Physical wedge-versus-Latin glyph identity is supported by the original source scans and historical audits, not computed by that JSON-only script.

**Fork:** Close the proposed *cross-artifact byte-decoder handoff* at its stated source-selector gate. Reopen only for an authenticated Cicada period instruction explicitly connecting the stamp's 1033/3301 to a fixed table byte/GP29 conversion and state; then require a frozen 40+ independently held-out p56 symbol prediction before touching p55.
