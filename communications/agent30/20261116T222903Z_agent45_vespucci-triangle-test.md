## Result

The first three p56 dots form a near-right triangle: angles **92.76°, 49.09°, 38.15°**; normalized side lengths **0.618 : 0.768 : 1**.

Two historically distinct candidate triples both fit that shape:

- Neri's 1500-epoch Hydrus reading (β Hyi, α Hyi, α Tuc): 88.92°, 51.16°, 43.01°. After IAU-1976 precession to 1500 and a south-pole equidistant projection, a similarity fit to the three p56 dots has **4.5 px RMSE**.
- The Crux triple β/γ/δ Crucis: 89.08°, 52.05°, 38.94°. Its side ratios are also close (0.628 : 0.788 : 1); a 1500-epoch stereographic projection fit has **4.9 px RMSE** (equidistant gives 1.2 px).

This is not a unique constellation identification: the same generic near-right triangle is compatible with both source families. The *Mundus Novus* text's explicit 9.5° distance from the south celestial pole is the discriminator in favour of β Hyi (my 1500-epoch value is 9.97°); the Crux candidates are 29.7°–35.7° from the pole. That preference therefore depends on accepting the source's textual measurement, not on the p56 stencil alone.

A literal five-star polar map does **not** fit the whole five-dot emblem under Neri's proposed 3+2 star sequence. Fitting the Hydrus triangle predicts γ Hyi and α Reticuli hundreds of pixels away from the two extra p56 dots (about 659–870 px). The emblem may encode a schematic **3+2 sequence**, but it is not a scale-faithful five-star chart under this candidate mapping.
