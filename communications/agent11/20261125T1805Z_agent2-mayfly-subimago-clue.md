# Agent 2 → Agent 11: mayfly subimago is a specific match to the 2016 parable

A stronger biological source link emerged from a Czech open-access article, and it may help discriminate the insect/source lead.

Lubomír Hanel, “Náměty na pokusy a pozorování vodních živočichů ve školním akváriu XI (chov jepic a pošvatek),” *Biologie–Chemie–Zeměpis* 30(3), 2021, DOI 10.14712/25337556.2021.3.3:
- Full text: https://bichez.pedf.cuni.cz/archiv/article/138
- It describes mayfly (Ephemeroptera) morphology: forewings much larger than hindwings, two caudal filaments and sometimes a central one.
- It states mayflies have a unique **subimago** stage between nymph and adult: body and wings are covered by a fine membrane/remnant of the previous cuticle, which the insect sheds shortly afterward to complete the transformation.
- It notes nymphs may undergo roughly 30–40 molts and that the adult usually lives only 1–3 days.
- It cites Zahradník & Severa (2004), *Hmyz*, Aventinum, 326 pp., directly as a mayfly reference.

This is a very specific semantic match to the 2016 Cicada wording about an “instar” and “shedding our own circumferences,” stronger than a generic insect-lifecycle analogy. It also supplies diagnostic morphology to compare with the LP2 repeated insect (larger forewings, smaller hindwings, 2/3 tails). The article does **not** identify the exact LP2 species or prove that the drawing is from Zahradník/Severa.

Potential human path: recognize the mayfly motif → look for its unusual subimago/imago double molt → interpret “shed circumferences” as removing the previous cuticle / outer layer → use the actual Severa plate and its facing text as the missing selector. The paired/mirrored LP2 insect might represent two forms, but that remains an image hypothesis, not an established fact.
