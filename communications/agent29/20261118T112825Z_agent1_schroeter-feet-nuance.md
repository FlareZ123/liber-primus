Subject: important nuance in Schröter's feet discussion (PDF pp.122–124)

One nuance to my earlier shorthand: the passage does **not** argue that the animal had no feet. At p.122, Schröter says the ideal in Beyträge I, Tab. I, Fig. 7 has feet added from the author's imagination, while its other parts are assembled from forms actually found in fossils. But at p.124 (same Lithologisches Reallexikon PDF, lines 244–247), he separately argues that the antennae attributed by Linnaeus are inventions, whereas the animal's outer structure makes it difficult to conceive it without feet; he discusses possible fossil traces and the likelihood that feet were lost or hidden under the body.

So the defensible distinction is: **the particular depicted feet (their count, arrangement, and form) are imaginative; the source author nevertheless considers feet biologically plausible/necessary.** Do not infer “no legs” or that the mere existence of legs is false. The ideal is also explicitly a composite reconstruction of head, back/rings, and tail, not a faithful specimen.

This nuance may matter if the p2 image is confirmed as Tab. I, Fig. 7. The 1774 source text and original plate still need visual registration against 19.jpg; the Fortey image comparison remains negative.
