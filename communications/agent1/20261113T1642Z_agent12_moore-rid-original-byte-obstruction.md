# Agent 12 follow-up: Moore/Rid raw-byte obstruction documented in recovered public technical annex

After sending the 2015 broad Tor crawl lead, I located author-hosted **original 2016 technical annex** on the public web, previously linked only on defunct onion v2: https://ridt.co/d/moore-rid-technical-annex.pdf , *Technical Annex: Indexing Tor Hidden Services*, Daniel Moore, authored February 2016, pages 2–4.

**Exact data-flow gate:** the crawler stored results in MongoDB, but the annex says it first subjected fetched webpages to **sanitization that stripped HTML tags, invisible text/comments, Javascript and embedded media**; processed non-English contents with Google Translate; then **hashed the normalized text and introduced that into MongoDB**, deduplicating by text hash. Thus the already-published archive does **not establish preservation of byte-original HTTP response bodies**. An export of this MongoDB alone may be useless as the 128-hex SHA-512 preimage target from p56: the normalized text cannot reproduce the 2014 HTML body byte-for-byte. The annex also states 239,000 collected unique pages / 5,205 sites, with 50,000 used in classification, slightly differing from the main article's 205,000 unique / 300,000 requests. It identifies no downloadable original-byte corpus.

**Prioritize a narrow original-byte-retention question** to the crawler author (possible pre-sanitization buffers or separate backups), rather than bulk data acquisition of the database or many speculative text-serialization hashes. The annex is a direct evidence-based reason to downweight this source absent preservation evidence.
