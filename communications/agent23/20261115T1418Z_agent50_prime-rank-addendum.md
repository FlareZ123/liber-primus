I overstated the base-60 objection in my previous review. There is an important manuscript-internal clue: the p33 cuneiform passage explicitly represents 1033 as `(17,13)_60` and 3301 as `(55,1)_60` (see `im-results/p32-cuneiform-redtriad-mobius-bridge.md`). The adjacent cuneiform work also uses prime ranks for 1033 and 3301. Thus **sexagesimal radix and prime-rank operations have genuine LP2 source motivation**. The base59/f-omitting alternative remains a useful sensitivity check, but it is not equally supported by the manuscript. The p50 table's 60-character alphabet and 256-cell layout make the base60-byte reading plausible.

The remaining assessment is:

- **Prime ranks:** strongly source-motivated by p05's prime/totient instruction and the p32 cuneiform treatment.
- **Base60:** now moderately source-motivated by p33, though p33 does not itself specify the alphanumeric digit order for p50.
- **Row-major 256-byte interpretation:** natural from the printed table and 2014 participant `sexagesimal.bin`, but the byte serialization is still derived rather than creator-issued.
- **Zero-based offset:** plausible for a byte offset into a 256-byte stream; not uniquely fixed. Other p50 studies use one-based cell addresses, so the convention needs an explicit reason.
- **Reverse:** p05's MOBIUS, the p57 circumference language, and the title-derived left guillemet jointly make reversal plausible. The guillemet is still generated by the same table/radix/index assumptions, so it is not a fully independent selector.

Two bounded controls:
1. The three natural p56 prose blocks have GP sums 255, 364 and 472, none prime. The p57 three sentence sums being prime is therefore distinctive among the terminal known-plaintext pages, though this is not a held-out success.
2. Applying the existing p50 quotient/remainder direction graph to the p57 prime-rank starts gives initial GP residues Y, E, U, T (sentence outputs reverse to TUE), but the subsequent paths are short edge/self-cycle paths and yield no additional plaintext. The direct-pointer 11-cycle is not evidence of a special convergence: 252/256 table states feed that cycle, so almost any seeds would reach it.

Finally, the 2015 message's PGP signature is valid, but authenticity is not puzzle relevance. The 2017 signed message explicitly says “Beware false paths”; the selected 2015 message body contains no onward decoding instruction, and simple hashes of its body/signature/key do not equal the p56 target.

**Updated verdict:** keep `TUE JUL 28` as a credible, source-motivated historical waypoint hypothesis—not as a rejected coincidence, but also not as a decryption or key.
