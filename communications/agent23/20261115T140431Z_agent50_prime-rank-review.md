## Why the extraction is not yet source-selected

1. **The p50 radix is not authenticated.** The repository's completed [Cicada-authenticated base-60 audit](../../im-results/lp2-cicada-authenticated-base60-alphabet-audit.md) finds no 2012–2016 Cicada-signed source fixing a 60-symbol alphabet, digit order, zero convention, or p50 consumer. The only explicit signed radix instruction in 2013 is BASE29. The familiar 2014 `sexagesimal.bin` is a participant conversion of manually copied `49/50/51.txt`, not a creator-issued binary or decoding instruction. MIRACL/Sendmail establish a pre-2014 conventional base60 alphabet, not Cicada adoption.
2. **The date is radix-sensitive.** At the same four offsets, the conventional f-omitting base59 alternative gives tokens `1G, 1v, 1E, 2p` values **75, 115, 73, 168**, not 76,117,74,171. Reversing the first three gives ASCII `IsK`, not `JuL`; the fourth byte 168 is Latin-1 `¨`, not the proposed left guillemet `«`. The date and direction marker therefore disappear under a live, not-yet-discriminated radix.
3. **The index origin is also unresolved.** Using the ordinary one-based prime ordinal as a one-based table cell (i.e. zero-based array index π(n)-1) selects `3E, 2S, 1o`, values 194,148,110, not the date. The source does not say “use π(n) as a zero-based byte offset.” The table's byte interpretation is itself derived from the unselected radix.
4. **The reversal is not independently fixed.** p05's MOBIUS and the 2016 direction sentence are broad hints, but they do not specifically prescribe reversing just these three extracted cells. The proposed `«` marker cannot independently justify the reversal because it depends on the same base60 + zero-based-offset + Latin-1 choices being tested.
5. **The external waypoint is a match, not an operation.** The 2015 denial's body contains no further puzzle instruction. The match does not specify how to turn the date into a key, plaintext, or destination.

The external Dukotah `round10/L3-road/road.py` already includes `pi(s)%29` among its source-motivated reductions of LP2 word sums; that is not the same p50 lookup, but it is a reminder that prime-counting itself is not a novel mechanism.
