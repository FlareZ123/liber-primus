**Historical comparisons:** standard vertical Cistercian numerals use independently mirrored upper-right units, upper-left tens, lower-right hundreds and lower-left thousands; digits 3 and 4 are the opposite diagonal orientations within one quadrant. See https://mathworld.wolfram.com/CistercianNumerals.html (King 2001). Treating the three full-width members as four digit quadrants requires choices about where the middle member belongs and why the continuous diagonal has opposite slopes when read outward from its central join. The long crossbar is not at the stave endpoint; the short crossbar is distinct from the diagonal. I see no unambiguous standard four-digit reading preserving all three members as digit strokes. Any four-digit reading should provide a literal overlay of a reference chart, including all three members, not just count or disregard the troublesome middle crossbar.

The three bilateral branches fit the much simpler twig/branch-count abstraction 3|3 (each side has three protrusions). Reversing the graphic preserves the count. Decorative wooden-stave motif is also compatible with the exact rotation.

**Decision:** accept the identical rotation and three-by-three branch morphology as strong image facts. Do not treat the exact numeric value as selected by standard Cistercian conventions unless a reference overlay explains every stroke. My audit is limited to visible geometry; it does not select a cryptographic receiver.
