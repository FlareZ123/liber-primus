## Result

The proposed prime sequence A97 → A11 → A47 → A5 → A17 → A83 does **not** form a continuous A-road route in that order. Some adjacent pairs do meet, but the chain breaks at multiple points:

- A11 meets A47 at Thickthorn near Norwich (National Highways).
- A47 meets A5 at Longshoot between Hinckley and Nuneaton (planning-inquiry transport assessment).
- A47 meets A17 at Pullover near King's Lynn (Norfolk transport strategy).
- A97 is a Banff–Dinnet road in Aberdeenshire; its endpoints are A98 at Banff and A93 at Dinnet. There is no direct A97→A11 transition.
- The A5/A17 pair has no direct junction in the candidate order. Their documented nearby A47 connections are at different ends of the A47, so joining them would require leaving the proposed sequence and revisiting A47.
- A83 is a separate Scottish trunk route from Tarbet through Inveraray toward Campbeltown, not a continuation from A17.

Thus the road list is not an executable contiguous route as presently specified. A looser itinerary using unspecified intervening roads is always possible, but it supplies no unique route-selection rule.

## Base-rate problem

The GP prime values are almost tailor-made to look like British A-road numbers: at least **25 of the 29** GP primes are existing A-road labels without any transformation beyond prefixing “A”:

- all 21 two-digit GP primes from 11 through 97 occur within the A10–A99 series;
- the single-digit GP primes 2, 3, 5 and 7 are also A-roads.

The current road directory lists the two-digit A-road series and the Scottish A7/A83/A97; the national numbering explanation says that A-roads are allocated across nine geographic zones, with the first digit indicating a zone. Therefore, the fact that all six selected values happen to be A-road numbers is not a selective result. It is expected for most values in the GP prime alphabet. This does not rule out a more specific path rule, but the prefix-A mapping itself contributes little evidence.

## What genuinely fits Inveraray

Historic Environment Scotland independently confirms:
- the A83 runs along Loch Fyne at Inveraray;
- the Oak Walk is the principal northward route through the designed landscape toward Carloonan Dovecot;
- the extended oak avenue to the dovecot was probably removed between 1750 and 1800.

That is a real thematic fit to the 2016 message’s “path lies empty” plus the archived image’s oak-tree description. However, the destination is selected only after the sequence ends at Ethel/83, and no evidence yet shows that the preceding road numbers uniquely lead there.

There is also a relevant alternative at the *other* end: Historic Environment Scotland records the Duff House designed landscape by Banff, with Eagles Gate Lodge on the A97 and the Sandyhills Dovecote overlooking the Deveron valley. This creates a second A-road/dovecot/estate coincidence, not a unique route to Inveraray. It may be a useful comparative control, but it also increases the need for a precommitted selection rule.
