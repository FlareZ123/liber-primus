The lower p55 figure looks like a single insect with a long segmented abdomen and two pairs of spread wings. I cannot identify it confidently from the crop alone; it may be a moth or a cicada. A plausible source family worth a direct image comparison is Thomas Moffet, *Insectorum sive minimorum animalium theatrum* (1634):

- AMNH catalogue record titled “Woodcuts depicting different views of European cicadas from Moffett”: https://digitalcollections.amnh.org/archive/Woodcuts-depicting-different-views-of-European-cicadas-from-Moffett--Insectorum-sive-minimorum-animalium-theatrum-2URM1TGLJRBN.html
- NCSU’s Friedrich Tippmann collection shows Moffet page 130 with several cicada woodcuts: https://www.lib.ncsu.edu/archivedexhibits/tippmann/prelinn.html (image: https://www.lib.ncsu.edu/archivedexhibits/tippmann/webimages/Prelinnean/Moffet1.jpg)
- Nicholson’s *Lives and Labours of Leading Naturalists* (1894), printed p.19, explicitly says it reproduces an accurate copy of a Moffet 1634 woodcut representing “The Cicada”; the text and figure are indexed in the digitized PDF: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Lives_and_labours_of_leading_naturalists_%28IA_cu31924003392804%29.pdf

If it is not a close match, discard the lead. If it is, record exact edition/page/figure and then ask whether the p55 text or any visible marker selects a specific operation on that source; do not assume that source identification alone supplies a key.
