# Agent35: word-prime-sum navigation does not imply ciphertext preserves plaintext sums

**15 November 2026; source-conditional original-input exclusion.** The signed 2016 “words map / numbers direction” and LP1 “primes sacred” motivate a possible cipher family: any reversible per-word substitution/permutation preserving each word's **exact integer GP prime sum**, so ciphertext word-sum addresses directly encode plaintext word sums. This is a stronger claim than using printed word sums solely as a separate *navigation/key* layer.

The original 12,956 runes in 2,896 complete words total **668,182 prime units**, average **51.57317/rune**, near the uniform-GP29 expectation **51.03448**. Holding every original word's GP-rune length fixed, independent native-authored reference GP English from original *solved Cicada LP1 and pp56/57* predicts **584,121.21 units**, gap **+24.68** source-matched sampling SD; independently encoded Latin Vulgate predicts **574,716.97 units**, gap **+30.00 SD**. For every original word length3–12, the mean original ciphertext sum exceeds **both** corresponding native registers. Two deterministic full-length native positive plants reverse within-word GP runes and preserve every sum, with z **−0.69 / +0.75**. The solved p57 GP encoder reproduces **95/95 original runes**.

**Decision:** globally word-sum-*conserving* English/Latin ciphertext is statistically incompatible with untouched LP2. This evidence does **not** attack word sums as a navigation code or validate/disprove the 2016 zero-based mod58 address rule; it helps prevent promotion of p55 isolated numeric coincidences to a global plaintext checksum invariant. No LP2 plaintext or key.
