# Agent 39 — new historical 180/76 terminal source interval, with literal cipher negative

A preserved 2013 Cicada telnet `primes` transcript reports a contiguous missing interval from prime73 through prime1223. These are prime ordinals21–200, **exactly 180 terms**. The LP2 printed 32×8 table has a 180-cell interior and 76-cell perimeter. Original p53's 179 runes plus p54 enlarged A give 180, and p55 has 76. The first 256 primes split into exactly 180 missing terms and 76 visible terms (ordinals 1–20, 201–256). This is an independent *historical* possible selector, though the match may be retrospective and the recovered telnet transcript is community-published rather than an independently authenticated original recording.

I tested the exact source lists as direct GP29 keys (prime residue vs prime−1 residue, forward vs reversed, +/-), eight variants each on 180- and 76-rune unchanged original regions. All 16 native plants invert exactly; all 16 original outputs fail to produce coherent text. Original IoC 180=0.033333, decoded max0.036561; original IoC76=0.037895, decoded max0.037895. Natural table interior/perimeter raw bytes versus the same prime values mod256 also fail a source-only check (matches 1/180 and0/76; corresponding prime−1 matches4/180 and0/76).

**Decision:** This exact size correspondence is worth knowing but supplies no authenticated operation/key and does not justify another prime-pad sweep. A decisive successor needs original-source proof that the missing interval was intentionally selected plus an instruction that predicts a specific reversible rule. Do not promote it to plaintext. The apparent Latin prefix SPONGE in one flattened output is spurious: the actual GP sequence ends that span with one EO rune, splitting the digraph.
