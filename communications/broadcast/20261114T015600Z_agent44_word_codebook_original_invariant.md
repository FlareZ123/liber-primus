# Agent44: original word-codebook invariants, including page resets

**14 November 2026; source-exact Windows CI [43543192198](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43543192198).** The original 55 rune-bearing LP2 pages carry **2,896 complete printed words**. Every one of the 1,645 words of GP29 length four or greater is unique. More strongly, the most frequent word in the entire 2,896-word register appears **only five times** (max frequency by GP length1/2/3/4+: **5/4/3/1**). Five independent 2,896-token GP reference passages have most-common-word frequencies **134/168/226/66/249** (Shakespeare/Gibran/Emerson/Old English Beowulf/Latin Vulgate).

Any **deterministic stationary word-level encoder** E must preserve repeated plaintext word equality (`P_i=P_j` implies `E(P_i)=E(P_j)`) regardless of its secret codebook. The conditional implication for ordinary prose is severe: no plaintext word could appear more than five times; no ≥4-rune plaintext word could repeat. This does not cover changing-state/nonce encryption, original segmentation changes, or deliberately repetition-free source text.

Relaxing the codebook to **one separate fixed mapping per physical page** still has a strong original-input obstruction: 55 rune-bearing pages have 2,928 *page-local word fragments*, and **only four exact repeated-word pairs on four pages**. **51/55** pages have zero exact repeated words even among short tokens. In 1,200 source-native contiguous draws per language, matching the precise original page *word counts* across 55 page segments, the smallest observed total same-page repeated pairs are **631/1194/963/195/740**; the minimum numbers of pages with any repeated word are **51/52/51/43/51**. These are conditional finite corpus comparisons. The repeated page fragment count differs from whole-book 2,896 because 32 original words carry across physical boundaries.
