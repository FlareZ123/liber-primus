# Agent 18: Ac → A-road → Inveraray Oak Walk / Carloonan Dovecot lead

## Hypothesis

The signed 2016 Cicada message's line “Its words are the map, their meaning is the road, and their numbers are the direction” may be a literal hint to the Great Britain A-road numbering system. A-road numbers encode a geographical zone in their first digit. The Old English Rune Poem / Gematria Primus may therefore be a semantic road map, with GP prime values acting as A-road numbers.

A source-constrained path from the oversized Ac rune on LP2 p54 is unusually suggestive:

- GP alphabet order is the 29 Futhorc rune-poem stanzas; Ac is one-based stanza 25, with GP prime 97.
- The solved p56 mechanism uses the prime-totient operation \u03c6(p)=p−1.
- Starting at Ac and advancing \u03c6(p_r) mod 29 positions in the 29-stanza map gives:
  **Ac → Rad → Eolh → Thorn → Gyfu → Ethel**
- The corresponding GP primes / candidate A-roads are:
  **97 → 11 → 47 → 5 → 17 → 83**
  i.e. **A97 → A11 → A47 → A5 → A17 → A83**.
- This is not a general search: start=Ac is motivated by the p54 Ac anchor and the 2016 oak-tree image; the p−1 step is motivated by the solved p56 prime-totient method; the modulo-29 ring is the 29-stanza map.

## Why A83 / Inveraray is worth testing

The British A-road system has nine zones; the first digit of an A-road number identifies the zone, with zones bounded by A1–A9 and the coastline. This makes road numbers literal geographic directions, matching the 2016 wording.

Historic Environment Scotland's official Inveraray Castle designed-landscape entry states:
- the **A83 runs along the shore of Loch Fyne**;
- Inveraray Castle is the focal point of a designed **estate** in Argyll and Bute (zone 8);
- the **Oak Walk** is the primary northward route through the estate;
- the Oak Walk terminates at the **Carloonan Dovecot**;
- the extended avenue of oak trees leading to the dovecot was probably removed between 1750 and 1800.

That creates a tight convergence:
- Ethel means estate; its GP prime is 83 → A83 → Inveraray Castle estate.
- Ac means oak; the 2016 image is described as an oak tree in winter.
- The signed message says “the path lies empty”; the Oak Walk's former oak avenue was removed.
- “epiphany seeks the devoted” could point to the dovecot / doves, though this religious-symbol interpretation is presently conjectural.

## Secondary A97 lead

A97 runs Banff–Dinnet in Aberdeenshire (zone 9) and passes the Duff House designed landscape. Duff House has woodland walks and a separate listed dovecot at Sandyhill Road, Banff. This could be a start-side echo of the oak/estate/dovecot motif, but the exact relationship to the 2016 image is unverified.

## Caveats / what would falsify it

- Many small UK A-road numbers exist; the mere fact that a GP prime is also an A-road number is weak evidence. Test the full sequence and semantic correspondences against a base-rate control.
- The road sequence has not yet been shown to be a contiguous or uniquely navigable route. Verify actual junctions and endpoints; do not assume a route merely because the roads exist.
- The original 2016 image bytes are not independently recovered in the archive. The signed text is reported as a good signature under the known Cicada key, but image identity/location remains provisional.
- “Epiphany” → dove/dovecot is conjectural. Look for a stronger historical or iconographic link, or reject it.
- The simple ciphertext-driven word-boundary walk over the rune-poem ring produced a near-uniform state distribution and no readable route; do not repeat it. A plaintext-feedback prime-weight clock family also failed to produce English. Those negatives do not test the A-road route interpretation.
