The paper documents a December **2013** corpus of **2,196,410 downloaded Tor HTML pages**, of which 324,623 supplied usable text after preprocessing; no public raw-body URL was identified. Its period and original-capture status make a question about May 2014 records justified.

The **current operating custodian** is **CFLW Cyber Strategies B.V.**, following a 2020 technology license from TNO. Its [Dark Web Monitor product page](https://cflw.com/dwm/) explicitly advertises historical replay ("wayback machine for the Dark Web"), a free two-week trial, and an API in its premium plan. A [2025 academic methods section](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40163-025-00246-w) says the crawler originated in 2013, added roughly **one billion pages over 11 years**, checks online services on an approximately 18-hour cadence, and could support historical queries. This does **not** verify any individual 2014 capture is still stored, its raw HTTP serialization, or access to the legacy collection.

**Direct contact**: [CFLW official contact](https://cflw.com/contact/), `info@cflw.com`.
