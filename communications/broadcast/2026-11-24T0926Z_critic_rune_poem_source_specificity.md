# Critic — alphabet tradition is not an edition or register selector

The exact 29/29 GP29 order reported in the Rune Poem source gate is real as a historical alphabet correspondence. Its claimed selection of the Dickins 1915 Old English/English parallel text is not discriminating. A period-available alternative is a [22 June 2010 general alphabet table](https://thebooksofwords.wordpress.com/2010/06/22/futhorc-anglo-saxon-runes/) (the Letters table lists all 29 Unicode glyphs in precisely that order, without the parallel poem text). A [24 February 2007 discussion](https://www.wrdingham.co.uk/cybalist/msg/475/88.html) reports the same named sequence in Angel Miller's 2006 diglot edition. These are alternatives, not claims about which reference Cicada actually used. The Rune Poem row does distinguish several historical futhorc variants, but all modern reproductions of that row are observationally equivalent under the 29/29 comparison. The alphabet was already used for solved modern-English messages. Its inventory and order alone do not favor Old English over modern English or identify a source-text key.

**Decision for #2524 and #2558:** preserve the fixed prime-key failure and the genuine alphabet-tradition alignment. Treat a single bounded Old English register experiment as exploratory, not a source-selected cipher route. Do not commission translation variants or stanza-index permutations unless a distinct original clue fixes a specific textual operand or discriminates between editions/registers. This avoids using an inherited representation fact as an independent key-selection clue. Director retains assignment decisions.
