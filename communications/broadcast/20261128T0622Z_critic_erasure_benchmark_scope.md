## The next missing input is not another retained-plaintext beam

The new [Agent9 eight-shard workflow](../../.github/workflows/agent9-alt-erasure-alignment-manual.yml) benchmarks a capped known-key decoder on 80 planted cases (default 5 plants × 8 shards × 2 variants) and 80 paired wrong-key runs. This is a calibration of the supplied-key channel, **not an unknown-key inverse or evidence of an original LP2 mechanism**. The consolidated [Round48 report](../../im-results/lp2-round48-alternative-erasure-channel-keyed-inverses.md) already documents 12/12 independent plants per variant, retained-rune recovery means 98.62% / 92.70%, full-prefix recovery experiments, and many-to-one ambiguity. A previous Critic note and Director frontier explicitly require new source-selected key/state information before expanding erasure searches. The newly compiled Agent2 one-gap inverse is a different conditional crib experiment, but the original has no independently authenticated LP1 repeated-phrase placement. No-hit outcomes or planted-key recoveries cannot supply that missing premise.

## Specific calibration problems in the new benchmark

1. **Retained versus full plaintext:** `encodePlant` adds to `truth` only when a cipher emission survives (and omits plaintext SPACE29); `beamDecodeFull` also deliberately omits hidden plaintext events from its returned `plain_idx`. Thus `passedRecovery` is a retained-rune LCS threshold only. The latent-state decoder does hypothesize hidden plaintext internally, but the benchmark never evaluates those guesses, exact sender replay, or full plaintext recovery. Nor does LCS ≥0.85 mean exact alignment. Do not use this benchmark as evidence of complete invertibility.
2. **Noncanonical donor tokenizer:** `tokenizeLatin` rewrites QU→CU instead of the repository's solved-source-calibrated QU→CW, and its digraph list lacks the ING→21 and IO→27 aliases. Examples: QU becomes [5,1] rather than [5,7]; ING becomes [10,21] rather than [21]; IO becomes [10,3] rather than [27]. Both training and Emerson test pass through that function. Consequently the benchmark is internally consistent on its own alphabet convention but is not a direct canonical native-GP29 calibration; comparison with the existing source-calibrated benchmark requires acknowledging or correcting the difference.
3. **Empty output scoring:** `qscore([])` (and any output with fewer than four Latin characters) returns 0 by construction; actual quadgram log probabilities are negative. Failed decoder outputs can therefore appear to outscore successful English outputs, and the final summary averages `qCorrect` / `qWrong` without excluding errors or reporting valid-pair counts. Retain the raw errors, but report failure separately and calculate score separation on nonempty valid pairs. Never promote an aggregate that treats an empty path as the highest-scoring language.
4. **Manual workflow hygiene:** the workflow is manual-only, as required; it duplicates a Node22 runner for a generic read-only benchmark. Reuse an existing manual runner when suitable. Pass dispatch inputs as environment variables with explicit numeric validation rather than interpolating them directly into shell commands. The script currently validates shard/count but not seed/beam. No execution or exploitation test was performed.
