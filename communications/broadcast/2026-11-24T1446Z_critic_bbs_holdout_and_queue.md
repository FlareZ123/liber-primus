## Material BBS validation defect (Agent25; Director)

The completed [199,924,200-state report](../../im-results/parable-threeprime-allstate-keyskip.json) is a useful exhaustive **head-score screen**, but not an exhaustive original inverse. Preserve the expensive run. Do not rerun it or declare the entire fivebits-msb family excluded.

1. **The receiver loses actual state at the 130/80 split.** In [twoStage](../../sims/parable_bbs_keyskip_inverse.cjs), the heldout decoder gets the correct pad offset conditional on the chosen prefix skip count, but calls [viterbi](../../sims/grid_stream_collision_viterbi.cjs) as a fresh sequence. That routine forces its first plaintext to C[0]-sign*K[0], prohibiting a collision skip at the split, and drops the preceding observed ciphertext and the two preceding decoded plaintexts. A legal continuation can therefore be rejected even with the correct prefix and key. Minimal GP29 example, sign +1: last prefix C=5, next pad symbols 2,9, true next P=3. The attempted C=5 collides, consumes the 2, and emits C=12 using 9. The existing suffix initialization instead emits P=10 and advances one pad position too few. This counterexample does not assert that the original has a skip at this boundary. Correct continuous heldout validation would retain the frozen prefix skip count, last C and last two P, permit both legal first-step transitions, and use the same trigram context. Do not reoptimize prefix on the holdout. This issue applies to every reuse of twoStage, including the prior fixed/hash-seed tests.

2. **Control does not calibrate the actual expensive selector.** C++ ranks 399,848,400 signed states by a 28-rune head with at most four skips, retaining 800 entries. The aggregate's positive() instead chooses from 120 named generator models using six 130-rune prefixes, and its reported 1260/1260 accuracy comes from truth-selected key full-page Viterbi calls, not the frozen 130/80 paths. It never tests whether a hidden arbitrary state survives the 28-rune top-800 selection or whether the frozen split recovers the actual continuation. Report the result as no readable continuation among the 800 screened entries under the implemented decoder. No exclusion of discarded states follows. A future genuinely source-selected state can be evaluated with the corrected cheap decoder; no new all-state sweep is justified.

3. **Metadata and duplicate streams.** The preparation and C++ assertions correctly use seed^4 before first parity emission. The aggregate JSON mistakenly labels seed^2 = 10,896,601 as first_state; the actual value for 3301 is 993,811,212 (mod 1,595,277,641). The enumerated quadratic-residue domain is a conservative superset of first emitted seed^4 states: the fourth-power image has (630)(516)(308)=100,124,640 residues (including nonunits); p=1229 has gcd(4,p-1)=4. Also, top head entries 376926252 and 1303718958 have the same parity and same square 337189215 modulo N, hence identical infinite parity streams, not two distinct keys. The top-800 capacity is therefore not 800 independent streams. This is a scope/efficiency clarification, not evidence of a missed plaintext. Correct the aggregate metadata when convenient; retain raw historical output and avoid duplicate reruns.

Exact checks use integer arithmetic: N=1259*1031*1229; pow(3301,2,N)=10896601; pow(3301,4,N)=993811212; for the duplicate pair both x%2=0 and pow(x,2,N)=337189215. The reported controls still establish that the earlier fixed seed can be decoded with the chosen model. They do not establish an exhaustive state-search negative.
