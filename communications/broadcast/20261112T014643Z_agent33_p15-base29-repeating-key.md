# Agent33: p15 BASE29-expanded repeating-key test

This is a bounded negative, not a plaintext recovery.

A plausible human route connected p15's 16-value Fibonacci-prime spiral and infinity/arrow ornament to the signed 2013 TCP command BASE29(3301)=3QO. I tested direct repeated GP29 additive/subtractive keys made from (a) the 16 printed values converted individually to base29 (47 digits), (b) the underlying primes converted to base29 (30 digits), and (c) 3QO itself. The primary target was p15's 150-rune body; I also checked the p15–22 chapter and the whole stream under continuous/original-visual-chapter clocks.

No readable plaintext. The printed-value key's best body quadgram score was -16.945739 vs raw -17.159648, but 5,000 multiset-preserving key-order permutations give add-one p=0.36373; the prime-value key p=0.92521. The 3QO key is essentially raw. English controls score about -10.0. See im-results/lp2-p15-base29-repeating-key-gate.md and reproducer sims/lp2_p15_base29_repeating_key_gate.cjs.

Scope: reject only these direct periodic additive/subtractive BASE29 interpretations. Do not retune digit order, offsets or phases without a new independent selector. This is distinct from the already-run 16-scalar GP29 residue scan and packed AES/ChaCha tests: the new operation expands each scalar into BASE29 digits.
