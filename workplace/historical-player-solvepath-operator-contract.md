# Historical Cicada solve-path contract: what a contemporary player could actually do

**Agent 19, 12 November 2026; Director Issue #471.** This is a source-cited reconstruction of earlier Cicada solve paths, not a new LP2 decryption. The key question is whether an operation was selected by something a player could actually see *before* decoding, and whether the source also fixed its parameters. **No earlier-round source examined here selects a key, cipher, clock, or inverse for the 56 unknown LP2 pages.**

## What genuinely recurs across rounds

- **OutGuess extraction** recurs in 2012, 2014 and 2016. This is a repeated tool family, not a common password, carrier format, payload grammar or proof that every LP2 JPEG carries a message.
- **Semantic book selection followed by explicit coordinates** recurs in the 2012 Encyclopaedia Britannica / Agrippa stages and 2014 Gödel, Escher, Bach stage; the 2013 Liber AL clue is a related example. In the successful examples, the clue identifies a particular text and the puzzle supplies a concrete coordinate grammar. The 2016 “words are the map” sentence does not identify an external book or supply an equivalent coordinate grammar.
- **Gematria Primus / prime-weight arithmetic** recurs in different roles: poster labels and the 761.MP3 title in 2013, the within-artifact 1033 Wisdom square in 2014, and the p32 563×569 tree dimensions in 2016. This is a recurring numeric vocabulary, not one repeated cipher or a source-backed page-address rule.
- **OpenPGP** recurs as an authenticity and submission mechanism. A valid signature authenticates bytes relative to the preserved key; it does not itself decrypt the message or prove the key's real-world identity.
- **XOR** appears in the 2013 participant-built phone/ISO route and the 2014 three-share reconstruction; both have stage-specific source data. **Transposition** appears in two distinct 2014 stages with different widths/orders. These are family-level similarities, not a shared cross-round key schedule.
- **One-off or stage-local mechanisms:** ROT−4 on the 2012 appended URL; MIDI note/timing substitution; 2013 BASE29/DH/NEXT protocol, Shamir threshold recovery and ICMP prime-ID hypothesis; 2014 Vigenère small-image key, 7-/14-column transpositions and OpenPuff v4.00. No evidence shows these parameters were intended to recur unchanged in LP2.

The evidence does not prove a universal authorial grammar. It does show a recurring solve-path shape: a visible clue identifies an action/input; a source or solved page fixes parameters; the result has a recognizable output or next-stage check. ### Three falsifiable LP2 predictions

1. **p15:** p15 infinity + p33–p39 header + 2013 prime_echo motivate F17=1597→p1598=13477=29×464+21. Promotion requires an original source selecting a reversible consumer; #479 owns that gate.
2. **p32:** the 2016 signed clue selects page and 11×11 row-major orientation. A path claim must predeclare an original-source start, movement/direction and stopping rule; these are still missing.
3. **p55:** p56 asks for a separate deep-web page hashing to a 128-hex target; p57's instar parable supplies no address grammar. Decode all 76 p55 runes first; only explicit plaintext/source evidence may select URL, Tor address, page pointer or preimage format (#490 found no justified format).

**Method:** require a visible cue, independently fixed parameters, a predeclared output contract and a held-out/original-cipher test. This is a ranking heuristic, not a claim that every LP2 page must follow earlier stages.

## LP2 transfer decision: one live structural candidate, no cipher consumer

The historical evidence still supplies **no source-selected LP2 cipher/key consumer or inverse**. Earlier solve paths begin with a visible, discriminating instruction: Caesar wording, a named book plus coordinates, “follow the chorus,” a signed protocol command, an explicit steganographic carrier, or a signed tree-image mapping. No earlier source tells a player to apply the 2012 Caesar shift, MIDI substitution, 2013 DH/SSSS/prime counter, 2014 Vigenère/OpenPuff/transposition key, or a 49-word grid to the 56 unknown LP2 pages.

**2014 columnar transfer gate (Director #638):** The authentic Stage05 period-7 operation is the row-wise permutation **[0,6,2,5,1,4,3]** (one-based key 1736254) on eight complete 7-character rows; its inverse is **[0,4,2,6,5,3,1]**, with no padding. A separate participant implementation uses a 5×14 row permutation with key **[2,8,9,1,12,13,11,4,5,7,3,0,6,10]**. These are distinct stage-local operations. The identity-changing Stage04 Vigenère (“Let the text guide you”, key phrase WELCOME PILGRIM TO THE) is another separate stage, not a source-specified composition with Stage05. LP2's authenticated p32 diagram is 11×11 row-major; p50's 32×8 table is not a columnar instruction, and p10's red text is not a 7-column grid. The exact Stage05 order has already been tested on the p33 91-rune unit and p54–55 308-rune chapter without plaintext (#485). No LP2-specific width/order selector or second identity-changing opcode exists. The exploratory `2014_7column_textguide_original_sft.cjs` uses a forward-GP29 Vigenère toy rather than Stage04's reversed-rune lookup, so its composite negative is **not** source-exact; use [the standalone Stage04 reproducer](../sims/stage04_small_image_vigenere_repro.cjs) for that operation. See [the Stage05 source chain](../im-results/2014-fourth-onion-response-chain.md), [the 14-column source report](../im-results/cicada2013-twitter-2014-column14-original-inverse.md), and [the exact LP2 seven-column gate](../im-results/lp2-p33-stage05-exact-seven-column-gate.md).

2. **LP2 p2 literal glyph reflection (rejected exact interpretation, #468).** The finite test reflected each original p2 glyph crop horizontally and evaluated all 201 runes. Registration was calibrated on 161 p2 training glyphs, 40 held-out p2 glyphs and 65 solved p57 controls; the controls passed 159/161 training leave-one-out, 40/40 held-out p2 and 65/65 p57. The p33–p39 cuneiform header is read as (17,13)₆₀=1033 and (55,1)₆₀=3301; its digits 13,55,1 occur in the p15 Fibonacci input set, while 17 is a plausible next term ordinal. ## Result

**Defensible finding:** earlier Cicada rounds establish recurring human-puzzle habits and several recognizable operation families, but provide **no source-selected cipher consumer, key, or plaintext route for unknown LP2 pages**. The 2013 prime_echo/p33-header/p15-infinity alignment supplies a plausible, exactly testable one-term arithmetic continuation (F17 → p1598=13477), but no source tells a solver how to consume it as a reversible operation. The p2 literal glyph-reflection interpretation has now been tested and rejected as noninjective/nonlinguistic. The 2016 signed tree message selects p32's layout but not its decoding rule. No unknown LP2 plaintext, key, or final destination was recovered by this audit.

## Prime values and prime ordinals: source-based address decision (Issue #291, 11 November 2026)

**Decision:** Original Cicada materials show deliberate prime *values*, index-ordered prime *streams*, prime-weight word arithmetic, and (inside LP2) a Fibonacci-indexed prime *generation* rule. They do **not** provide a player-facing precedent that says to take the ordinal of a prime-valued filename and address that LP2 physical page, or to take a base-29 rune-word numeral and treat its value as a Fibonacci index. These address routes remain unselected hypotheses, pending a new explicit source clue or the independently assigned original-image comparison in #287. This finding concerns **the instruction grammar**, not the mathematical correctness of any rank correspondence.
