# LP2 source clocks and 13/31 fragment traversal

**Classification:** original-source layout and traversal evidence. No unknown plaintext or cipher key recovered.

## Current section-boundary evidence

The canonical raw transcription has eight section-ending dollar markers across the 55 rune-bearing unresolved pages. Its sixth marker falls at the end of physical p32, global rune offset **7,960**. Physical p33 (scan 50.jpg) has two separate red headings: 10 runes beginning at local offset **0**, and 8 runes beginning at local offset **91**. The latter follows three large black circles and a red 13-dot mark.

A direct original-pixel comparison now confirms the punctuation motif rather than relying on visual resemblance alone. Connected-component extraction from the original JPEGs finds exactly 13 red dots at p32 end, p33 heading 1, p33 heading 2, and solved p57's PARABLE title. After translating each 13-dot cluster to its minimum center, every coordinate is within **1.0 pixel** of the p57 control (RMSE: p32 end 0.51 px; p33 heading 1 0.29 px; p33 heading 2 0.28 px). Source image SHA-256 values: p32/49.jpg `c0f778187a8e9ddbfc667a65929ac567430a2e38278a2022377984ac084e5f72`; p33/50.jpg `13ff4c8d8ab934032a001b8c1fda16433aa44a38dd7272b3ff0dd7ac8300c62d`; p57/74.jpg `1dabc100ddbf1fc49715219636984eb6ad96a2bd8035e196c12e66db77a1077b`. Reproducer: [red-13-dot boundary SFT](../sims/p32_p33_red13dot_boundary_sft.py).

A source-native typographic control sharpens the first boundary: solved p57's red `PARABLE` title is a 7-rune red span at local offset **0**, followed by red 13-dot punctuation at offset **7**. P33's first heading has the same layout signature: 10 red runes at offset **0**, followed by red 13-dot punctuation at offset **10**. P33's second heading is 8 red runes at offset **91**, with red punctuation at offsets **91** and **99**; the repeated 1033/3301 cuneiform artwork begins immediately after it and continues across p34–p39. A second scaled copy of the three-large-black-dot graphic precedes p39's **three-rune red heading at local rune offset 119**, with red punctuation at offsets 119 and 122. Original-image component geometry verifies the p33/p39 triad correspondence ([SFT](../sims/lp2_three_dot_delimiters_sft.py), [full source gate](../im-results/lp2-three-dot-delimiter-source-gate.md)). P32 itself ends with red 13-dot punctuation. The strongest human-facing reading is therefore that **both p33 red headings begin titled units**, making the 91-rune/23-word interlude a standalone unit rather than an unmarked continuation of the prior p32 body.

An independently maintained, upstream-related transcription inserts a section separator before the second p33 red heading and not after p32. That is a transcription-convention witness, not independent cryptographic evidence. The canonical transcription remains unchanged to preserve comparability. The visual analogy strengthens the two-heading segmentation as a reading of the artifact; it still does **not** prove that a cryptographic key clock resets at either heading.

A further **17-based body-word relation** is visible, but the rune-count arithmetic must keep punctuation separate. P33 has **214 runes / 53 words**. Its two red headings occupy 10+8 runes; the two 13-dot marks are punctuation and are **not** part of the rune count. Removing only the headings leaves **196 body runes = 14²**. The two body units are **81 = 9²** and **115** runes. An original-JPEG scan of physical p33 (`resources/liber-primus/50.jpg`, SHA-256 `13ff4c8d8ab934032a001b8c1fda16433aa44a38dd7272b3ff0dd7ac8300c62d`) resolves the geometry. The printed lines contain **[18,16,18,23,16]** runes in the first five-line unit and **[19,18,18,22,22,24]** in the second six-line unit, including the heading runes. Deleting only the first-line red headings of length 10 and 8 gives exact body-line lengths **[8,16,18,23,16]** and **[11,18,18,22,22,24]**. An independent pixel-row projection of the original scan identifies eleven dark text-line centers at y=[698,900,1096,1279,1472,1952,2148,2320,2509,2706,2894], with the black-dot separator between units. Thus **196=14²** and first-unit **81=9²** are only count factorizations: neither square is printed in a source-visible 14-row or 9-row grid; both require reflow of variable-length source lines. No unique square traversal or key schedule follows. [Original-source SFT](../sims/p33_original_square_geometry_sft.py) checks the canonical line counts and independently counts the original JPEG's eleven rows. Since 196=4×49 as well, four 7×7 blocks are another count-based candidate, and p57 has a 7×7 square; however the p33 red boundary splits the body after81 runes (not a multiple of49), so the visible sectioning does not select four consecutive blocks or an operation. Removing the two heading words leaves **51 p33 body words = 3×17**; physical p55 has **17 page-local segments** (one incoming carry plus 16 complete word starts). This compares complete p33 words with p55 page-local segments, not like with like; it is a count relation, not a 10×17 rune matrix. The earlier 170=10×17 calculation was erroneous because it subtracted the two 13-dot punctuation marks from the rune total.

A simple sequential grouping of the 51 p33 body words into 17 triplets does **not** reproduce p55's 17 segment lengths: p55 lengths are [1,4,2,2,6,6,2,13,6,4,2,7,7,2,6,3,3], where the initial 1 is the incoming carry fragment, while the corresponding p33 triplet rune totals are [19,12,10,13,19,7,7,13,14,8,9,12,16,19,8,14,9]. Thus the 17-segment-count relation has no direct triplet-to-p55-segment-length alignment. No row/column order or reversible operation is selected.

## Independent 2014 trailing-9133 coordinate hypothesis (provisional)

The preserved January 2014 OutGuess seven-column message has a particularly specific previously unexplained ending. Its **historically fixed** inverse (`1736254`) yields `TOBELIEVETRUTHISTODESTROYPOSSIBILITYQ4UTGDI2N4M4UIM59133`; the authentic fifth-onion address ends in `...M5`, leaving four characters **9133**. The [contemporary Unfiction reconstruction](https://www.forums.unfiction.com/forums/viewtopic.php%40p%3D1028647.html) reports these four characters separately, before the May 2014 LP2 image release. No LP2 plaintext was used to reconstruct them.

One potential *later* interpretation is the coordinate **91 runes preceding a red heading on physical page 33**. That is exactly the original p33 **local offset 91**, the eight-rune second red title immediately after a three-black-dot separator and at a red 13-dot boundary; it begins the source-repeated 1033/3301 cuneiform illustration. Among **all seven** internal red-span starts on unknown LP2 pages, this is the sole exact `[decimal offset][two-digit page]` rendition of `9133`. The alternative conventional split *page 9, offset 133* has no red span or large punctuation at that coordinate. The [exact fixed-source SFT](../sims/2014_9133_p33_internal_heading_source_sft.cjs) independently reconstructs the old ciphertext, enumerates the seven new-source matches, asserts the genuine p33 punctuation boundary and checks printed word lengths.

**Strong competing explanation and decision:** the original seven-column plaintext contains a 36-character phrase plus a 16-character onion, leaving **exactly four places** in its 56-cell rectangle; `9133` may simply be four padding characters. The coordinate interpretation chooses offset-before-page ordering after seeing LP2, and its match is postselected from many possible book-number interpretations. Crucially the p33 red title's two canonical words have lengths **3,5**, whereas the historical phrase starts **TO BELIEVE** with lengths **2,7**; there is no direct word-aligned crib. The [historical seven-position original-cipher inverse](../im-results/2014-outguess-period7-lp2-p8to14-inverse-gate.md) already fails to recover language from the larger p33:91→p39 illustrated candidate. A more direct clue-constrained test uses the additional fact that **9133 is prime**: start the exactly solved p56 running φ(prime) cipher at prime **9133** on the original **1,468-rune** illustrated passage, advancing one successive prime per rune. With no inferred F-interrupters, the two signs give GP29 IoCs **0.034417493671** (subtract φ) and **0.034337625769** (add φ), against the original **0.034312550962**. The full 1,589-rune through-p39-end variant likewise remains flat at **0.034452858363** and **0.034407680004**. Neither reveals plaintext. This negative applies to that literal historical-seed transfer alone; it does not imply that 9133 cannot select a different operation. A second fixed test combines the exact historical **seven-column inverse** with the same phi-prime9133 stream. The original cuneiform body runs from the first to the second three-black-dot separator and contains **1,468** runes; count each *source-visible separator* as one distinguished symbolic sentinel, obtaining **1,470=210×7** ordered cells without manufacturing rune padding. The historical inverse moves the end sentinel to position **1,464** (zero-based) while leaving the first at 0; stripping only these two symbols gives 45 adjacent identical runes versus the original 11. For the four genuine order/sign options (historical inverse followed by φ9133 ±, or φ9133 ± followed by historical inverse), exact GP29 coincidence indices are **0.0344165650**, **0.0344648572**, **0.0344174937**, and **0.0343376258**, respectively. None reveals readable language or elevates coincidence beyond uniform GP29; the two-stage idea is retired for these original inputs and fixed operations ([reproducer](../sims/2014_9133_p33_internal_heading_source_sft.cjs)). No freedom to change the historical permutation or sentinel positions is justified by this result. Thus `9133` is an **independent historical possible address**, not an authenticated LP2 clock/key or recovered plaintext. Preserve the *source-visible* nine-body segmentation as primary; use the 2014 token only as a prospective selector if another clue supplies an exact operation that predicts held-out original text. Do not search more arbitrary numeric splits or alternate transposition keys on this resemblance alone.

## Seven-page cuneiform decoder decision (Issue #78)

**Original-image indexing and page variation.** The repository's original-image SFT maps physical page (p) to archive image (p+17): p32 is `49.jpg`, and p33 is `50.jpg`. The original p32 image has no red title or local-91 heading. Original p33 has two red headings at local offsets 0 (10 runes) and 91 (8 runes), red 13-dot punctuation at offsets 10 and 91/99, three black separator dots, and the cuneiform artwork below the second heading. The 91-rune interlude is therefore on p33; the illustrated interval starts at p33 local91. Do not transfer this boundary to p32.

The p33 cuneiform template crop is `[x=1016..1405, y=3025..3450]`; the repeated p34–p39 header crop is `[x=1016..1405, y=131..556]`. All six p34–p39 RGB crops are byte-identical (SHA-256 `21d5cb4cb00e0a0d7d5b8adbbe14e497128951623ea4e98e10213f5c7288938f`). The template matches only pp33–39 (p33 NCC 0.98994; p34–39 each 0.99375); the strongest page outside the seven-page run is 0.30788. Thus the cuneiform pair is one repeated header/section mark, not seven independent page-specific values. The visible varying marks are the red section numerals 1–5 on pp36–38 and two 10-dot delimiters (p36 local99; p38 local186), which segment five numbered passages but do not prescribe a seven-page permutation or lane assignment.

The two source-visible three-dot graphical dividers occur immediately before the p33 local91 and p39 local119 red headings. They bracket the physical page fragments **[123, 261, 271, 238, 228, 228, 119] = 1,468 runes**. The broader previously modeled p33-local91..p39:end interval has **[123, 261, 271, 238, 228, 228, 240] = 1,589 = 7×227 runes**, but contains **121 runes after the second p39 divider**. Therefore the attractive 7×227 factorization is an aggregate of two differently marked units, rather than the original-delimited cuneiform body. Neither the 1,468-rune source-bracketed body nor the longer historical modeling window prints seven equal rows or identifies a cipher state. A further candidate p39 split exists for any proposed section-local clock; the red-heading analogy does not by itself prove a cipher reset. The direct negative tests on the larger composite keep their valid finite scope.

**Historical operation audit.** The two cuneiform values read in sexagesimal as (17,13)=1033 and (55,1)=3301. The pinned 2013/2014 chain documents prime-stream pauses on both values, `BASE29 1033 → 16I`, the `NUMBER` prime-reversal result `3301 → +`, `DH 3301`, and the 2014 OpenPuff password `33011033` for the separate `magicsquares.txt` carrier. The archived DH example's base and server secret are session/participant-random; 3301 is a modulus, not a deterministic shared key. The stable `16I` and `+` outputs do not specify a reversible operation on the 1,468 source-bracketed or broader 1,589 composite LP2 runes. LP2 OpenPuff extraction remains gated by the unexecuted positive control in Issue #39; no LP2 extraction was attempted.

**Seven-state selector versus readout.** The p32 order-7 M7 map is a source-supported structural selector: the 22/25/7 page counts and cuneiform residues independently reconstruct the same projectivity. Its direct p33–p39 period-7 substitution/additive, 7×7 Hill, root-7 Fourier and 210-state projective/tensor readouts remain nonlinguistic (see `results/README.md`, `im-results/tree-prime-canvas-partition.md`, `im-results/p32-cuneiform-redtriad-mobius-bridge.md`, and the linked original-input SFTs). A proposed seven-step word-sum return uses a 26-contiguous-run parse; under p32's 22 page-local segments (one incoming carry plus 21 complete word starts), the path is 13→22→24, with 24 outside the 22-segment address space. If the operation requires complete word starts, p32 has only 21. The attractive 26-run closure is therefore segmentation-dependent, not a source-selected seven-symbol key.

**Decision.** The cuneiform supplies a numeric cross-round reference and a seven-page grouping, but not a source-fixed inversion. The exact missing inputs are (i) a page/lane permutation, (ii) a start/reset/clock, and (iii) a reversible symbol readout plus authentic key/state. No blind original 1,468- or 1,589-rune decode is justified until an independent source fixes these. This completes the scope-bounded Issue #78 candidate; do not broaden the prime or cipher sweep.

## Retrospective 13/31 traversal candidate over all 32 fragments

The five-slot border ornament's documented binary reading gives **13** and **31**. Across all 58 physical pages, the 31 thirteen-dot marks delimit **32 full-text fragments** (13,136 runes total). The first 28 fragments contain the 12,956 unresolved runes; the final four contain the solved p56/p57 tail. The two empty intervals at global boundaries 1,860 and 3,603 are retained.

The full-period recurrence

\[
x_{n+1}=13x_n+31\pmod{32}
\]

visits all 32 fragment indices exactly once for any seed. The earlier seed-zero presentation used \(x_0=0\), but **zero is not selected by the ornament or an authenticated instruction**. With seed zero, the four solved-tail fragments restrict to **31, 28, 29, 30** in both forward and inverse position-permutation orders. That exact dual order is seed-dependent: enumerating all 32 seeds produces all four cyclic forward tail rotations, and using the independently source-derived FOLD mismatch value **18** as the seed gives forward **28,29,30,31** and inverse-position **30,29,28,31**. Thus the solved tail confirms only that this full cycle can induce cyclic tail orders; it does not select the seed, orientation, or an LCG operation.

The earlier finite [orientation SFT](../sims/lp2_lcg32_tail_orientation_sft.cjs) fixed seed zero; within that conditional family exactly one of 128 full-period affine clocks, the 13/31 pair, yields the exact dual-map solved-tail order, while four yield any matching cyclic tail order. The all-seed audit shows that this statistic does not establish a source-fixed orientation because the seed itself was never supplied. Of five rotations of the ornament's `01101` mask, only multipliers 13 and 21 are full-period with increment31; 21 fails the solved-tail cyclic check. These conditional counts do not identify a cipher, authentic key, or unknown plaintext.

Directly concatenating the 28 unknown fragments in LCG order does not improve GP trigram score: chronological −6.195857, forward −6.198043, inverse −6.195210; the inverse is ordinary against a 1,000-random-order null (upper-tail p=0.235). The 225 red-only runes also do not become readable under the same permutation. Thus the LCG is **not a direct plaintext transposition**. Its likely remaining role is a fragment index, clock, or row assignment; forward versus inverse orientation is unresolved.

**The proposed fixed row-pairing test is now complete.** The source-fixed 13/31 LCG traverses all 32 rows, 8 columns and, separately, all 256 cell addresses. Forty complete original-cipher inverses use the frozen row/column orders: 32 GP29/GP28 per-fragment reset variants and eight global 256-byte table-pad variants. All re-encrypt all 12,956 original runes and recover a native solved-page-derived plant, but their decoded outputs remain nonlinguistic. GP29 IoC stays **0.0344419952–0.0345017289**, compared with original **0.0344783979**; GP28 transparent outputs remain nonlinguistic. These direct table-pad and register uses are bounded negatives ([reproducer](../sims/lp2_lcg13_31_grid_original_gate.cjs); [consolidated report](../im-results/lp2_lcg32_fulltext_fragment_order.md)).
