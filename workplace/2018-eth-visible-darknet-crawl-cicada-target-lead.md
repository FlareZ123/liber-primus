# ETH Zurich 2018 visible-darknet crawl: bounded Cicada continuation lookup

**15 November 2026, Agent 8. Classification: candidate historical URL-index custodian, not an LP2 decryption or p56 preimage.** A separate crawl described in an independently published 2018 primary research paper could contain old Tor v2 URLs or readable text that points to the web page requested by the solved Liber Primus p56. Its collection period follows the original May 2014 release, so the site would need to have survived into the later crawl. Neither that survival nor any Cicada host hit is established.

## Original source and what its authors preserved

Georgia Avarikioti, Roman Brunner, Aggelos Kiayias, Roger Wattenhofer and Dionysis Zindros, [*Structure and Content of the Visible Darknet*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01348) (November 2018 preprint), reports a newly implemented Tor crawler seeded with **20,000 previously collected onion addresses**. The crawler found **34,714 distinct hidden-service addresses**, of which **10,957** responded in its first run. It tracked **7,355,773 discovered paths**, **67,296,302 hyperlinks** and **1,561,590 downloaded paths** over a stated **one-week** collection. The original preprint does not provide the exact calendar dates of that week in its methods, so the collection date must not be invented from the 2018 publication date.

Its database schema is promising for *address discovery*: the authors explicitly describe storing hidden-service hostnames, paths, link relationships, HTTP status, MIME type, crawl timestamps and downloaded textual content. The crawler traversed outgoing onion URLs, offering a way to find another service linked from the signed May 2014 Stage11 distributor `ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion`, **if that host or another relevant link was actually encountered**.

**Byte-fidelity constraint:** Their section `Storage and Processing of Illegal Content` describes MIME allowlisting, discarding nontext responses and **replacing embedded nontextual data URIs with placeholders** before durable storage. Thus their content table is *not automatically a raw 2014–18 HTTP entity-body archive*. Raw response headers, on-wire transfer encoding and immutable original bytes are not documented as preserved. The crawl may help discover old addresses or literal text; applying the printed p56 512-bit digest directly to its sanitized strings would be unjustified.

The preprint calls the crawler MIT-licensed and open source, but its own footnote 5 explicitly redacts the Git repository URL pending a proceedings version. The conclusion says making the collected *dataset* public would be future work. No public original content/URL dataset or stable repository link was found in the scoped published-paper and GitHub-name/description searches made for this note. This is a bounded discovery failure, **not** proof that a current private archive no longer exists.

## Source-constrained next decision

The independently established p56 challenge is a **128-hex digest of an unidentified deep-web page**; its hash algorithm, original bytes and target hostname are not authenticated. The [current source-integrated research frontier](integrated-original-inversion-challenge.md) and [archive preimage audit](../im-results/archive-hash-preimage.md) document why decryption requires a historically selected, byte-exact candidate. This ETH cohort is temporally and technically distinct from the better documented 2015 Moore–Rid *sanitized text* harvest and the May–June 2016 EURECOM/Deusto PhantomJS crawl.

Only if a custodian explicitly permits a **non-content, bounded index query**, ask whether the original hostname `ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion`, the exact 128-hex p56 target, or text referring to Cicada/Liber Primus occurs in their stored hostnames, URLs or searchable text, and request only a historical URL, capture date and page metadata for any hit. Do not request or distribute the full mixed-purpose dark-web corpus. If an authentic URL arises, seek its *independently byte-preserved original HTTP body* before any digest claim. If neither an index nor a host hit survives, retire this specific cohort. No researcher has been contacted, and the original data has not been acquired.

**Priority judgment:** weak-to-moderate potential and **currently blocked by source access and maintainer contact approval**. The 2016 crawl with specifically documented raw-response retention is the stronger existing candidate. This note adds a bounded independent 2018 retention cohort, without inventing cipher operations or revisiting exhausted hash variants.
