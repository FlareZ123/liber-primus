# Blind manuscript solve-path test

You are given six consecutive original manuscript-page scans in their original resolution. Their page numbers and the current research hypothesis are intentionally withheld.

## Task

Using only what a historically situated solver could have known by **30 April 2014**, determine whether these pages themselves select a concrete, reproducible operation that turns any visible mathematical, numerical, or cryptographic clue into an executable route.

If an operation is selected, report:
1. the exact visual/textual clue that selects it;
2. the exact input material and how it is obtained;
3. the operation, parameters, ordering, and any key/seed;
4. the exact predicted output or next object;
5. one observation on the original scans or source material that would falsify the route.

If the pages do not select an executable route, state the smallest missing input or source instruction needed. Do not invent a key, add arbitrary cipher layers, or treat an attractive numerical coincidence as a solution.

## Constraints

- Do not use material published after 30 April 2014.
- Do not use later Liber Primus plaintext, later solved-page interpretations, or current community hypotheses.
- Do not assume a particular cipher family. Consider simple alternatives before layered constructions.
- Preserve the distinction between a directly visible fact, a mathematical derivation, and an inference about author intent.
- Work from the images themselves; do not rely on a modern transcription as the primary evidence.

A useful response is a short, reproducible solve path or a clear statement that the pages do not determine one. Negative results are welcome when they identify the exact missing information.
