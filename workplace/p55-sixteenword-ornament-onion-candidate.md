# Conditional p55 16-word / 5-bit-ornament Tor-v2 locator candidate

**Research classification:** an exact, reproducible **unverified locator hypothesis**, not a decipherment, recovered unknown-page text, authenticated key, or validated onion service. The proposed host is **`6sws36unkmvdy4b7.onion`**. It must not be described as Cicada's destination without an independently attributable historical copy or another source-selected validation.

## Source-first causal route

The original physical p55 (2014 scan `72.jpg`) contains one leading rune finishing a p54 word, followed by **16 complete printed words**. The immediately following solved p56 asks the reader to find a page in the “deep web”; Tor v2 services used **16-character Base32 hostnames** in 2014 ([Tor's historical rendezvous specification](https://torproject.gitlab.io/torspec/rend-spec-v2.html#_alice_receives_a_zonion_address)). The same floral frame appears on all original pp40–55. An earlier independent original-image audit read the **five-slot left and right ornaments as 01101₂=13 and 11111₂=31** under a single declared topmost/clockwise black-bit convention ([source-visible report](original-visual-clue-operation-map.md); [red-event report](red-256-event-grid-correspondence.md)). Five bits suggest Base32, though their orientation and application as a codebook are **not** explicitly given by Cicada.

Earlier solved Cicada material provides a genuine operation for assigning numbers to GP words: **add the first-29-prime weights of their runes** (2014 LP1 Wisdom/magic-square stage). A concrete possible endgame readout is therefore: omit the single carry fragment; retain the 16 complete printed words in order; sum each word's GP prime values; reduce modulo 32; translate digits with the historical Tor Base32 alphabet `abcdefghijklmnopqrstuvwxyz234567`. This is **one frozen original-input hypothesis**. The reduction modulo32 and treatment of words as an onion ID are interpretive additions, not verified authored instructions. One simpler competing interpretation is that 13 and 31 already count the manuscript's **13 dots per stencil and 31 stencil occurrences**; those original-source facts require no Tor connection.

## Exact original result and the two ornamental anchors

Original source-canonical GP-prime sums, word1 through word16:

```text
222 114 118 306 283  30 596 365
138 108 341 291  88 284 129 159
```

Reduced modulo32, in two proposed 8-symbol groups:

```text
30 18 22 18 27 30 20 13 | 10 12 21  3 24 28  1 31
 6  s  w  s  3  6  u  n |  k  m  v  d  y  4  b  7
```

The **eighth** word, six-rune `MSDIAPF`, has GP-prime sum **365 ≡ 13 (mod32)**. The **sixteenth** word, three-rune `BEI`, has GP-prime sum **159 ≡ 31 (mod32)**. These match the independently audited 13/31 left/right border bit motifs in that order if one partitions the 16 words into two consecutive groups of eight. The printed p55 lines begin **7/2/5/2** complete words, so **this 8+8 division is not a printed line break**. The separate 32×8 numerical table elsewhere in the manuscript motivates an eight-column convention, but does not explicitly authorize applying it here.

The two distinct source-defined window universes must be kept separate. Across **2,103** complete, 16-fragment windows **confined to one runic physical page**, the fixed constraints “word8 sum modulo32 is 13” and “word16 sum modulo32 is 31” jointly occur **once**, at p55's first complete word (local fragment1). Across the **15** rune-bearing floral pages p40–55, the same test on each page's **last** 16 local fragments also matches only p55.

A stronger whole-book control joins rune words carried across original physical page boundaries using the established [continuous source-word parser](../sims/lp2_word_pages.cjs). It yields exactly **2,896 complete words**, **32** words crossing printed page boundaries, and **2,881** consecutive 16-word windows. The *same two frozen 13/31 constraints* have **three** matches: global zero-based complete-word index **1750** spanning physical **p31–32** (`3nmvm46nsg6e5lf7`); index **2730** spanning **p51–52** (`6bjupjsnnheadah7`); and index **2880** contained in physical **p55** (`6sws36unkmvdy4b7`). Only the p55 match is entirely inside one physical page. These other Base32-shaped strings are outputs of a mathematical readout, **not** verified Tor services. The source-known 2014 LP2 distribution host `ky2khlqdf7qdznac` occurs **zero times** among all 2,881 literal outputs. Thus **p55 is not unique in an unconstrained manuscript-wide window search**.

Under an idealized independent uniform two-digit null, one expects approximately **2.05** matches among 2,103 page-contained windows and **2.81** among 2,881 continuous-stream windows. The observed counts **one** and **three** are unexceptional. The special choice of last page and 8+8 positions is partly retrospective, so a nominal 1/1024 digit-pair rarity does not authenticate this onion candidate. ## Cross-round Stage10→Stage11 checksum (13 Nov 2026)

The original 2014 Stage10 square source has row sums **3301, 1033, 1033**. The archived original Stage11 onion7 HTML, present in the 2014-05-11 source commit [`6c3421a4d245596e403c88e037509d162a2afd17`](https://github.com/micheloosterhof/cicada-2014/commit/6c3421a4d245596e403c88e037509d162a2afd17), contains `<title>133</title>` and `<div id="331">`. Deleting the single zero from the two distinct Stage10 sums gives **331** and **133**; Stage11 stores these values in reverse order (title **133**, div ID **331**). Taking the first and last decimal digits of those Stage11 values gives **13** and **31**.

That ordered pair independently matches (a) the original p55 floral-frame values **13/31**, and (b) the eighth and sixteenth base32 digit indices of the frozen p55 candidate **`6sws36unkmvdy4b7`**. The Stage11 numeric title/ID pair is unique among the archived Stage index HTML files inspected. The same prime-sum/mod32 recipe applied to the competing 16-word 2016 signed sentence gives **`ptpits7uimxshpir`**, whose eighth/sixteenth base32 indices are **20/17**, not 13/31. This makes the new check discriminating between the p55 block and that obvious alternative 16-word block.

This is a new cross-round **source-linked checksum hypothesis**, not a decryption or proof that the candidate ever hosted a service. It is distinct from the previously tested raw 133/331 modulo-58 page-address and GP29 pair-mixer interpretations (Director #135; `im-results/onion7-133331-gp29-pair-inverse.md`), which remain negative. Neither zero deletion nor outer-digit extraction is explicitly prescribed by the sources. The candidate still requires an attributable historical service descriptor or page bytes; page bytes must independently match the p56 512-bit digest before any destination claim.

## What remains missing and current archive evidence

Any 16 Base32 characters form a syntactically permissible historical Tor-v2 service ID. The construction is therefore **not self-validating** and does not demonstrate the site's existence or possession of its corresponding RSA private key. Under the [historical Tor-v2 address construction](https://torproject.gitlab.io/torspec/rend-spec-v2.html), the hostname is the Base32 encoding of the first 80 bits of a SHA-1 hash of the service's RSA identity public key. A genuine source-intended hostname would therefore require its creator to possess a corresponding service key, for example by generating a service first and tailoring the printed word sums to its hostname. Merely computing a valid-looking string from this page does not provide such a key. The same inputs admit alternative reductions/word readings, and the border bit-order convention itself has no original textual endorsement. The historical v2 service protocol is retired, and an unindexed old service may be unrecoverable. A stronger word-indexed 32×8 table-consumer was tested independently: using the word's GP-prime sum modulo32 to select a table row and rune offset modulo8 as a column yields nearuniform direct outputs; the plaintext-self-indexed variant leaves **1,020–1,066 original words** without any consistent row, depending on sign. [Original-input and native-positive gate](../im-results/lp2-wordprime-grid32x8-selfindexed-gate.md). This excludes that direct cipher architecture without invalidating the Base32 readout itself.

A strong exact-archive negative now supersedes the earlier web/GitHub-index-only check. Agent29's successful manual run searched five byte-pinned public participant IRC logs from 2013–2016: **54,969,358 bytes and 707,345 lines**, with **zero** full-host, bare-host or ten-character-prefix matches for 6sws36unkmvdy4b7.onion. The historically known LP2 distribution host ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion appears **11** times in the same corpus, and the matcher has native insertion controls. See [the consolidated report](../im-results/lp2-p55-16word-onion-five-pinned-irc-search.md) and [successful pinned-source run 43349259614](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/actions/runs/43349259614).
