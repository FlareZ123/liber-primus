# KAIST 2017 Tor-crawl custody route for the LP2 AN END hash target

**Current decision (13 November 2026; Agent 8 discovery, Agent 10 issue #787 follow-through):** a large, independently documented 2017 onion-web crawl exists in the historical research literature. A **named potential custody chain** now connects its named researcher to S2W's contemporary retained-data platform. The public paper, lab research site, repository index, and dataset-deposit searches **do not expose an original URL/HTTP-body manifest or a downloadable raw archive**. No Cicada target-page bytes, digest preimage, plaintext, key or final destination have been recovered. This is a **custodian-access prerequisite**, not a negative test on the underlying private crawl.

## Original collection and why it could matter

The WWW'19 paper [Doppelgängers on the Dark Web](https://wsp-lab.github.io/papers/yoon-www19.pdf) (Yoon, Kim, Kim, Shin and Son; DOI [10.1145/3308558.3313551](https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313551)) describes **21,537,119 textual webpages** across **28,928 onion domains** and **13,326 distinct second-level onion services** collected **January–July 2017**. Section 3 says 16 Scrapy/Splash crawler instances expanded from 10,000 Ahmia/FreshOnion seeds by following onion links; section 4.1 explicitly says they scraped and stored **HTTP(S) responses with textual content** and separately extracted visible homepage texts/titles for grouping. That supports the possibility that response text, rather than only aggregate counts, once existed. It does **not** prove that historical raw, byte-identical response entities, their per-page boundaries, headers, URLs or dates still survive.

The related [MFScope NDSS'19 study](https://wsp-lab.github.io/papers/lee-mfscope-ndss19.pdf) by Lee, Yoon et al. documents **27,665,572 webpages** from **36,864 onion domains** collected **January 2017–March 2018**. Its section IV-A says it extracted text and stored it in a distributed database. The time periods, seed engines, institutions and authors overlap. The corpus relationship is unknown: **never add their page counts as independent acquisition coverage**.

The authentic solved physical LP2 p56 refers to an otherwise unidentified deep-web page hashing to the 128-hex target recorded in [the existing preimage analysis](../im-results/archive-hash-preimage.md). The familiar **ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion** is the manuscript's distribution host; it is not an identified p56 target host. An original 2014 page might have remained accessible in 2017, but this is an unverified temporal premise. A 64-byte target hash does not independently prove SHA-512; that family is a clearly labeled candidate.

## Public archive availability gate, executed for Director #787

The following public entry points were examined, with **no URL-level 2017 crawl manifest and no original-byte HTTP response files recovered**:

- The [2019 author's full paper](https://wsp-lab.github.io/papers/yoon-www19.pdf), [ACM DOI/proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3308558.3313551), and [KAIST research publication record](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/doppelg%C3%A4ngers-on-the-dark-web-a-large-scale-assessment-on-phishin/): method and aggregate counts, no raw-corpus download endpoint or byte-fidelity statement.
- The [WSP Lab public research-site GitHub configuration](https://github.com/WSP-LAB/wsp-lab.github.io/blob/main/_config.yml): links the publication, PDF and a KAIST news summary, with no companion raw-response archive beside the paper entry. A bounded scan of the laboratory's public GitHub organization and searches for the title, exact 21,537,119 count, MFScope, and a paper-named dataset disclosed no matching downloadable original-body collection.
- Focused public search for those study names plus dataset, supplement, GitHub, Zenodo and Dataverse produced no verifiable original HTTP-body release. This is a **discoverability result**, never proof that a nonpublic archive was deleted. The later [2022 CoDA paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.412/) explains that scarcity of **publicly available** dark-web datasets motivated the independent release of 10,000 documents; CoDA is not the 2017 original crawl.

**Byte-fidelity consequence.** The paper's text/title clustering material and MFScope's extracted text are insufficient for exact hashing unless the underlying original **per-response entity bytes** were kept. Scrapy/Splash access and textual HTTP(S) response storage do not by themselves guarantee that surviving files preserve original bytes (rather than decoded/normalized/rendered text). There is no public candidate body on which to execute a meaningful fixed-target equality test, so the authorized public **SHA-512 comparison count is zero**.

## New concrete archival custodian route (not a confirmed 2017 raw-data transfer)

The continuity lead is grounded in contemporary author affiliations and the vendor's explicit archival capabilities:

1. The **original [2019 WWW paper's author block](https://wsp-lab.github.io/papers/yoon-www19.pdf)** lists **Changhoon Yoon as S2W LAB Inc.** and **Seungwon Shin as KAIST / S2W LAB Inc.**; the independent [KAIST institutional publication record](https://pure.kaist.ac.kr/en/publications/doppelg%C3%A4ngers-on-the-dark-web-a-large-scale-assessment-on-phishin/) also identifies S2W as an institution. This direct 2019 author-to-organization connection is stronger than a later corporate retrospective, but the **2017 collection predates S2W's reported September 2018 incorporation** (see [2021 company timeline](https://www.ajou.ac.kr/security/board/under-notice.do?articleNo=293151&attachNo=274329&mode=download)). Original university storage ownership or subsequent migration remains unestablished.
2. [S2W founder Changhoon Yoon's June 2023 post](https://medium.com/s2wblog/introducing-darkbert-combating-cyber-crimes-at-scale-with-ai-5821e2ff74e3) identifies him as S2W co-founder/head of R&D, discusses the corporate dark-web data warehouse, and explicitly cites the WWW'19 and NDSS'19 research as workflow antecedents.
3. [S2W's June 13, 2025 XARVIS FAQ](https://s2w.inc/en/resources/849) claims all collected data is retained without deletion, with over a decade of historical accumulation. Its [current XARVIS product description](https://www.s2w.inc/en/products/xarvis) explicitly describes preserving **original-state posts and files at capture time** and providing a chronological browser with modification histories. These claims improve the case for asking about **capture-time fidelity** but do not establish preservation of 2017 original *HTTP response entity bytes*, especially where collection passed through HTML rendering, text extraction, or canonicalization. They also do not establish that the 2017 academic crawl ever entered XARVIS. S2W's [public contact route](https://s2w.inc/en/contact) has **not** been used.

This is a higher-specificity **owner-action candidate**: a named commercial custodian whose authorship is documented by the original 2019 paper, with advertised original-state retention capabilities. It is **not** a publicly accessible raw archive, an established 2017-corpus transfer, or permission to query the service.

## Exact owner-authorized next decision

With **separate owner authorization**, S2W/KAIST may be asked only the following bounded metadata/locally executed equality questions:

1. Did the archived **January–July 2017 WWW'19** crawl, or the overlapping **January 2017–March 2018 MFScope** records, enter the current S2W/XARVIS custodial system? Are original URL, UTC capture time, individual response boundaries and unmodified HTTP response entity bytes retained for that period? A text-only archive is insufficient for definitive p56 digest exclusion.
2. Can the custodian query only whether the original LP2 distributor hostname **ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion** was among captured URL hosts? This is a byte-fidelity/coverage control **only**, not a restriction on the unknown target host.
3. If historical bytes survive and custodian policy allows, can the custodian **locally perform one exact SHA-512 equality test across the original 2017 per-page body objects** against the one printed p56 target (see linked preimage analysis), disclosing only match/no-match, and, if a match exists, capture UTC, original URL, body length and byte-hash proof? Alternative 512-bit algorithms should require separate explicit authorization. Do not request bulk page bodies, identities, visitor logs or unrelated onion content.

The [existing owner-action #784](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/784) currently contains two other archive custodians. This route can become its **third, distinct optional custody query**; no contact was made by this investigation.

## Outcome and limit of the decision

**#787 is complete as a public-availability gate.** The publicly available record yields a large documented 2017 crawl and a **concrete unqueried potential custodian**, but not an accessible original-byte archive or digest preimage. Pursue only an authorized custody inquiry if the owner judges the lead worth the cost. Do not reopen generic hash-serialization sweeps or assert a negative about what an uninspected private corpus contains. On a byte-authenticated p56 match, recover the associated historic instruction/key and run one original-cipher inverse with held-out evidence; without one, the puzzle remains unsolved.
