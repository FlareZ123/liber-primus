# p22 three-crossbar shaft: possible manuscript Ogham `ᚒ` (Úr), unselected

**Status, 15 November 2026, Agent40:** A tentative **iconographic** identification, not a decrypted page, source-authenticated key, or chosen reversible cipher. Keep this separate from [the closed three-rail route #1146](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/1146) and [the three-state-source gate #1164](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/1164). It should not become a new exhaustive Ogham key search.

## What is actually visible in the published original

The original 2014 combined-archive scan [`resources/liber-primus/39.jpg`](../resources/liber-primus/39.jpg) is LP2 physical p22, the end of the p15–22 illustrated chapter. Beneath the last black rune line and its small red terminal punctuation, and above a full-width drawing of a recumbent human, is a **black horizontal rod/shaft with three prominent thick upright bars crossing it**. The right-hand end tapers to a pointed, arrow-like shape; several disconnected black dots are scattered around the rod. At 2400×3600 original pixel resolution, the distinctive rod lies approximately within x590–1900,y2150–2600. The 3 crossing bars themselves are visibly **unequal in width and spacing** and drawn organically. The original does not label them or supply a numbered sequence of 3 glyph values.

A quick literal artwork-duplication control loaded **all 75 original images** from the maintainer original archive and ran grayscale `cv2.matchTemplate(TM_CCOEFF_NORMED)` on a fixed staff-only crop of scan39, `[x620:1770, y2210:2550]`, after 1/4 area downscale. Original scan39 self-match **0.98504**; highest different scan **0.43700** (scan08). This is a reproducible exact-parameter observation that the sampled image geometry is distinctive, *not* a calibrated or language-relevant p-value. The repeated source trees pp8–14 and identical floral borders pp40–55 have their own separate audits. No image was altered.

## Stronger first-party artwork context and competing interpretation

The original *same book* supplies a far more constrained comparator: physical LP2 pp0–2 (archive `17.jpg`–`19.jpg`) have large vertical **three-bar crosses at both page margins**. The left and right crosses on p0 are **the same ink illustration centrally rotated exactly 180°** (source thresholded ink overlap **0.98747722**), and the cross frame is reused unchanged across p0–2. This was already demonstrated quantitatively in [the p0 source cross-plate report](../im-results/lp2-p0-p2-orthodox-cross-source-plate-gate.md) and [the original inverted-cross source readout](../im-results/p0-rotated-cross-source-readorder-gate.md). A rotated visual comparison makes p22's horizontal triple-crossbar rod visibly part of **the same design family**, though the p22 rod itself does not pixel-register as the exact same asset under the crude normalized crop. In a short source-only binary comparison of p22's shaft with quarter-turned p0 margin crosses, best foreground IoU was about **0.47** under 1/4ish normalized crop matching, with no exact-copy claim. The prior analysis found an independently documented **Orthodox or papal triple-cross** iconographic reading and quotes the contemporary artwork inventory assigning that class to **both the pp0–2 and p22 ornaments**. This is a stronger explanatory competitor than treating p22 as a lone new script letter.

The original 2014 book also reuses LP1's mirrored **reclining human** image at p22, as established in [the independent cross-release illustration audit](../im-results/lp1-lp2-crossrelease-artwork-raster-copies.md). These two image-reuse patterns weaken any assumption that the shaft and person were composed together as a single coded inscription. Their source-exact reuse has **not** produced an LP2 unknown key or plaintext.

## One independently documented historical script correspondence

Manuscript **Ogham** can be written **horizontally**, with a stemline and **vertical vowel strokes crossing it**. The Ogham letter **`ᚒ` U+1692, UR (Úr)** is represented by **three perpendicular marks through the stem**. Thus a human noticing the black rod on p22 could plausibly ask whether the three tall bars are an *Ogham-shaped U*, a Celtic cipher sign or an allusion to a second alphabet. This interpretation is available without fitting a GP29 key, because the number and orientation of Ogham vowel strokes are standardized. The Unicode Standard states that manuscript horizontal Ogham is read left-to-right, that vowels are rendered with vertical strokes, and that special feather marks may delimit texts. Sources:
- [Unicode, Ogham script description](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode12.1.0/ch08.pdf) and [the U+1680–U+169F code chart](https://unicode.org/charts/PDF/U1680.pdf);
- [M. S. University of Glasgow OG(H)AM project on actual historical feather marks](https://ogham.glasgow.ac.uk/index.php/2024/08/23/feather-marks/);
- [1882 Burton, *The Ogham-Runes and El-Mushajjar*, descriptions of crossed-stem and vowel groups](https://www.burtoniana.org/pamphlets/1882-The-Ogham-Runes-and-El-Mushajjar/burton-1882-runes.pdf).

**Crucial objections:** the existing **three-bar Christian cross** classification fits the repeated source art without inventing an additional alphabet. The three original brush strokes are **much wider and less uniformly spaced** than a normal printed Ogham U; the rod tapers into a **right-pointing head** that may simply be a depicted weapon, tool or key, rather than the manuscript Ogham end feather (which often points back). The floating dots have no unambiguous Ogham assignment, and the underlying recumbent figure is an illustration rather than signed text. Ogham also has only 20 principal plus six supplementary characters, whereas the actual GP alphabet used in LP2 has **29**. A simple one-to-one 29→26 substitution would be information-losing unless extra source-selected semantics are supplied. The bare 3-stroke visual correspondence is therefore insufficient to say Cicada deliberately depicted Ogham, let alone that it selects GP29 decipherment.

## Complete-inscription gate on the original p22 engraving (15 November 2026)

**Decision:** The Ogham reading does **not** produce a complete source-authored inscription. The original yields one very loose `ᚒ` / Úr-shaped three-crossbar graph but no second unambiguously placeable Ogham letter, word separator, start marker or key. This satisfies the bounded [Director #1168](https://github.com/FlareZ123/liber-primus/issues/1168) decision with a source-exact measurement; it closes the speculative decipherment route without challenging the artwork's existence.

On the SHA-256-pinned physical p22 `39.jpg` (`e232eb4649f89125d0edeab7022cbe189185d46ccbf0823fd964b6e71e9bafe7`), take the **fixed source crop x=555:1845, y=2130:2670** at native 2400×3600 scale. The central thick ink structure joins the long shaft to **three prominent transverse uprights** with irregular widths and spacings. The rightmost shaft tapers and includes diagonal ink. ### Historical graph test and rival explanation

The **2010 Unicode Standard v6** [chapter 8, Ogham](https://fulmanski.pl/zajecia/wdi/zajecia_20142015/materialy/UnicodeStandard-6.0.pdf) documents horizontal manuscript Ogham, its left-to-right reading, vowels as **strokes crossing the stemline**, and optional `᚛`/`᚜` feather delimiters. The older historical [Burton (1882)](https://www.burtoniana.org/pamphlets/1882-The-Ogham-Runes-and-El-Mushajjar/burton-1882-runes.pdf) records the cross-stroke form. Exactly **three actual crossings** could spell the one vowel `ᚒ` (U/Úr), but the separate dots float above the long line and supply no next conventional vowel sequence. Treating a set of 3+4 dots as two more vowel letters would require moving them onto the stemline and imposing ad hoc group breaks; likewise the pointed terminal is not demonstrably a historical Ogham feather mark. Nothing on the source p22 page names Ogham or marks a textual start/end.

The same original manuscript uses the three-bar **Orthodox/papal Christian cross family** on pp0–2, including an exact 180° image rotation, making decoration a source-grounded competing explanation. The p22 artwork is thick, freehand, and asymmetrical; its organic sloping shaft and isolated ornament dots also suit graphic decoration rather than any text. This cannot be resolved into a standard multi-glyph Ogham inscription by the declared historical stroke rules.

**Cryptanalytic disposition:** No instruction, numbered key, selected GP29 interval, reversible symbol-changing operation, or held-out p20 plaintext follows. The 131-rune p22 original text was left unchanged. The failed standalone inscription gate is distinct from the previously closed three-rail, three-state, p22 mirror, or arbitrary Ogham substitution tests; no new classical cipher sweeps are justified. Reopen only if a new period-authentic independent Ogham selector identifies how every drawn mark belongs to a **complete** source inscription, or supplies a decoder and unseen authentic plaintext.
