## Why this one
- #2750 is the highest *conditional discrimination per known rune*: an independent signed difference at any of four p49:62..65 → p51:0..3 pairs separates all five frozen rows. At the first pair the predictions are 0 (no jump),17 (p40+1),18 (p42+2),10 (p46+2),12 (p40+3). Any other residue rejects the restricted family. But no actual historical source for either known rune is identified. Even selecting a row does not yield an absolute key (no-jump row still has 899 keys). Do not ask a librarian for an unidentified plaintext.
- #2749/#41 require an original first-party typed ring-to-consumer instruction (ring/order/radix/serialization/operation/all76-rune receiver). This would be highly valuable, but no named missing object with locator is established. The literal p57 wording "WE MUST SHED OUR OWN CIRCUMFERENCES" licenses the geometric peel; it does not establish the receiver. Do not ask for an imagined signed message.
- #2752 has a named printed book, edition, illustration and one specimen independently registered to LP2. Acquisition can determine whether the original page itself contains an unambiguous further literal selection or whether the source is merely artwork. It is lower immediate cipher discrimination than a genuine #2750 witness, but much higher actionability.

### Exact scan target: request the text/image pair, probably p.89–90

A 1982 review of the 1976 German edition says the identification section begins at p.87 and runs through p.311; each text page describes 5–10 species and the corresponding facing image page shows those species in typical positions. If the reported p.90 source image is an illustration page, its identifying text/caption is therefore likely on the preceding page (probably p.89). This is **German-edition comparator evidence**, not proof that Dutch pagination is identical.

For every library or seller request, ask for the complete text/image pair around p.90—specifically p.89–90 if the Dutch edition uses the same pagination—plus the title/copyright pages.
