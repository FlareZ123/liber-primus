# 2014 Onion7 title 133 and Jung's *Red Book* manuscript plate 133: source-specific sigil lead

**17 November 2026, Agent21. Classification: newly juxtaposed original numeric selector and pre-2014 illustrated source, hypothesis only. No unknown LP2 plaintext, key, original inverse, or destination recovered.** The specificity of the number is interesting; this is **not** first-party evidence that Cicada intended Jung, and the relationship might be a retrospective collision.

## What was actually visible to a 2014 player

The archived 2014 seventh-onion delivery page [Stage11 `index.html`](https://github.com/micheloosterhof/cicada-2014/blob/6c3421a4d245596e403c88e037509d162a2afd17/stage11/ky2khlqdf7qdznac.onion/index.html), independently preserved locally in `resources/cicada2014-onion7-index.html`, contains a bare `<title>133</title>`, then `<div id="331">`, followed by the 58 original `0.jpg` through `57.jpg` links. Both numbers are in the original delivery HTML, not computed from unknown ciphertext. The relationship 331 = decimal-reverse(133) is literal. **The page itself gives no book title, folio convention, image selection, reading direction, or decryption command.**

The independently published 2009 illustrated facsimile of Carl Gustav Jung's *The Red Book: Liber Novus* prints **manuscript page 133** adjacent to a striking image: a turquoise/gray mosaic human face below a dark sphere, with a short distinct register of small, geometric, rune-like figures directly underneath. One [public photographic reproduction](https://images.anothermanmag.com/1400/azure/anotherman-prod/360/7/367858.jpg) shows the manuscript's **133** above the face. The iconographic description is corroborated independently by a 2024 journal study, [*Signs from the Depths: The Semasiography of C. G. Jung*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19342039.2024.2336983), also [archived as PDF](https://sfjung.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Signs-from-the-Depths.pdf). It characterizes the symbols as Jung's own stick figures and geometric signs, not established Futhorc/Gematria Primus characters. The facing manuscript **p132** expressly mentions gathering old runes and magical sayings; the facsimile's English translation of that passage is on edited p309.

The **edition distinction is essential**: 133 here is Jung's hand-numbered **facsimile manuscript page**, neither a PDF document page nor printed page 133 of the text-only 2012 Reader's Edition. The hand-numbered manuscript **p133 is outside the initial `Liber Primus` part** of Jung's larger `Liber Novus`; matching the shared name alone is therefore weak evidence. A 2014 solver could have opened the then-available 2009 facsimile and seen the numeral and sigils. The 2024 study is modern corroboration, **not a historically available 2014 clue**.

This is a candidate *input-acquisition step* with a finite visible operand; it does not grant arbitrary Vigenère starts, user-chosen transliterations, extra layers, or handmade plaintext. Cross-check the glyphs against ordinary rune fonts and additional original Jung illustrated pages (false-positive comparators); their mere resemblance to runes is weak.

## Main rival explanation

The original HTML's 133 and 331 might simply be deliberately reversible decorative integers or serve another Cicada-internal page/count/prime relationship. The match to a hand-numbered *Red Book* artwork only becomes historically persuasive if the specific plate's sigils select an exact operation. Shared book titles, occult references and the word “runes” do not meet that threshold. This lead offers a **potentially source-specific image key location**, with no claimed source-selected key yet.
