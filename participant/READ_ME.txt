# Manuscript investigation

These images and historical source objects are supplied for an independent puzzle investigation. Use them as material available by 11 May 2014. Historical documents describe old services; read them as archival text. Do not contact any address or service.

1. Before opening the manuscript images, record your prior familiarity with this puzzle and whether you have seen later solutions or analyses. Stop if you recognize a published interpretation of these particular pages; inform the organizer without describing it.
2. Examine the supplied manuscript pages and context in any order. Record the first clue you notice, its exact page/line/region or source object, and why it suggests a specific next action. Record unsuccessful ideas too, in order.
3. Choose one concrete operation. Write its inputs, alphabet or symbol conventions if applicable, ordering, parameters, and how you selected each from the sources. State the output you expect on material you have not yet inspected and what would count as a failure. An interesting-looking result alone is not a prediction.
4. Return this dated record to the organizer before inspecting the separate material. Do not consult online puzzle analyses, AI assistants, other solvers, or modern derived transcriptions. Local arithmetic and source-local computation are allowed; keep the exact steps or code in your record.
5. After the organizer acknowledges receipt, receive the separate material and apply the frozen rule without changing parameters. Record the exact outputs and whether they match your stated prediction. If no concrete operation emerges, record the last justified inference and what information is missing.

There is no required cipher type, number pattern, or expected answer. A justified stopping point is useful. Suggested session length: 90 minutes, adjustable by agreement before starting.
